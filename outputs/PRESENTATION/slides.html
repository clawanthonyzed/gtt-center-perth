How a visit works

  1. Book and pay online, 2 to 4 weeks ahead.
  2. Arrive fasted 10 minutes before your draw (06:50 for the 07:00 draw). Check in and consent.
  3. Draw 1 (about 5 minutes), then the glucose drink.
  4. Wellness service 1 (30 or 45 minutes). It never interrupts a draw.
  5. Draw 2 at 60 minutes, then wellness service 2 (30 or 45 minutes).
  6. Draw 3 at 120 minutes.
  7. Relax in the lounge, have something to eat or drink from the Private Refreshment Counter, or continue to pre-booked standalone services.
  8. One courier pickup per day. GTT does not diagnose or interpret results.

Roster = phlebotomists + therapists. Staff are called in to match confirmed bookings. Margin of safety: 9.7 clients a day. If every booking stacks in pairs from 07:00, break-even rises to 12.655.

Why 8 therapists at about 12.6 clients?
Staffing follows overlap, not headcount. Each client has two services and pairs start 25 minutes apart. At about 12.6 booked clients neighbouring double-booked slots put four first services in the room at once, so 8 therapists are needed. If second-chair bookings fall in alternate slots, 6 are enough.
Fill rate is the central risk
The plan assumes every released morning place fills. Each 1% of fill at capacity is worth about A$1,185 a month. At 85% fill profit is about +A$17.4k a month; at 70% GTT roughly breaks even. That is why the waitlist matters.

What is GST-stress?
The safety test that assumes the Australian Taxation Office treats the whole package as taxable, so one eleventh of every sale is paid away as GST. GTT still earns A$23,443 a month. First-year net under the test: A$254,038 (rent A$10,500) to A$281,311 (rent A$8,000). Any GST-free element the ATO confirms would be extra profit.
What is the A$422k a year steady state?
At full capacity, 18 expecting mothers every morning, GTT earns A$35,186 a month in net operating profit (24.6% margin). Twelve months of that is A$422,237 a year.
