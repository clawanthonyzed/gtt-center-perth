Circulation notes

  • Client route: main entry, arrival lobby, reception and lounge, then the clinical corridor to a collection room door. Toilets, massage, beauty, hair and nails all open from the lounge or the clinical corridor.
  • Staff route: staff entry (north wall) straight into the back-of-house corridor and staff room; the staff room also opens to the clinical corridor, so staff can reach clients without using the back-of-house corridor.
  • Dirty route: collection room back doors, along the staff-only corridor, into the dirty zone and out through the service door. It never crosses the lounge, reception or the clinical corridor.
  • Refreshments and specimens: the Private Refreshment Counter (glucose drink service) is 3.6 m from the nearest collection room wall and 9.6 m from the clinical waste room, with no shared wall. Each collection room has its own under-counter specimen fridge.
  • Supervision: the lounge sits beside the clinical corridor so staff can see clients waiting after the glucose drink (the public hospital guideline stresses staff oversight of glucose tolerance waiting areas).
  • Shared amenities: one accessible WC and one standard WC serve clients and staff. Number and type are subject to NCC Part F4 and the building surveyor (TO CONFIRM).

Open questions for the architect

  1. Collection room size: the plan uses 19.0 sqm each (middle of the 18 to 20 sqm range). Please confirm with the pathology partner. The venue documents note the recovery recliner allowance of about 2 to 3 sqm may sit on top of 18 sqm, which would mean 20 to 21 sqm. The pathology partner room specifications (WDP, PathWest) have not been seen in full. TO CONFIRM.
  2. Sanitary facilities: the plan has one accessible unisex WC and one standard WC, shared by clients and staff (about 10 treatment and collection staff plus a venue manager, and clients and companions). The National Construction Code Part F4 (Table F4D4a and the sharing rule in F4D4) decides the count and whether separate male and female facilities or unisex facilities are acceptable. The building class (5, 6 or 9b) must be set by the building surveyor. TO CONFIRM.
  3. Dual egress: the public hospital guideline notes enclosed collection rooms may need two exits depending on jurisdictional policy. Each room has two doors (client side and back-of-house). Confirm whether the pathology partner or WA Health requires this.
  4. Second centrifuge, second recovery recliner and second specimen fridge: the plan shows one fridge and one recliner in each room (two of each) and space for a centrifuge in each room. The procurement register carried one of each, so the second units are quantity changes to be priced. TO CONFIRM.
  5. Massage and beauty bays are drawn 2.2 m wide by 4.8 m (10.7 sqm), below the 12 and 11 sqm in the room schedule. Confirm this is acceptable or trade lounge area for bay size.
  6. Nails sit beside reception on the east wall with a local exhaust duct to the outside wall; the open-plan fume control method needs the ventilation engineer and WorkSafe.
  7. Accessible WC: door swing, clear floor space and grab rails to be set to AS 1428.1 by the architect. Doors drawn at 1.0 m.
  8. Fire exits, travel distances and the use of the service and staff doors as exits need the building surveyor.
  9. Reception is drawn open-plan; the venue documents describe it with solid walls. Confirm.
  10. The plan is a typical 18.5 m x 15.6 m rectangle. A real site will change room positions; the principles (separate dirty route, equal collection rooms, staff route not through the dirty zone) should carry over.
