## Verdict in two lines

1. On the text of the Bill as introduced, the 30 percent minimum tax lands on trustees of discretionary trusts, so a company that pays 25 percent tax and franked dividends straight to individual shareholders sits outside it. It is a Bill, not law, and it can change in the Senate.
2. I cannot give you a legal certainty or a "definitive proof". I can give you what is verified, what is reasoned, and the list your accountant must sign off. The one trap is who owns the GTT shares.

## Reasoned

**(b) How Route B is built.** GTT Center Perth Pty Ltd pays 25 percent company tax (base rate entity, E). After tax, locked profit is A$19,973.98 a month (R). Franked dividends go direct to Anthony and his partner as individual shareholders. No trust sits in the chain.

**(c) Why the trust Bill should not reach it.** The charge is on a trustee of a discretionary trust. A company is not a trust estate. Shareholders hold shares personally. So no trustee receives the profit and the 30 percent floor has nothing to bite on. This follows Catherine's reading of the Bill text. **Trap:** if the shares were ever held by a discretionary trust such as the YETI Holding Trust, the dividend becomes trust income and the floor can apply from 1 July 2028. Keep shares in personal names.

**(d) Why repaying the owners' own money is not income.** A shareholder loan is money the owners advanced to the company. Giving back the principal returns what they lent, so it is not wages, not a dividend and not their income. It only works if:
- there is a written loan agreement signed before the money moves;
- the money really came from the lenders, in their own names. If the partner's savings are in it, she is a lender for her share;
- the company keeps a running loan account and never repays more than the balance;
- no interest is paid unless the accountant sets a defensible rate (interest on prepaid money is locked out of every financial);
- the start-up money is not left as "loan or share capital, decide later". Capital repayment has different rules (question 1).

**(e) Division 7A points.** Owners lending to the company is outside it. Watch: the company lending to either owner, paying a private bill, or private use of a company asset. Those become deemed unfranked dividends unless repaid by the lodgment day or put under a written agreement at the benchmark rate. Never pay the owners a "loan" from the company to get cash out early.

**(f) Dividends and franking.** A company can pay a dividend only out of profits and while it can pay its debts (Corporations Act solvency and "assets exceed liabilities" test, U, accountant to confirm). The franking account builds from company tax actually paid, even while the loan is repaid. In my timeline tax first falls due in month 7 and the loan clears in month 20, so credits will already have built up for the first dividend in month 21 (E, tax timing NOT MODELLED). Credits are 25/75 of a fully franked dividend at the 25 percent rate (E; the rate depends on the company's turnover and passive income tests, U).

**(g) Founder personal position.** Closed for this venture (Round 23, docs/GTT-READ-FIRST.md). Anthony is owner and director with 0 operational hours. Nothing further is written here.
