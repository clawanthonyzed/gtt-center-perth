ELOWEN -- Monogram Test

Single-letter E, lightA capital E alone is one of the most generic letterforms in the alphabet -- three horizontal strokes off a vertical spine, no distinctive curve or counter to anchor it as ownable. It could belong to almost any brand.
E
Two-letter EL, lightTested honestly, not adopted: an EL ligature is legible but arbitrary -- it doesn't correspond to a natural syllable break the way ELOWYN's tested "EY" did, and building one anyway would repeat exactly the artificial-competitiveness pattern this brief explicitly warned against.
EL
Verdict: no monogram is recommended for ELOWEN. Neither the single letter E nor a constructed two-letter mark earns its place the way SOLENA's S does -- forcing one here would be decoration without a real reason, not restraint. The wordmark alone is the correct, complete identity for this name. This is not a weakness relative to Solena's optional monogram -- it is the more disciplined outcome, and entirely consistent with this system's own governing principle that restraint is the point.
