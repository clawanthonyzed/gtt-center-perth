Chapter 2

Business Concept

What GTT Center Perth actually is, in plain terms.

GTT Center Perth is a concept for Western Australia's first premium wellness venue built around the Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT) — a mandatory pregnancy screening test undergone by an estimated ~33,570 WA women per year Verified (KPMG analysis of ABS Births, Australia 2024). Instead of a bare pathology waiting room while fasting, a woman referred for a GTT books into GTT Center Perth and spends the test window receiving massage, nails, brows, or hairdressing.

The venture runs two distinct businesses under one roof, sharing a physical space and a brand:

The morning (AM) — GTT + wellness

Blood draws happen on-site, performed by phlebotomists employed by GTT Center Perth, operating under a NATA-accredited pathology partner's umbrella (WDP, priority partner — active, ongoing conversation, not yet a signed agreement Waiting on third party). GTT Center Perth does not diagnose, interpret, or hold its own pathology accreditation. The wait between draws is filled with wellness services, sold as one of two fixed packages, venue/lounge access bundled free.

Locked launch prices, not yet externally market-tested — per docs/CURRENT-STATE.md §2.

The afternoon (PM) — standalone wellness venue

The PM shift is a standalone wellness venue in its own right, not an add-on restricted to GTT patients. Any woman — pregnant or not, GTT client or not — can book massage, nails, hair, or beauty directly, the same way she would book at any day spa. PM offers both individual a-la-carte services and set/fixed PM packages (PM Refresh, PM Restore).

Operating rhythm

Saturday reuses the AM+PM model as a bolt-on shift. Sunday is closed — a reviewable business choice, not a legal requirement (the WA Retail Trading Hours Act exempts this business type from restriction).

Explicitly not current scope

Three items are sometimes conflated with this venture's launch scope and must be kept separate:

  • In-house pathology accreditation — investigated and rejected (12–18 month accreditation timeline incompatible with a faster path to operating). GTT Center Perth partners with an already-accredited provider instead.
  • 3D keepsake ultrasound scanning — a disclosed future/Phase 2 income stream under consideration, not part of current launch scope, and never framed as diagnostic.
  • A landlord fit-out contribution — a possible upside if negotiated, never assumed or counted in any headline startup-capital figure.

Chapter 7

GTT Service

What the test is, who is responsible for what, and exactly how a client moves through it. The clinical process this whole venture is built around.

What the GTT is

The Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT/GTT) screens for gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM). It is offered to all pregnant women at 24–28 weeks' gestation as the Australian standard of care, per the ADIPS 2014 Consensus Guidelines and FIGO 2015 (75g glucose, 2-hour test). Verified This mandatory, non-discretionary demand — roughly 2–2.5 hours of fasting waiting time per test, undergone by an estimated ~33,570 WA women per year (Chapter 3) — is the market gap this venture exists to fill.

Who is responsible for what

GTT Center Perth providesPathology partner (WDP, priority) provides
Venue, ACSQHC-compliant collection roomNATA accreditation umbrella (Licensed Collection Centre)
Employed phlebotomists (Cert III/IV Pathology Collection)Phlebotomist credentialing as an approved collector under the partner's accreditation
Patient scheduling and the wellness experience around the drawsSpecimen processing and laboratory analysis
Collection room equipment, sharps/biohazard disposal, specimen refrigerationResult reporting to the referring doctor/midwife, Medicare billing
Operational boundary, stated plainly GTT Center Perth does not diagnose, interpret, or communicate results to the patient — all clinical decisions are made by the referring doctor or midwife. GTT Center Perth also earns zero revenue from pathology billing; every dollar of AM revenue comes from the wellness packages sold alongside the test, not the test itself. A phlebotomist must be registered as an approved collector under the partner's own NATA accreditation before performing any collection — a credentialing precondition, not a formality.

Referral requirement

Every client brings a GP or midwife's pathology referral form (paper or eReferral) and her Medicare card. Reception collects and copies both at check-in; the phlebotomist verifies patient name, date of birth, and gestational age against the referral before the first draw. Needs research The specific WA regulatory basis for this requirement (beyond standard pathology-collection practice) was not re-confirmed with a primary citation this round — carried forward from docs/gtt-clinical-protocol.md as an established operational practice, not independently re-sourced against current WA Health guidance this pass.

Fasting requirement

10–12 hours fasted, water only — sourced from PathWest's own 2024 patient instructions (the current version; an earlier 2019 PathWest document stated a 16-hour maximum, superseded). Verified No food, tea, coffee, juice, soft drink, gum, or nicotine from the fasting start time; no exercise the morning of. Communicated via booking confirmation, 48-hour and 24-hour SMS reminders (personalised fasting cutoff), and a 1-hour day-of SMS.

Records and what the centre does not retain

Retained 7 years: collection log (name, DOB, gestational age, draw times, collector ID), specimen dispatch log, adverse event register, fridge temperature log, referral form copies.
