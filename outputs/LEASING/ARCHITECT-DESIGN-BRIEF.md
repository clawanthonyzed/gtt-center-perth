# GTT Center Perth: Architect Design Brief

**Status:** Draft for Anthony's approval, 2026-10-18. Written by Idea Lobster from repo documents. This is a brief, not a drawing. No agent will produce a floor plan (Anthony, 2026-10-18). Every layout decision belongs to the registered architect.

This is general knowledge, not your personal documents. Verify with a qualified adviser before acting.

## 1. What we are building

A **luxury day spa and maternal sanctuary** in Perth, WA, for expecting mothers. It runs a two-hour glucose tolerance test morning inside a warm, calm spa setting, and operates as a day spa and hair and nail studio in the afternoon.

**The feeling we want:** natural, soft, earthy, feminine, private. A place a mother is glad to spend two hours in.
**The feeling we do not want:** a hospital, a pathology collection centre, or a clinic waiting room. Clinical items must be present and compliant, but hidden or quiet in the design.

## 3. Required spaces

| Space | Brief |
|---|---|
| Blood collection rooms | **Two rooms, identical size, 19 sqm each** (the middle of the 18 to 20 sqm range given by the pathology partner brief; the partner must confirm). Solid walls and a door. Each has: recovery recliner, collection chair, **under-counter specimen fridge**, hands-free (sensor) handwash basin, documentation bench, space for a centrifuge. No separate fridge room. Finishes calm and spa-like; clinical surfaces kept cleanable and non-porous behind the styling. |
| Open luxury lounge | The heart of the venue. Large, soft seating for 8 or more, natural light, a view of greenery if the site allows. About 45 sqm in the concept, treat as a guide. |
| Private refreshment counter | For GTT clients only. Kept physically apart from specimens and clinical waste. Fridge, kettle, sink. |
| Massage and beauty bays | Four bays, about 10.7 sqm each as a guide. Partition walls with privacy curtains (not full rooms). A hands-free handwash basin in every bay. Acoustic treatment so the lounge stays quiet. |
| Hair area | Open plan, 4 chairs and 2 backwash basins, sized for 5 to 6 sqm per chair. Kept modest: hair is an ancillary service. |
| Nail area | Open plan, 4 stations and 4 pedicure chairs, 5 to 6 sqm per station. Local exhaust ventilation to the outside for nail products. |
| Reception and arrival lobby | Welcoming desk, two point-of-sale screens, bag and coat storage. |
| Staff room | Lockers, table, kitchenette. **Entered without passing through the dirty zone.** |
| Service area | Clean linen, dirty linen and clinical waste (about 4 sqm dirty), storage for consumables and retail stock. A separate service door for deliveries and waste. |
| Toilets | **One accessible and one standard, shared by clients and staff. No separate staff toilet.** Architect and building surveyor to confirm the count under the National Construction Code. |

## 4. Circulation rules (non-negotiable)

1. Every room opens onto a corridor or the lounge. No room is reached only by walking through another room.
2. Staff reach the staff room, and both collection rooms, without crossing the dirty zone.
3. Dirty linen and clinical waste leave by a staff-only route to the service door and never cross a client area.
4. Clients move from reception to lounge to collection room to a treatment bay without meeting waste, specimens or service traffic.
5. Corridors are as short as they can be. The first concept spent far too much floor on corridors. Please keep circulation efficient.

## 5. Day spa design direction

| Topic | Direction |
|---|---|
| Palette | Warm stone, clay, soft olive, natural timber, warm white. Nothing hospital white, grey or blue. |
| Materials | Real or realistic timber, stone, linen and woven textures. Cleanable surfaces used where infection control needs them, finished to look like the rest. |
| Light | Warm, dimmable. Natural light into the lounge. No fluorescent glare in client areas. |
| Sound and scent | Quiet rooms, acoustic ceilings, no hospital smell. |
| Clinical items | Fridges under benches, sharps and waste in joinery, handwash basins styled as spa vanities. |
| Signage | Soft and welcoming. No "pathology" or "collection centre" signage on the street front. |

## 6. Compliance the architect must design for (from the repo)

| Item | Note |
|---|---|

| Medicare collection centre approval | Held by the pathology partner, not GTT. Rooms must suit the partner's application and floor plan. |

| National Construction Code | Building class, accessibility, toilet count, fire rules. |
| Local council | Planning, signage, change of use. |

## 7. Questions for the architect

1. Can both collection rooms be kept at an identical size and still sit naturally off the lounge?
2. What is the best way to hide the clinical elements inside the spa design?
3. How little circulation floor can the plan use?
4. Is one accessible and one standard toilet enough for staff and clients under the code?
5. What does the layout cost per square metre to fit out, so Anthony can compare it with the repo's fit-out budget?
6. Can the pathology partner's afternoon public collection sessions share the same rooms without clashing with spa afternoons?
