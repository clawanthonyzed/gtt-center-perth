# China Sourcing Strategy for the GTT Center Perth Fit-Out

Prepared 2026-10-16 for use alongside the Concept Floor Plan and the FF&E Schedule ([FFE-SCHEDULE.md](../../../outputs/DESIGN/FFE-SCHEDULE.md)). This document is research and planning only. **No supplier, agent or inspector has been contacted, no enquiry sent, no sample or order placed.** It builds on, and does not replace, the earlier [CHINA-AUSTRALIA-SOURCING-STRATEGY.md](../../architecture/CHINA-AUSTRALIA-SOURCING-STRATEGY.md) (agent comparison, three procurement models), which is still current.

This is not legal, tax or regulatory advice. Where a rule needs a regulator, customs broker, building surveyor or accountant to confirm, it is marked **TO CONFIRM**.

## 1. Recommendation in one paragraph

Import direct from China only the low-risk, high-saving categories (lounge furniture, decor, partitions, curtains and tracks, cabinetry and shelving, signage, massage and beauty beds, pedicure chairs, mirrors, soft lighting) and only after each item's compliance evidence has been checked. Buy locally, or through an Australian sponsor who already holds the approval, anything that is a medical device, a hard-wired or mains electrical fitting, a plumbing fixture, or a fire-rated lining. The reason is simple: when GTT imports a regulated item itself, GTT becomes the legal supplier and carries the compliance duty. The earlier Model C (hybrid: an Australian-based agent for the China-suitable categories, local trades for everything regulated, independent pre-shipment inspection) remains the recommended route.

## 4. Compliance gates, with sources

### 4a. Therapeutic goods (medical devices)

- Therapeutic goods must be in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) before they can lawfully be supplied in, imported into or exported from Australia, unless exempt or authorised ([TGA: overview of supplying therapeutic goods](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/regulations-all-products/overview-applying-market-authorisation/supply-therapeutic-good/overview-supplying-therapeutic-goods-australia), fetched 2026-10-16).
- Every ARTG entry belongs to a sponsor. The TGA says that someone else holding an entry for a product does not make it legal for you to import it. If you import directly from the manufacturer and are first point of supply, **you are the sponsor** and must apply for market authorisation (same page).
- The ARTG contains Class I entries for both electric and manual "examination/treatment chairs" (example sponsor entries: [ARTG entry 1](https://www.tga.gov.au/node/310805), [entry 2](https://www.tga.gov.au/node/277807), listed in a TGA search 2026-10-16). Class I non-sterile, non-measuring devices need an ARTG inclusion application with the manufacturer's Declaration of Conformity before supply ([TGA application and market authorisation](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/application-and-market-authorisation)).
- **Which GTT items are medical devices (planning view, TO CONFIRM with the TGA's classification tool or a regulatory consultant):**
  - Phlebotomy chairs (E01, E02): intended for blood collection, so treat as a medical device. Buy a model already in the ARTG from its Australian sponsor. Do not import a generic chair.
  - Vasovagal recovery chair or couch (E04): treat as a medical device if it is sold as an examination or treatment couch. A plain recliner is not, but a "medical" listing should be checked.
  - Centrifuge and specimen fridge: pathology and laboratory equipment. Supply through the pathology partner or an Australian distributor.
  - Massage and beauty beds, pedicure chairs, lounge furniture: not medical devices when sold for personal care or comfort. Check the supplier makes no therapeutic or medical claims.
  - Defibrillator (AED) and any 3D ultrasound machine: medical devices. Buy only from an Australian sponsor.
- The classification decision is for the TGA and the sponsor. This document does not make it.

### 4b. Electrical items

- In Western Australia, electrical equipment sold or supplied must be registered under the Electrical Equipment Safety System (EESS) and meet the Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) requirements ([WA Government factsheet on selling, hiring or advertising electrical equipment](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2025-09/electrical_appliances_and_equipment_selling_hiring_or_advertising_factsheet.pdf)).
- The EESS requires manufacturers and importers to register as a Responsible Supplier, an Australian legal entity, and to declare that the equipment meets the relevant standards. Products are placed in three levels (low, medium, high risk) ([EESS about page](https://eess.gov.au/about); [EESS registrations](https://www.eess.gov.au/registration/eess-registrations-purpose/)).
- The importer carries the duty. If GTT imports a nail lamp or heated blanket directly, GTT must be registered as the responsible supplier or must use a supplier that is. An overseas CE mark is not an RCM.
- Practical rule: buy mains-powered items locally unless the factory supplies the certificate and the Australian importer of record registers the model. Whether GTT, as a business using equipment, must itself register when importing for its own use is **TO CONFIRM** with Building and Energy WA (eess.gov.au).

### 4c. Fire and building materials (National Construction Code)

- For Class 2 to 9 buildings, the fire hazard properties of internal linings, materials and assemblies must comply with NCC Specification 7. Wall and ceiling linings must fall in group number 1, 2 or 3, and materials can be assessed under AS 5637.1:2015 ([NCC 2022 Volume One, Part C, fire hazard properties](https://cpd.nccb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/c-fire-resistance/7-fire-hazard-properties)).
- What this means for imports: any panel, lining, acoustic board or fixed finish needs a test report that a building surveyor accepts. Loose furniture and curtains are not wall or ceiling linings, but whether fire-retardant fabric is needed for curtains and upholstery is the building surveyor's call for the chosen tenancy. **TO CONFIRM** when a site and surveyor are in place.
- The accessible toilet must meet AS 1428.1; local trades and the architect handle it.

### 4d. Plumbing fixtures (WaterMark)

- WaterMark is the mandatory national certification scheme for plumbing and drainage products. The Australian Building Codes Board runs it, accredited bodies certify products, and certified products appear in the WaterMark product database ([ABCB WaterMark scheme overview](https://watermark.abcb.gov.au/about-watermark/scheme-overview); [WaterMark Product Schedule 2025](https://cpd.nccb.gov.au/sites/default/files/resources/2025/WaterMark%20Schedule%20of%20Products%202025-1.pdf)). Whether a given product needs certification depends on the Schedule of Products and the excluded list.
- Practical rule: basins, taps, clinical sinks, backwash units and pedicure-chair water connections are bought locally, or imported only with a WaterMark licence number shown on the product. A licensed plumber installs them in every case.

### 4e. Timber, biosecurity and packaging

- Regulated timber products (including wood furniture, HS chapters 44 and 94) need an importer due diligence system under the Illegal Logging Prohibition Act 2012. Consignments with a combined customs value of A$1,000 or less are exempt ([DAFF: regulated timber products](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/agriculture-land/forestry/policies/illegal-logging/importers/regulated-timber-products); [DAFF: due diligence for importers](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/agriculture-land/forestry/policies/illegal-logging/importers/due-diligence)).
- Solid wood packaging (pallets, crates) must be treated and marked to ISPM 15, and timber and bamboo articles carry import conditions ([DAFF: ISPM 15](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/timber-packaging/ispm-15); [DAFF: timber and bamboo packaging](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/timber-packaging)). Specify ISPM 15-marked or non-wood packaging in the purchase order.

## 5. Vetting suppliers

1. **Shortlist from verified tiers.** Alibaba Trade Assurance and Made-in-China Audited Supplier are screening aids, not guarantees.
2. **Check the business.** Business licence, factory versus trading company (ask for production photos and a live video walk-through), export history to Australia.
3. **Ask for compliance evidence in writing before any sample**: test reports, RCM or EESS details for any electrical item, WaterMark licence number for any plumbing item, ARTG number for any medical item, fire test reports for linings, timber species and legality statement.
4. **Compare three quotes** for each category with the same written specification (dimensions, materials, finish codes, colour swatch, packaging).
5. **Contract terms in the purchase order**: specification, delivery date, inspection right, replacement or refund for defects, who pays return freight, Incoterm (see section 7).
6. **Red flags**: refuses video call or factory visit, no export record, "CE only" for an electrical item, prices far below peers, requests payment by bank transfer to a personal account.

## 6. Samples and quality inspection

- **Samples:** order one production-standard sample of each furniture and bed type before the bulk order, paid for and kept as the approved reference. Test it for stability, finish, staining, odour and wipe-clean performance. Keep the retained sample to compare against the shipment.
- **Inspection:** use an independent third-party inspection firm (the earlier strategy lists SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, TUV, QIMA and V-Trust; none contacted). A typical sequence is a pre-production check, a during-production check and a final random inspection before the supplier is paid the balance. The earlier strategy recommends this regardless of whether an agent is used.
- **After arrival:** open and check on delivery, photograph damage, and report to the freight forwarder and supplier at once.

## 8. Lead times and a planning order

Reliable dates need supplier quotes; none are available yet. Planning frame, **flagged as an assumption, not a fact**: the long items are the custom ones (cabinetry, signage, partitions), then sea freight of about 2 to 5 weeks door to port (source above), then customs clearance and delivery. Fit-out trades, the architect's drawings, the building permit and the lease must be settled before any custom order, because dimensions depend on the real tenancy. Working back from opening, orders for custom joinery should be released only when the architect's drawings are final.

Suggested sequence once a site is chosen:

1. Architect and building surveyor confirm layout and finishes, then share the compliance list in section 4.
2. Request written quotes and compliance evidence for the section 2a categories (no contact until Anthony approves).
3. Order samples; approve one reference sample per item.
4. Place bulk orders on Trade Assurance (Alibaba) or through an Australian-based agent, with written specification and inspection rights.
5. Third-party inspection before the balance payment.
6. Ship (FOB, FCL if volume suits), clear customs through a broker with the ChAFTA certificate.
7. Deliver to site, check, and keep records (certificates, test reports, invoices) in one compliance file for the building surveyor and insurer.

## 9. Payment protection

- Pay through Alibaba Trade Assurance where available, so payment is held until delivery and covered terms (shipping date and quality) are met ([Alibaba Trade Assurance guide](https://activities.alibaba.com/alibaba/buyer/cp/tradeassurance/guide.php)).
- Pay a deposit (not the full price) and the balance only after independent inspection passes.
- Avoid paying a personal account; match the account name to the company on the business licence.
- Use a card or platform payment where possible, since they carry dispute rights. Bank transfer terms and any card or chargeback rights for business purchases are **TO CONFIRM** with the bank.
- Keep every written quote, specification, message and receipt.
- Payment, signing and any order are Anthony's decision. Nothing in this document authorises any commitment.
