## Verification
| Claim | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The GTT is recommended for all pregnant women at 24 to 28 weeks | VERIFIED for ADIPS. The 2025 ADIPS consensus recommendations state that all women (without diabetes already detected in this pregnancy) should be advised to undergo a 75 g two-hour oral glucose tolerance test at 24 to 28 weeks. | Australasian Diabetes in Pregnancy Society 2025 consensus recommendations, Medical Journal of Australia 2025;223(3): https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2025/223/3/australasian-diabetes-pregnancy-society-adips-2025-consensus-recommendations |
| Earlier testing (10 to 14 weeks, before 20 weeks) applies to women with previous gestational diabetes or early HbA1c 6.0 to 6.4 per cent | VERIFIED (same source) | same |
| RANZCOG recommends it | TO CONFIRM. RANZCOG's current position statement was not retrieved in this pass. Check RANZCOG's gestational diabetes statement (Pregnancy Care Guidelines also referenced) before naming RANZCOG on the page. | https://ranzcog.edu.au |
| GPs recommend it | Supported by routine antenatal practice; TO CONFIRM with the Australian Pregnancy Care Guidelines | https://www.health.gov.au/resources/pregnancy-care-guidelines |
| It is voluntary / optional | Consistent with consent principles (a client may decline any test), but no source in this pass states "voluntary" in terms. TO CONFIRM against the Pregnancy Care Guidelines. "Advised" in ADIPS language is a recommendation, not a requirement. | as above |

Repo flag: Anthony's 7 August email to WDP says the GTT is "a mandated test every pregnant woman in the state needs". That is not supported; do not repeat it in any document.

## Exact wording to use (Page 5)
Verified version, ready now:
"The glucose tolerance test is optional: every expecting mother chooses whether to have it. It is recommended for all pregnant women, usually between 24 and 28 weeks, by the Australasian Diabetes in Pregnancy Society (2025 consensus recommendations), and it is routinely advised by doctors and midwives."

Version to use once RANZCOG's statement is confirmed:
"The glucose tolerance test is optional, but it is recommended for all pregnant women at 24 to 28 weeks by the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists and the Australasian Diabetes in Pregnancy Society, and is routinely advised by doctors and midwives."

Do not say: "required", "mandatory", "compulsory", "must".
