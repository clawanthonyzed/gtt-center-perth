# Doctor Referral Perks and Telehealth GP Partnership (P12a, P15d)

Prepared 2026-10-16 by Grace. For Anthony, Catherine and the solicitor. Not legal advice. TO CONFIRM items go to a health lawyer.

## 1. P12a: doctor referral perks must go. Correct law

Anthony's instruction is right in result. One correction on the citation: sections 129AA and 129AAA of the Health Insurance Act 1973 were the earlier pathology referral provisions; they were replaced by **Part IIBA (prohibited practices), in force from 1 March 2008**. Cite Part IIBA, not s129AA/129AB, and AHPRA only for the practitioner side.

**Health Insurance Act 1973, Part IIBA (ss23DZZIA onward)**
- s23DZZIK: a requester (doctor, dentist, participating midwife, participating nurse practitioner; chiropractors, osteopaths, podiatrists and physiotherapists for imaging) must not ask for or accept a prohibited benefit from a pathology provider or a person connected to one.
- s23DZZIL: a provider (or person connected to it) must not offer or provide a prohibited benefit to a requester. s23DZZIQ and s23DZZIR add criminal offences where a benefit is given or taken to induce requests (up to 5 years imprisonment).
- Civil penalty up to 6,000 penalty units for a body corporate (about A$1.9 million at 2023 values), 600 for an individual.
- "Connected" persons include relatives, employers, employees and members of the same body corporate or partnership (s23DZZIJ). A benefit is prohibited if reasonably likely to induce requests or related to the business of rendering pathology, and is not a permitted benefit (s23DZZIF).
- Sources: Avant Law summary https://avant.org.au/resources/practices-and-pathology-providers-prohibited-practices-and-the-red-book-know-your-obligations ; Department of Health Red Book https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2021/05/the-red-book.pdf ; Mills Oakley https://www.millsoakley.com.au/insights/pathology-approved-collection-centre-acc-rental-agreements-back-under-the-department-of-health-compliance-microscope/ ; PM Lawyers https://www.pmlawyers.com.au/?p=5680. Primary text of ss23DZZIK/L/Q/R was not directly read in this pass (the legislation site blocked automated access). TO CONFIRM against the Act.

**Why GTT must still care although GTT is not itself a pathology provider or requester.** GTT is "connected" to the lab if it is its employee or contractor in the relevant sense, and to a doctor or midwife if it employs, is owned by, or shares a body corporate with them. A GTT perk (free packages, gifts, payments, discounts, sponsorship) given to a referring doctor or midwife, where the lab benefits from the referral, can bring GTT inside Part IIBA as a connected person. Even where it does not, the doctor or midwife is exposed.

**AHPRA and practitioner codes (practitioner side)**
- Health Practitioner Regulation National Law, advertising restrictions (s133): no false, misleading or deceptive advertising, no testimonials, no gifts or discounts without stated terms and conditions, no creating unreasonable expectations. Applies to regulated health practitioners and to those advertising their services, so a GTT advertisement that offers a doctor a perk to refer risks drawing the doctor into a breach. Section numbers and the current AHPRA Guidelines for advertising a regulated health service: TO CONFIRM at https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Resources/Advertising-hub.aspx
- Medical Board of Australia, Good medical practice: a code of conduct for doctors in Australia, sections on financial and commercial dealings and conflicts of interest (doctors must not let a financial interest affect advice or referral). TO CONFIRM the current section numbers.

**What to remove (Page 12):** any free or discounted package, gift, payment, commission, hospitality, vouchers, co-marketing funding or free use of GTT facilities offered to GPs, obstetricians, midwives or their practices in connection with GTT referrals.

**What to keep:** neutral information about the service that is available to everyone (website, brochure, a letter or practice-visit pack describing what GTT is, with no inducement), educational content about gestational diabetes screening that is not branded as a reward, and the patient's own choice of where to go.

## 2. P15d: partnering with telehealth GPs for instant GTT referrals (safe model)

The aim: an expecting mother with no referral can get one quickly. The law does not forbid a patient seeing a telehealth doctor. It forbids money or value moving to a requester to induce requests.

**Safe model**
1. **Independent providers, no commercial link.** GTT lists telehealth services as one of several options for a patient who needs a referral, alongside "ask your own GP, midwife or obstetrician". No exclusivity, no preferred partner paid or rewarded.
2. **No payments or value either way.** No referral fee, no revenue share, no bonus per GTT referral, no free services to the telehealth company, no data sale, no marketing contribution. GTT does not pay for patients to consult the doctor, and the doctor does not pay GTT.
3. **Clinical independence.** The doctor decides, after their own consultation, whether a GTT is clinically appropriate, whether early screening applies (ADIPS 2025), and which pathology provider to request. GTT staff never fill in, pre-tick or suggest a request. The doctor's form is not branded to steer to one lab.
4. **Patient choice.** The patient chooses the doctor and the pathology collection site. The lab on the request is whichever the patient and doctor choose; GTT's lab partner cannot be required.
5. **No connection.** GTT does not employ, own, share premises or a body corporate with, or pay the doctor. No GTT director or officer is a requester in the same entity.
6. **Transparency.** GTT's website says: "We do not receive payment from any doctor or telehealth service, and you are free to use your own doctor." The telehealth doctor's own disclosures are theirs.
7. **Medicare rules for telehealth.** Eligibility for Medicare video consultations (for example, the existing-relationship rule) varies and changes. TO CONFIRM with the chosen service, as the patient may have to pay privately. GTT must not describe any telehealth consultation as free or bulk-billed.
8. **Advertising.** Copy about telehealth is a draft for Anthony's approval and an AHPRA-compliant review. No testimonials about clinical outcomes.

**Avoid**
- Referral or "lead generation" fees, revenue shares, co-branded doctor pages, a doctor on GTT's payroll or advisory board paid for GTT referrals.
- GTT running the telehealth platform itself or booking the consult on the doctor's behalf for reward.
- Offering free packages or discounts to a doctor or midwife who refers, or to staff at GP practices.
- Any statement that the test is mandatory.

**Open points for the solicitor:** whether a simple "information listing" of telehealth providers is itself an advertisement under the National Law; Services Australia and Department of Health guidance on requester-provider arrangements; the effect of any lab–telehealth links on GTT.
