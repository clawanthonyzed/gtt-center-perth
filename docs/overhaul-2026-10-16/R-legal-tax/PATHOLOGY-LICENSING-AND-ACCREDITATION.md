# Pathology Licensing and Accreditation: Collection Room, Medicare ACC, NPAAC and NATA (P11g, P12b)

Prepared 2026-10-16 by Grace. Research for Anthony, Catherine (legal and lease) and the solicitor. Not legal advice. Items marked TO CONFIRM must be settled in writing with the pathology partner, Services Australia, the WA Department of Health or a solicitor.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No WA Health licence category for a private blood collection room was found.** The WA Department of Health Licensing and Accreditation Regulatory Unit (LARU) licenses private hospitals, psychiatric hostels, nursing homes, nursing posts and human reproductive technology practices under the Private Hospitals and Health Services Act 1927. Pathology collection rooms are not in that list. TO CONFIRM in writing with LARU (LARULicensing@health.wa.gov.au) that a room staffed by collectors, or by a registered nurse, is not a "private hospital" or "nursing post".
2. **Medicare approval belongs to the pathology company, not to GTT.** An Approved Collection Centre (ACC) must be owned by an Approved Pathology Authority (APA) that owns an eligible laboratory, on premises the APA owns, leases, sub-leases or is otherwise entitled to occupy. GTT can never hold the approval.
3. **Critical problem: the collector.** For Medicare to pay for the GTT, the specimen must be collected in a way that fits s16A(5AA) and (5AB) of the Health Insurance Act 1973. At an ACC the collector must be the pathologist or an employee of the APA (the one that owns the lab, or the one that referred the specimen). A phlebotomist employed by GTT is not an employee of the APA. The 19 September 2026 decision that GTT employs both phlebotomists directly and the lab only transports and tests (CURRENT-STATE.md) therefore **may not allow the lab to bill Medicare**, which would remove the bulk-billed model. TO CONFIRM urgently, before fit-out spending (see section 5).
4. **Removing "subletting to pathology providers" (P13c) can create a problem** if it means the lab has no right to occupy the room. The ACC application needs a lease, sub-lease or signed occupation agreement naming the APA. The fix is wording, not structure (section 6).

## 4. Can GTT's own employed phlebotomists collect specimens that the lab bills to Medicare, at a site that is not the lab's ACC?

Short answer: **No, not on the face of the law.**

- s16A(5AA): Medicare benefit is not payable for the pathology service unless the specimen was collected (c) by the patient, the treating practitioner, or someone employed or engaged by the treating practitioner (or a medical entrepreneur employing that practitioner), or (d) by a qualifying person at the patient's residence, an approved collection centre, a hospital or an aged care facility, or (e) by hospital or aged care staff.
- s16A(5AB): the qualifying persons in (d) are the rendering pathologist, the referring pathologist, and employees of the APA that owns the lab (or the APA that referred the specimen).
- So a GTT-employed phlebotomist in GTT's own premises, where the room is not the lab's ACC, fits none of the routes. Even inside a lab's ACC, GTT's employee is not an employee of the APA.
- Route (c) (collector engaged by the treating practitioner) is not workable at scale: clients arrive with referrals from many different doctors and midwives.

What structures work (all TO CONFIRM with the lab and Services Australia):

| Structure | How it works | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A. Lab-employed collectors in a lab-held ACC (WDP's "placement" model) | WDP employs and supplies the collector; WDP holds the ACC and the occupation right; GTT supplies the room and venue. | Fits the statute. This was the option on the table in July to August 2026 and was closed on 19 September in favour of GTT employment. Re-open it. |
| B. GTT-employed collectors "engaged" by the lab | GTT employees work under a contract with the lab, lab supervises and takes responsibility. | Unclear. ABLIS wording suggests "engaged" is accepted for approval; s16A(5AB) wording says "employee". Ask Services Australia and the lab in writing. |
| C. GTT staff become lab employees | Collectors hired and paid by the lab, GTT reimburses via a market-value arrangement. | Possible; check Part IIBA (reimbursement must not become a benefit tied to request volumes, and GTT must not be connected to requesters). |
| D. Private pay, no Medicare | Lab bills the client privately. | Not compatible with the "$0 to the client" model; not recommended. |

Cost consequence: the model's AM direct labour includes two phlebotomists (about A$9,075 per month, CURRENT-STATE.md). Under A or C that cost moves to the lab, or is reimbursed. This figure is from the repo and is not changed here; the models agent (M) should rerun the model if Anthony decides.

## 6. P13c: removing "subletting to pathology providers"

Recommended wording for D to replace it: "The pathology partner holds the Medicare collection centre approval and an occupation agreement for the collection room." Do not say "sublet" or "tenant". Keep a written occupation licence in the partner's name at nil or market-value consideration. Without it, the ACC application has nothing to attach. If rent is charged, it must be at market value, assessed independently, and not linked to client numbers.

## 7. One large room with a privacy curtain (model for S18)

- Model: one collection room, one ACC, one APA, two chairs separated by a clinical privacy curtain, a hand wash basin per bay, a fully enclosed room with a door to the corridor, and a reclining couch for faintness (matches floor-plan-concept.md gaps).
- Why it helps: a single room and entrance fits "one application per ACC location" and avoids a second approval; two rooms under one APA at the same entrance is also covered by the relocation rule, so two rooms is not automatically blocked.
- Risk: curtains versus solid walls. NPAAC treatment of privacy partitions is TO CONFIRM against the 2013 document and the lab's assessor. The repo has CURTAIN-COMPLIANCE-CLOSURE.md; the lab's view prevails.
- If the lab insists on two labs, one room per lab with separate entrances is the only layout that fits the lease and floor-plan rules; ask question 4 above first.

## 8. NATA

- NATA accredits the pathology laboratory (ISO 15189, NPAAC standards). Collection centres operated by an APA are assessed against the NPAAC collection requirements under the lab's quality system. GTT, as venue and employer, does not need NATA accreditation in the model where the lab holds the ACC. The repo's own Option B (GTT-owned collection-only accreditation) is not available in law because only an APA can hold an ACC (option-b-collection-centre.md notes this at s23DNBA). TO CONFIRM exact assessment scope with the lab.
- Business versus collection room: the business (GTT) is not accredited; the collection room is covered by the lab's ACC and accreditation.
