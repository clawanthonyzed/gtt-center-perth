## P15d Expecting mothers without a request form (commercial side only)

A glucose tolerance test needs a request from a treating doctor or midwife. Most expecting mothers already hold one from an antenatal visit. For those who do not, the booking page can say: "You will need a request form for the test. If you do not have one, speak to your GP, midwife or obstetrician. Some online telehealth doctors can also issue pathology requests when it is clinically appropriate." The page may point to providers that publicly advertise pathology requests, such as https://nextclinic.com.au/blood-tests-online and https://updoc.com.au/telehealth/telehealth-appointment, as information only and not as a partnership. Rules: no payment, commission or inducement between GTT Center Perth and any telehealth provider or doctor; the doctor decides whether a test is clinically appropriate; we never suggest a clinical decision. Whether named providers may be referenced at all goes to the regulatory workstream.

## P15e Public hospital patients wanting comfort (King Edward Memorial, Fiona Stanley)

Position: an additional choice, not a criticism. Suggested wording: "Public hospital collection centres are an excellent option for your glucose tolerance test. If you would prefer a calm, private setting with somewhere comfortable to sit, a treatment while you wait and a refreshment counter, GTT Center Perth offers an appointment-based alternative. You will need your request form from your doctor or midwife." Do not claim shorter waits, better care or better accuracy, and do not use hospital names or logos in advertising. Targeting: Meta and Google location settings around the King Edward and Fiona Stanley catchments.
