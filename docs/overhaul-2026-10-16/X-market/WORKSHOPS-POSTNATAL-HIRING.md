# After-hours workshops, postnatal strategy, dual-qualified hiring (P16b, P16c, P17c)

## P16b After-hours workshops (evening and weekend)

Why: the morning runs 07:00 to about 13:00 and the afternoon until close, so the lounge is empty on weekday evenings and Saturday afternoons. A workshop uses the same room without new rent. The venture pricing file already lists future services of this kind: prenatal yoga A$25 to A$35 a person, hypnobirthing course A$150 to A$300, lactation consultation A$150 to A$200, pelvic floor physiotherapy A$160 to A$200.

Market check: a Perth obstetric practice, Western Obstetrics in Balcatta, sold "Labour and Birth Basics" at A$150 per couple for two Thursday evenings of two hours (https://trybooking.com/events/landing/1318287, 2025 classes, sold out). St John of God Subiaco runs a four hour monthly Saturday workshop for its own booked patients (https://www.sjog.org.au/our-locations/st-john-of-god-subiaco-hospital/services/maternity/parent-education).

How it works: instructors are independent, insured and qualified, and run their own class content. The venue provides the room, bookings, refreshments and marketing, and shares revenue. A suggested split is 60% instructor and 40% venue, an estimate to test with the first instructor. Example: Birth Basics at A$165 for 12 couples = A$1,980 a course; the venue's 40% = A$792 less refreshments. Four courses a year adds about A$3,000. Workshops are a service for clients and a funnel for the afternoon menu, not a main profit line. Classes are education, not medical advice, and instructors carry their own professional indemnity. Requires: public liability cover that includes visitors, a workshop booking page, and permitted use at the lease.

## P16c Postnatal strategy (due date capture)

1. Capture the due date and preferred contact method on the intake form at booking, as an optional field with a plain-language consent box. Wording: "We would like to send you occasional offers for our treatments before and after your baby arrives. We use your due date only to time them. You can say no and still book. You can withdraw at any time."
2. Store the due date and consent on the booking system record only, not in marketing tools that are not covered by the privacy policy.
3. Automated sequence, only for clients who said yes: a thank-you after the test; a pregnancy massage reminder at 34 to 36 weeks; one message about two weeks after the due date offering a postnatal treatment (for example PM Restore at A$135 or a back and shoulder massage) with a note that bookings suit a partner or grandparent minding the baby; a six month "treat yourself" message.
4. Gift voucher for an expecting mother's friends and family, as a baby shower gift.
5. Stop the sequence immediately on request, or if there is a pregnancy loss or no reply (do not chase). Never use the test result or any health detail in a message.

Privacy note: a due date is health information, and health information is sensitive information under the Privacy Act 1988. Collecting it needs the person's consent, and using it for direct marketing needs consent that is clear, specific and can be withdrawn (Australian Privacy Principles 3 and 7; OAIC guidance https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing and https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/health-service-providers/communications-with-patients). Do not use this wording as legal advice; the privacy policy and consent form go to a solicitor (ITC-45). The regulatory workstream owns the final privacy wording.

## P17c Hiring priority: therapists dual-qualified in massage and nails or beauty

Current plan: 8 morning treatment staff, all hired dual or multi-qualified (4 massage and beauty, 2 nails, 2 hair) so any therapist can fill a gap. Priority order for the Venue Manager's first hires:

1. Massage plus beauty therapists (Cert IV massage with pregnancy training, plus Diploma of Beauty Therapy or equivalent).
2. Massage plus nails (Cert IV massage plus Certificate III Nail Technology) as the second choice, since nails run in the same 30 and 45 minute windows.
3. Hair plus nails or beauty, only where the candidate holds both a trade certificate and a second qualification; otherwise hire hair as a specialist.

Why it matters: a dual-qualified therapist can deliver either service in the same client slot and in the afternoon, which supports adaptive rostering because one person covers two service types and less idle time is paid. The same person can follow a testing client into an afternoon treatment. Advertise as "dual-qualified massage and beauty or nails therapist, casual or permanent, early starts from 06:30 for a 07:00 first client". Pay is set by the Hair and Beauty Industry Award and the Health Professionals Award as verified by the payroll adviser (ITC-37); no premium is stated here. Qualification codes are as in the venture staff profiles (HLT42015 massage, SHB30321 nails). Confirm the availability of dual-qualified candidates in Perth (ITC-51).
