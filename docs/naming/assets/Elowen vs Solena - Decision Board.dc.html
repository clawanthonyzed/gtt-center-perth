Naming decision board · Phase 2 · Two contenders

Elowen or Solena

Equal effort, no inherited conclusion.
New typography and new palettes — the Phase 1 board's fonts and colours are treated as discarded drafts.
Verdict at the end, with the trade-off named.
The ambition

A place every expecting mother wants to go to — and wants to come back to.

The GTT is the reason she finds the house. It is not the reason she stays. So the brand is judged twice: once by a woman who is fasting, nervous and pregnant, and again eighteen months later by the same woman choosing where to spend a Thursday afternoon. Only identities that pass both tests are on this board.

The proposition
2.5 hours she cannot avoid.
Three blood draws taken on site, under a NATA-accredited partner's umbrella.
Between them: massage, hands, brows, hair, breakfast.
Afternoons and evenings open to any woman, pregnant or not.
The waiting is the product.
What Phase 1 got right
Wordmark-led, no symbol-first thinking. The morning-as-itinerary card. Splitting the menu into mornings and afternoons on one card. Elowyn correctly eliminated.
What is discarded
Italiana — beautiful, unreadable below 22px. The sage-and-terracotta reflex. Elowen's near-monastic quiet, which read as thrift rather than restraint. Solena's Mediterranean-holiday cues.
The real question

Which name gives us the strongest foundation for the destination women actively want to visit?

Not which word is prettier. Name + identity + room + feeling + money, assessed together — including how much money each identity needs before it looks like itself.

An inherited English name, so credibility arrives with the word. Reframed for Phase 2 as warm and lived-in rather than austere: Caslon, mushroom and limestone, burgundy ink, oiled timber, lamplight. Less gallery, more townhouse.

Same moment, both brands

Three moments that decide the business: the first ten seconds on the phone, the treatment-room door, and the sentence she reads while fasting at 10pm the night before.
Phone answered
"Good morning, Elowen."
Sounds like a firm with a reputation. Reassuring, slightly formal, unmistakably a place. Almost certainly followed by "sorry — how do you spell that?" less often than Elowyn, but the WA counselling practice of the same name means she may find the wrong business first.
"Good morning, Solena."
Sounds like somewhere she'd want to be. Warmer and easier in an Australian mouth; spelled right first time by almost everyone. But it also sounds like a beauty business, and there are already two Australian skin businesses using the word.
Treatment room
Oak, wool, lamplight, wine-red door
Feels like a beautiful private room in a good building. Depth comes from materials — oak, bouclé, limewash, brass — which is real money at fit-out. Photographs well only when the room is genuinely good.
Plaster, sheer curtain, brass, light
Feels like a luminous, contemporary suite. Depth comes from light and one plaster finish, so a modest room reads expensive. Cheaper to reach 80% of the intended effect — and harder to reach 100%.
10pm, fasting
"Nothing to eat from now, Sarah. We'll look after you in the morning."
Calms. Speaks to the anxiety directly.
"Nothing to eat from now, Sarah. Breakfast is on us at ten."
Distracts, which is arguably better. Gives her something to look forward to instead of something to endure.

The recommendation

Changed from Phase 1
Build SOLENA.

I recommended Elowen in Phase 1. Having developed both properly, I think that was the wrong answer, and I want to be precise about what changed my mind.

Phase 1 optimised for the first visit. Elowen genuinely wins that: inherited credibility is exactly what a nervous, fasting, pregnant woman needs in the first ten seconds. But the first visit is a referral. She is coming because her midwife sent her, and she will come whether or not she loves the name. The visit that actually builds this business is the one she books eighteen months later with no clinical reason at all — and that visit is bought with desire, not reassurance.

Solena wins on desire, and it wins on the things desire runs on: a feed that reads in a thumbnail, a name that survives being said across a table, a leaving box with a product in it, an identity that a woman would mention to a friend without explaining. It also wins the constraint you named — it is the cheaper brand to execute at 80%, because its luxury comes from light and one plaster finish rather than from joinery and materials.

And Solena's weakness is fixable in a way Elowen's is not. Solena risks reading as a beauty business; that is solved by a descriptor line, the pathology partner's name on the door, and staff who sound like clinicians on the phone. Elowen risks reading as flat when under-funded, and the only fix for that is money.

Two conditions on this recommendation. Register and use the descriptor lockup from day one, so the category is never in doubt. And treat the Class 3 cosmetics finding as a real constraint on the product line specifically — sell the hand oil as a house amenity, and get advice before you build a retail range around the word.

The trade-off, stated plainly

You are choosing between borrowed credibility and built desire. If you believe the GTT mornings are the business and the afternoons are a useful sideline, choose Elowen — trust converts referrals. If you believe the afternoons and the returning customer are where the value is, choose Solena. Your own brief says the second thing, twice.

ELOWEN

Strengths: sounds established before it is; strongest at the anxious first appointment; the more credible voice for difficult conversations; scales beautifully by address; the Caslon system is the more usable of the two in print and long copy.

Weaknesses: needs an expensive room to look like itself; weakest on social and in a thumbnail; no viable product asset; an exact-name WA business and an occupied .com.au; and it is the less desirable of the two — nobody books a Thursday afternoon because a brand is trustworthy.

SOLENA

Strengths: the more desirable brand and the better second-visit brand; easiest to say and spell; strongest social and photography at the lowest cost; a real product asset in the foil S; the most commercial energy without losing warmth; cheapest route to looking premium.

Weaknesses: less distinctive as a word; must manufacture the credibility Elowen inherits; a Class 3 cosmetics mark and two interstate skin businesses sit on its retail lane; and the serif-plus-grotesque system is the more common one, so specificity has to come from the palette, the writing and the room.

If you overrule me

Elowen is a defensible choice and I would build it willingly — but only with the fit-out budget it needs, because its whole argument is material quality. If the first venue has to be modest, Solena is the safer choice by a distance.

Next, once you decide

Full identity system: final wordmark drawn rather than set, licensed type, the graphic system, the environment brief for a fit-out designer, the photography shot list, and the writing guide. Nothing here is locked — the wordmarks are treatments and the fonts are close stand-ins for licensed families.
