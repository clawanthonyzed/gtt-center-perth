## 1. What the Existing Research Actually Establishes, With Confidence

`docs/naming/NAME-INVESTIGATION-REPORT.md` and `docs/naming/AUSTRALIAN-TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-BRIEF.md` were built using a direct, live query against IP Australia's own public trade mark search tool (ATMS) — not a third-party aggregator, not a guess. This is a real, meaningful piece of research, not a placeholder. It establishes, with reasonable confidence:

- **SOLENA has a live, registered Australian trade mark: AU 2202790, Class 3 (cosmetics/skincare/fragrance), owned by an individual (Owen Matthews), registered 2022, renewal due 2031.** This is a fact from the register itself, not an inference — high confidence.
- **ELOWEN has three existing registrations, none in Class 44 (the business's core services class) or Class 3** — Class 20 (furniture), Class 33 (wine), and a Class 42 device mark (Merck KGaA, medical/clinical research, filed late 2025). None is a direct conflict with this business's actual services.
- **ELOWYN returned zero registered or pending marks in any class** — the cleanest search result of the three.
- **Real, currently-trading Australian businesses were identified and their live status independently re-confirmed** (Elowen Counselling, Eaton WA; Solena Skin Spa, Griffith NSW) — not assumed still active from an old search, actually re-checked.

This is genuinely useful, credible pre-clearance research — it is not nothing, and it is not guesswork.

## 2. What Genuinely Remains Uncertain, and Why

Two specific limitations, neither fixable by more AI/internet research, however careful:

1. **The live ATMS query has a real ceiling.** It can tell you what's on the register today. It cannot tell you about a pending application not yet published, cannot search for phonetically or conceptually similar marks the way an examiner's trained judgement does, and cannot assess "confusing similarity" — a legal test, not a text-matching exercise. Two marks can look completely different in a keyword search and still be found confusingly similar by an examiner (same market, same sound, same visual impression) — or look identical in a search and still coexist safely because the goods/services are different enough. **This is not a research-effort problem — it's a different kind of judgement that only comes from trademark-law training and experience with how IP Australia's examiners actually decide these cases.**
2. **Class boundary judgement calls.** Whether SOLENA's existing Class 3 registration (cosmetics) would actually block or complicate this business's use of SOLENA in Class 44 (services — massage, hairdressing, beauty, wellness) is not a fact the register itself states. It depends on whether an examiner would find the two uses "confusingly similar" despite being in different classes — a genuinely contestable legal judgement, not something a search tool answers.

## 3. The Actual Legal Risk of SOLENA Specifically, in Plain Terms

**My honest read: manageable, not major — but not zero, and not something I can fully close out myself.**

- SOLENA's Class 3 conflict (AU 2202790) covers **cosmetics, skincare, fragrance products** — physical goods. This business's actual, current, committed retail model is reselling *third-party* brands (Gaia, Weleda, Mustela) — not manufacturing or branding its own Class 3 products under the SOLENA name (`docs/DECISION-LOG.md`, 2026-08-14, confirmed closed). Since the business isn't actually operating in Class 3 in any committed sense, the practical day-to-day collision risk is lower than the existence of a Class 3 registration alone might suggest.
- The business's actual core activity — massage, hairdressing, nails, beauty, wellness services, pathology-adjacent collection — sits in **Class 44**, a different class from AU 2202790's Class 3. Different classes don't guarantee no conflict (see §2's class-boundary point), but they meaningfully lower the risk relative to operating in the exact same class as the existing mark.
- The one thing that *would* turn this from "manageable" to "needs real attention" is if the business's product-line ambitions ever change — i.e. if GTT Center Perth (or whatever name is chosen) ever wants to sell its own branded cosmetics/skincare under the SOLENA name. That's explicitly not the current plan (§7 of `STRATEGIC-REPORT.md`, `docs/DECISION-LOG.md`), so this isn't a live risk today — but it's the one condition that would reopen the question, and is already recorded as a reversal trigger in `docs/DECISION-LOG.md`.
- Separately, a small NSW business ("Solena Skin Spa") already trades under the identical name in a directly overlapping category (skin/wellness treatments) — geographically distant (Griffith NSW vs. Perth WA) and small-scale, which lowers but doesn't eliminate a coexistence question.

**Bottom line: SOLENA's risk is real and worth understanding, not a reason to panic or automatically rule it out. It is genuinely the kind of question where a professional's judgement adds real value over a keyword search — but the underlying facts (what's registered, by whom, in what class) are already known and haven't changed.**

## 4. Useful Additional Certainty vs. Mandatory Requirement

These are two different things, and this brief keeps confusing them if read carelessly — kept explicitly separate here:

- **Useful additional certainty:** a licensed attorney's opinion on the confusing-similarity question (§2) would genuinely reduce uncertainty in a way this session's own research cannot — that's a real, honest benefit, not a sales pitch for using an attorney.
- **Mandatory requirement:** there is no law requiring pre-clearance before *using* a name commercially (as opposed to registering it as a trade mark) — trading under an unregistered name is legal, it just carries the ordinary business risk that someone else's registered mark could eventually force a rebrand, and that this business's own eventual attempt to register could be refused or opposed. **Nothing about GTT Center Perth's current pre-launch, pre-revenue, no-venue-yet stage legally requires an attorney engagement now.** The existing `docs/DECISION-LOG.md` decision to defer trademark clearance to a later funding/validation milestone is a legitimate, defensible business timing choice, not a corner being cut.

## 5. What This Means Practically, Left for Anthony to Decide

- **If the goal is simply to keep operating under a working name pre-launch, with no public commitment, no signage, no filed application** — the existing research is sufficient for that purpose. No attorney action is required right now.
- **If the goal is to lock a final name, put it on signage/a lease/marketing material, or file a trade mark application** — a licensed attorney's opinion on the confusing-similarity questions (especially SOLENA's Class 3/Class 44 boundary) is genuinely the only way to close the remaining uncertainty described in §2. AI/internet research, however careful, cannot substitute for that specific judgement.
- **This is not an all-or-nothing decision.** A lower-cost middle path exists: a single, narrowly-scoped paid consultation (not a full clearance search) asking specifically the Class 3/Class 44 confusing-similarity question for SOLENA, rather than the full 7-question brief already drafted (`docs/naming/AUSTRALIAN-TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-BRIEF.md`) — worth considering if cost is the concern rather than the value of the advice itself.
