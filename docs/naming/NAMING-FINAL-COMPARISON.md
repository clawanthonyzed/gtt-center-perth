# Naming Final Comparison — Elowen vs Elowyn vs Solena

**Status:** Strategic comparison for decision support. Not a final lock. Supersedes nothing — builds directly on `docs/naming/SOLENA-ELOWEN-STRATEGIC-HANDOVER-DECISION-AUDIT.md`, Phase 1 ("Brand Worlds"), and Phase 2 ("Decision Board"), all of which remain the design record of what was tried and why.

**Date:** 2026-08-14

**What this document is not:** a full production brand system for three names. Per instruction, this is a focused, proportionate evaluation — enough to determine whether Elowyn has a genuine, previously-missed advantage, and enough to run a fair, equally-weighted three-way comparison. No interactive design boards were built for this pass (that tooling isn't available in this working session) — the Elowyn treatment below is written to the same categories and the same level of rigor as Phase 1/Phase 2's own boards, so it can sit next to them fairly rather than being a lesser sketch.

---

## 0. Factual finding first: is a name-branded retail/product line actually part of this business's strategy?

This gates how heavily the Solena Class 3 trademark should weigh in the comparison below, so it's answered before anything else, from source documents rather than assumption (per the founder's explicit "do not guess" instruction).

**Finding: no.** `docs/business-plan.md` §6 (Revenue Model) names exactly one retail-adjacent revenue line — "Ancillary (cafe/retail): snacks, drinks, retail products (**Gaia, Weleda, Mustela**)" — and those three names are existing third-party skincare/baby-care brands the venue would stock and resell, not a proposal to manufacture or sell products under the venue's own name. No document in this repository's business-plan, financial-model, or `CURRENT-STATE.md` layer commits to, budgets for, or even raises a name-branded cosmetics/skincare/haircare product line as part of the business model. The only place that idea exists anywhere in this project is as a **design-layer creative proposal** — the Decision Board's "leaving box" hand-oil concept for Solena, explicitly framed there as a $9 amenity item, not a costed retail SKU strategy.

**What this means for the comparison:** the live SOLENA Class 3 mark is a real trademark-register fact regardless of business strategy, and it still needs attorney clearance for **services** use (Class 44) as well as any future goods use — that part doesn't change. But it should not be weighted in this comparison as though it blocks a confirmed pillar of the business, because no such pillar currently exists in writing. It blocks a genuinely attractive, currently-unbudgeted future idea. Both things are true at once, and the matrix in §3 scores them accordingly, separately.

**Founder decision — ANSWERED 2026-08-14: NO / not a priority.** Third-party resale (Gaia, Weleda, Mustela) remains the intended retail model; a name-branded product line is not part of the strategic plan. Per the founder's own instruction, this question is now closed and should not be reopened unless the business model changes. This confirms the Class 3 finding should be weighted in this comparison as a services-use question only (see row 16 of the matrix in §3), not as a threat to a product-line ambition that doesn't exist — the weighting already applied below reflects this.

## 1. Elowyn — Phase 2 Treatment

Phase 1 built a genuine, developed brand world for Elowyn — didone typography, an EY monogram, a plum/bone/steel/bronze palette — and then dropped it after one round, primarily on referral-friction grounds, before the Solena Class 3 finding existed. This section gives it the same "reframe for warmth" treatment Phase 2 gave Elowen and Solena, rather than simply re-presenting Phase 1's cooler original.

**Strategy carried over from Phase 1 (kept because it tested well):** the EY monogram — Phase 1's own verdict was that Elowyn is "the one name that earns a monogram... unusual enough to be ownable," and nothing in Phase 2's critique of Elowyn touched the monogram itself, only the temperature of the rest of the system.

**Reframed for Phase 2, the way Elowen went from "gallery calm" to "warm townhouse":** Elowyn moves from cold fashion-editorial toward **a considered maison that has warmed up** — the didone stays (it is genuinely Elowyn's best asset, and both boards agree it's the most striking wordmark of the three) but is paired with warmer supporting choices than Phase 1's graphite/plum-only system.

**Character:** Composed · Precise · Quietly warm · Fashion-literate without coldness · Self-possessed

**What she feels:** The same "flattery, in the good sense" Phase 1 identified, but landed more gently — being somewhere with real taste that also, unmistakably, knows how to look after her on the morning she's least composed.

**Positioning:** Owns *exacting care* — the appointment in your week that is both the most beautiful and the most precisely run.

**Line:** *The hours, done properly.*

**Wordmark direction:** Didone display caps retained as primary (Phase 1's strongest page), tracking loosened slightly from Phase 1's "tight +0.10em" to reduce the coldness both boards flagged. EY monogram retained as the one ownable device — no additional symbol.

**Descriptor lockup:** `ELOWYN` / `WOMEN'S HOUSE · PERTH` — tested the same as the other two, see §2.

**Typography:** Didone display (Bodoni Moda in Phase 1's treatment) for the one emotional headline per page, paired with a warmer-set grotesque than Phase 1's tracked Archivo caps — body text set larger and looser than Phase 1's "hard left, hairline rules" system, borrowing Phase 2 Elowen's insight that generous, unhurried typesetting itself communicates warmth.

**Colour:** Phase 1's bone/steel neutrals retained, plum ink retained as the text-black replacement (a genuinely good, distinctive choice — feminine without pink, per Phase 1's own reasoning), but blush is allowed slightly more presence than Phase 1's "never as an accent" rule, and bronze hardware is warmed rather than kept cool-toned. Net effect: recognisably the same family as Phase 1's palette, turned about 15% warmer.

**Premium perception:** High — didone display type reads unambiguously expensive, and the EY monogram is a real, ownable premium asset neither other candidate has an equivalent of.

**Pronunciation:** Identical to Elowen — "EL-oh-wen." Not a pronunciation problem in isolation.

**Spelling — the real issue, evaluated honestly, not minimised:** Both design boards independently flagged this as severe, not mild: "it must be spelled out forever in a referral-driven business," "E-L-O-W-Y-N... recurs on every phone call, every referral pad, every Google search for the rest of the brand's life." This is Elowyn's one genuinely disqualifying-strength weakness, and it is a real commercial cost in a business whose stated primary growth channel is word-of-mouth and referral. It is not solved by warming the palette.

**Referral usability:** Weak, for the reason above. A woman recommending Elowen or Solena to a friend needs zero spelling support. A woman recommending Elowyn needs to spell it, every time, indefinitely.

**Digital usability:** Strong — the cleanest trademark and business-conflict picture of the three (§0 aside, see §3 row 16), and no same-state or same-category business competing for the search term.

**Signage/interior concept (proportionate, not a full brief):** Etched or engraved typography reads best for a didone at architectural scale — signage should lean toward cut/engraved letterforms rather than applied vinyl, consistent with Phase 1's "smoked glass, etched" instinct, warmed with timber or brass fixtures rather than kept in Phase 1's cooler travertine/smoked-mirror-only palette.

**Photography direction:** Editorial portraiture retained from Phase 1 as the base instinct (it is genuinely Elowyn's strongest differentiator visually), softened: less hard contrast/unsmiling-only casting, more of the "mid-conversation, unaware" naturalism Phase 2 developed for Elowen and Solena, so the morning customer isn't greeted exclusively by fashion-cold imagery.

**Hospitality positioning:** Composed, precise, warmly efficient — closer to an excellent concierge than a friend (Phase 1's own framing, retained because it's accurate and distinct from both other names' voices).

**Women's-house positioning:** Genuinely strong — Phase 1's own "Direct answers" already called Elowyn's afternoon-only, beauty-house fit the strongest of the three alongside Solena.

**Scalability:** Untested in depth by either board; reasonably strong on current evidence — the monogram is a real multi-application asset (embroidery, engraving, app icon) in a way Elowen's deliberately symbol-free system is not, though Elowen's "house of addresses" naming logic remains the more proven multi-venue architecture of the two.

**Honest verdict on Elowyn Phase 2:** warming the system closes some of the gap Phase 2 identified (it is no longer coded exclusively "cold fashion editorial"), and it confirms the EY monogram and the clean trademark position are genuine, real advantages. It does **not** close the referral/spelling gap — that is a structural property of the spelling itself, not an execution problem a warmer palette can fix. Elowyn is meaningfully stronger than Phase 1 gave it credit for as a *brand*, and still meaningfully weaker than the other two as a *word* for a referral-driven business. Both things are true; see the matrix in §3 for how that nets out.

## 2. Descriptor Architecture — Tested Against All Three, Not Assumed

The Decision Board proposed `[NAME] · WOMEN'S HOUSE · PERTH` for Solena specifically, as one of two explicit conditions on its own recommendation. Tested here against all three rather than assumed transferable.

**Option A — `Women's House · Perth`:** Does real category work — it tells a first-time reader this is a venue for women, not a clinic, not a generic spa, and anchors it geographically. Aspirational in tone (a "house" is not clinical), but functionally explanatory (it answers "what is this"). Works well underneath any of the three names precisely because none of the three names themselves explain the category — all require *some* supporting language, so this isn't a Solena-specific need, it's a shared need across all three finalists (a genuine, useful finding: the original Decision Board framed this as a Solena-specific fix for Solena's "could be mistaken for a skin clinic" risk, but Elowen and Elowyn need the same category signal for a different reason — neither name says "women's" or "wellness" or "Perth" either).

**Option B — `Women's House` (no locality):** Removes the geographic anchor, which costs local SEO/discoverability in the near term but avoids baking a single-city identity into the master brand before multi-venue expansion is a real plan. Given `docs/business-plan.md`'s own framing (Australia-wide expansion "an acknowledged future possibility, not yet committed or scheduled"), dropping "Perth" now is premature — there's no confirmed second-venue timeline this would need to pre-empt, and the immediate commercial need (local referral partners, local search, a single confirmed venue) favours keeping it.

**Option C — an alternative descriptor:** No alternative tested here was judged strategically superior to "Women's House." It is short, correctly aspirational-leaning-explanatory, doesn't lean on "wellness" or "clinic" (both explicitly on the founder's avoid-list), and reads consistently across all three name treatments without needing to be reworded per name.

**Recommendation on this specific question:** `[NAME] · Women's House · Perth` for all three candidates, not just Solena — adopt this as a shared architecture decision now, independent of which name wins, since it doesn't materially favour one candidate over another. Revisit dropping "Perth" only once a second-venue plan is real, not before.

**How this differs from the prior audit's near-tie (Elowen 70.8% vs Solena 71.1%), and why the difference is legitimate, not inconsistent:** two things changed between that audit and this matrix, both disclosed. Second, §0's sourced finding — no confirmed name-branded retail strategy exists in the business plan — means "future product potential" is correctly weighted as a modest, not dominant, factor, which stops the Class 3 finding from dragging down more of Solena's score than the actual evidence supports. The live Class 3 mark still costs Solena heavily on the "trademark position" row specifically (its worst score of the whole matrix, on the second-highest-weighted criterion) — that finding hasn't softened, it's just no longer allowed to also depress an unrelated row on the basis of a plan that was never actually confirmed.

## 4. Decision Brief

**What is known:**
- All three names have a real, evidenced conflict of some kind; none is a clean slate.
- Elowyn is the cleanest on trademark grounds by a clear margin, and the weakest on referral/spelling by an equally clear margin — both independently confirmed by two separate design passes.
- Solena carries the single most concrete trademark-register conflict (a live Class 3 registration) but scores highest on the criteria the founder has explicitly named as most economically important (repeat-visit desirability, execution cost).
- Elowen sits in the middle on most axes, wins clearly on clinical credibility and scalability, and carries a real but lower-severity (unregistered business, not a registered mark) conflict.
- No confirmed business-plan commitment exists to a name-branded retail/product line — this is a live idea, not a funded pillar.

**What is unknown:**
- Whether the live SOLENA Class 3 mark meaningfully obstructs Class 44 (services) registrability specifically, as distinct from Class 3 goods — this is a genuine open legal question, not something informal research can answer.
- Whether the founder actually wants a name-branded amenity/retail line within a realistic horizon — a business decision, not a research question.
- Real, current `.com.au`/social-handle availability for all three at the point of decision (informal research only, not re-checked at transaction speed).

**What requires attorney advice — deliberately deferred, not a current action item.** Per explicit founder instruction (2026-08-14), no trademark attorney is being engaged or paid at this venture stage. `docs/naming/AUSTRALIAN-TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-BRIEF.md` (updated per §5 of this document's companion changes) is prepared and held, ready to send the moment the business reaches an appropriate validation/funding milestone. Until then, treat the questions below as the documented open legal risk, not a pending task:
- Registrability of all three names in Class 44 and (if relevant) Class 3.
- Specifically, whether AU 2202790 (SOLENA, Class 3) presents a practical bar to Class 44 use, and separately, whether it would block any future Class 3 filing.
- Confusing-similarity risk between Elowen and Elowyn given their identical pronunciation and near-identical spelling.
- Passing-off/common-law exposure from the Elowen Counselling (Eaton, WA) and Solena Skin Spa (Griffith, NSW) conflicts.

**What requires a founder decision, not further research:**
- Whether a name-branded retail line is a real 1–3 year ambition (§0) — this single answer changes how much weight the Class 3 finding should carry.
- Whether the referral-clarity cost of Elowyn is acceptable in exchange for its legal cleanliness, once the founder has read §1's honest treatment of it.
- Final risk tolerance: proceed toward Solena now on strategic strength while attorney clearance runs in parallel, or wait for clearance before committing creative/marketing effort to any one name.

**What evidence would cause Solena to win outright:** an attorney opinion that AU 2202790 does not meaningfully obstruct Class 44 registrability, combined with a founder decision that a name-branded retail line is not a near-term priority (which reduces the one area where the mark genuinely bites).

**What evidence would cause Elowen to win outright:** an attorney opinion that the Elowen Counselling conflict and occupied domain are more serious than currently assessed (e.g. a realistic passing-off risk, not just a search-sharing inconvenience) — this would need to emerge for Elowen to lose ground, since on the current evidence its conflict is already the least severe of the two live-business conflicts, and this matrix's own numbers already favour Solena on strategic grounds.

**What evidence would cause Elowyn to win outright:** a founder decision that trademark cleanliness should be weighted above referral economics even given the evidence in §1 — a legitimate but not currently the leading position given how the founder's own brief frames repeat-visit/referral economics as central.
