## 1. The Business

GTT Center Perth (current working/descriptive name, not the brand under consideration) is a pre-launch premium wellness venue in Perth, Western Australia. The business is built around the Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT), a mandatory ~2.5-hour pregnancy blood-test screening. Clients receive on-site pathology blood draws — performed by phlebotomists employed by the business, operating under a NATA-accredited pathology partner's accreditation umbrella (partner provides lab processing and result reporting; the business does not diagnose or hold its own pathology accreditation) — combined with premium beauty and wellness services (massage, nails, brow/beauty treatments, hairdressing) during the waiting period.

The business also operates a **standalone afternoon wellness offering**, open to any woman — not limited to GTT clients or pregnant clients — delivering the same service menu as a conventional premium day-spa/salon booking. This is a confirmed, current part of the operating model, not a future aspiration, and it is central to the naming brief: **the brand must function as an independent premium women's beauty/wellness brand in its own right, not merely as a medical-service name.**

The entity is not a pathology provider, medical clinic, hospital, or corporate healthcare company, and the brand should not present as one.

## 2. Geographic Market

- **Immediate:** Perth metropolitan area, Western Australia (single venue, pre-launch).
- **Near-term:** Western Australia-wide.
- **Reasonably foreseeable expansion:** Australia-wide (multi-venue growth is an acknowledged future possibility, not yet committed or scheduled).
- International use is not currently contemplated and is out of scope for this clearance instruction.

## 3. Services the Master Brand Will Cover

- Pathology/blood-collection services delivered on-site (via employed phlebotomists under a third-party NATA-accredited partner's umbrella)
- Massage therapy
- Manicure/pedicure (nail services)
- Brow and general beauty/aesthetics treatments
- Hairdressing
- A wellness lounge/venue experience (seating, refreshments, ambience) bundled with the above
- Potential future retail sale of skincare/wellness products under the brand (not yet committed, but a reasonably foreseeable extension the attorney should factor into class advice)

## 9. Questions for the Attorney

1. **Registrability:** Based on a formal Australian Trade Mark Search, is each of ELOWEN, ELOWYN, and SOLENA registrable in the recommended classes (see §4), and are there any absolute grounds for refusal (e.g. descriptiveness, prior use) beyond the relative-grounds conflicts noted below?
2. **Similarity/confusion — ELOWEN:** Do the existing Class 20 (furniture) and Class 33 (wine) ELOWEN registrations, and the recent Class 42 device mark (Merck KGaA), present a genuine bar to registering ELOWEN in Class 44 and/or Class 3? Does the unregistered but actively trading "Elowen Counselling" business present a passing-off or common-law risk independent of the trade mark register?
3. **Similarity/confusion — ELOWYN:** Could ELOWYN be refused or opposed on the basis of confusing similarity to the existing ELOWEN registrations, notwithstanding ELOWYN's own clean direct search result? Does the unrelated-industry use by Elowyn Pty Ltd (accounting) present any practical risk to registration or use in Class 44/Class 3?
4. **Similarity/confusion — SOLENA:** Does the existing registered Australian word mark SOLENA (AU 2202790, Class 3, owner Owen Matthews, cosmetics/fragrances) present a bar to registering SOLENA in Class 44 and/or a further Class 3 filing? Is coexistence realistic given the different specific goods/services, or does this represent a genuine registrability obstacle? Separately, does the existing "Solena Skin Spa" business (Griffith, NSW) present an additional registrability, opposition, or enforcement/coexistence risk given the direct service-category overlap, notwithstanding its small scale and different geographic market?
5. **Recommended classes:** Given the business description in §3, does the attorney recommend filing in Class 44 only, or should Class 3 (and/or Class 41/35) be filed at the same time rather than later? Is a single combined-class application advisable, or separate applications?
6. **Filing strategy:** Would the attorney recommend a standard Australian application, or is an international filing basis (e.g. via the Madrid Protocol) worth considering given the founder's stated interest in eventual Australia-wide expansion?
7. **Overall recommendation:** Considered together, does the attorney see a clear front-runner among the three candidates on trade mark grounds alone, independent of the founder's creative/brand preference?
