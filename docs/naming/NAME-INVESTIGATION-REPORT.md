## 1. Executive Summary

Of the seven frozen candidates, two are commercially clean with strong brand fit (**ELOWYN**, **SOLENA**), two carry a real but non-fatal Australian conflict that needs conscious risk acceptance or professional clearance (**ELOWEN**, **SOLENNE**), two are commercially clear but weaker on brand character and usability (**ELAVEN**, **ELAWEN**), and one has a concrete, disqualifying Australian conflict (**ELOVEN**).

The founder's benchmark, ELOWEN, is still viable — but it is not clean. An active Australian business (Elowen Counselling, a women's/teen counselling service) holds the exact `elowen.com.au` domain and trades under the identical name in a wellness-adjacent category. This is not fatal, but it is real, and it should be weighed consciously rather than assumed away.

**ELOWYN** — the closest vowel variant to ELOWEN — is the standout finding of this investigation. It is pronounced identically to ELOWEN ("EL-oh-wen"), carries the same Cornish-name elegance the founder responded to, returned a completely clean Australian Trade Mark Search, and has no direct-category Australian business using it. The only Australian entity trading as "Elowyn" is a Queensland accounting firm — a different industry, low confusion risk. This makes ELOWYN the strongest combination of brand potential and commercial viability in the set.

**SOLENA** is the strongest name in the Solenne territory: no Australian trademark conflicts found, the most intuitive pronunciation of all seven candidates for an Australian ear, and only one small, brand-new, regional-NSW direct-category competitor (a skin spa in Griffith that only just opened). Its sibling **SOLENNE** has real brand character but collides with an active Adelaide "lifestyle medicine" wellness clinic that holds the domain and trades in adjacent territory (women's health, diabetes, wellness) — close enough to GTT's own positioning to warrant caution.

**ELOVEN should be eliminated now.** It sits in direct, concrete collision with ELEVEN Australia — a real, decade-old, internationally distributed Australian professional hair-care brand — which matters specifically because hairdressing is one of GTT Center Perth's planned service lines. It also has a documented slang meaning and a strong search/phonetic pull toward the number "eleven." This is not a hypothetical or unrelated-industry conflict; it is the single clearest disqualifying finding in the whole investigation.

**ELAVEN and ELAWEN** turned up no Australian conflicts of any kind — but that is largely because almost nobody uses either string for anything. Both lose the phonetic connection to ELOWEN that the founder actually liked (they don't sound like "EL-oh-wen"), and both introduce spelling/pronunciation friction the founder explicitly asked to avoid. There is no reason to prefer either over ELOWYN, which achieves the same "more distinctive spelling" goal without sacrificing the sound or the brand character.

**Recommendation: send ELOWYN, SOLENA, and ELOWEN to a trade marks attorney for formal clearance. Stop creative naming now.** Full reasoning below.

## 2. Business / Name Brief

Sourced from `docs/business-plan.md` (v3.0, current) and `docs/brand-guide.md`, cross-checked against `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` for anything materially different. No conflicts found between these documents that affect the naming brief itself — the business-plan's operational figures (client volumes, staffing) have moved on since v3.0, but the identity, positioning, and expansion plan relevant to naming are stable and consistent across all three documents.

- **Concept:** Western Australia's first premium wellness venue built around the Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT), a mandatory ~2.5-hour pregnancy blood-test screening undergone by an estimated ~33,570 WA women/year. Instead of a bare pathology waiting room, clients spend the test window receiving massage, nails, brow/beauty, or hairdressing.
- **Structural fact that matters for naming:** GTT Center Perth employs the phlebotomist and provides the collection room; a NATA-accredited pathology partner (WDP, priority partner) provides the accreditation umbrella and lab work. GTT Center Perth does not diagnose or interpret results, and does not itself hold a pathology accreditation — it is a beauty/wellness services business that hosts a pathology partner's collection activity on-site under partnership, not a pathology company. This matters directly for trademark-class reasoning (§8) and for why a clinical-sounding name would misrepresent the actual entity.
- **Expansion plan (confirmed, not speculative):** the business-plan explicitly states the afternoon shift is "a standalone wellness venue, not an add-on restricted to GTT patients" — any woman, pregnant or not, can book massage/nails/hair/beauty directly. The brand name has to work as a standalone women's beauty/wellness brand independent of the GTT association, not just as a clinic name with a tagline.
- **Current brand status:** `docs/brand-guide.md` confirms explicitly that "GTT Center Perth" is a working placeholder / descriptive place-name, not a locked final brand, and that the entire existing placeholder visual identity (sage/terracotta palette, leaf mark, Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans type system) is illustrative work product to be revised once a real name is chosen — not something this report needs to preserve or work around.
- **Positioning the brand-guide already articulates well (useful raw material, not to be discarded just because the name changes):** "We are not a pathology lab pretending to be a spa. We are not a day spa pretending to be a clinic. We are something new." Brand personality described as "a 35-year-old women's health physiotherapist who also runs a small curated ceramics shop" — warm but not gushing, professional but not corporate, knowledgeable without showing off, excellent taste without price-gouging.
- **Target customer:** Perth metro, reproductive age (most commonly late 20s–late 30s), 24–28 weeks gestation for the AM/GTT segment, broader range for the PM/standalone segment, assumed mid-to-upper SES given the discretionary spend layered on a mandatory medical test.
- **Comparables named in the business plan:** MIWM (Mums in Waiting, Melbourne) and The GTT Lounge (Sydney) — both descriptive/literal names. No WA competitor identified. This is relevant to naming strategy: a non-literal, non-descriptive name (like the seven candidates under review) would differentiate GTT Center Perth from both known comparables, which lean descriptive.

**Founder's naming direction** (from the handoff brief and `docs/naming/CLAUDE-NAMING-INVESTIGATION.md`, already filed in the repo as the assignment spec for this work): fashion/beauty character, European luxury influence, feminine, elegant, soft, refined, sophisticated, modern, warm, aspirational, premium, established-feeling, memorable, visually beautiful as a wordmark, easy for Australians to read and pronounce, able to stand alone without a descriptive suffix. Explicitly avoid clinical, healthcare-adjacent, hospitality, corporate, tech-startup, or generic-wellness naming; avoid deliberately strange spellings purely for uniqueness; avoid unnecessary doubled letters. ELOWEN is liked despite its botanical (elm-tree) association — this is treated as an exception for the name's overall character, not evidence the founder wants a botanical naming territory generally (the botanical exploration was tested separately and rejected).

## 3. Methodology

Research was conducted across three parallel research passes (grouped ELOWEN/ELOWYN, ELAVEN/ELAWEN/ELOVEN, and SOLENNE/SOLENA), each covering: IP Australia Australian Trade Mark Search (ATMS), Australian business/company use, domain registration status (.com.au / .au / .com), social media presence (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok), Google/SEO collision, and pronunciation/spelling assessment.

**Material methodology limitation, disclosed rather than glossed over:** IP Australia's ATMS is a JavaScript single-page application. For the ELOWEN/ELOWYN research pass, the `search/quick/result?q=` endpoint happened to return server-rendered HTML that could be read directly — this produced genuine, verified, live government-database results for those two names specifically (confirmed matches with registration numbers, owners, classes, and status). For the other five names (SOLENNE, SOLENA, ELAVEN, ELAWEN, ELOVEN), the same endpoint returned only an empty Angular template shell with no injected data — a real ATMS query was not achieved for these five. Findings on those five rely on secondary trademark-aggregator sites (TrademarkElite, Justia) surfaced via web search, which are incomplete and sometimes stale mirrors of the real register, not a substitute for a live search. **This asymmetry matters: the ELOWEN/ELOWYN trademark findings below are more reliable than the other five, and a formal ATMS search by a trademark professional is needed for all seven regardless, but especially for SOLENNE, SOLENA, ELAVEN, ELAWEN, and ELOVEN.**

Other disclosed limitations: the ASIC Business Names Register requires an interactive form and could not be queried directly for any candidate — business-name-register status below is inferred from web search and live company websites, not a direct ASIC pull. WHOIS lookups were frequently blocked by anti-bot checks; domain findings rely on direct site fetches (confirming a domain resolves and who it belongs to) more than formal WHOIS records. Instagram/Facebook/TikTok could not be fetched directly (platforms block non-authenticated scraping) — social findings rely on search-engine snippets, which can be stale or approximate on follower counts. Where a finding could not be verified, this report says so explicitly rather than guessing.

## 5–11. Detailed Candidate Analysis

### 5.1 ELOWEN

**Trademark (verified via live ATMS query, high confidence):** Three registered Australian marks for the exact word ELOWEN, none in a directly relevant class:

| Mark | Number | Class | Owner | Status | Goods/Services | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ELOWEN (word) | 2236465 | 20 | Eugene Badenhorst | Registered | Baby/nursery furniture | None — furniture, different class |
| ELOWEN (word) | 2469495 | 33 | Vinarchy Australia Limited (formerly Accolade Wines) | Registered | Wines, wine-based beverages | None — alcohol, different class |
| ELOWEN (figurative device — stylised leaf/butterfly mark) | 2618889 | 42 | Merck KGaA | Registered (AU filing 25 Nov 2025, EUIPO priority 28 Aug 2025) | "Medical, clinical and scientific research" | **Worth flagging** — closest thing to overlap with GTT's medical/pathology adjacency, though it's a device (logo) mark, not a plain word mark, filed by a large multinational pharma/life-sciences company, and Class 42 (research services) rather than Class 44 (medical/beauty treatment services). Not a direct block, but recent and adjacent enough that a trademark attorney should look at it specifically. |

None of the three sit in Class 3 (cosmetics), Class 41 (wellness education), or Class 44 (medical/beauty/spa/massage/hairdressing services) — the classes most directly relevant to GTT Center Perth's actual services (see §8). On a pure word-mark-in-relevant-class basis, ELOWEN is not blocked.

**Australian business use (the real issue):** **Elowen Counselling** (elowen.com.au) is a live, currently trading Australian business — online counselling for women, teens, and parents of teens, run by a sole trader. This is the closest category collision found for any of the seven candidates in this section: exact name match, women-focused, wellbeing-adjacent, live and current. It is not identical to GTT's beauty/wellness/pathology model, but it is close enough (women's wellbeing services, Australia-wide) that a customer searching "Elowen" could land on the wrong business, and it means the exact `.com.au` domain is unavailable. Separately, **Elowen Rose lashes & beauty** (Sydney, Facebook) and a now-closed **Elowen Beauty** Instagram account show the name has already been used by small Australian beauty operators before, though neither is currently a going concern at scale. Non-competing but locally relevant: "The Elowen" (a Perth home design by WA builder Move Homes), Elowen earrings (WA jewellery brand Ohzo by J), and an Elowen homewares product (West Perth retailer Cavania) — these are product names, not services, so they don't create service-category confusion, but they do show the word already has some retail-brand currency in Perth specifically.

**Domain:** `elowen.com.au` is **registered and live**, run by Elowen Counselling — not available. `elowen.com` resolves but returned no readable ownership info. `elowen.au` status unconfirmed.

**Social:** Fragmented — a closed Australian lash/beauty Instagram account, an active-but-unconfirmed-origin "Elowen Studio" beauty account, a large-ish fashion-adjacent "@elowenoriginal" account, and a recurring unrelated TikTok meme character ("Elowen Fawn") that a brand would be competing with for search/discovery attention. No dominant player, but no clean slate either.

**SEO:** Page 1 for the bare word is dominated by baby-name reference sites and two unrelated US apartment-complex brands also called "Elowen" — no beauty/wellness brand currently owns the term globally, so search authority is winnable, but not on a completely empty field.

**Pronunciation:** A real, established (if uncommon) Cornish given name meaning "elm," reliably pronounced **EL-oh-wen**. Reads intuitively for an Australian audience — three clear syllables, no unusual clusters. Low correction risk. The main risk is written-from-speech ambiguity (people may write "Ellowen," "Elowin," or "Elowyn" after hearing it spoken), which is a real but minor and manageable friction point, not a disqualifying one.

### 5.2 ELOWYN

**Trademark (verified via live ATMS query):** **Zero results.** A direct, current ATMS query for ELOWYN returned no registered or pending Australian trade marks in any class — the government's own message: "You have no results." This is the cleanest trademark finding of all seven candidates, verified rather than inferred. Standard caveat that applies regardless of this clean result: a plain-word ATMS search does not itself surface phonetically similar marks — a formal examiner search would likely flag the ELOWEN registrations above (§5.1) as related, even though a literal ELOWYN search shows nothing today.

**Australian business use:** **Elowyn Pty Ltd** is a live, active Australian accounting and financial advisory firm, based in Chermside, Queensland (not Perth). This is an exact-entity-name match, but in a completely unrelated sector — low real-world confusion risk for a beauty/wellness brand, though it does mean the entity name "Elowyn Pty Ltd" is already in use in the ASIC company register (inferred, not directly confirmed). **Elowyn Hair Studio** is a real hairdressing business under this name, but it is US-based (Utah-style address), not Australian — a category-relevant name (hairdressing is one of GTT's planned services) but not a domestic risk. No Perth or WA use of this name in any category was found.

**Domain:** `elowyn.com.au` is **registered and live**, belonging to the Queensland accounting firm — not available as-is, but low confusion risk given the industry mismatch; a slightly modified domain or an alternate TLD strategy would work cleanly. `elowyn.com` resolves to a near-empty personal placeholder page (not a business). `elowyn.au` status unconfirmed.

**Social:** Minor and fragmented — a US hair-studio account, an unrelated (but sizeable) art/craft creator account, no established beauty/wellness brand under this exact name anywhere, Australian or otherwise.

**SEO:** Search space is comparably open to ELOWEN, arguably more open — no equivalent of ELOWEN's competing apartment-brand results. Dominated by baby-name sites and a small musician's profile.

**Pronunciation:** Per name-reference sources, ELOWYN is explicitly a spelling variant of ELOWEN with the **identical pronunciation** — "EL-oh-wen." The "-wyn" ending is not unfamiliar to Australian ears (Bronwyn, Delwyn are established Australian names using the same pattern), so it doesn't read as an artificially altered spelling the way some vowel-swaps do. The practical trade-off versus ELOWEN is purely orthographic: because the two spellings sound identical, a customer who's only heard the name spoken has no way to know from sound alone whether to write "-en" or "-yn" — a minor, manageable friction shared by both names, not unique to ELOWYN.

### 5.3 ELAVEN

**Trademark:** No exact "ELAVEN" trademark found via secondary-source search (not a verified live ATMS query — see §3 methodology caveat). Closest near-matches indexed: ELVANSE (Takeda Pharmaceuticals, an ADHD medication, different spelling and industry) and ELAVE (a US cosmetics mark, not Australian). No collision evidence found, but confidence in this "clean" result is lower than for ELOWEN/ELOWYN given the unverified search method.

**Australian business use:** None found in any industry, including beauty/wellness. A few superficially similar but distinct Australian names exist (Elevon, Elevyn, Elvin Group, Elven Group WA) — none is a genuine match.

**Domain:** `elaven.com` is registered and parked for sale via GoDaddy. `elaven.com.au` status unconfirmed (empty fetch result).

**Social:** No beauty/wellness account found under this name, Australian or otherwise. A handful of small personal/music/clothing accounts exist, none consequential.

**SEO:** Relatively open — dominated by small personal profiles and a UK company of unknown industry, no dominant brand or dictionary-word collision.

**Pronunciation:** Reads as **el-AY-ven** or **el-AH-ven** — this is the crux of the problem with ELAVEN as a spelling: it does not preserve ELOWEN's "oh" sound, so it doesn't actually carry forward the pronunciation the founder responded to. It's a different-sounding name that happens to share five letters with the benchmark, not a genuine variant of it. There is also a mild echo of "eleven" given the shared "-aven/-even" ending pattern, though weaker than ELOVEN's.

### 5.4 ELAWEN

**Trademark:** No exact match found via secondary-source search (unverified live ATMS — see §3). Closest near-matches (ELAWN, ELAW) are unrelated US marks in different industries.

**Australian business use:** None found under this name. One adjacent, established Perth-connected entity exists under the near-miss spelling "elaw" (elaw.com.au, an eLitigation/legal-tech consultancy with a Perth office among others) — different spelling, different industry, low relevance, noted for completeness only.

**Domain:** `elawen.com.au` status unconfirmed. `elawen.com` resolves to a live but essentially content-empty page — occupied by someone, not confirmed available.

**Social:** No beauty/wellness account found anywhere under this name. The only notable hits are a video-game wiki character and a small non-English-language Facebook page, neither relevant.

**SEO:** The most open search space of the three vowel-variant names — no dictionary word, no dominant brand, nothing competing for attention.

**Pronunciation:** This is where ELAWEN loses ground. It reads as **el-AH-wen** or, for many readers, pulls toward **el-AW-en** (rhyming with "lawn") because "-awen" is not a common English letter pattern. This is the highest first-read hesitation risk of all seven candidates, and the spelling most likely to be reconstructed incorrectly after only hearing it spoken (people could plausibly write it back as "Elawyn," "Elowen," "Elowyn," or "Alawen"). It is commercially clean, but it is the candidate most exposed to the exact "constant pronunciation correction" problem the founder explicitly asked to avoid.

### 5.5 ELOVEN — recommend elimination

**Trademark:** No exact "ELOVEN" mark found, but substantial adjacent Australian trademark activity around "ELEVEN" in genuinely relevant channels — including registrations held by Eleven Australia IP Pty Ltd, the entity behind the real-world brand below.

**Australian business use — the disqualifying finding:** **ELEVEN Australia** is a large, established, homegrown Australian hair-and-body-care brand, founded 2011 by Joey Scandizzo (four-time Australian Hairdresser of the Year) and Andrew O'Toole. It sells in 27 countries with a dedicated Australian storefront, is distributed through professional salon channels, and is active across Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, and Pinterest under "Eleven Australia." This is not a distant or unrelated-industry conflict: GTT Center Perth's business plan explicitly includes hairdressing as one of its core service lines, meaning ELOVEN would be trading a phonetically-near-identical name directly alongside a real, nationally distributed hair-care brand in the exact professional channel GTT's own hairdressing stylists would be using. This is the single most concrete commercial-collision finding anywhere in this investigation.

**Digital/SEO — confirms the risk rather than softening it:** Urban Dictionary documents an existing slang meaning of "eloven" (a portmanteau of "eleven" + "lovin'"). Google search behaviour pulls toward the dictionary word "eleven" for near-miss queries. IKEA sells an unrelated product literally called "ELLOVEN." A Chinese consumer-electronics seller trades globally as "ELOVEN" on Amazon. None of these individually would be fatal, but together with the Eleven Australia finding, they confirm — rather than merely suggest — that ELOVEN sits in genuine collision territory with the number word "eleven," exactly the risk the founder's brief asked to be checked carefully rather than glossed over.

**Pronunciation:** Reads most naturally as **el-OH-ven** — ironically the closest of the three vowel-variants to preserving ELOWEN's actual sound — but that same "oh" sound is precisely what makes it the easiest of the seven to mishear or misread as "eleven" (differing by a single letter, O vs E, in the second syllable). Said aloud in a reception/booking context ("Welcome to Eloven"), this is a real and specific risk, not a theoretical one.

**Verdict:** Eliminate. This is not a case of an unrelated business somewhere in the world owning the word — it is a real, current, Australian, hair-care-industry brand directly adjacent to one of GTT's own planned services, compounded by a genuine number-word collision. No other candidate in this set has a comparably concrete conflict.

### 5.6 SOLENNE

**Trademark:** No live Australian trade mark found for SOLENNE via secondary-source search (unverified live ATMS — see §3). One dead US mark exists (cosmetics/skincare, cancelled 2020) — not Australian, not currently enforceable, but shows the exact word has been claimed before in the exact beauty category, which is a mild signal worth a professional's attention even though it's expired.

**Australian business use — the real issue:** **Solenne Pty Ltd** (Glenside, Adelaide, South Australia) is a live, active, currently trading Australian business — a "Lifestyle Medicine Health Services" clinic covering GP-led care, exercise physiology, physiotherapy, nutrition/dietetics, podiatry, health coaching, infrared sauna, and lab testing, explicitly including women's health, weight management, chronic disease, and **diabetes**. This is a meaningful category collision: not identical to GTT (no beauty/nails/hair/massage), but the positioning language — "lifestyle medicine," wellness, diabetes, women's health — sits close enough to GTT's own conceptual territory (a diabetes-screening test delivered inside a wellness setting) that customer or search confusion is a real, not hypothetical, risk. It is Australian, current, and trades under the bare word "Solenne."

**Domain:** `solenne.com.au` and `solenne.au` are **both registered and active**, both belonging to the Adelaide clinic. `solenne.com` is registered but parked by a domain-speculation service, not in use — theoretically negotiable, but not a clean primary-domain outcome either way given the `.com.au` conflict.

**Social:** The Adelaide clinic runs a small, active Facebook presence (287 followers). Separately, **Solenne Fragrance** (Tampa, Florida — artisan perfume/candles, "@hellosolenne") is the most commercially substantial global use of the bare name: founded 2024, self-reported 10,000+ customers and 50+ US retailers by 2026. No evidence of Australian retail presence for this brand, so it's a lower-priority consideration per the brief's Australia-first framing, but it does mean the word is actively being built into a real beauty-adjacent brand elsewhere.

**SEO:** Fragmented rather than dominated by one player — baby-name sites, a Palworld video-game character (an odd but low-stakes association), Solenne Fragrance, and the French given name Solène generally. No single incumbent to out-compete, but the Adelaide clinic's own domain strength in Australian search specifically is a real, not just theoretical, factor.

**Pronunciation:** Correct French pronunciation is **soh-LEN** (rhymes with "hen," stress on the second syllable). The "-enne" ending is a recognisable pattern for English speakers via names like Yvonne and Cheyenne, so most Australians would land close to correct on first read. Minor risk of "so-LEEN" (by analogy to "saline") or an extra syllable ("so-LEN-uh"), but overall this sits in the "usually gets it roughly right, with an audible minority needing light correction" band — acceptable, not a disqualifier.

### 5.7 SOLENA

**Trademark:** No Australian or historical trademark conflict found for SOLENA via secondary-source search (unverified live ATMS — see §3) — cleaner than SOLENNE on this specific point, since SOLENNE at least has a dead US mark on record and SOLENA has none.

**Australian business use:** **Solena Skin Spa** (Griffith, regional NSW) is the one direct-category conflict found — a "luxury skin & wellness spa" (facials, advanced skin treatments, infrared sauna, ice bath, salt therapy) that only just launched (grand-opening content dated mid-2026). This is a real, current, Australian, same-category business — but it is small, brand new, and located in regional NSW rather than Perth or a major metro, which meaningfully limits real-world customer confusion compared to SOLENNE's more established Adelaide clinic. A second, separate small business (Solena Skin & Beauty, Morisset NSW) also exists at a similarly small scale. **Solenis Australia** (Osborne Park, Perth WA) is a near-miss on spelling but an unrelated industrial water-treatment chemicals company — genuinely low relevance despite the Perth location, noted for completeness.

**Domain:** `solena.com.au` returned no usable content on direct fetch — registration status genuinely unconfirmed, but this is at minimum not an obviously taken-and-active domain the way SOLENNE's or ELOWEN's are. `solena.au` is registered by an unrelated small Brisbane bookish-accessories brand (different category, low confusion risk). `solena.com` is parked by a domain-speculation service.

**Social:** `@solenaskinspa` exists for the Griffith spa but its scale could not be confirmed (Instagram blocked direct verification) — reads as small/new based on the grand-opening framing. Internationally, **Solena New York** is a meaningfully scaled US makeup/skincare brand (18,300 TikTok followers) — a real beauty-industry use of the name, though branded with "New York" appended and with no confirmed Australian retail presence, so lower priority under the brief's Australia-first framing.

**SEO:** Fragmented — Wikipedia disambiguation pages for unrelated biological genera (a bivalve mollusc and a flowering-plant genus), Solena New York, and a couple of unrelated global businesses. Slightly unusual (the biology association) but not brand-blocking, and no single dominant incumbent.

**Pronunciation:** The most phonetically intuitive of all seven candidates. No diacritics, no silent letters, no unusual clusters. Natural pronunciation is **soh-LEE-nah**, consistent with its resemblance to the Spanish "Solana" (from "sol," sun) — fitting thematically with the brand's warm, restorative positioning. Plausible minor variants an Australian might produce ("so-LAY-na," "SOL-en-a") all land close enough to sound correct and elegant without needing correction.

## 18. Final Decision Framework

**1. Is ELOWEN still viable enough to proceed?**
Yes. The evidence does not disqualify it. But it is not clean — Elowen Counselling is a real, active, exact-name Australian business in wellbeing/women's services, and it holds the `.com.au` domain. Proceed to formal clearance with this disclosed, not silently.

ELOWYN — same sound, same brand character, cleaner trademark and business-use position.

**3. Which candidate is the safest commercially?**
ELOWYN, narrowly ahead of SOLENA. ELOWYN has a verified-clean live trademark search; SOLENA's clean result relies on secondary sources only.

**4. Which candidate has the strongest brand potential?**
SOLENNE, by raw creative score (8.5) — but it carries the second-worst commercial conflict in the set. On a brand-potential basis alone, ELOWEN, SOLENA, and ELOWYN are functionally tied just behind it (8.3–8.4).

**5. Which candidate has the best combination of brand potential + commercial viability?**
ELOWYN. It is within 0.2 points of the top creative score and carries the cleanest verified commercial position of any candidate.

**6. Which names should be eliminated now?**
ELOVEN — concrete, disqualifying, category-relevant Australian conflict. ELAVEN and ELAWEN are not disqualified by evidence, but should be set aside: neither offers any advantage over ELOWYN, and both sacrifice the brand character the founder is actually chasing.

**7. Which 2–3 names should proceed to formal professional trademark clearance?**
ELOWYN, SOLENA, ELOWEN — in that order.

**8. Should the founder stop creative naming at this point?**
Yes. Two of the seven frozen candidates are commercially clean with excellent brand fit. There is no evidentiary basis for generating a new naming territory, and doing so would contradict the explicit instruction governing this investigation.

## CLAUDE DESIGN / BRAND STRATEGY HANDOFF

This section prepares context for the next agent, once the founder makes a final selection from ELOWYN / SOLENA / ELOWEN following formal trade mark clearance. It does not lock any visual decisions.

**Winning-name rationale (applies to all three shortlisted names, adjust once one is chosen):** each of the three surviving names shares the same underlying rationale — a real, established-sounding given name (not an invented or algorithmically-generated string) with European/Cornish/French roots, feminine and elegant without being overtly floral, maternal, or clinical, and capable of carrying a brand well beyond the GTT service into the standalone women's-wellness offering the business plan already commits to.

**Emotional territory:** calm confidence, quiet luxury, restorative warmth, being genuinely known and cared for rather than processed. Consistent with the "35-year-old women's health physiotherapist who also runs a small curated ceramics shop" personality already articulated in `docs/brand-guide.md` — that character description survives the name change even though the visual system built around the old placeholder name does not.

**Positioning implications:** the chosen name must do double duty from day one — carrying the GTT/pathology-adjacent premium-wellness concept at launch, and standing entirely on its own for the standalone PM wellness offering serving any woman, pregnant or not. Any tagline or descriptor work should treat the name as the permanent asset and "GTT Center Perth" language as a transitional/descriptive layer that can recede over time.

**Words/ideas the brand should evoke:** calm, considered, restorative, unhurried, cared for, known, quietly premium, European-influenced without being derivative, modern heirloom, warmth, softness with substance.

**Words/ideas to avoid:** clinical, sterile, urgent, corporate, hospitality/hotel-adjacent, tech-startup, "journey," "empowered," "amazing," "game-changer," anything overtly maternal-cute or baby-themed (the brand serves women, not babies), anything that reads as a generic day spa.

**Preliminary visual territory (not locked):** the existing placeholder system (`docs/brand-guide.md`) used a sage/terracotta/deep-slate palette with a botanical leaf motif and Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans typography — this was built around "GTT Center Perth" as a descriptive name and should not be assumed to carry over automatically, especially the leaf motif, which was a literal response to a place-name rather than to ELOWEN/SOLENA/ELOWYN specifically. Worth carrying forward conceptually: the instinct toward warm neutrals over clinical brights, and a serif/sans pairing for an elegant-but-legible type system. Worth reconsidering fresh: whether a botanical mark still makes sense once the name itself is doing more of the brand's storytelling work.

**Typography direction:** an elegant, slightly editorial serif for the wordmark/display (in the spirit of what Cormorant Garamond was reaching for) paired with a clean, highly legible sans for body/UI copy — this general pairing logic held up well in the existing placeholder work and is a reasonable starting brief, not a locked choice.

**Colour-world direction:** warm, muted, premium neutrals (the existing sage/terracotta/parchment direction is a reasonable starting point) — avoid clinical blue/green, avoid maternity-cliché pink, avoid anything that reads as a franchise/corporate palette. Final palette should be developed against whichever name is actually chosen, since SOLENA/SOLENNE's Spanish/French warmth and ELOWYN/ELOWEN's Cornish/botanical-adjacent character could reasonably pull the palette in slightly different directions.

**Photography direction:** the existing brief's instincts are sound and should carry forward regardless of name — natural diffused light, no clinical equipment ever visible, real diverse Perth-representative subjects, unhurried and intimate rather than posed-corporate.

**Tone of voice direction:** the existing tone-of-voice table in `docs/brand-guide.md` (calm/confident/direct for headings, warm/real/dry for social, reassuring/practical for transactional copy, plain-English/never-alarming for clinical information) is strong, name-independent work and should be preserved rather than rebuilt from scratch.

**Competitor brands worth studying:** MIWM (Melbourne) and The GTT Lounge (Sydney) for direct-category positioning (both lean descriptive/literal in naming — the chosen name's non-literal, evocative approach is a genuine differentiator worth designing into, not softening). Broader premium wellness/beauty brand territory worth studying for visual/tone reference: independent premium beauty and wellness brands with European-name branding and quiet-luxury positioning (the founder's own reference point, not enumerated here since specific brand studies are a task for the next agent, not this report).

**Brand risks to avoid:** do not let the visual identity drift toward "generic day spa" or "hospital-adjacent medical" — both explicitly rejected in the founder's brief. Do not let a botanical name (ELOWEN) pull the visual system into an overtly floral/garden aesthetic — the founder liked ELOWEN for its overall character, not for its botanical meaning, and over-literalising the elm-tree etymology would misread the brief. Whichever name is chosen, do not build a visual identity that only makes sense next to the word "GTT" — the name has to work standalone, since the business itself is built to outgrow that descriptor.
