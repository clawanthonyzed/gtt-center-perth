## 4. Current Brand Strategy — Before This Round

`docs/brand-guide.md` (June 2026, explicitly still governing until superseded) establishes a positioning built on four pillars: clinical confidence, premium warmth, maternal knowing, calm authority. It explicitly instructs: "we are NOT a pregnancy day spa... don't lead with spa." **This is worth naming plainly: the founder's new brief for this round directly overrides part of this.** The new brief says not to optimise "solely around reassurance, clinical trust or 'pregnancy healthcare,'" and to weight the voluntary repeat visit — which is desire-driven, not trust-driven — as the economically important interaction. ## 6. What Claude Design Changed

Two sequential, self-contained design passes exist, and they reach opposite conclusions:

- **Phase 1 ("Brand Worlds," three-way comparison)** built full brand worlds for ELOWEN, ELOWYN, and SOLENA and recommended: **Build Elowen. Keep Solena as a future product brand. Drop Elowyn.** Reasoning centred on Elowen's "inherited credibility" mattering most at the point a nervous, fasting, referred client is deciding whether the business is legitimate.
- **Phase 2 ("Decision Board," two-way, Elowyn already dropped)** rebuilt both Elowen and Solena from scratch — new typography, new palettes, Phase 1's specific fonts and colours explicitly discarded as drafts — and reversed the call: **Build Solena.** Its own words: *"I recommended Elowen in Phase 1. Having developed both properly, I think that was the wrong answer."*

Both documents are self-aware about this reversal and explain it rather than hiding it — this is a genuine, disclosed change of mind across two rounds of the same design process, not a contradiction to be silently resolved. The founder's brief accurately reflects the *current* state (§3 of the founder's brief: "the latest Claude Design recommendation is: BUILD SOLENA") — that framing is correct as of the most recent board.

## 7. Why Claude Design Now Recommends Solena

Phase 2's reasoning, stated plainly: Phase 1 optimised for the first, referred visit — and Elowen genuinely wins that visit. But the first visit happens because a midwife made a referral; it happens roughly the same whether the brand is beautiful or merely competent. The visit that actually builds the business is the one booked eighteen months later with no clinical reason at all, and that visit is bought with desire, not reassurance. On that test, Solena wins: it is easier to say and spell (mattering directly for a referral-driven growth model), performs better in a photo/social feed, is meaningfully cheaper to execute to a convincing standard (light and plaster versus joinery and materials), and — this was Solena's single strongest structural argument — carries a real, ownable device (a "foil S") that makes a future name-branded retail product line plausible in a way Elowen's deliberately symbol-free system does not.

## 8. What Evidence Supports and Contradicts That Recommendation

**Supports it:** the founder's own brief for this round independently arrives at the same emphasis — repeat-visit desirability as the critical economic driver — before having seen Phase 2's reasoning. That is a real, convergent signal, not manufactured agreement. Both boards' qualitative "voice test" and "same moment, both brands" comparisons hold up under scrutiny; Solena's afternoon-only, non-pregnancy-coded positioning is genuinely the stronger fit for the standalone PM business the business-plan already commits to.

**Contradicts or weakens it:** Phase 2's own scoring put Elowen and Solena six points apart out of 170 — under 4% — and says so explicitly ("the totals are not the argument"). This was already a close call on creative grounds alone. Since Phase 2 was written, the single most concrete piece of new evidence in this whole audit has emerged, and it lands directly on Solena's strongest claimed advantage: see §11. Phase 2's own recommendation was conditioned on two things — using a descriptor lockup from day one, and treating the Class 3 finding as "a real constraint on the product line specifically." That second condition has since gone from a research flag to a confirmed live registration. The recommendation was reasoned correctly against the evidence available at the time; the evidence has materially changed since.

## 9. Contradictions Between the Business Plan and Brand Work — and Within the Brand Work Itself

Three genuine tensions, none silently resolved:

1. **Positioning-level shift** (§4 above): the new brief's "don't optimise solely around reassurance/clinical trust" instruction is a real revision to `brand-guide.md`'s existing pillars, not a continuation of them. Worth the founder's explicit acknowledgment before it's treated as settled, since it changes what the *first* visit is allowed to feel like, not only what the brand looks like.
2. **ELOWYN's disappearance from the design layer versus its survival in the trademark layer.** The design boards eliminated Elowyn after Phase 1 on referral/spelling grounds — a real, legitimate design concern ("it must be spelled out forever in a referral-driven business"). But Elowyn is also, by a clear margin, the *cleanest name of the three on every commercial-conflict measure this whole investigation has produced* — no live trademark found in any class, no same-state or same-category business conflict identified, versus real conflicts on both other names (§11). Phase 1 eliminated Elowyn *before* the Solena Class 3 mark was known. The founder's own current brief still lists Elowyn as a "surviving candidate" for attorney clearance. This is not resolved anywhere in the existing work, and it should not be resolved by silent omission here either — see §14 and §16.
3. **Illustrative pricing/package names in both boards** ("The Quiet Morning $80," "The Long Morning $145," "The Whole Morning $210," a three-tier structure) do not match the current committed two-tier package structure (Package 1 A$250 / Package 2 A$300, `CURRENT-STATE.md`). Both boards flag their own figures as illustrative and point back to `CURRENT-STATE.md` — this is disclosed, not a silent error — but the three-tier *naming convention* itself (as distinct from the dollar figures) has not been reconciled against the real two-package structure, and shouldn't be treated as adopted until it is.

## 11. The Fact That Has Changed Since Phase 2 — Read This Before Accepting Either Recommendation

On 2026-08-13, independent verification against IP Australia's public trade mark search tool found what the original 2026-08-08 naming research missed: **a live, currently registered Australian word mark for SOLENA — AU 2202790, Class 3, owner Owen Matthews, registered 24 March 2022, covering cosmetics for skin/hair/face and fragrances, not due for renewal until 2031.** This is not a business quietly using the word; it is a registered mark on the federal trade marks register, in the exact class (Class 3, cosmetics) that both design boards identify as Solena's core structural advantage over Elowen — the retail/product-line runway, the "foil S" device, the hand-oil-as-amenity idea Phase 2 built its closing argument around.

Elowen's known conflict (Elowen Counselling, trading in Eaton, WA — the same state, confirmed still active) is a real business-use risk and a same-state search-sharing problem, but it is not a registered trade mark in any class relevant to the actual services (its three existing registrations are furniture, wine, and an unrelated medical-research device mark). **The two names' conflicts are not the same kind of risk.** One is an unregistered business a lawyer might assess as a manageable coexistence or passing-off question; the other is a live federal registration directly in the one class Solena most needs for the exact commercial capability that was the deciding argument in its favour.

This does not make Solena unusable — the venue and its Class 44 services (massage, hair, nails, beauty, wellness) are a different question from a Class 3 retail line, and coexistence in different classes/goods is genuinely common and something the attorney should assess directly (see the clearance brief's §9 question 4). But it does mean **Phase 2's recommendation was made without this fact, and its central argument is the part of Solena's case most exposed by it.**

## 12. Commercial Implications

If Elowen is chosen: the founder should expect to fund the "warm townhouse" execution properly — both boards agree a modest Elowen fit-out reads as merely plain, not restrained; the identity's whole argument is material quality. Retail/product extension is a weaker, longer-term lever under this name (not blocked, just less naturally suited).

If Solena is chosen: cheaper to reach a convincing 80% execution (light, plaster, one curtain), stronger near-term social/referral economics, but the founder should not plan a name-branded retail line without attorney sign-off first, and should budget for the coexistence/negotiation conversation that implies — a real cost and timeline risk that didn't exist in Phase 2's version of the decision.

If Elowyn is reconsidered: cleanest legal position of the three by a clear margin, but it has never had a properly funded Phase 2-equivalent design pass, and the one real design objection against it (referral/spelling friction) is legitimate and unresolved.

## 14. Independent Recommendation

## BUILD ELOWEN

Not because the scorecard favours it — it doesn't, meaningfully. Because in a genuine tie, the newest and most concrete piece of evidence in this entire investigation (§11) is a live federal trade mark registration sitting directly across Solena's single strongest structural argument, while Elowen's equivalent conflict remains at the lower-severity, unregistered-business-use level. Rule 8 of the founder's own working rules — "the brand must work in the real world, not only in a presentation" — is the tie-breaker here, not a preference for Elowen's aesthetic over Solena's.

**What must be true for this to work:** the Eaton, WA "Elowen Counselling" conflict and the occupied `.com.au` domain need the attorney's explicit view on manageability (a suffixed domain and clearly distinct visual/service register are the working assumption, not yet confirmed safe). The identity needs real execution investment — this is not the cheap-to-execute option, and underfunding it is a named failure mode in both boards ("a nice room with a serif logo").

**What risks remain:** Elowen is the harder brand to make desirable on its own; its whole case rests on inherited credibility converting into genuine warmth once the room and photography exist, not before. If the founder's actual five-year bet is that the afternoon/product business will materially outgrow the GTT mornings, Solena's case (§7) is real and should not be dismissed — it is a legitimate, closely-argued alternative, not a rejected option.

**What should be legally validated before this is final:** send the existing `AUSTRALIAN-TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-BRIEF.md` to an attorney now, unchanged in substance, for all three names — including Elowyn (see §16). If the attorney's read on the Solena Class 3 mark comes back materially more comfortable than this audit assumes (e.g. genuine coexistence in Class 44 with no practical exposure, and the founder confirms no name-branded retail line is actually planned), that finding would be enough to flip this recommendation back toward Solena, given how close the underlying scorecard already is.

**Why not Solena:** its case is strong and current, but it was built and scored before the Class 3 finding existed, and that finding lands on exactly the argument that separated it from Elowen in Phase 2.

**Why not "continue naming exploration":** the frozen seven were investigated properly, narrowed to three defensible survivors, and two of the three (Elowen, Solena) now have full, competent design treatments. Nothing in this audit shows the existing candidates failing to solve a problem that a new name would solve better — the open question is a trade-off between two known, well-understood options, not a gap in the naming territory itself.

5. **Reconcile the illustrative three-tier package-naming convention** ("The Quiet/Long/Whole Morning") against the real, committed two-tier Package 1/Package 2 structure in `CURRENT-STATE.md` before either identity's service menu is treated as adoptable.
6. **Get the attorney's read on the Eaton, WA Elowen Counselling conflict and the occupied `elowen.com.au` domain** specifically — same-state conflicts carry more real-world weight than the original research treated them as having.
