## 1. What Real Australian Collection Centres Actually Look Like Day-to-Day (Beyond the Repo's Accreditation Paperwork)

**Scale context, real and current:** NSW Health Pathology alone operates 150+ collection centres statewide and employs 5,000+ staff — a Tier 1 government source establishing that collection centres are a genuinely large-scale, routine part of the Australian health system, not a niche or unusual site type. This scale is a useful sanity check: GTT Center Perth would not be pioneering an exotic model by hosting a collection centre — the pioneering element is combining it with wellness services, not the collection centre itself.

**Appointment vs walk-in — the specific finding, not previously investigated in this repository:** a real example (ACT/Canberra Hospital's own collection centre, healthdirect listing) confirms that even a hospital-based, largely drop-in collection centre **requires an appointment specifically for GTT** — walk-in is not the norm for this particular test even at a facility that otherwise accepts drop-ins for other pathology tests. This directly corroborates, from an entirely independent source, GTT Center Perth's own committed appointment-based, synchronized-chair model (`docs/scenario-c-sync-timetables.md`) — this is not a case of GTT choosing appointments for wellness-experience reasons alone; **GTT-specific collection is commonly appointment-based in real Australian practice regardless of venue type**, because of the fixed 2-hour+ multi-draw structure. This closes a small but real gap: the existing repository never independently verified that appointment-only GTT scheduling is standard practice elsewhere, only that WDP requires it for GTT Center Perth specifically.

**International throughput evidence, flagged as directional not Australian fact:** Quest Diagnostics' own patient guidance states plainly that appointments "take priority over walk-ins," walk-ins can face waits up to two hours or be turned away at capacity, and Patient Service Centres are "usually busiest in the early morning." Labcorp, by contrast, accepts walk-ins more freely but carries a real, repeatedly-cited reputation cost for it (long waits, inconsistent service quality, per multiple independent complaint sources). **Lesson for GTT Center Perth, stated as inference not fact:** the AM segment's committed appointment-only, synchronized model is the pattern that avoids Labcorp's specific reputational failure mode (unpredictable queuing) rather than Quest's more successful appointment-priority model — this is a genuine piece of supporting evidence for a decision this venture already made, not a reason to reconsider it.

## 2. What Pathology Partners' Commercial Teams Actually Evaluate — Their Side of the Deal

**Confirmed for the 4 Australian candidates (WDP/PathWest/Clinipath/ACL), re-checked this round, same finding as `docs/architecture/PARTNER-ACCREDITATION-STANDARDS-COMPARISON.md`, not newly discovered but independently re-verified:** none of the 4 publishes commercial evaluation criteria. QML Pathology and Laverty Pathology (two further Australian pathology companies, checked fresh this round as additional data points since they are not among GTT's 4 named candidates but are large national players) were directly fetched and show the identical pattern — a one-line "contact us to establish a Licensed Collection Centre" invitation, no published evaluation criteria, no volume threshold, no commercial-term detail. **This is now confirmed across 6 Australian pathology companies (the original 4 plus QML and Laverty), not just 4 — a broader, more confident finding that this is a genuine, universal industry pattern (individually negotiated, never published) rather than a quirk of the 4 originally-researched candidates.**

**Directional-only evidence from outside Australia (India, flagged explicitly as non-Australian and not applied as fact):** Indian pathology franchise programs (Metropolis, Accuhealth, Apollo Diagnostics) do publish concrete site-evaluation criteria: expected patient volume, patient catchment location/demographics, ground-floor accessibility (explicitly citing "expectant mothers" as a reason), and proximity to a referring doctor's clinic as "an added advantage," not a requirement. **These are Indian-market criteria and must not be read as Australian requirements** — the Indian pathology-franchise industry operates on a fundamentally different commercial model (direct franchise fees, different regulatory environment) to the Australian collection-centre-hosting model this venture is pursuing. They are logged here only as a directional pattern worth being aware WDP/PathWest/Clinipath commercial teams may implicitly weigh even though they don't publish it: **expected volume, patient catchment and accessibility (ground-floor, easy access — directly relevant for pregnant clients), and proximity/relationship to referring practices.** This is broadly consistent with, not contradictory to, what Carole Rivers (WDP) has already told this venture directly in correspondence (volume, referring-doctor base, location/accessibility, "overall business opportunity" — `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 1c) — the international pattern corroborates rather than adds new criteria to what WDP has already stated.

**Genuine remaining gap, not closed this round:** no source, Australian or international, reveals the actual dollar economics a pathology partner uses internally to price a collection-centre rental/commercial arrangement (e.g., cost per specimen collected and transported vs revenue per Medicare rebate claimed on the resulting test). This is commercially sensitive information no pathology company publishes, and this venture's own correspondence with WDP (Carole Rivers) is already the most advanced real-world source of insight into this that exists anywhere in searchable form — there is no external research shortcut around it. **Recommendation: continue relying on direct negotiation (already in progress) rather than further external research on this specific point — external research has hit genuine diminishing returns here, now confirmed across 6 companies rather than assumed after checking only 4.**

## 3. Real-World International Analogue: Any Lab Test Now (US Direct-to-Consumer Lab-Draw Franchise)

**Source (Tier 2/3):** Franchise Disclosure Document data analysed by FranchiseChatter and SharpSheets (independent franchise-analysis publications, not the franchisor's own marketing — a materially more reliable source category than a company's own website).

**What it is:** a US retail-storefront model where consumers walk in or book and pay directly for lab tests (blood, drug, DNA, etc.) without necessarily needing a doctor's order for many tests — structurally different from GTT Center Perth (which operates within the Australian Medicare-referral pathology system, not a direct-pay consumer lab model), but a genuine, real-financials analogue for "what does a small, single-site blood-collection storefront actually earn."

**Real figures found (multiple years, showing a range rather than a single misleading number):**
- 2023 average gross revenue, standalone franchised locations: ~US$339,000/year
- Top-quartile locations: average US$489,507/year, median US$461,672/year, some over US$1 million
- Initial investment range: US$60,000-298,000
- Royalty structure: 7% of gross revenue + 2% national marketing fee

**Relevance and limits, stated plainly:** this is a US, direct-pay, no-Medicare-referral-required model — it is not a substitute for GTT Center Perth's own Australian, Medicare-referral-based, pathology-partner-dependent economics, and no dollar figure from this source should be imported into GTT Center Perth's financial model. Its value is narrower and more honest than that: it is real evidence that a small, single-site blood-collection-focused storefront business **can** be a viable, revenue-generating unit on its own in a market that allows it, which indirectly supports the underlying commercial logic of collection centres as a business category worth partnering into — a soft, directional confirmation, not a number to reuse.

## 4. MIWM — Corrected and Substantially Deepened (Real Pricing Found, Not Previously in This Repository)

**This is a genuine, material correction to existing repository research, not just an addition.** `docs/market-research-findings.md` (2026-06-09) describes MIWM's package *structure* (Standard/Deluxe) but records **no dollar prices at all**. A fresh, direct fetch of MIWM's own live GTT-package page this round found real, current pricing that changes the competitive picture:

| Tier | Price (AUD) | What's included |
|---|---|---|
| Standard GTT (pathology only, no spa) | **$50.00** | Pathology collection + light snacks — this is the *non-wellness* base option, not what the existing repository's "Standard GTT Experience" row describes |
| MIWM GTT Experience | **$220.00** | 60-min massage OR facial + pathology collection + a short **bulk-billed GP consultation** + snacks/café break |
| MIWM Deluxe GTT Experience | **$400.00-435.00** | Two 60-min treatments (two massages, or massage+facial) + pathology + bulk-billed GP consult |
| Add-on | **$35.00** | Baby-minding/childcare, available on request |

**Staffing model, also newly confirmed:** MIWM uses **"experienced nurses"** for the blood collection itself, not phlebotomists specifically, and separately partners with a GP team on-site (bulk-billed consult bundled into the paid packages) — a materially different clinical-staffing model to GTT Center Perth's own phlebotomist-plus-external-pathology-partner structure, and one that bundles a GP consult as a value-add GTT Center Perth does not currently offer.

**Direct, honest pricing comparison — a genuine new competitive data point:**

| | GTT Center Perth (locked) | MIWM (real, current) |
|---|---|---|
| Entry package | Package 1, A$250 (2x30min) | GTT Experience, $220 (1x60min + bulk-billed GP consult) |
| Higher package | Package 2, A$300 (2x45min, or 1x45+1x30) | Deluxe, $400-435 (2x60min + bulk-billed GP consult) |

**What this means, stated as observation not a recommendation to change a locked price:** GTT Center Perth's entry package (A$250) sits slightly above MIWM's entry package ($220), while GTT Center Perth's own top package (A$300) sits well below MIWM's Deluxe ($400-435) despite MIWM's Deluxe offering less total treatment time per package (2x60min = 120min vs GTT Center Perth Package 2's own 2x45min = 90min, so MIWM is charging more for less time, but including a bundled bulk-billed GP consult GTT Center Perth does not). **This is a genuinely new pricing data point for `docs/price-increase-comparison.md` and the naming-adjacent pricing-timing question (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 36) to consider next time either is reviewed — not applied to pricing here, since pricing is locked.**

**Also newly confirmed:** MIWM states a fasting window of "minimum 8 hours, maximum 16 hours" for accuracy — a slightly wider stated tolerance than the 8-14hr ADIPS-sourced window already used in this venture's own Table 1 staffing verification (`docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` §1). This is not a contradiction requiring action (MIWM's own figure may reflect its own clinical protocol rather than a universal standard, and this venture's own 8-14hr figure is independently ADIPS-sourced, a more authoritative citation than a competitor's patient-facing web page) but is logged as a data point worth being aware of, not adopted.
