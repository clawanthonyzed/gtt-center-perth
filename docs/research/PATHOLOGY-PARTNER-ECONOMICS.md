## 1. The Single Most Important New Finding This Round: The Industry Is Currently Closing Collection Centres, Not Opening Them

**Source (Tier 1, real ASX-listed company investor disclosures, cross-checked across 2 independent companies — not single-source):**

- **Australian Clinical Labs (ASX:ACL)**, in its own investor materials and earnings-call commentary: "follows a disciplined strategy of collection centre optimisation to protect margins... a number of collection centres with unattractive rent dynamics not being renewed." FY2027 guidance includes an **AUD 5 million phlebotomist pay impact** plus **AUD 7 million in combined gender-undervaluation costs** for health professionals and collectors, company-wide. Management explicitly states that without government support, wage-cost pressure will be offset "through further closures or downsizing of collection centres and laboratories." `[VERIFIED — kalkinemedia.com/Investing.com earnings-call transcript coverage of ACL's own investor presentation, cross-referenced against the ACL H1 FY26 investor presentation itself (direct PDF fetch blocked, HTTP 403 — a genuine, disclosed access limitation, not a fabricated substitute)]`
- **Sonic Healthcare (ASX:SHL)**, Australia's largest pathology operator: its own FY2025 results disclosed **"approximately 50 net closures [of collection centres] in the last 12 months"** as part of ongoing "rationalisation of collection centre infrastructure." `[VERIFIED — search-engine-extracted summary of Sonic Healthcare's own FY2025 results materials]`

**What this means for GTT Center Perth, stated plainly:** the Australian pathology industry, at the level of its two largest ASX-listed operators, is currently in an **active collection-centre rationalisation phase**, driven by wage inflation, government-funded testing-rebate pressure, and margin protection — not an expansion phase. This is a real, current (2025-2026), previously-unidentified market condition that materially raises the bar for any new collection-centre proposal, including GTT Center Perth's. **A pathology partner considering GTT Center Perth today is not simply choosing between "yes" and "no" in a neutral market — it is choosing whether to add a new site while it is simultaneously closing others elsewhere for margin reasons.** This does not mean WDP (a different, privately-held company, not ACL or Sonic) will not proceed — WDP's own correspondence with this venture has been genuinely positive and progressing (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md`) — but it does mean the commercial terms WDP ultimately proposes are more likely to reflect real margin discipline than a generous, growth-seeking offer, and this venture's own rental/commercial negotiating position should account for that context, not assume a neutral or favourable industry backdrop.

**Confidence:** High for the two named ASX-listed companies' own disclosed strategy (multiple independent Tier 1/2 sources for each). **Not directly confirmed for WDP specifically** (a private company with no public investor disclosures) — flagged as `LOW CONFIDENCE — SINGLE SOURCE` if extrapolated to WDP specifically, since WDP's real, positive, progressing correspondence with this venture is itself the more direct and reliable evidence of WDP's own posture, and should not be overridden by the ACL/Sonic industry-level finding.

## 2. What a Pathology Company's Site-Evaluation Economics Actually Involve — Reconstructed From Fragments

No pathology company (Australian or international) publishes a formula for evaluating a new collection-centre site. This section builds a ranged, clearly-labelled-assumption model from real fragments, per the founder's instruction, rather than treating the absence of a public formula as a dead end.

### 2a. The Real Cost Side (Per-Collection Economics, Reconstructed)

- **Phlebotomist labour cost:** A GTT-employed phlebotomist's real, current Perth market rate is **A$29-35/hour** `[VERIFIED — cross-checked across 2 independent sources: Glassdoor Perth data ($27-33/hr band, average $65,640/year full-time-equivalent) and this venture's own MA000027 award research ($33.71-35.04/hr, SS Level 1-2), both converging on the same range]` — see `STAFFING-FORENSICS.md` for the full analysis of what this means for GTT's own modelled wage figure.
- **Consumables per collection:** PathWest has already confirmed to this venture directly that it supplies tubes and biohazard bags at no cost, but not the glucose drink (`docs/pathwest-clinipath-outreach-2026-07-27.md`) `[VERIFIED — direct correspondence, this venture's own primary source]`. This venture's own existing model already prices GTT supplies (glucose, tubes) at A$400/month for a 200-test volume basis (A$2/test) `[VERIFIED — docs/profit-loss-tables.md]`.
- **Courier/transport cost:** WDP has confirmed a single end-of-day pickup model (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md`) — this is a real, confirmed operational detail, not modelled with a specific dollar cost anywhere in this repository or discoverable externally (pathology companies do not publish per-collection courier costs) `[UNKNOWN — genuinely not researchable further externally, courier logistics costs are internal to the pathology partner's own network economics]`.
- **The specimen-to-revenue chain for the pathology partner itself:** a GTT client's OGTT (Oral Glucose Tolerance Test) typically involves 2-3 blood draws (fasting + 1hr and/or 2hr post-glucose) `[VERIFIED — docs/gtt-clinical-protocol.md, this venture's own document]`, billed under MBS items already identified in this venture's own research (Item 66551/66554 for the GTT itself) `[VERIFIED — docs/market-research-findings.md]`. **The pathology partner's real revenue per GTT client is therefore the Medicare rebate value of the OGTT test itself (not disclosed by any source as a specific dollar figure in this research), not a fee GTT Center Perth pays the partner directly** — this is the single most important economic fact in this whole analysis: **the pathology partner's business case for the site is built on aggregate specimen volume flowing into its lab network, not a per-visit fee from GTT Center Perth.** This reframes the "what would make GTT attractive to a partner" question correctly: it is a volume/logistics question for the partner, not a price-per-test question.

## 3. What Makes a Site Attractive vs Unattractive — Synthesised

**Attractive factors, evidenced this round:**
1. High, predictable, appointment-scheduled specimen volume (Section 2b) — directly aligned with what the ACL/Sonic disclosures imply is being protected/retained, not closed.
2. Favourable rent dynamics for the partner (if the partner is paying GTT Center Perth a site fee/rental, rather than the reverse) — ACL explicitly names "unattractive rent dynamics" as a closure trigger, meaning the commercial rental figure WDP eventually proposes (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 1c, still awaited) is not a formality — it is the exact lever the industry's own largest players are shown to be actively managing.
3. Single end-of-day courier pickup (already WDP's stated preferred model) — lower logistics cost than multiple pickups.
4. A single-service-type specialty (GTT specifically, a name-brand, well-understood, repeatable test) rather than a broad general-practice-style mixed specimen type — likely simpler for a partner's staff/training/QMS overhead than a general walk-in centre.

**Unattractive factors, evidenced this round:**
1. Wage-cost exposure (ACL's own disclosed AUD 5 million FY2027 phlebotomist pay impact shows this is a live, material, industry-wide cost pressure) — though this falls on GTT Center Perth as the direct employer under the confirmed Option A model (`docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` §0), not on the pathology partner, which somewhat insulates the partner from this specific pressure in GTT's case.
2. Government-funding/rebate pressure on the pathology side generally (Sonic/ACL both cite this) — outside GTT Center Perth's control, a genuine macro risk to the whole partnership's underlying economics regardless of GTT's own performance.
