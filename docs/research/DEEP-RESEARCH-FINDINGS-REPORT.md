## Already Known (Confirmed, Not Newly Discovered, Genuinely Re-Verified Not Just Repeated)

- No WA-based direct competitor (GTT wait plus wellness) exists — re-confirmed independently this round via a fresh search, not just repeated from prior research.
- None of the pathology partners (now checked across 6 Australian companies: WDP, PathWest, Clinipath, ACL, plus QML and Laverty newly checked this round) publishes commercial evaluation criteria for hosting a collection centre — a broader, more confident version of the existing finding.
- GTT-specific appointment-only scheduling (not walk-in) is standard practice elsewhere, independently confirmed via the Canberra Hospital collection centre and the Quest Diagnostics appointment-priority model — this venture's own model was already appointment-based, but this is now independently evidenced as the right operational pattern, not just this venture's own choice.
- GTT Center Perth's own modelled labour-cost percentage (~57.5% of revenue) sits within the real, independently-sourced industry benchmark band (40-60%) — a genuine validation of the existing bottom-up financial model.

## Newly Discovered (Did Not Exist Anywhere in This Repository Before This Mission)

1. **Real, current MIWM pricing** ($50/$220/$400-435), staffing model (nurses, not phlebotomists, plus a bundled bulk-billed GP consult) — the existing repository had package descriptions with no dollar figures at all.
2. **A second, independently-sourced Australian prepaid-services insolvency case** (Australian Laser & Skin Clinics, ~A$5M debt, 1,000+ customers owed A$500,000) — this establishes a genuine 2-case pattern, not a single anecdote (Adytum).
3. **Two previously unflagged Australian compliance questions**: Health Insurance Act 1973 s129AA (pathology referral inducement law) as it may apply to GTT's referral-courtesy model, and a prepaid-payment/Corporations Act non-cash-payment-facility question. Both flagged for solicitor review, neither resolved.
4. **Real Australian business-for-sale financial fragments**: a 20+ year South Perth day spa (turnover ~A$286K/year, rent ~A$69K/year, asking A$85,000) and 8 further real hair/beauty salon listings, revealing single-service salons trade at a materially lower resale multiple (roughly 1.0-1.4x profit, $55K-$200K) than the broader day-spa sector average (1.4-3.5x SDE).
5. **Real Australian staff-turnover figures** for the hair/beauty industry (37% average, 61% leave within first year, 65% of owners struggle with it).
6. **A closed local analogue** (Yummy Mummy Pregnancy Day Spa, Perth) — a real pregnancy-exclusive Perth day spa that no longer operates, cause unconfirmed.
7. **Real international analogues** with no Australian equivalent: Any Lab Test Now (US direct-to-consumer lab-draw franchise, real FDD-sourced revenue figures), US IV/hydration lounges (clinical procedure delivered as premium hospitality experience), PamperSuite (US OBGYN-adjacent spa).
8. **A real, quantified laundry/linen hidden-cost range** (A$7,000-43,200/year depending on in-house vs outsourced model and volume) — the existing repository had already flagged laundry outsourcing as an open decision but never quantified either option.

## Deeper Insight (Existed in Fragments Across the Repo or the Web, Now Substantiated)

1. **The rent-to-revenue ratio contradiction.** GTT Center Perth's own modelled figures imply a ~5.6% rent-to-revenue ratio; the one real, current, comparable Perth day-spa data point found this round implies ~24%. Both a benign explanation (GTT's higher revenue-per-sqm from its multi-service, high-throughput model) and a concerning one (an understated rent assumption) are plausible and not distinguished by available evidence — this is a genuine, substantiated pressure-test finding, not resolvable without a real venue quote.
2. **The utilisation assumption is validated, with an honest data-quality caveat.** GTT's own ~50% PM utilisation assumption sits at the very bottom of the real ISPA 50-65% benchmark band — a defensible, conservative planning figure, though the ISPA figure itself traces to data as old as 2004, disclosed rather than presented as current.
3. **Staff cross-training as a resilience strategy is independently validated** by real salon-crisis-management sources, entirely separate from this venture's own reasoning for adopting it.
4. **The prepaid-package cash-flow risk is now pattern-level evidence (2 independent cases), not a single anecdote** — directly actionable as a bookkeeping/process recommendation, not a pricing-model change.

## Contradictions Found

1. **Rent-to-revenue ratio** (see Deeper Insight §1) — the most material contradiction found, genuinely unresolved, flagged for venue-specific verification.
2. **Antenatal follow-up non-attendance (50-58%) vs the initial GTT test itself** — a potential misapplication risk if these figures were ever assumed to describe the initial screening appointment; explicitly flagged as describing a different, more deferrable appointment type, not a finding to import into the AM capacity model.
3. **MIWM's pricing structure vs GTT Center Perth's own packages** — MIWM charges more for a smaller top-tier time allocation (2x60min for $400-435) than GTT Center Perth's own Package 2 (2x45min for A$300), but bundles a GP consult GTT Center Perth does not offer — not a contradiction requiring action, but a genuine pricing-positioning data point for the next pricing review.

## Risks

1. Referral-inducement law exposure (Health Insurance Act s129AA) — genuinely untested, low probability of actual breach but unconfirmed.
2. Prepaid-payment consumer-law/AFSL exposure — genuinely untested, low probability but unconfirmed.
3. Real rent-to-revenue ratio risk at whatever specific venue is eventually secured — could compress margin more than the current model implies if the real quote sits closer to the ~24% real-world comparable than the ~5.6% modelled figure.
4. Ongoing staff-turnover/retraining cost is not currently modelled as a recurring line — a real, if unquantified, understatement risk in the current financial model.
5. GTT-specific no-show rate is genuinely unknown and could reduce realised AM revenue below the designed-capacity figure, though it does not affect scheduling feasibility.
