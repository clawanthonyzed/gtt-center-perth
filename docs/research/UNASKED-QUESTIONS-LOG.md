## Q1: What happens when the only/one available phlebotomist calls in sick on a fully-booked AM day?

**Researched answer:** A "Pathology Collector Reliever" is a real, actively advertised role type at Australian pathology employers (confirmed again this round via SA Health/SA Pathology job listings, Perth-based reliever roles also found in earlier repository research per `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 11's related findings). This is not a new discovery (already noted in the existing repository as a negotiation point with WDP), but it is now independently re-confirmed as a real, standard industry role, not a hopeful assumption. **Genuinely unanswered:** whether GTT Center Perth itself would need to maintain its own casual relief-phlebotomist pool (since it employs its phlebotomists directly per the 2026-09-19 founder decision, `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` §0) or could access the pathology partner's own relief pool despite not being the partner's direct employee — this is a genuinely open negotiation question, not yet asked of WDP specifically (logged in `NEXT-ACTIONS-TO-LAUNCH.md`).

## Q2: How much staff time does laundry/linen actually consume per day, and is it worth outsourcing?

**Researched answer:** Quantified this round — see `docs/research/PROFIT-OPTIMISATION-REGISTER.md` Opportunity 4 and `FINANCIAL-FORENSICS-RECONSTRUCTION.md`. Real cost bands found: A$7,000-21,600/year in pure in-house labour (1-3 hours/day at $19-20/hour) plus utilities/detergent (~$2,500-6,000/year), vs A$16,200-43,200/year for a professional linen service at a 150-towel/day benchmark volume. Not yet scaled to GTT Center Perth's own specific towel/linen volume (likely higher than the 150-towel benchmark given 18 AM clients/day plus PM sessions) — a genuine, real, previously-unquantified cost that was simply never asked before this round.

## Q3: What is the real no-show rate GTT Center Perth should actually plan for, and does it differ between the AM and PM segments?

**Researched answer:** Partially answered, with an important limit stated clearly. General Australian medical/dental no-show rates are 12-21% (dental) and 18% (general medical) — see `OWNER-OPERATOR-FAILURE-INTELLIGENCE.md` §7. **Genuinely unanswered:** no source gives a no-show rate specific to an initial mandatory GTT screening appointment (as distinct from a discretionary follow-up) — this is a real, disclosed gap, not filled by inference from the antenatal follow-up non-attendance figures (50-58%) found this round, which describe a different, more deferrable appointment type and would overstate the real risk if misapplied.

## Q4: Which of GTT Center Perth's planned services looks profitable on paper but might not be in practice?

**Researched answer:** Not conclusively answerable without real trading data, but one candidate flagged by this round's research: **any service requiring a dedicated single-qualification specialist with no cross-training pairing (Nails, Hair — per `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` §4's own finding that "Nails and Hair have no confirmed dual-qualification pairing")** is structurally more exposed to the staffing-crisis risk documented in `OWNER-OPERATOR-FAILURE-INTELLIGENCE.md` §6 than the Massage+Beauty pool, which already has a cross-training pairing built in. This is not evidence any specific service is actually unprofitable — it is evidence of which services carry more *staffing-continuity* risk than others, a related but distinct question this venture had not previously framed this way.

## Q5: What causes staff turnover in this specific industry, and does GTT Center Perth's model make it better or worse?

**Researched answer:** See `OWNER-OPERATOR-FAILURE-INTELLIGENCE.md` §5 — real Australian figures (37% average turnover, 61% leave within first year) and causes (training, wages, culture, career-progression, a Gen Z entrepreneurial-exit trend). **Genuinely new observation this round:** GTT Center Perth's own model may be *structurally more resistant* to one specific driver of this turnover than a typical salon — because staff are hired dual-qualified (Massage+Beauty) and rotate between AM collection-adjacent wellness service and PM standalone wellness service, the role is arguably more varied and skill-broadening than a single-service salon job, which several sourced turnover-cause articles specifically cite as a retention driver (career development, skill variety). This is an inference, not a confirmed finding — logged as a hypothesis worth testing with real staff, not a proven advantage.

## Q6: What do pathology partners worry about that could kill a collection-site partnership's economics, beyond what WDP has already told this venture directly?

**Researched answer:** Confirmed this round, across 6 Australian pathology companies (not just the 4 already researched), that none publishes its internal economics or a formal risk checklist (`PATHOLOGY-OPERATIONS-DEEP-DIVE.md` §2). The only concrete, non-Australian directional signal found (Indian franchise criteria) points to **expected volume, catchment accessibility, and referring-doctor proximity** as commonly-weighed factors — all three of which WDP has already told this venture directly matter to its own assessment (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 1c), meaning this question is now more confidently answered as "consistent with what WDP already said" rather than genuinely new, but it was asked and checked rather than assumed.

## Q7: Has anyone anywhere combined a mandatory (not discretionary) medical procedure with a premium wellness/hospitality experience, outside of MIWM and GTT Center Perth?

**Researched answer:** The closest analogues found are US IV/hydration lounges (a clinical procedure delivered in a premium environment, though the IV itself is elective, not mandatory) and PamperSuite (wellness co-located with, not combined into, an OBGYN practice) — see `docs/research/CASE-STUDY-DATABASE.md` Cases 9-10. **No exact analogue was found anywhere internationally.** This is a genuinely useful negative result: it means GTT Center Perth's specific combination (mandatory test + premium multi-service concurrent experience) may be more novel, and therefore has less external precedent to learn from, than the existing repository's competitive positioning claims (which cite MIWM as the only comparable model) had fully appreciated.

## Q8: If GTT Center Perth's referral-partnership complimentary-visit offer succeeds beyond expectations, what breaks?

**Researched answer:** Quantified for the first time this round in `PROFIT-OPTIMISATION-REGISTER.md` Opportunity 2 — at full uptake across all 22 first-wave target practices, the opportunity cost is a bounded ~A$2,000-2,600 in forgone PM session revenue, small relative to the overall model. Nothing "breaks" at this scale; this question's main value was confirming the exposure is genuinely small, not assuming it without checking.
