## Opportunity 1: Realised No-Show Rate Likely Better Than the Generic Benchmark Suggests

**Evidence:** `STAFFING-FORENSICS.md` §4 found that GTT Center Perth's own already-locked choices (full payment at booking, Fresha's automated reminders) place it structurally closer to the low end of real no-show benchmarks (5-10%) than the general Australian medical no-show average (18%). **This is not a new opportunity to build — it is upside already latent in decisions already made, not yet reflected anywhere in the financial model, which currently implies (by omission) a no-show rate of 0%.**

**Recommendation:** when the AM revenue-ramp model is next revisited, model a realistic 5-10% no-show band explicitly rather than implicitly assuming 0%, so that the venture's own revenue expectations are calibrated to reality from Day 1, rather than discovering a "shortfall" against an unrealistic 100%-attendance baseline once trading begins.

## Opportunity 2: Real Specimen Volume as Genuine Negotiating Leverage With the Pathology Partner

**Evidence:** `PATHOLOGY-PARTNER-ECONOMICS.md` §2b calculated GTT Center Perth's real, solver-verified specimen volume (9,500-14,000/year at Table 1) and §1 found that the two largest ASX-listed pathology operators are actively closing lower-volume, less-predictable collection centres to protect margin. **This means GTT Center Perth's own volume and appointment-scheduled predictability are a genuine, real asset in any commercial negotiation with WDP (or any partner)** — not just a description of the venture's own operations, but a specific point to make explicitly in commercial negotiation: a single, predictable, end-of-day-pickup site generating this volume is easier and cheaper for a partner's logistics network to serve than the same aggregate volume spread across several smaller sites, which is exactly the type of site the industry's own largest players are shown to be protecting, not closing.

**Recommendation:** Reed (or whoever leads the WDP commercial negotiation) should use GTT's own real volume figures explicitly in that conversation, framed against the industry-wide rationalisation context found this round — this is a genuine, evidence-based negotiating point, not spin.

## Opportunity 4: Customer Acquisition Cost / Lifetime Value Benchmarks (Priority 8)

**Real benchmarks found this round:** a defensible CAC:CLV ratio for a service business is commonly cited at 1:3 or better (CLV should exceed CAC by at least 3x) `[BENCHMARK — Tier 3/4]`; spas with a formal loyalty program grew revenue 14%/year on average vs 7% for the industry overall, with "nearly all of the outperformance" attributed to retention and visit frequency, not new-client acquisition `[REPORTED — Tier 3/4, source did not disclose its own underlying methodology, flagged as a real claim not independently re-verified]`.

**Direct relevance to GTT Center Perth:** this venture's own referral-partnership plan (`docs/referral-partnership-plan.md`) is fundamentally a low-CAC acquisition channel (referral-driven, not paid-ads-driven, for the AM segment) — this is already a strong structural position relative to the 1:3 CAC:CLV benchmark, since referral-driven acquisition typically costs materially less than paid channels. **No GTT-specific CAC or CLV figure could be calculated this round** — pre-launch, with no real client data, this remains genuinely unknown `[UNKNOWN]`, not estimated with an invented number.

**Recommendation:** once trading begins, track CAC (marketing spend + referral-program cost, per `PROFIT-OPTIMISATION-REGISTER.md` Opportunity 2's quantified referral-cost estimate) against realised CLV (repeat PM visits + package upgrades) from Month 1, so the real ratio is known within the first few months rather than assumed.
