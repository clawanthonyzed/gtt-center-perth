# The Return Loop

**Status:** Current, name-agnostic. Companion to `docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`. This is the single highest-priority strategic mechanism in the current phase of work, per direct instruction — the business must not rely on GTT volume alone, and this document maps exactly how a referred, one-off AM visit becomes a self-directed, ongoing relationship.

---

## The Loop

```
Visit 1 (GTT, referred — she didn't choose this appointment)
        ↓
Experience (unexpectedly premium — see CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md's arrival/GTT stages)
        ↓
Discovery (she learns the PM/standalone business exists, on its own terms)
        ↓
Follow-up (personalised, useful — not a marketing blast)
        ↓
Visit 2 (optional wellness/beauty service — she chose this one)
        ↓
Relationship (she's now a known, returning guest, not a one-off referral)
        ↓
Advocacy (she recommends it to friends/family — this is where the growth model actually lives)
```

## Why This Loop, Not a Generic Retention Funnel

`docs/business-plan.md`'s own numbers make the strategic case plainly: committed AM capacity is a fixed daily ceiling (see `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` for the current figure), gated entirely by referral volume the business doesn't control. The PM/standalone business has no such ceiling tied to clinical referral — it is bounded only by demand the business can generate itself. **A visit-1-only business is capped by other people's referral behaviour. A business that converts visit-1 guests into self-directed repeat customers is not.** This is the commercial argument for treating the loop above as a first-order priority, not a nice-to-have.

## Stage-by-Stage Mechanism

### Visit 1 → Experience
**Mechanism:** Everything in `CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s Arrival and GTT sections — competence at the needle moment, an itinerary she can see, no unmanaged waiting. This stage's only job is to overturn the expectation she arrived with ("braced for fluorescent light and a plastic chair").
**What breaks it:** Any friction at the anxious points (unclear timing, clinical-feeling reception, an underwhelming environment) resets her expectations back to "this was just a medical appointment," and the rest of the loop doesn't get a chance to start.

### Experience → Discovery
**Mechanism:** The leaving moment (`CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s "leaving experience" stage) is where discovery of the PM business should first happen — not as a sales pitch during her GTT visit, but folded into the same physical object/gesture that ends the visit. A single line on the leaving card/box naming one specific thing she could book next (not a generic services list) outperforms a menu handed over at reception.
**What breaks it:** Upselling during the clinical visit itself, before the leaving moment — this reads as transactional at exactly the point trust is still being built, and undoes more than it gains.

### Discovery → Follow-up
**Mechanism:** A single, genuinely useful follow-up message (when her results reach her doctor — a real, non-marketing piece of information) is the vehicle. The PM mention rides alongside it once, briefly, not as the message's main purpose.
**What breaks it:** Leading with a marketing message before the outstanding clinical question (her results) is addressed. Sequencing matters — usefulness first, invitation second.

### Follow-up → Visit 2
**Mechanism:** A low-friction, specific booking prompt (not a generic "book now" link) — ideally referencing the same treatment category she experienced in Visit 1's wellness component, if she had one, since familiarity lowers the barrier to a first self-directed booking.
**What breaks it:** A generic marketing cadence (weekly emails, broad discounting) reads as exactly the kind of business this brand is positioned against — see `BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`'s voice rules on avoiding sales language.

### Visit 2 → Relationship
**Mechanism:** Staff continuity and recognition — being remembered by name and treatment history on a second visit is what converts "a nice business" into "my place." This is a staffing/operations commitment (booking system + Venue Manager continuity), not a marketing one.
**What breaks it:** Being treated as a new customer on Visit 2 undoes the relationship-building the whole loop exists to create.

### Relationship → Advocacy
**Mechanism:** A specific, describable experience (see `CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s "Discovery" stage on why specificity travels by word of mouth and genericness doesn't) is what actually gets repeated to a friend. Advocacy isn't requested — it's a byproduct of the experience being specific enough to describe.
**What breaks it:** Asking directly for reviews/referrals before the experience has earned them makes the ask feel transactional rather than a natural consequence of a genuinely good visit.

## What This Loop Requires Operationally (Not Just Creatively)

- A booking/CRM system that actually tracks visit history and preferences well enough to make "relationship" real, not aspirational (per `docs/business-plan.md`'s existing booking-system notes).
- A follow-up message that is genuinely useful (results timing) before it is promotional.
- Staff continuity policy — `docs/staff-plan.md`'s existing "consistency of staffing... continuity of care" principle (already established, sourced from Carole/WDP's own stated preference in a different context, but the same logic applies to treatment staff) directly supports this loop and should be read as reinforcing it, not a separate concern.

## What This Loop Does Not Require

Loyalty programs, discount codes, or aggressive marketing cadences are explicitly not part of this mechanism — none of the six stages above depend on them, and `BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`'s voice/positioning rules argue directly against leading with discounting at a premium price point. If a membership tier is explored later (both design boards raise this as a possibility — "The Elowen Year," a Solena membership line), it should be evaluated as a way to formalise an already-working loop, not as a substitute for the loop itself.
