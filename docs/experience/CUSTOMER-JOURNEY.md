# Customer Journey

**Status:** Current, name-agnostic. Companion to `docs/strategy/BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`. Grounded in `docs/business-plan.md`'s confirmed operating model (AM GTT + PM standalone) and the welcome-card concept from both Phase 1/Phase 2 design explorations, which is treated here as a validated idea worth carrying forward regardless of which name wins — not name-specific creative. **UPDATED 2026-08-19: the "leaving box" departure-gesture concept previously referenced here was REMOVED per Anthony's explicit decision (not approved, must not appear in forward-facing material) — see the Departure Experience section below.**

Two journeys exist in parallel: **AM (GTT, referred)** and **PM (standalone, self-booked)**. They share a physical space and a brand but start from different emotional positions — the AM guest didn't choose to be here today; the PM guest did. Both are mapped below where they diverge.

---

## BEFORE

### Referral (AM only)
**Emotion:** Neutral-to-anxious. A clinical requirement, not a choice.
**Expectation:** A standard pathology-collection experience.
**Brand opportunity:** The referral itself is a low-control moment for the brand — the midwife/OB's own description of the venue does real work here. See `BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`'s referral-strategy note on giving referrers a precise, repeatable description.
**Design opportunity:** A short, specific one-pager for referring practices (not a generic brochure) that describes exactly what happens, in the same plain language the venue itself uses.
**Friction:** If the referrer's own description undersells or mischaracterises the experience (e.g. "there's a nice waiting room"), the guest arrives with the wrong expectation and the "unexpectedly lovely" effect is weakened.

### Discovery (PM primarily, AM secondarily)
**Emotion:** Curious, evaluating.
**Expectation:** Enough clarity to trust this isn't a generic day spa or a clinical outfit.
**Brand opportunity:** The single highest-leverage discovery moment for a word-of-mouth business — a specific, repeatable sentence a friend can say, not a marketing description.
**Design opportunity:** Social presence built around specificity (real moments, real language) over polish-for-its-own-sake — both design explorations independently reached this instinct.
**Friction:** Genericness. A generic "wellness experience" doesn't get repeated; a specific one does.

### Website
**Emotion:** Information-seeking, low patience for ambiguity.
**Expectation:** Clear answers — what happens, how long, what it costs, how to book.
**Brand opportunity:** Answer the anxious AM question ("what actually happens to me") in plain language above the fold, and let the PM offering stand entirely on its own page, addressed to a woman with no idea a GTT service exists here.
**Design opportunity:** Two clearly separated entry paths (book a morning / book an afternoon) rather than one undifferentiated booking flow.
**Friction:** A single homepage that reads as "pregnancy clinic" first will cost PM conversions from women who aren't pregnant and don't want to feel like they've wandered into a clinic.

### Booking
**Emotion:** Transactional, wants speed and clarity.
**Expectation:** A simple, modern booking flow — not a clinical intake form.
**Brand opportunity:** Collect only what's needed; ask for a first name early and use it from that point on.
**Design opportunity:** Fasting/preparation instructions delivered as hospitality copy, not a compliance checklist (both design explorations converged independently on this — see the "nothing to eat after 10pm, water whenever you like" register versus a clinical fasting notice).
**Friction:** Any step that reads as a medical intake form rather than a booking confirmation undoes work the rest of the brand is doing.

### Confirmation / Pre-arrival
**Emotion:** AM: rising anxiety the night before. PM: mild anticipation.
**Expectation:** A reminder, practical information.
**Brand opportunity:** The single highest-leverage pre-arrival moment for AM guests — a reassuring, specific message the night before (both boards' "10pm, fasting" copy tests this directly and both pass). One pre-emptive line addressing the most common fear (the needle) reduces first-ten-seconds anxiety on arrival.
**Design opportunity:** First-name, plain-language, one clear action (add to calendar), one open door ("reply here — it reaches the front desk, not a system").
**Friction:** An automated, reference-numbered confirmation reads exactly like the clinical experience she's hoping this won't be.

### Preparation
**Emotion:** AM: mild dread (fasting), practical planning. PM: none beyond normal appointment logistics.
**Brand opportunity:** none beyond what confirmation already delivers.

## ARRIVAL

### Parking / Entry
**Emotion:** AM: nervous, possibly running on no breakfast. PM: neutral.
**Expectation:** Easy to find, doesn't look like a medical building from the street.
**Design opportunity:** Deliberately under-signed exterior (both boards independently favour restraint over a lightbox/vinyl-heavy clinical shopfront) — a genuine premium signal, not a compromise.
**Friction:** Confusing wayfinding at the exact moment she's most anxious is the worst possible place for friction to live.

### Reception / Welcome
**Emotion:** AM: this is the moment competence needs to be legible within the first ten seconds. PM: wants to feel like any premium beauty booking.
**Expectation:** Being expected, by name, without having to explain why she's there.
**Brand opportunity:** No counter/clipboard/reference-number model — a table, one chair on the guest side, first name used immediately (both boards independently designed this exact reception model).
**Design opportunity:** The environment itself does credibility work here — see `PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`.
**Friction:** Any moment of "what's your name/DOB/reference number" here reads as clinical intake, undoing everything else.

### Sensory environment / Waiting
**Emotion:** AM: this is where "braced for fluorescent light and a plastic chair" either gets confirmed or overturned. PM: expects a normal premium-salon environment.
**Brand opportunity:** The single biggest "relief, then quiet pleasure" moment in the whole journey, per both design explorations' own emotional-territory language.
**Design opportunity:** Natural light, seating built for a genuine wait (not a transactional lobby), no visible clinical equipment outside the one room that needs it.
**Friction:** A beautiful lounge next to a visibly clinical collection area undermines the whole effect — visual separation matters as much as visual quality.

## GTT (AM only)

### Clinical process
**Emotion:** The needle moment — the single highest-anxiety point in the entire journey.
**Expectation:** Competent, quick, not dwelt on.
**Brand opportunity:** Competence delivered warmly, without being made into an event either way (neither over-clinical nor over-performed reassurance).
**Design opportunity:** One pre-emptive line acknowledging the needle before it happens (tested and confirmed working in both design boards) — normalises the fear without amplifying it.
**Friction:** Under-acknowledging it reads as cold; over-acknowledging it reads as anxious itself.

### Comfort / hospitality / timing / privacy
**Emotion:** Should shift from clinical to hospitality the moment the draw is done.
**Expectation:** A clear itinerary — she should never have to ask "what happens next" or "how much longer."
**Brand opportunity:** The welcome/itinerary card — both design explorations independently identified this as the single most valuable physical object in the business ("does more brand work than the website"). Converts a clinical protocol into a schedule she can see and trust.
**Design opportunity:** Physical card, handed over at the point of arrival, listing every draw and every treatment by clock time — letterpressed or otherwise considered, not printed on office paper. **Card contents, expanded 2026-08-16 (F16), per founder instruction:** her personal timetable (every draw/treatment by clock time, as above), the name of the staff member assigned to each treatment (personalises the schedule, sets an expectation of being looked after by a specific person), her selected food/drink choice for the leaving moment (confirmed at booking or on arrival, printed on the card so it's ready without her needing to ask again), and a Google review link/QR code (placed here rather than only verbally requested at departure, per `staff-plan.md`'s existing "collect Google review requests" duty — giving her the link early, on a card she keeps, is lower-friction than a verbal ask at the door). Low-cost, real addition to an already-proven concept, not a redesign of it.
**Friction:** Silence or ambiguity about timing is the single largest source of anxiety in a multi-hour medical wait; this is the highest-leverage fix in the entire journey and should not be deprioritised for cost reasons.

## AFTER

### Departure experience
**Emotion:** Should land as "that was unexpectedly lovely," not "my appointment is finished."
**Expectation:** Nothing — this is a moment of genuine upside potential if done well.
**Brand opportunity:** A genuine design priority — the departure moment, once she is no longer fasting, is a real opportunity for the visit to end well. **REMOVED 2026-08-19, per Anthony's explicit decision: the previously-described "leaving box"/physical parting-gesture concept (a small considered parcel handed to the client at departure) is NOT approved and must not appear in any forward-facing material.** No replacement mechanism is proposed here — this remains an open design question.
**Design opportunity:** Not yet designed. See `docs/architecture/CAFE-FOOD-SERVICE-INVESTIGATION.md` for the separate, legitimate question of on-site food/beverage service generally (a distinct topic from the removed departure-gesture concept).
**Friction:** A rushed or generic send-off ("thanks, see you next time!") is a missed opportunity, though the specific fix is not yet designed.

### Refreshments
A separate, legitimate food/beverage topic — see `docs/architecture/CAFE-FOOD-SERVICE-INVESTIGATION.md`. Not tied to any departure-gesture mechanic (see above — that concept is removed).

### Gift / touchpoint
**REMOVED 2026-08-19** — no gift/parting-item mechanic is currently approved. If a future design solution is proposed for the departure moment (see above), it should be evaluated fresh, not by reference to the removed concept.

### Follow-up
**Emotion:** AM: mild residual anxiety about results. PM: neutral-positive.
**Expectation:** To be told what happens next, not chased for a rebooking.
**Brand opportunity:** A genuinely useful follow-up (when results reach her doctor, stated plainly) does more relationship-building work than a marketing message, and earns the right to mention other services once, briefly, afterward.
**Friction:** Leading with an upsell before addressing the actual outstanding question (her results) reads as transactional and undoes trust built during the visit.

### Service discovery
**Emotion:** AM: mild curiosity, if the visit itself was good. PM: already engaged.
**Brand opportunity:** This is where the AM guest first learns the PM business exists on its own terms — not as an upsell during her GTT visit, but as a genuine discovery afterward. See `RETURN-LOOP.md`.

## The Key Question This Journey Is Built to Answer

At every stage above, the design/brand opportunity column exists to answer one question: **what makes her want to return?** The honest answer, drawn from the evidence across both design explorations and this map: not one grand gesture, but the absence of friction at the anxious points (arrival, the needle, timing). **CORRECTED 2026-08-19, Phase 4 Group A build:** this closing summary previously also cited "one genuine, specific, low-cost moment of generosity at the leaving point" as part of the answer — a residual reference to the removed leaving-moment/leaving-box concept that survived the 2026-08-19 repo-wide removal pass because it lived in this summary paragraph rather than a dedicated departure-stage section. Removed here for the same reason it was removed everywhere else: not approved, and the departure moment remains a genuinely open design question (see Departure Experience above), not one with an implied answer. Nothing above requires an expensive intervention to work — see `PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md` for the cost-discipline treatment of each idea above.
