## 2. Division 7A in plain English (V, s 109B outline and sections named)
Division 7A stops owners taking company profits out tax-free. If a PRIVATE COMPANY gives money or value to a SHAREHOLDER or a shareholder's ASSOCIATE (a spouse or partner counts as an associate), the law can treat it as an unfranked dividend in that person's hands. Three triggers:
1. Payments (s 109C): money paid, credited or property transferred to the owner or associate. A loan is NOT a payment (s 109C(3A)). Letting them use a company asset can count (s 109CA).
2. Loans (s 109D): a loan from the company not fully repaid by the company's tax return lodgment day. "Loan" includes advances, credit and any deal that is in substance a loan (s 109D(3)).
3. Forgiven debts (s 109F): the company lets an owner off a debt.

Safe harbours:
- Complying loan agreement (s 109N): in writing before lodgment day; interest at least the benchmark rate each year after the first; maximum term 7 years (unsecured) or 25 years (registered mortgage over real property worth at least 110 percent of the loan).
- Minimum yearly repayments (s 109E): miss one and the shortfall becomes a dividend.
- Paying the owner what the company owes them is not a dividend, if it is no more than an arm's length amount (s 109J).
- Loans in the ordinary course of business on usual terms (s 109M); payments that are already taxable income, such as wages (s 109L).
- Repayment can be made by setting a declared dividend off against the loan (s 109R(3)(a)). Round-robin repayments funded by a new loan do not count (s 109R(2)).
- Deemed dividends are capped at the company's "distributable surplus" (s 109Y).
- The ATO Commissioner may disregard an honest mistake or let the dividend be franked (s 109RB).

Unpaid present entitlements (Subdivisions EA and EB) only matter when a TRUST owes money to a company. Route B has no trust in the chain, so they are not relevant to a directly owned company.

Benchmark interest rate (V, ATO page): 8.77 percent for the income year ending 30 June 2027 (RBA indicator rate published 5 June 2026); 8.37 percent for the year ending 30 June 2026.

## 3. GTT note (Route B: GTT company pays 25 percent tax, franked dividends direct to Anthony and his partner; no trust)

| Money flow | Is it Division 7A? | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Anthony and his partner LEND their savings TO the company | No. Division 7A is about value going FROM the company TO them | Write a simple loan agreement (amount, at call or term, interest or none, repayment) and record it in the company accounts as a shareholder loan |
| Company REPAYS that loan | Normally no (s 109J), as long as it repays no more than it owes | Keep a running loan account; never repay more than the balance |
| Company pays them INTEREST on that loan | Interest at a commercial rate is ordinary interest income to them (deductible to the company). Interest above an arm's length rate risks being a s 109C payment | Accountant to set a defensible rate. Note: interest on prepaid money is excluded from every financial (locked) |
| Company pays a dividend | Not Division 7A; it is a normal franked dividend (Route B) | Declare and minute it; the franking account must support it |
| Company LENDS to either of them, pays a private bill, or lets them use a car or property for private purposes | YES. Deemed unfranked dividend unless repaid by lodgment day or put under a s 109N agreement | Use a written loan at or above 8.77 percent (2026-27), maximum 7 years unsecured, minimum yearly repayments; or set off against a declared dividend |
| Wage to Anthony as administrator and venue manager | Wages are covered by s 109L (taxable income), not Division 7A | This is the open question in GTT-READ-FIRST.md (wage line and MRCA work-hours). Do not pay a wage until Anthony decides |

Three things to get right from day one:

2. Keep company money and personal money apart. One company card for business only.
3. If the company ever lends to Anthony or his partner, do it on a written s 109N agreement at the benchmark rate, before the company's lodgment day.
