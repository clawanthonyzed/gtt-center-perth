## Short answer for Anthony
Yes. A 5 to 10 minute grace for a consult is normal. Most published Australian rules sit at 10 minutes (GoldCare, GV Dental) or 15 minutes (a Perth obstetrics practice). Nobody I found gives a grace for a timed blood test. I found no published late rule from a pathology centre, so the "no grace for the test" rule is GTT's own, backed by the fasting and timing facts.

## Why 10 minutes is the natural cap for a consult
- The midwife item 82105 needs at least 10 minutes with the client (MBS Online factsheet, last updated 18 Feb 2025: https://mbsonline.gov.au/internet/mbsonline/publishing.nsf/Content/2B1C8044CA9E9671CA258C3600834CBF/$File/PDF%20version-Factsheet%20-%20Participating%20midwife%20MBS%20changes%20-%201%20March%202025.pdf). V
- A 25 minute block less 10 minutes late leaves 15 minutes, still above the 10 minute minimum. At 15 minutes late exactly 10 is left, no safety margin. So 10 is the sensible hard stop. E
- The midwife must not run into the next client, because blocks run back to back from 08:15 to 10:30. E

## Recommended rule in plain words (for Anthony to approve)
1. Blood test: your time is your time. If you arrive after your appointment time, we cannot fit the draw in, because draws run in pairs every 25 minutes and you may be fasting. We will rebook you. (No grace. This matches the locked rule.)
2. Midwife consult: arrive 10 minutes early. If you are up to 10 minutes late, the midwife will still see you, but the visit ends at the normal time, so it will be shorter. If you are more than 10 minutes late, or the midwife cannot finish in the time left, we rebook you.
3. Phone us if you are running late. The venue manager tells the midwife at once.
4. If a client is late for a consult on the same morning as her test, the test slot comes first and the consult must end before her draw.
5. Fees for late or rebooked visits: NOT IN REPO. See QUESTIONS.
