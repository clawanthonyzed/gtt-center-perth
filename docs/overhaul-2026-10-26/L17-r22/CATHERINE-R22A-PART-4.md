## The law (Nurses Award 2020 [MA000034], consolidated to 1 August 2026, library r21 raw copy, re-read 2026-10-26; Fair Work Act s 45, PART-2)

| Clause | Text (quoted) | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 14.1(a) | "An employee who works in excess of 5 hours will be entitled to an unpaid meal break of 30 to 60 minutes. ## Is "the midwife chooses on the day" lawful?

**As worded (choose whether to take it): no.** Her shift is 8 hours, so she is entitled to the break, and only a shift of 6 hours or less can forfeit it, even by her agreement (cl 14.1(a)). A standing "skip it if you like" rule invites a s 45 contravention every shift it is skipped without overtime. V, U

**Nearest lawful version (recommended):** the break is always rostered and always offered. On the day she chooses:
1. **when** to take it, any 30 minutes between 12:00 and 14:00 (the 4th to 6th hour after an 08:00 start);
2. **whether** she leaves the building (she is free from duty either way unless told otherwise);
3. if a client need stops her, she works on, tells the venue manager, and takes the break as soon as she can; that time is paid at overtime (cl 14.1(b)).

Nearest alternative if a midwife truly prefers no break: a shift of 6 hours or less, where she may forfeit it by written agreement. That changes the roster; Anthony decides. U

**What GTT must still ensure:** the booking system blocks no consult for at least one 30 minute gap between 12:00 and 14:00; nobody contacts her during it unless she is told she must stay reachable; two 10 minute tea breaks fit around consults; the timesheet records what happened (below) and payroll pays it; records are kept 7 years (FW Act s 535, regs 3.33, 3.34). V, U

## Pay in each case (worked example at A$41.59 an hour, Nurses Award RN level 2 weekday, E from FINANCIAL-CENTER-OF-TRUTH; 08:00 to 16:00 weekday)

| Case | Rule | Paid | Day's pay (E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Took the break, free from duty | cl 14.1(a) unpaid | 7.5 h ordinary | A$311.93 |
| Stayed reachable because GTT told her to, not recalled | cl 14.1(c) paid ordinary for 30 min | 8.0 h ordinary | A$332.72 |
| Stayed reachable and was recalled | cl 14.1(c) then (b): overtime for the recall time until the rest of the break is taken | 8.0 h plus recall at 150% less ordinary | about A$332.72 plus A$20.80 per recalled hour |
| Worked 12:00 to 13:00, then took the break 13:00 to 13:30 | cl 14.1(b): overtime until taken | 6.5 h ordinary + 1.0 h at 150% | A$332.72 |
| Worked through and never took it | cl 14.1(b) | Minimum view: 7.5 h ordinary + 0.5 h at 150% | A$343.12 |
| (same) | Strict reading: "all time worked until the meal break is taken", so 12:00 to 16:00 is overtime | 4 h ordinary + 2 h at 150% + 2 h at 200% | A$457.49 |

The strict reading is a real risk. **Lawyer to check.** Until then, plan on the strict figure for any day the break is not taken. Theodore's locked case already assumes 7.5 paid hours, so taken breaks cost nothing extra. Saturday and casual rates differ (cl 19.1(c), 19.2). E, U

If she chooses to stay on site in the staff room without being told to stay reachable, the break stays unpaid. Once the venue manager asks her to keep her phone on or to "come if needed", it is a paid break. U

## One-page rule for the venue manager

> **Midwife meal break: what you do every shift**
> 1. Every midwife shift has a 30 minute meal break between 12:00 and 14:00. Check the booking system shows a free 30 minute gap in that window. If it does not, move a booking or tell Grace before the day starts.
> 2. The midwife picks the time inside the window and whether she leaves the building. She does not pick whether to have a break.
> 3. During her break, do not ask her to do anything, and do not ask her to stay reachable unless it is truly needed. If you ask her to stay reachable, tick that box: it is paid.
> 4. If a client need stops her break, she takes it as soon as possible. Write the times. That time is paid at overtime.
> 5. Before you leave, check the timesheet box is ticked, times are filled in and both of you have signed. A blank box means GTT has to prove the break happened.
> 6. Never write a break that did not happen. A false record breaks the law (Fair Work Act s 535(4)).
> 7. Two missed breaks in a fortnight for the same midwife: tell Anthony, so the booking pattern is fixed.
