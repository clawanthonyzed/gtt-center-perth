## 4.1 Legal limits for the venue manager's decision table

| Limit | Rule | What it means for the table | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| How much proof | Collect only what is "reasonably necessary" (APP 3.2); health information needs her consent (APP 3.3) | Ask "is an exception reason present?", never "what is wrong?". Statement first; certificate (no diagnosis) only on a repeat claim | V (R21) |
| Pregnancy emergencies | Clinical safety first | Bleeding, fluid, labour, reduced movements: tell her to call her midwife or hospital now. No proof, ever | U |
| Health detail on a booking note or timesheet | Health information includes information about her health and personal information collected to provide a health service (Privacy Act s 6FA); keep it secure (APP 11.1) | Record a code only: "EX-1 approved [date], rebooked [date]". No words like "bleeding", "hospital" or "vomiting". No hospital name | V |
| Who sees it | Sensitive information is not shared between related companies under s 13B(1) | The venue manager (Lounge) takes the call and records the code only. Anything with health detail (a certificate) goes to GTT Medical and is deleted after the decision (APP 11.2) | V |
| Pregnancy discrimination | A rule that disadvantages pregnant women is discrimination unless "reasonable in the circumstances" (SDA s 7(2), s 7B); WA: EOA s 10(2) | Every client is pregnant, so a rule that refuses pregnancy-related reasons (complications, hyperemesis, sudden pregnancy illness) is the risky kind. Accept every pregnancy-related reason, not just the first one | V |
| Unfair term (ACL) | A term is unfair if it causes "a significant imbalance", is not "reasonably necessary" (presumed not, s 24(4)) and causes detriment (s 24(1)); example: a term that penalises only one party (s 25(c)) | A clock rule with **no** exceptions is the riskiest version. A clock rule with written exceptions is defensible | V |
| From 1 July 2027 | New ACL s 28B bans "unfair trading practices", including "failing to disclose material information" and unclear or untimely disclosure | Show the late rule before payment, in plain words, every time | V (R20 library) |
| Fees for lateness | Not banned, but must be disclosed before booking and linked to real loss (penalty risk). Reed's fee options are a separate decision | If a fee is ever added: same price for everyone, written in the terms, waived for every exception | L (R20), U |
| Repeat claims | Escalation is fine | Escalate to Grace or the lead midwife, not to Anthony (keeps health detail inside GTT Medical and keeps Anthony's hours down, PART-2) | U |

## 4.3 Consult grace of 5 to 10 minutes

| Point | View | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Is a grace lawful? | Yes. It makes the rule fairer. | U |
| Objective wording | "If the midwife can finish" lets GTT alone decide, which is the kind of term ACL s 25(h) flags. Use a fixed number: **up to 10 minutes late, seen; the consult still ends at its normal time**. Over 10 minutes: rebooked. | V (s 25), U |
| Medicare | Item 82105 needs at least 10 minutes. A 25 minute block less 10 minutes late leaves 15. Notes must show start and end times. | V |
| Clinical exception | If the midwife decides a shortened consult would not be safe or complete, she rebooks the client free. That is a clinical reason, not a penalty. | U |
| Same-morning consult | The consult must still end before her draw; the test slot is never moved. | R |
