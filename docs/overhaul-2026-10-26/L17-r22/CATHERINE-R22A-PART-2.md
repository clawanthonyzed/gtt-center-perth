## 2(a) Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) s 45: contravening a modern award

**Library file:** /opt/openclaw/second-brain/knowledge/legal/r22/FWA-S45-MODERN-AWARD-AND-RECORDS.md (Compilation No. 73, 7 July 2026, confirmed latest; Fair Work Regulations Compilation No. 57, in force from 1 October 2026). V.

| Section | What it says (plain English) | What it means for GTT | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| s 45 | "A person must not contravene a term of a modern award." It is a civil remedy provision. | Every Nurses Award term for GTT's midwives (breaks, overtime, rosters, rates) is law, not guidance. | V |
| ss 46, 47(1) | An award only binds a person it "applies to": it must cover them, be in operation, and not be displaced (for example by an enterprise agreement). | The Nurses Award covers midwives employed by GTT Medical (cl 4.1(b), Sch A, read R21, L). No enterprise agreement exists, so it applies. | V, L |
| s 539(2) item 2 | The midwife, a union or a Fair Work inspector can sue. Maximum 60 penalty units, or 600 for a serious contravention. | Even one unpaid break, repeated each shift, is a series of contraventions. | V |
| s 546(2) | A company's maximum is 5 times the listed penalty units. | GTT Medical's exposure is 300 penalty units per ordinary contravention. Dollar value of a penalty unit NOT checked. | V, U |
| s 550 | Anyone "knowingly concerned in" a contravention is treated as having contravened. | The venue manager who signs timesheets, and the directors who set the rule, can be personally liable. | V |
| s 535(1) | Keep employee records for 7 years. | Keep timesheets and payroll for 7 years. | V |
| Regs 3.33, 3.34 | Record hours for casual and irregular part-time staff; where overtime rates apply, record overtime hours each day or the start and finish. | Each worked-through or delayed break must be written with times. | V |
| s 557C | If the required record is missing, GTT carries the burden of disproving the employee's allegation. | No break record = GTT must prove she took her break. | V |
| s 327A(1) | An employer commits an offence if its conduct results in a failure to pay a required amount in full when due. | Deliberate underpayment is a crime. Fault elements not read here (U). | V |

**Bottom line:** the meal-break rule in PART-4 depends on one record per shift. Without it, GTT loses any dispute by default.

## 2(b) OAIC: cloud computing, overseas disclosure (APP 8) and security (APP 11)

**Library file:** /opt/openclaw/second-brain/knowledge/legal/r22/OAIC-APP8-APP11-CLOUD-GUIDANCE.md (APP Guidelines ch 8 and 11, updated 3 October 2025; OAIC "Sending personal information overseas"; OAIC "Guide to securing personal information"). V.

| Point | Source | What it means for GTT | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| GTT is bound regardless of turnover | Privacy Act s 6D(4)(b): a business that "provides a health service ... and holds any health information" is not a small business operator | Both GTT Medical and GTT Lounge (booking data shows pregnancy) follow the APPs | V |
| Overseas cloud is allowed | OAIC: "Generally, no. The Privacy Act does not prevent ... a cloud service provider to store or process personal information overseas." | GTT may pick an overseas-hosted tool, but with extra duties below | V |
| It is usually a "disclosure" | APP Guidelines 8.12: providing personal information to an overseas contractor is in most cases a disclosure | APP 8.1 applies: reasonable steps so the provider does not breach the APPs; s 16C can make GTT liable for the provider's acts | V |
| Narrow "use" exception | 8.14: storage-only, binding contract limiting the provider to storage and access, same terms for subcontractors, GTT keeps effective control | Even then GTT still "holds" the data: APP 11, 12 and 13 still apply | V |
| Security steps scale with sensitivity | 11.8, 11.9: health information is sensitive; areas include governance and training, ICT and access security, third-party and cloud providers, breaches, physical security, destruction | Two-step sign-in, role-based access, audit log, encrypted backups, breach plan (R21 PART-4 list stands) | V |
| Cloud contract | Securing guide: contractual clauses, verify the provider's security claims, regular reporting, cooperation on data breaches | Ask any provider for: data location, security certification, breach notice to GTT within a set time, deletion on exit | V |
| Privacy policy | APP 1.4(f), (g): say whether GTT is likely to disclose overseas and, if practicable, the countries | List every tool that stores data overseas (email, SMS, payments, forms) and its country | V |

**Recommendation (unchanged, now backed by OAIC text):** an Australian-hosted clinical and booking system, written into the contract and the privacy policy, plus APP 11 clauses. For unavoidable overseas tools: an APP 8 contract and a named country in the policy. U

**Draft privacy policy line:**
> We store your health and booking records with providers that keep them in Australia. Some everyday tools we use (such as email and text messages) may store limited contact details in [country, filled from the provider contract]. We take reasonable steps so those providers protect your information as the Australian Privacy Principles require.

(The square-bracket item is filled from the signed provider contract, not unfinished text.)
