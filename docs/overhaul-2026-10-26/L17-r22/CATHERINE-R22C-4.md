## 1. Does the Bill exist?
YES, but not under that name (V). There is no Bill called the "Chalmers 2026 Discretionary Trust Minimum Tax Bill". The real Bills, introduced by Treasurer Jim Chalmers, are:
1. Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 3) Bill 2026 (APH ID r7554), and
2. Income Tax Rates Amendment (Minimum Tax on Discretionary Trusts) Bill 2026 (APH ID r7555).
One joint Explanatory Memorandum (EM, 207 pages, document JC019191) covers both.

Status checked 2026-10-26 on the APH Bill pages (V): "Before Reps". Introduced and second reading moved 14 October 2026. Referred to the Senate Economics Legislation Committee on 15 October 2026; report due 13 November 2026. No amendments circulated. Not passed. Not law.

## 2. What the EM says (re-read 2026-10-26, V unless marked)
| Question | Answer | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who it applies to | Discretionary trusts. Not fixed trusts (including most widely held trusts), complying super funds or charitable trusts | EM paras 1.9, 1.11 |
| Who pays | The trustee | EM para 1.10 |
| The 30 percent floor | A minimum tax of 30 percent on the trust's net income. It is a top-up: if the trustee already pays some tax on that income, it tops up to at least 30 percent | EM paras 1.9, 1.10; rates Bill new s 12AB |
| Start date | Income years starting on or after 1 July 2028 | EM para 1.9; Bill Sch 1 Part 2 application item (V, Bill text line read 2026-10-26) |
| Beneficiaries | Individual beneficiaries get a non-refundable offset for the trustee's tax (s 101AG). Corporate beneficiaries get NO offset | EM para 1.69 |
| Franking credits inside a trust | Trustee must use them against the minimum tax; excess is refundable to the trustee | EM paras 1.77 to 1.79 |
| Excluded income | Primary production, some income of vulnerable minors, some charity amounts, non-resident withholding amounts, some testamentary trust income | EM para 1.12 |
| Companies | The tax falls only on "the trustee of a trust estate" that is a minimum tax trust (Bill s 101AA, s 101AB). Nothing in the Bill changes how a company is taxed or how it pays franked dividends | Bill text and EM Chapter 1, round 9 reading; EM paras 1.9 to 1.11 re-read today |
| Restructure relief | Roll-over relief for 3 years from 1 July 2027 to move out of a discretionary trust into, for example, a company | EM paras 2.1, 2.4 (round 9 reading, not re-read today) |

## 3. What it means for GTT Route B
Route B: GTT company pays 25 percent tax as a base rate entity, then pays franked dividends DIRECT to Anthony and his partner as individual shareholders. No trust.
1. Outside the measure. A company is not a trust estate, and the shareholders hold their shares personally, so the 30 percent trustee tax never applies to GTT's profits. V on the Bill as introduced.
2. The trap to avoid: if GTT shares were ever held by a discretionary trust (for example the YETI Holding Trust), GTT's dividends would become trust income and, from income years starting on or after 1 July 2028, the trustee would face the 30 percent floor (franking credits usable). Route B is locked, so keep the shares in personal names.
3. Not final. Bills can change in the Senate. Re-check after the committee report (due 13 November 2026) and again on passage.
