## 2(c) National Law s 133 and the Ahpra advertising guidelines

**Library file:** r22/NATIONAL-LAW-S133-AHPRA-ADVERTISING.md. WA applies the National Law (Queensland text) from 15 May 2024; version read current from 10 April 2026. Ahpra guidelines in effect from 14 December 2020. V.

| Rule | What it means for GTT | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| s 133(1): no advertising of "a regulated health service, or a business that provides a regulated health service" that (a) misleads, (b) offers a gift, discount or inducement without its terms, (c) "uses testimonials or purported testimonials", (d) creates an unreasonable expectation of benefit, (e) encourages unnecessary use. Penalty up to A$60,000 (individual), A$120,000 (company) | The midwife consult is a regulated health service (s 133(4); midwifery is a health profession, s 5). GTT's whole brand is "a business that provides" one, so the website, booking page, emails, social posts and welcome card are covered | V |
| Ahpra 3.1, 3.2: advertising is any communication that promotes and seeks to attract a person to the service; the advertiser is whoever controls or authorises the content | GTT is the advertiser for everything it publishes or tells staff or an agency to publish | V |
| Ahpra 4.3.1: a testimonial is a positive statement about clinical aspects (symptom, diagnosis or treatment, outcome, practitioner skill). Comments on "customer service or communication style" alone are not testimonials | "Lovely lounge, friendly staff" can be shared; "the midwife picked up my diabetes early" cannot | V |
| Ahpra 4.3.3: whoever controls the website or business social media is responsible, and may need to disable a reviews function; not responsible for third-party platforms it does not control, but "should take care" when engaging with reviews there | No reviews widget, carousel or "what clients say" section on GTT's website. Replies on Google are a neutral thank-you that repeats nothing clinical | V |
| Ahpra 4.2: "free" means absolutely free; a cost recovered through "Medicare" is not free; offers must state terms and conditions or link clearly to them | Never "free consult". The 10 percent post-test discount must show its terms with the offer | V |
| Ahpra 4.5: no urgency words linked to health ("don't delay", "limited time") | Waitlist emails stay factual (as R21) | V |

**Welcome card and Google review request.** Asking for a review is not itself banned; the ban is on GTT using testimonials in its advertising (Ahpra 4.3.2, V). Google Business Profile is a platform GTT does not control the review function of; whether a request that steers reviews there counts as GTT "using" them is not settled. **Lawyer to check.** Safe pattern (U):
1. Ask every client the same way, on every card. Never ask only happy clients.
2. No reward, discount, prize draw or entry for a review.
3. Ask about the visit, not the clinical care or results.
4. Never quote, screenshot, embed, pin or "share" a review that mentions the test, the midwife's care, symptoms or results.
5. Reply with a neutral thank-you only.

**Draft welcome card line:**
> Thank you for visiting GTT Center Perth. If you would like to, you can tell others about your visit on Google: [QR code]. We do not offer anything in return for reviews, and we never use reviews about health care in our advertising.

**Open point:** whether blood collection by phlebotomists is itself a regulated health service. It does not change the answer, because the GTT business provides the midwife service. U

## 2(d) Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) s 47 and s 45: one lab for 3 years

**Library file:** r22/CCA-S45-S47-EXCLUSIVE-DEALING.md (Compilation No. 167, 16 September 2026; ACCC "Exclusive dealing" page). V.

| Question | Answer | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Is exclusive dealing illegal? | Only if it has the purpose, effect or likely effect of substantially lessening competition (s 47(10); s 45(1) for other contract terms). ACCC: "It is only illegal when it substantially lessens competition", more likely where the service "can't be bought elsewhere" or the business setting the conditions "is powerful" | V |
| Which forms could apply? | Third line forcing (s 47(6)): GTT sells its package on condition the client uses one named unrelated lab. Lab-side exclusivity (s 47(2), (4)): the lab supplies or buys on condition GTT does not use other labs | V |
| Is GTT's deal a risk? | **Low.** Perth has several large pathology groups and many collection centres (WA-PATHOLOGY-DIRECTORY.md, L). GTT is one venue, about 18 morning clients. The client can use Path B or take her request to any lab | L, U |
| Between GTT's own companies? | Not exclusive dealing (s 47(12), related bodies corporate) | V |
| ACCC notification? | Available as a safe harbour; not needed at GTT's size. Fee not read | V, U |
| The bigger risk | **Pathology law, not competition law.** A GTT midwife requests the test, so any benefit from the lab to GTT linked to requests (cheap rent, free staff, cash per client, marketing money) risks HIA Part IIBA prohibited-benefit penalties (LICENSED-COLLECTION-CENTRES-AND-SLA.md, REFERRAL-TELEHEALTH-BILLING-MECHANICS.md) | L |

**Contract must-haves (U):** no benefit to GTT linked to requests; everything at market value and in writing; "You can take your request to any pathology provider" on the site; exit if the lab loses accreditation or approval, misses service levels, or changes price without notice; no automatic renewal at 3 years without review. **Lawyer to check** the final contract.

## 2(e) WA privacy law for private providers

**Library file:** r22/WA-PRIVACY-PRIVATE-PROVIDERS.md. V.

| Law | Binds GTT? | Why | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Privacy and Responsible Information Sharing Act 2024 (WA) | **No**, unless a public-sector contract switches it on | It binds "IPP entities": Ministers, Parliamentary Secretaries, public entities and "contracted service providers" (s 14(1)). A private firm is one only under a "State services contract" with a public entity (s 8), and the WA privacy principles apply only if that contract says so (ss 129, 130). Principles commenced 1 July 2026; other parts 1 January 2027 | V |
| Health Services Act 2016 (WA) | **No**, unless GTT contracts to provide health services to the State | It runs the public health system; a "contracted health entity" is a non-government body providing health services to the State under contract. s 219 confidentiality (fine A$5,000) binds people engaged "under or for the purposes of this Act" | V |
| A WA health records Act (like Victoria or NSW) | **None found** in the WA in-force lists checked | Search limited to the in-force lists for P and H. **Lawyer to check** | U |
| Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) | **Yes** | Health service providers are covered whatever their turnover (s 6D(4)(b)) | V |

**Watch point:** if the partner lab is PathWest (WA public system) and the contract has GTT collecting on PathWest's behalf, read it for a clause applying the WA privacy principles (s 129). A private lab does not trigger the WA Act. U
