## 1. Job 3: itemising the Path B receipt so it looks like the Path A one

Path A lines today: consult A$130.00 (GST-free) + lounge and service A$162.15 (taxable) = A$292.15 to GTT, and the lab fee A$27.85 billed by the lab.
Path B truth: GTT supplies lounge and service only. There is no consult, so the whole A$272.15 is taxable (GST A$24.74).

General rules to check (U): a tax invoice is needed for a taxable sale over A$82.50. It names the seller and its ABN, the date, what was supplied, and the GST (or says the total includes GST). If an invoice mixes taxable and GST-free items, it must show which are which. The company that makes the sale is the one that invoices: the Lounge company for the lounge part (the repo has Medical supply the consult; no consult on Path B, so Medical does not invoice).

### Can a different allocation of shared costs lawfully cut tax or fees?

**No. What I checked (general knowledge plus the repo, U, Catherine to confirm):**

| Area | Why allocation does not help |
|---|---|
| Company tax | Planned: the three companies elect tax consolidation and lodge one return. One taxpayer, one 25 percent bill. Moving a cost between members changes nothing |
| GST | Planned: one GST group, so charges between members create no GST. Costs tied to GST-free consults still earn input tax credits (E, accountant to confirm), so allocating a cost to the consult or the lounge does not change credits |
| Payroll tax (WA) | Related companies are grouped for the A$1m threshold, so the whole wage bill counts once. Allocation cannot move wages out of the group |
| Card fees, Medicare | Charged per transaction or per client, not per cost block. Nothing to allocate |

Nearest compliant levers (these are real, not allocation tricks): the group threshold and its tapering, the choice of card provider once quoted, and the timing of the GST group and consolidation elections. All sit with the accountant.
