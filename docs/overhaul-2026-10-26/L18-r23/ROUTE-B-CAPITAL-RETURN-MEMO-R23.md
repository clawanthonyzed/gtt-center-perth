## Reasoned

**How Route B is built.** Medical earns the consult (A$61,612.20 a month, GST-free); Lounge earns the lounge, service and afternoon sales (A$69,863.50 + A$22,350.34 ex GST) (R). With tax consolidation, Holdings lodges one return at 25 percent (E). Profit after tax, A$19,973.98 a month (R), goes to Anthony and his partner as fully franked dividends. No wage.

**Why the trust Bill should not reach it.** The charge is on a trustee. Holdings shares are held by two people, not by a trust. **Trap:** if the shares were moved into a family trust, the dividends would become trust income. Keep them in your two names.

**Why repaying your own money is not income.** A shareholder loan returns what you lent. It works only if: the loan is written and signed before the money moves; each lender's money really comes from that lender (if your partner's savings are in it, she is a lender for her share); the company keeps a loan account and never repays more than the balance; no interest unless the accountant sets a defensible rate; and the money is not left as "loan or shares, decide later".

**Which company holds the loan.** NOT IN REPO. Usually the owners lend to the company that spends the money, or to Holdings, which passes it down. The accountant decides; the timeline is the same either way.

**Dividends and franking.** A dividend needs profits and a company able to pay its debts (U). The franking account fills from tax actually paid. Year 1 tax of about A$79,895.91 is paid around month 23 (U), so dividends from month 14 may be franked only as far as tax has been paid by then (E): ask the accountant about the timing.
