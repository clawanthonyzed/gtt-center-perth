The 13 advance clients needed per test morning are exactly the 13 advance blocks per day. Supply equals demand with no spare block, so any cancelled and re-booked consult needs another day.

### 3.5 Fasting and clinical comfort

A fasted expecting mother sitting through a 20 minute consult is a comfort issue. This is not medical advice; the lead midwife and Catherine confirm the clinical wording.

What the booking page, the reminders and the midwife routine must cover:

| Where | What it must say or do |
|---|---|
| Booking page, when a same-morning consult is offered | "If you choose a consult on the morning of your test, stay fasted. Water only, as your instruction sheet says. Your consult comes first, then your first blood sample." |
| Booking page | Show her the real timeline: arrive 10 minutes before the consult, consult 20 minutes, a short wait, first blood sample. The time on site before the first sample is about 50 minutes. |
| Booking page | "If you would rather not wait fasted, choose a consult on an earlier day instead. It costs the same." |
| Reminder messages | Fasting and water-only wording on the day before and the morning of. Wording in GRACE-R20-CHECKOUT-FLOW.md section 7. |
| Consult room | Water available, a seat with back support, and the midwife asks at the start how she is feeling. |
| Midwife routine | If the client feels unwell, the midwife decides whether to carry on, move her to the lounge to rest, or postpone the test. The client is not pushed through. The decision is recorded. (Clinical wording: lead midwife and Catherine.) |
| Overrun | The block has a 15 minute wait after it. If a consult runs over by more than the wait, the draw is not held; the client takes the next free pair or rebooks under the terms. |

Longest fasted day: the 10:20 pair. The partner lab's maximum fasting window is NOT IN REPO (question 5). A same-morning consult does not lengthen the fast (she is fasted until her draw either way), but it does mean she sits in the building for longer first.
