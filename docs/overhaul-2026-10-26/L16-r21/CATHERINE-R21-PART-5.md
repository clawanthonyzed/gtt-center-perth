## Job 6. What a health lawyer might charge

**General knowledge, not a quote, not checked against any firm's price list (tag U, E). Not for the financials (Anthony's decision 10).**

| Item | Rough range | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly rates, Australian health and healthcare-practice lawyers | about A$350 to A$600 for a senior associate; about A$600 to A$900 or more for a partner | U, E |
| Fixed-fee review of booking terms, privacy notice and policy, checkout page and consent forms | about A$2,500 to A$8,000 | U, E |
| Adding the Medicare and Part IIBA lab structure, two-company setup and the s 23DNL notice | could push it toward the top of the range or above | U, E |
| Small boutique health firms and online fixed-fee services | often at the lower end for a review only | U, E |

**What drives the price:** how many documents and pages; review only or redrafting; how many Medicare and pathology-law questions; two companies instead of one; urgency; the lawyer's seniority; whether a written advice letter is wanted.

**How to keep it down:** send the final drafts (these parts plus Grace's checkout and booking pages) in one bundle, with a numbered list of questions, and ask for a fixed fee in writing before work starts.

## QUESTIONS FOR ANTHONY

1. **Option A pricing (job 2).** At A$265 / A$315 all-in, GTT receives A$237.15 / A$287.15 (all GST-taxable), not the same as option B.
   - (a) Keep option A at A$265 / A$315, with the s 23DNL notice; Theodore models the lower take. **(Recommended if you want option A at all.)**
   - (b) Price option A so GTT receives A$292.15 (all-in A$320, no rebate), with the notice.
   - (c) Drop option A: outside-request clients either book a GTT consult or use the lab's own collection centre (R20 Question 1).

2. **Change window (job 3).**
   - (a) 48 hours, with reminders and the waitlist. **(Recommended: easier to defend in law.)**
   - (b) 1 week.
   - (c) 72 hours.

3. **Illness proof (job 3).**
   - (a) Short written statement first time; certificate with no diagnosis for a second claim in the same pregnancy. **(Recommended.)**
   - (b) Medical certificate every time.
   - (c) No proof; her word is enough.

4. **Late rule exceptions (job 3).**
   - (a) Clock rule strictly, plus the R20 exceptions (illness, complication, hospital, serious emergency). **(Recommended.)**
   - (b) Clock rule with no exceptions at all (higher unfair-term risk).
   - (c) A small grace period (for example 5 minutes past the appointment time).

5. **Midwife booking blocks and breaks (job 5).**
   - (a) 20-minute consult blocks, with the meal break blocked 12:00 to 14:00 and two tea breaks blocked. **(Recommended: the only option that fits 18 consults in one lawful shift.)**
   - (b) 25-minute blocks, accepting fewer than 18 consults per midwife shift (about 16 fit).
   - (c) 30-minute appointments (20 consult, 10 paperwork), which needs extra midwife hours on busy days.

6. **Is the midwife free from duty during her meal break?**
   - (a) Yes: she may leave and other staff cover the floor; break unpaid, 7.5 paid hours. **(Recommended.)**
   - (b) No: she must stay reachable; break paid, 8.0 paid hours (Theodore R20 sensitivity).

7. **Record storage (job 4).**
   - (a) Australian-hosted clinical system with audit log and two-step sign-in. **(Recommended.)**
   - (b) Any reputable provider, with an APP 8 contract if overseas.

8. **Health lawyer review (job 6).**
   - (a) One fixed-fee review of all documents before launch. **(Recommended.)**
   - (b) Only the booking terms and privacy notice.
   - (c) No review.
