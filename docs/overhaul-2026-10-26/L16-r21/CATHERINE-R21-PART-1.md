## Job 1. Charging A$130 for a 20-minute item 82105 consult

### 1.1 The item itself (V, MBS Online item 82105, schedule fee updated 1 July 2026)

| Fact | Text |
|---|---|
| Descriptor | "Short antenatal professional attendance by a participating midwife, lasting at least 10 minutes" |
| Schedule fee | A$38.75. Benefit 85% = **A$32.95** (matches the locked figure) |
| Extended Medicare Safety Net cap | A$20.40 |
| MN.13.16 | "Medicare Benefits are only payable for clinically relevant services", meaning a service "generally accepted by the midwifery profession as necessary to the appropriate treatment of the patient's clinical condition" |
| MN.13.16 | Midwife services "will be subject to Medicare Audit and Professional Review Processes" |

A 20-minute consult is inside the descriptor ("at least 10 minutes"). The schedule fee is only the figure Medicare uses to work out the rebate. It is not a price limit.

### 1.2 The rules that matter

| Question | Answer | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Is there any "cannot charge more than" rule for a private fee? | **No.** Nothing in the HIA or the 2018 Regulations I have read caps what a provider charges a private client. The rebate is a share of the schedule fee, not of GTT's fee (HIA s 10, L: R20). A higher fee does not raise or lower the rebate. | L, U |
| Is the gap (A$130 less A$32.95 = A$97.05) lawful? | **Yes.** The client pays the gap herself. That is normal private billing. | U (well settled) |
| Safety net | The gap counts towards her Extended Medicare Safety Net only up to the A$20.40 cap for this item. So do not tell her the gap is "covered later". | V |
| Who gets the rebate | The person who incurs the expense (HIA s 20(1)). If she has not paid, Medicare pays by a cheque made out to the provider (s 20(2)). So: take full payment, then she claims. | L |
| Receipt or account (HIA s 19(6)) | "A medicare benefit is not payable in respect of a professional service unless" the provider has recorded the prescribed particulars "on the account, or on the receipt". | V |
| What must be on it (Regulations 2018 ss 49 to 51) | s 49: patient's name; date the service was rendered; amount charged for the service; total paid; any amount outstanding. s 50: a description sufficient to identify the item (write "MBS item 82105"). s 51 (general medical services items, which include Group M13): the midwife's name and practice address, or her provider number for that location, and "a statement that the professional service was provided by that person". | V |
| Informed financial consent | Not a statute for out-of-hospital care. It is an expectation: the MBS midwife notes say "Providers are expected to obtain informed financial consent from patients prior to providing the service including providing details regarding their fees and any out-of-pocket costs" (written for the telehealth items, V). The Department's out-of-pocket page points to the Ombudsman's guidance (S). The ACL backs it up: a fee not disclosed before the service invites a misleading-conduct claim. | V, S, U |
| Professional Services Review (PSR) | PSR reviews whether a practitioner's "conduct in connection with rendering or initiating services" would be "unacceptable to the general body of the members of that profession" (HIA s 82(1)(d)). Its object is to protect patients and the Commonwealth from "inappropriate practice" (s 79A). **Fee level is not the test.** The triggers are clinical: services not clinically relevant, consults too short for the item, unusual volumes, poor notes. | V |
| Will Medicare flag a high fee? | **No rule makes a high private fee a compliance flag.** Medicare compliance looks at item use, timing, clinical relevance and records. | V (s 82, MN.13.16), U on practice |
| ACL and price | No law requires GTT to match Perth comparators. A higher price is lawful. The risk is only what GTT says: ACL s 18 (misleading conduct) and s 29(1)(i) (false price representation) (V in R20). | V |

### 1.3 The real risk points (where trouble would come from)

| Risk | Why | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Consult used as a gateway, not care | If the consult exists only to unlock the package, it may not be "clinically relevant" (MN.13.16). That is the PSR and audit risk, not the price. | V text, U application |
| Consult notes thin or under 10 minutes | Claims for an item whose time or content is not met. | V |
| Receipt issued before the consult | s 49 needs the date the service "was rendered". A receipt at booking (before the consult) cannot carry that. | V, U |
| Calling the fee a "Medicare fee" | Implies Medicare set or approved A$130. Misleading (ACL s 29(1)(i), (m)). | V (R20), U |
| "Free consult after Medicare" | Option A and option B net to the same A$265 after rebates. Saying the consult is free misstates the price and the rebate. | U |
