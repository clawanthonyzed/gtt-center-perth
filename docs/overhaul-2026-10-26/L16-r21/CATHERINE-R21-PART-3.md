# CATHERINE R21 PART 3: changes, waitlist, illness proof and the late rule (job 3)

DRAFT, 2026-10-26. Tags as in PART-1. Builds on R20 section 3 (ACL ss 23 to 27, 29, 60 to 64, V in R20).

## 3.1 Change window: 1 week or 48 hours

| Point | View | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Test | A term that limits her changes is part of a standard form contract (ACL s 27), so it must not cause a significant imbalance, must be reasonably necessary for GTT's legitimate interests, and must be transparent (s 24). | V (R20) |
| 1 week | Lawful but harder to defend. A pregnancy changes fast; a 7-day lock catches clients for things they could not foresee. | U |
| 48 hours | Easier to defend, and the waitlist helps: if GTT can refill a slot from the waitlist, its real loss from a 3-day-out change is small, so a long lock is less "reasonably necessary". | U |
| Recommendation | 48 hours, with reminders before it closes (as R20). Anthony decides. | U |

## 3.2 Waitlist with automatic "slot open" emails

| Rule | What it means here | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Spam Act s 6(1) | A message whose purpose is "to offer to supply goods or services" is a commercial electronic message. A slot-open email is an offer. Email and SMS are both covered. | V |
| s 16(1), (2) | Do not send one without consent. Consent is "express consent" or consent reasonably inferred from conduct and relationship (Sch 2 cl 2). Use express consent: a tick box she chooses herself. GTT bears the burden of proving consent (s 16(5)). | V |
| s 17 | Each message must clearly identify who authorised it and how to contact them, valid for 30 days. | V |
| s 18 | Each message must contain a clear, conspicuous unsubscribe statement and a working address for at least 30 days. | V |
| Booking reminders | A reminder that only states facts about her own booking may be a "designated" message (Sch 1 cl 2: "no more than factual information"), exempt from ss 16 and 18 but still needing s 17 sender details. Keep reminders free of promotions or they become commercial. | V, U |
| Privacy: direct marketing | Waitlist status shows she is pregnant and needs a glucose test, which is health (sensitive) information. Using sensitive information for direct marketing needs her consent (APP 7.4). The same tick box covers it. | V |
| Can the email say "pregnancy test"? | The Spam Act does not stop it. Privacy does not ban it once she consents. But emails and SMS previews are seen by others (shared inboxes, lock screens), so APP 11.1 "reasonable steps" favours neutral wording. **Recommendation: no "pregnancy", "diabetes" or "glucose" in the subject line or SMS preview.** | V (APP 11.1), U |

**Draft consent at booking (unticked by default):**
> ☐ Add me to the cancellation list. If an earlier or better slot opens, GTT Center Perth may email or text me about it. I can leave the list at any time with the unsubscribe link or by replying STOP.

**Draft slot-open email:**
> Subject: A slot has opened at GTT Center Perth
> Hello [first name], a morning has opened on [date] at [time]. It is first come, first served. Book here: [link]. If it is not right for you, no action is needed.
> Sent by GTT Lounge Sanctuary Pty Ltd, [address], [phone], [email]. To leave the cancellation list, click Unsubscribe or reply STOP.

## 3.3 Proof of illness

| Point | View | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Can GTT ask for proof? | Yes, as a condition of the free-rebook exception, if the term says so in advance. | U |
| How much health detail? | Only what is "reasonably necessary" (APP 3.2), and health information needs her consent (APP 3.3). GTT needs to know only that an exception applies, not the diagnosis. | V |
| Certificate or statement | A medical certificate costs a pregnant client a GP visit for a missed fasting test. **Recommend:** a short written statement first time; a certificate "unfit to attend on [date]" (no diagnosis) only for a second claim in the same pregnancy. | U |
| Who receives it | Send it to GTT Medical, not the Lounge. Sharing sensitive information between related companies is not covered by s 13B (V in R20). Medical tells the Lounge only "exception approved". | V, U |
| How long kept | Record "exception approved, evidence sighted, [date]". Delete the statement or certificate once decided (APP 11.2). | V |

**Draft wording (booking terms and illness form):**
> If you are unwell, have a pregnancy complication, or are in hospital or labour, tell us before your appointment if you can. We will rebook you free. We may ask for a short written statement, or for a second claim in the same pregnancy a medical certificate saying you were unfit to attend. You do not need to tell us your diagnosis. Our midwife team sees this, not lounge staff, and we delete it once we have decided.

## 3.4 The late rule (Anthony's decision 7)

| Point | View | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Objective test | "Late" from the appointment time is clock-based and does not let GTT alone decide breach, which avoids ACL s 25(h). Lawful and enforceable. | V (R20) |
| Why it is reasonably necessary | The glucose test runs on a fixed timed sequence and a 25-minute pair cadence; a late start cannot be fitted without moving other clients. Write that reason in the terms (transparency, s 24(3)). | V, U |
| Consequence | A late arrival is treated as a missed test under the R20 missed-test term. | U |
| Mirror duty | If GTT runs late, the client is never charged or moved without her choice (s 25(c)). | V (R20) |
| Emergencies | "No wiggle room" on the clock is fine. Keep the R20 exceptions (illness, complication, hospital, serious emergency) so the term is not wider than necessary. **Lawyer to check.** | U |
| Where it must appear | Transparency needs it before she pays, not only in a linked file (s 24(3), R20). | V |
