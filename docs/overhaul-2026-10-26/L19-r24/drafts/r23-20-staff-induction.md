## Deliverable
**1. What GTT is.** A calm lounge for pregnant clients having a glucose tolerance test. It feels like a day spa, not a clinic.

**2. How a test morning runs.** The venue manager opens at 06:30. First draw is 07:00. Two clients are drawn every 25 minutes. We see 18 clients a morning, Monday to Saturday. The midwife is on site 08:00 to 16:00 and the first consult is 08:15.

**3. Who does what.** The venue manager does reception and welcome. Phlebotomists (employed by GTT Medical) do the blood draws. Service staff give massage, beauty, nails and hair services between draws. Midwives do consults and phone abnormal results.

**4. The late rule.** Please arrive at least 10 minutes before your appointment, because late means from your appointment time onwards and we cannot fit a first blood draw in after that. Draws have no wiggle room. Consults have a 10 minute grace window.

**5. Privacy.** Use first names only in the lounge. Never put health details in a text or email. The day list stays in the locked drawer and is shredded at the end of the day.

**6. What you never do.** Never work through your meal break. Never tell a client her result. Never give medical advice. Never share client details with the lab or anyone outside GTT.

**7. If something goes wrong.** A client is unwell or upset: stay with her and get the midwife. You are running late: tell the venue manager now. A mistake with a card or list: tell the venue manager before the client leaves.

**8. Your first day.** Arrive 15 minutes before your start, meet the venue manager, read this sheet, shadow a colleague for the first pair of clients, and ask questions.

**Meal breaks.** Your break is rostered. You are never asked to work through it. You can stay fully off duty at GTT or leave. If you are called back (midwives never are), log MB-R on your timesheet. Otherwise log MB. Service staff also get one paid 10 minute rest break.
