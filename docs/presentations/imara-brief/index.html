01 · The OpportunityGTT Center Perth
Why this exists

A test every pregnant
woman has to take.

Every pregnant woman in Australia is required to take a gestational glucose tolerance test, usually between 24 and 28 weeks. It isn't optional, and it isn't a small appointment: a fasting blood draw, a glucose drink, then two more blood draws over the following two hours.

For almost everyone, that two-hour wait happens in an ordinary pathology clinic — fluorescent lighting, plastic chairs, no phone signal to speak of, nothing designed for the person sitting through it. Anthony has been through it. It's the reason this venture exists.

Because the test is mandatory and recurring, this isn't a business that has to invent demand. Perth's metro area sees an estimated 515 births a week, which means a steady, predictable stream of women who must attend, for medical reasons, regardless of the economy or how much is spent on marketing.

The old way

Two hours in a waiting room, designed for nothing but waiting.

The GTT Center Perth way

Two hours of massage, beauty and care, with the blood draws built quietly into the schedule.

02 · The IdeaGTT Center Perth
What it actually is

One venue, two businesses,
running from the same rooms.

Mornings belong to the GTT test. Afternoons belong to everyone else.

Mornings — the GTT experience

A pregnant client arrives for her test, is greeted by name, and spends her two required waiting hours receiving massage and beauty treatments from GTT Center Perth's own employed staff, in between the three blood draws the test requires.

Afternoons — the wellness salon

From early afternoon, the same rooms, staff and brand become a stand-alone premium massage, beauty, nail and hair salon open to anyone — no test, no referral, just a booking.

What GTT Center Perth is not

GTT Center Perth does not draw blood without a qualified phlebotomist, does not process or interpret the test, and does not employ a pathologist. The clinical sample is collected by employed, qualified staff and handed to an external, independently accredited pathology partner, who processes it and reports results to the referring doctor — exactly as happens today, just without the plastic chairs. GTT Center Perth is a wellness venue with a clinical collection service inside it, not a pathology company.

04 · How It WorksGTT Center Perth
The commercial logic

The same premium room, earning
twice in one day.

A specialised clinical service in the morning, when demand for it exists; a broader wellness business in the afternoon, when it doesn't. One fit-out, one lease, one brand — two revenue periods.

No handover meeting

Different people, different shifts — the switch from morning to afternoon is a rostering fact, not a ceremony. A morning staff member may work into the afternoon on a given day if bookings justify it, but that's a day-to-day choice, not the structural model.

No spare capacity in the morning

At the committed 18-client volume, every morning treatment slot already belongs to a GTT client's schedule. The morning does not also sell walk-in wellness appointments — that revenue lives entirely in the afternoon.

Inside one client's morning

What actually happens during
the two-hour test.

The clinical process itself — three blood draws exactly one hour apart — can't be shortened; it's what the test requires. What changes is everything around it.

Capacity, verified

This has been checked against a hard-constraint solver across all 153 pairwise combinations, with zero chair or phlebotomist clashes — and it's the same timetable that has been shared with WDP for their own review.

Two honest limits
  • The last first-draw of the day is 10:20am, inside WDP's own guidance of not normally starting a test after 10:30am.
  • 18 clients/day is the physical ceiling of this schedule at the current staffing level, not a promise every day will be full.
