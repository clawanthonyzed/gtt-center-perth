## 1. ABS Births Data — Australia, Western Australia, Perth

**Source:** Australian Bureau of Statistics, *Births, Australia*, 2024 release, published 15 October 2025 (`abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/births-australia/latest-release`).

- **Latest available year: 2024.** No 2025 or 2026 birth data has been published by the ABS as of this research (2026-08-21). The next ABS release, covering 2025 data, is scheduled for 6 November 2026. Any figure for 2025/2026 births would be a projection, not a published fact — none is used here.
- **Australia, 2024:** 292,318 registered births, up 1.9% (5,320 births) on 2023.
- **Western Australia, 2024:** 30,516 registered births. Total fertility rate 1.434 babies per woman. Median age of mothers 31.9 years.
- **Perth (2024, capital-city breakdown):** approximately 27,000 births, up 5.7% on 2023 — the highest rate of recovery of any Australian capital city that year. Regional WA (outside Perth) recorded 6,780 births, up 8.0% on 2023.
- **Context on the 2024 WA figure:** WA's registered-birth count for 2024 was affected by a registration-backlog correction (a backlog of 2023 births was cleared in early 2025, meaning some 2023-registered births actually occurred earlier) — the ABS itself flags this administrative effect. The underlying regional trend (Perth +5.7%, regional WA +8.0%) is the more reliable growth signal than the raw year-on-year national comparison.
- **No sub-Perth (suburb-level) birth counts are published by the ABS at this level of the release** — only state and capital-city/regional splits are available without commissioning a custom ABS data extract.

## 2. Gestational Diabetes (GDM) Prevalence and Prior-GDM Market

**Sources:** Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), *Diabetes: Australian facts — Gestational diabetes* (most recent update reporting 2023-24 hospital data); peer-reviewed recurrence-rate literature (*Diabetes Care*, American Diabetes Association; multiple cohort studies).

- **Current national GDM diagnosis rate: 18% (almost 1 in 5) of women aged 15-49 who gave birth in an Australian hospital in 2023-24 were diagnosed with gestational diabetes** (AIHW). This is the most recent published figure.
- **Trend:** registered GDM cases increased by almost 70% nationally between 2016 and 2021 (AIHW, cohort of 1.8 million+ Australian pregnancies). Growth has been particularly steep in Victoria (incidence doubled) and the Northern Territory (nearly tripled) over the same period; WA-specific trend data was not found in this pass and would need a dedicated AIHW/WA Health data request if a WA-specific figure is required.
- **Risk-group variation:** Southeast Asian-born women in Australia saw GDM incidence roughly double (12.2% to 22.5%) over the six-year study period; incidence is also higher among socioeconomically disadvantaged groups. This is relevant to demand forecasting only insofar as Perth's demographic mix affects local incidence — no Perth-specific incidence breakdown was found.
- **Prior-GDM recurrence in subsequent pregnancies:** published recurrence rates vary by study and population, commonly cited in the **30-41% range** (individual studies report 35%, 35.6%, and 41.3%; a systematic review found recurrence rates as wide as 30-84% depending on population). Recurrence is lower in non-Hispanic white populations (30-37%) and higher in some minority populations (52-69%) — ethnicity-specific WA figures were not found. Strongest independent predictors of recurrence: pre-pregnancy BMI ≥25, weight gain between pregnancies, higher maternal age/parity, and family history of diabetes.
- **What this means for GTT Center Perth's addressable market, stated plainly:** at an 18% current GDM diagnosis rate and a ~30-41% recurrence rate in subsequent pregnancies, a meaningful share of the ~27,000 annual Perth births represents women who will require a GTT at least once in a given pregnancy (essentially all pregnant women, per universal 24-28 week screening — see §3 below), and a smaller but real subset (women with a prior GDM history who become pregnant again) carries a materially elevated chance of a repeat positive test and repeat monitoring engagement. No repository document should state a specific "number of repeat customers" projection from this data — the recurrence-rate literature supports a directional finding (prior-GDM clients are a real, elevated-probability repeat-engagement segment), not a specific customer-count forecast, which would require GTT Center Perth's own booking data over time to establish.

## 3. GTT Referral Process — How It Actually Works

**Sources:** PathWest (WA's public pathology provider) patient information pages; Clinpath Pathology, NSW Health Pathology, and ACT Health patient GTT information sheets (used for the standard, consistent, cross-jurisdiction description of how a GTT referral works, since these elements do not differ materially state to state); general Australian primary-care referral practice.

- **Who can refer:** a valid pathology request form from a GP, obstetrician/specialist, or a midwife with a Medicare provider number. This is the standard requirement described consistently across every Australian pathology provider's patient information.
- **What the client needs to bring:** a valid Medicare card and the completed pathology request form from their referring practitioner.
- **When the test is done:** the standard oral GTT (OGTT) for gestational diabetes screening is performed at 24-28 weeks' gestation for women without earlier-identified risk factors; women with risk factors may be tested earlier in pregnancy (this is standard RANZCOG/ADIPS screening guidance, consistent across the jurisdiction-specific patient sheets reviewed).
- **Which pathology providers can process which request forms:** the general Australian pathology industry norm (confirmed via PathWest's own patient information) is that a pathology provider will generally accept a valid request form regardless of which specific provider's letterhead it is printed on — the request form identifies the ordering practitioner and the required test, not a specific collection-centre brand. This means, in principle, a client referred by their GP or midwife does not need a WDP-specific request form to be collected at a WDP-operated site.

This was not found in any WDP correspondence reviewed in this repository and should be confirmed directly with Carole Rivers as part of the operational handover, not assumed either way. **This is the one item from this research question that stays a genuine dependency, per instruction — everything else above is now answered, not "needs research."**
