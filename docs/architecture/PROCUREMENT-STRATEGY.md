## Methodology

Per category: Australian-only, China/Asia import, or hybrid approach; expected savings potential (where a repo-sourced figure exists — no new price research invented beyond what `docs/hire-purchase-china.md` and this phase's own curtain-compliance research already established); compliance risk; shipping/logistics against the opening timeline (per `docs/equipment-costs.md`'s own Month 2–3 China-sourcing window); and a clear recommended approach.

---

## 1. Furniture (lounge chairs, side tables, reception seating)

- **Recommended approach: Hybrid.** Lounge seating (the highest client-contact-time item in the venue) sourced Australian — no repo-based China cost comparison exists for lounge chairs specifically (`docs/hire-purchase-china.md` §1G lists Australian/commercial-fitout sources only), and this is exactly the furniture category where all-day comfort/durability for a 2+ hour pregnant-client wait matters most. Smaller ancillary furniture (side tables, non-critical seating) is a reasonable China-import candidate — low compliance complexity, no client-contact-duration sensitivity.
- **Savings potential:** unquantified for lounge seating specifically (no existing repo price comparison); plausible but modest for ancillary items.
- **Compliance risk:** low for non-electrical furniture generally; AS/NZS 4088 (public-seating stability/upholstery flammability) applies regardless of source — a standard, verifiable requirement.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits comfortably inside the existing Month 2–3 China-sourcing window if pursued for ancillary items; lounge seating (Australian) follows the standard local-supplier lead time, not a constraint.

## 2. Cabinetry (storage units, non-custom)

- **Recommended approach: Hybrid.** Standard flat-pack commercial storage (already the MVP scenario's own preferred lower-cost approach over custom joinery, per `docs/architecture/STARTUP-COST-OPTIMISATION.md` §3.1) is a mature, low-complexity import category. **The reception counter build itself is not an import candidate at all** — it is a site-installed local trades job (cabinetmaker/shopfitter), not a shippable product, and should not be considered for import sourcing.
- **Savings potential:** plausible for flat-pack storage units, unquantified — no existing repo price research for this specific sub-category.
- **Compliance risk:** low — non-electrical, no TGA/electrical-safety trigger.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the Month 2–3 window if flat-pack storage is imported; the reception counter build proceeds on the local fit-out trade schedule regardless.

## 3. Treatment Beds (massage tables, facial/beauty beds)

- **Recommended approach: Hybrid, with a mandatory local reference unit.** `docs/hire-purchase-china.md` §1E already confirms a real, quantified saving: China-sourced pregnancy massage tables at A$300-800 vs. A$800-2,500 locally — the single largest already-documented percentage saving in this entire review. **Condition, already disclosed in the source, reaffirmed here:** buy at least one Australian-brand reference/backup unit, given the source's own flagged risk ("shipping damage; weight and size make returns difficult").
- **Savings potential:** up to ~A$500-1,700/unit — the clearest, most confidently quantifiable saving in this whole procurement strategy.
- **Compliance risk:** low — non-electrical, no TGA trigger; general product-safety standards apply regardless of source.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the Month 2–3 window; the disclosed shipping-damage risk means ordering with enough buffer before the fit-out completion milestone (Week 15, per `docs/grace-startup-plan.md`) to allow for a damaged-unit replacement cycle if needed — not currently built into the existing purchase timeline, flagged as a scheduling refinement worth making if this recommendation is adopted.

## 4. Pedicure Chairs

- **Recommended approach: China/Asia import, with the same local-reference-unit discipline.** The clearest, most confidently quantified saving of any category: `docs/hire-purchase-china.md` §1D confirms A$500-1,500 (China, pipeless/electric-massage) vs. A$800-2,000 (basic) to A$1,200-3,500 (with massage) locally.
- **Savings potential:** up to ~A$1,500-2,000/unit across up to 4 units (2 day-one per the approved MVP staffing timing, 2 staged by Month 4 per the PM ramp trigger) — genuinely the largest-magnitude opportunity identified in this whole review.
- **Compliance risk:** moderate, manageable — requires CE/SAA electrical certification (massage/electric function) and confirmed pipeless design (hygiene) per the source's own explicit checklist. A standard, checkable requirement, not a genuine barrier with an established supplier.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the existing 4-8 week China-sourcing window comfortably; stations 3-4 (Month 4 purchase) have even more lead-time buffer than the day-one units.

## 5. Salon Equipment (nail tables, UV/LED lamps, hair dryers/straighteners, backwash hardware)

- **Recommended approach: Hybrid, case-by-case.** Nail tables and UV lamps are only listed against "AU beauty supply" in the existing repo research, without a direct China cost comparison — a genuine gap, not previously quantified. Hair dryers/straighteners (branded professional equipment — Dyson, GHD-equivalent) are poor import candidates on brand/warranty grounds specifically, not a blanket sourcing-country judgement. Backwash basin+chair hardware (`docs/hire-purchase-china.md` §1F) is listed as "AU beauty supply or China" without a quantified comparison either.
- **Savings potential:** unquantified across this category — flagged as the category most in need of a direct quote comparison before any procurement decision, not assumed favourable or unfavourable to import.
- **Compliance risk:** SAA electrical certification required for all powered items, regardless of source — standard, not a genuine barrier.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the existing window if pursued; no timeline-specific concern identified.

## 6. Lighting (general commercial, non-clinical)

- **Recommended approach: Hybrid, with a firm exception for clinical lighting.** General ambiance/task lighting throughout open-plan and Lounge zones is a mature, low-risk China/Asia import category — many Australian retailers themselves resell China-manufactured fixtures. **Clinical-grade lighting in the Blood Collection Room (Ra>90 colour rendering) should be sourced from a verifiable-spec supplier, not a budget import claiming this rating without a checkable test report** — carried forward unchanged from `docs/architecture/CHINA-ASIA-SUPPLIER-FEASIBILITY.md`'s original finding, reaffirmed here given the Collection Room's own clinical-credibility stakes.
- **Savings potential:** plausible for general fixtures, unquantified — no existing repo price research.
- **Compliance risk:** SAA approval mark required for any mains-connected fitting, standard and verifiable.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the existing window for general fixtures.

## 7. Privacy Systems (curtains/curtain track)

- **Recommended approach: Australian-sourced — confirmed, not just recommended, by `docs/architecture/CURTAIN-COMPLIANCE-CLOSURE.md`.** This is the one category where this phase's own research materially strengthened the prior recommendation rather than merely restating it: genuine, purpose-built Australian suppliers (Imported Theatre Fabrics, Freedom's Halcyon range, Blinds Plus, Lifestyle Curtains, eSafety Supplies, Creative Systems) already sell curtain products combining both AS 1530.2/1530.3 fire-retardancy and acoustic performance in one product — removing any genuine need to pursue an import solution for this specific, safety-and-privacy-critical line item.
- **Savings potential:** not pursued as an import-savings opportunity, by design — the verification burden for an overseas fire-rating claim is disproportionate to the modest total cost of this line (~A$1,350-4,874 across the reconstruction/optimisation phases).
- **Compliance risk:** the base fire-rating question is now closed (item 51) — remaining risk is limited to final building-classification confirmation (Class 5 or 6, likely, per the closure document) once a venue exists.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** Australian commercial-curtain suppliers operate on standard local lead times, well within the existing fit-out schedule — no China-sourcing-window dependency at all for this category.

## 8. Décor (non-electrical accessories, throw blankets, small display items)

- **Recommended approach: China/Asia import.** No compliance complexity, low per-unit cost, the same general China-bulk-goods logic already successfully applied to hire-fleet items elsewhere in this repo transfers cleanly.
- **Savings potential:** likely material on a percentage basis, low in absolute dollar terms given the small-ticket nature of this category.
- **Compliance risk:** minimal — Australian Consumer Law general product-safety standards only.
- **Logistics vs. timeline:** fits the existing window comfortably; lowest-priority category to sequence.

## Risks Identified

- **Two categories (pedicure chairs, treatment beds) carry the only genuinely quantified savings in this entire strategy** — every other "unquantified" category risks being pursued on the general assumption that import = savings, without a real comparison. This document does not recommend acting on any unquantified category without first obtaining a direct quote comparison.
- **Shipping-damage/warranty-support risk is real and applies across every import category**, not just treatment beds — the existing repo disclosure for massage tables should be treated as a template caution for every category recommended for import, not an isolated note.
