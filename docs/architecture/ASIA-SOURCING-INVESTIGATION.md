## 2. Real Market Finding — Consolidated Sourcing Exists as a Real Category

Web research (2026-08-16) confirms this is a real, established sourcing channel, not a hypothetical: Alibaba's own showroom categories include dedicated "Australia import furniture," "salon equipment Australia," and "shop fit-out" consolidation listings, aimed specifically at Australian small businesses importing commercial fixtures. A local Australian intermediary, Ali Baba Furniture (South Australia-based, two physical showrooms, Australia-wide delivery), exists specifically to bridge this gap — buying in bulk from Chinese manufacturers and reselling/distributing within Australia, which removes the direct-import complexity (customs, freight consolidation, minimum order quantities) at the cost of a margin on top of factory pricing.

**What a genuine kit/consolidated strategy would look like for this venture:** rather than sourcing each category (nail stations, styling chairs, lounge furniture, trolleys) from separate suppliers, a single consolidated shipment or a single Australian import-intermediary relationship covering multiple non-clinical furniture categories at once — reducing per-shipment freight/customs overhead relative to ordering each category separately.

## 3. What Must NOT Be Compromised — the Hard Boundary

Per this investigation's own explicit brief, four things are never on the table for cost reduction via sourcing country:

- **Clinical safety** — anything touching the Blood Collection Room or pathology-adjacent equipment (§7 of `ITEMISED-PURCHASE-LIST.md`) is sourced through the WDP/pathology-partner relationship, not general retail/import channels, regardless of cost. Not evaluated for Asia sourcing here.
- **Compliance** — TENS machines must be ARTG-registered regardless of manufacturing origin (per `equipment-costs.md`'s own existing TGA note) — a genuinely fixed requirement, not something country-of-origin changes. Curtain fabric must meet AS 1530.2/3 fire-retardancy regardless of origin (`docs/architecture/CURTAIN-COMPLIANCE-CLOSURE.md` already confirmed real Australian suppliers combining fire-retardancy and acoustic performance in one product — that finding is unaffected by this investigation and not reopened here).
- **Quality** — the premium positioning this venture is built on (`docs/strategy/PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`) depends on materials and finish quality being genuinely good, not merely cheap. Cheaper sourcing that visibly reads as cheaper defeats the purpose.
- **Premium positioning** — anything client-facing and visually prominent (lounge chairs, reception counter, styling chairs) carries more brand risk from a wrong sourcing call than anything back-of-house.

## 4. Category-by-Category Assessment

| Category | Client-facing? | Asia-sourcing suitability | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nail tables, tool trolleys, pedicure chairs | Semi (client sits in it, doesn't scrutinise brand) | **Good candidate** | Already identified in `equipment-costs.md` — functional equipment, quality differences less visible to a client than a lounge chair's fabric/finish |
| Styling/manicure/pedicure chair frames (non-fabric components) | Yes | **Reasonable candidate, with a fabric/upholstery caveat** | Frame quality is less visually scrutinised than fabric finish — a hybrid approach (imported frame, locally-reupholstered in a premium fabric matching the brand palette) is a genuine middle path worth costing, not evaluated to a dollar figure here |
| Lounge furniture (the single highest-scrutiny purchase per `ITEMISED-PURCHASE-LIST.md`) | Yes, highest | **Poor candidate for full import** | This is the one category `equipment-costs.md` itself already flags as needing in-person testing before bulk purchase — a category where getting the tactile/visual quality wrong is a real premium-positioning risk, not just a cost line |
| Reception counter, signage | Yes | **Case-by-case** | A generic imported counter with a locally-fabricated custom top/branding element is a plausible hybrid, not evaluated to a dollar figure here |
| Trolleys, tool carts, back-of-house storage | No | **Strong candidate** | Not client-visible, functional equipment only |
| Curtain track hardware (not the fabric itself) | Partially | **Reasonable candidate** | The fabric itself must be AS 1530.2/3-compliant (already resolved, Australian suppliers identified) — the track/hardware mechanism is a separate, lower-risk component |
| Birth-prep hire-fleet items (TENS, balls, combs) | No (hired discreetly) | **Strong candidate, already identified** | Already the existing repo's own finding, F5's parked opportunity |
