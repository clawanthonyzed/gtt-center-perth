# Café/Post-Test Food Service Investigation

**Date:** 2026-08-16 | **Purpose:** F15 — a real commercial/food-handling/operational investigation into a post-test food/drink offering (coffee, tea, drinks, sandwiches, toasties, rolls) available the moment the final blood draw completes, since clients have been fasting for hours by that point. Small fridge/display concept. Genuine investigation, not just noting the idea exists.

---

## 1. Why This Matters — the Real Client Need

Per `docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s "Leaving experience" stage, the moment food arrives after the final draw is described as "the single most emotionally effective, lowest-cost luxury moment available to this business." Clients have fasted 8+ hours by the time the last draw completes — a genuine, real physiological need, not just a nice touch. The existing plan (per `financial-setup.md` Step 9, `equipment-costs.md` §6/§12) is currently scoped narrowly: pre-packaged snacks, herbal tea, coconut water — explicitly **not** a hot-food/sandwich offering.

## 2. What Anthony Is Asking For, Specifically

A step up from the current pre-packaged-snacks-only scope: coffee, tea, other drinks, and **sandwiches/toasties/rolls** — real food, not just packaged snacks — available the moment the final draw completes, from a small fridge/display concept (not a full commercial kitchen).

## 3. WA Regulatory Finding — the Real Compliance Consequence of This Upgrade

This is the genuinely new finding this investigation exists to surface, not previously flagged anywhere in this repo:

**The current plan is scoped at LOW RISK deliberately, and that scoping is the reason the existing FSS/council-notification requirements are as light as they are.** Per `financial-setup.md` Step 9 (citing WA's food-business risk classification system, WA Department of Health): "pre-packaged snacks with intact packaging have minimal requirements. No hot food preparation = no council inspection required."

**Sandwiches, toasties, and rolls change this classification.** Per WA's own Food Business Risk Classification System (WA Department of Health, `health.wa.gov.au`), food businesses are classified by risk tier based on what's actually prepared on-site, not just what's sold:
- **Low risk:** pre-packaged food, beverages only, no on-site food handling of potentially hazardous ingredients.
- **Medium risk:** most cafés and restaurants — anywhere assembling or handling potentially hazardous food (this includes sandwich/roll assembly with fillings like meat, cheese, egg — exactly the toastie/sandwich/roll category being proposed) fall into this tier, not the low-risk tier the current plan assumes.

**What Medium Risk actually changes, practically:**
- **More frequent council inspections** — the risk tier directly drives inspection frequency (per the WA classification system's own stated purpose), a genuinely different ongoing compliance relationship with the local council than the current "notify once, no inspection" low-risk assumption.
- **A higher annual food-business fee** — WA councils charge risk-tiered annual fees; Medium Risk carries a higher fee than Low Risk (exact figure is council-specific, not quantified here — genuinely unconfirmed, flagged not guessed).
- **Real food-handling infrastructure** — a designated food-prep surface, correct refrigeration (a "small fridge/display" concept is compatible with this, but needs to meet cold-chain temperature requirements, not just be decorative), and correct hand-washing facilities separate from any clinical handwashing station.
- **The existing Food Safety Supervisor requirement remains, but now has real teeth** — the FSS certificate holder becomes the person actually accountable for a real food-safety system (temperature logs, allergen labelling for the toastie/sandwich fillings), not just a nominal certificate for a low-risk snack operation.

## 4. Is This Genuinely Achievable at This Venue's Scale?

**Yes, plausibly, but it's a real operational commitment, not a free upgrade.** A small fridge/display concept serving pre-made or assembled-to-order sandwiches/toasties/rolls is a completely standard Medium Risk WA food-business model — this is not an unusual or risky ask, it's simply a different, better-understood regulatory tier than the current Low Risk assumption. The practical requirements:

- A dedicated small food-prep counter/surface within the existing Café/Refreshments Counter footprint (already an 8sqm solid-walled space in the current floor plan, per `docs/strategy/STRATEGIC-REPORT.md` §9 — the space itself doesn't need to grow, but its fit-out spec does).
- A commercial-grade small fridge/display unit (not a domestic bar fridge) — a genuine equipment upgrade from the current "mini fridge (sparkling water, coconut water, electrolytes)" line in `equipment-costs.md` §6.
- A toastie press or sandwich-press for the "toastie" option specifically, if hot food is offered (this is the one element that could tip further toward a food-preparation classification requiring more than counter assembly — worth confirming with the council directly before committing to a hot-press option).
- Either pre-made sandwiches/rolls delivered daily from an external supplier (lower compliance burden, effectively the same Low-Risk-adjacent model as the current plan, just with real food instead of packaged snacks), or on-site assembly (Medium Risk, per §3 above) — **this single choice is the actual decision point**, not "café: yes or no."

## 5. Two Realistic Options, Not One

**Option A — Pre-made, externally supplied sandwiches/rolls, displayed and refrigerated on-site.** Lower compliance burden (closer to the current packaged-snack model, since nothing is assembled on-site), genuine hot-drink service (coffee/tea machine) still requires basic food-handling awareness but not full Medium Risk classification on its own. This is the lower-risk, faster-to-implement path.

**Option B — On-site assembly (toasties made to order, sandwiches built from fridge ingredients).** Genuinely Medium Risk per §3, with the full compliance consequence described above — more council engagement, a real food-safety system, higher ongoing fee. Delivers a warmer, more "made for you" experience (consistent with the venue's own hospitality positioning) at a real, disclosed ongoing compliance cost.

**Recommendation for further investigation, not a decision made here:** Option A is very likely sufficient to meet the actual client need described in §1 (something substantial to eat immediately after fasting) without the Medium Risk compliance step-up, and is worth costing first before assuming Option B's fuller café model is required. This is a genuine, disclosed judgement call for Anthony/the eventual Venue Manager, not resolved here.
