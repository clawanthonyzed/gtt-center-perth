## 0. The Trigger: New Legislation, 2026-09-03

On 2026-09-03 the federal Treasurer released exposure draft legislation for a **30% minimum tax on discretionary trusts**, intended to apply from **1 July 2028** `[VERIFIED — ABC News, 2026-09-03, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-03/government-offers-small-businesses-a-trust-tax-workaround/107112000; ATO "Tax reform — introducing a minimum tax on discretionary trusts", https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/businesses/tax-reform-introducing-a-minimum-tax-on-discretionary-trusts]`. Public consultation on the exposure draft is open for roughly two weeks from release, closing **18 September 2026** `[VERIFIED — Financial Standard, "Treasury launches minimum tax on discretionary trusts draft legislation", 2026-09-03, https://www.financialstandard.com.au/news/treasury-launches-minimum-tax-on-discretionary-trusts-draft-legislation-179813861]`.

**YETI Holding Trust (GTT Center Perth's parent structure, corporate trustee YETI Tipi Holdings PTY LTD) is a discretionary trust**, per this repo's own established facts (`docs/revenue-extraction-options.md` §"Structural Starting Point", `docs/financial-setup.md` Step 1). This legislation targets discretionary trusts specifically, so it applies directly to YETI Holding Trust unless an exemption or election is used.

**Core mechanics `[VERIFIED — ATO / William Buck Federal Budget 2026 Trust Analysis, https://williambuck.com/tools/federal-budget-2026/trusts/]`:**
- From 1 July 2028, trustees of discretionary trusts pay a baseline 30% tax on trust income at the trustee level.
- Beneficiaries receive non-refundable credits for tax already paid at the trustee level. Beneficiaries on a marginal rate above 30% owe top-up tax; beneficiaries on a marginal rate below 30% simply forfeit the excess credit — it is not refunded.
- Primary production income is specifically excluded, which is not relevant to GTT Center Perth (a health/wellness services business, not primary production).
- General industry commentary (William Buck) suggests corporate structures may become the preferred default for *operating* entities going forward, while discretionary trusts retain value for asset-protection purposes — but this is general market commentary, not advice tailored to this venture, and is explicitly not adopted as a recommendation here.

**The alternative to restructuring — a new election, not previously available:**

The same draft legislation introduces an option to **elect fixed distributions to pre-nominated beneficiaries** as an alternative to restructuring into a company, which would exempt the trust from the 30% minimum tax without triggering a restructure `[VERIFIED — Accountants Daily, "Government offers alternative to restructuring in trust tax legislation", 2026-09-03, https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/tax-compliance/22858-government-offers-alternative-to-restructuring-in-trust-tax-legislation]`. This is genuinely new: it did not exist as an option before the 2026-09-03 draft, and it is the reason this write-up is happening now rather than at some other time.

This is why the entity-structure question, previously a two-way "trust-direct vs. new PTY LTD" decision, is now genuinely three-way. See Section 1.

## 1. The Three Live Options

Laid out neutrally. None is presented as preferred.

### Option (a) — Trust-direct (default if no other option is chosen — NOT yet decided)

GTT Center Perth has not launched yet, so there is no trading arrangement actually in operation for any option to be "current" against. This option means GTT Center Perth would trade directly under YETI Holding Trust as planned in `docs/financial-setup.md` and `docs/revenue-extraction-options.md` — no new entity — if no other option is ever chosen. It is the default/status-quo path by omission, not a decision that has been made. The trust would retain full discretionary power to vary distributions year to year among its beneficiaries, subject to the trust deed.

Under the 2026-09-03 draft, from 1 July 2028 this option would, unless one of Options (b) or (c) is also taken, become subject to the 30% minimum trustee-level tax described in Section 0.

### Option (b) — New operating PTY LTD, owned by/under the trust, trading as GTT Center Perth

A new proprietary limited company is incorporated, owned by YETI Holding Trust (or by YETI Tipi Holdings PTY LTD as trustee, structure-dependent), and that company becomes the operating/trading entity for GTT Center Perth — employing staff, holding the lease, entering the pathology partnership agreement, collecting revenue, and bearing day-to-day liability. The trust sits above it as owner, receiving dividends rather than trading revenue directly.

This was already flagged as a live question in this venture's own documents before the 2026-09-03 legislation — `docs/financial-setup.md` Step 1 point 6 asks the accountant to address "2028 trust distribution tax risk — should we add an operating PTY LTD under the trust now?", and `docs/external-resources-and-advisors.md` references "the proposed 2028 minimum trust distribution tax and whether an operating PTY LTD is worth establishing ahead of it." The rationale given in this venture's own documents is liability ring-fencing: GTT Center Perth is a physical venue with clinical activity (blood draws, fasting patients, potential adverse reactions), staff, and public foot traffic — a real liability exposure profile that a pure discretionary trust structure does not ring-fence from Anthony/Imara's other assets as cleanly as a limited company would. Companies also have well-understood tax treatment for minimum-tax-avoidance purposes (a company is not a discretionary trust and is not captured by the 2026-09-03 draft at all), at the cost of losing the trust's income-splitting flexibility described in `docs/revenue-extraction-options.md` Mechanism 1.

### Option (c) — Fixed-distribution election under the 2026-09-03 draft legislation

YETI Holding Trust elects, under the new mechanism described in Section 0, to make **fixed distributions to pre-nominated beneficiaries**. If the election is validly made and maintained, the trust is exempt from the 30% minimum tax without needing to restructure into a company, and the election is **not expected to trigger state/territory stamp duty** `[VERIFIED — Accountants Daily, 2026-09-03, as cited above; Sterlinx Global "Daily Australia Tax Update: 3 September 2026", https://sterlinxglobal.com/daily-australia-tax-update-3-september-2026-discretionary-trust-minimum-tax-draft-released-fixed-distribution-election-sidesteps-stamp-duty-ato-gic-warning-on-payg-instalments/]` — a real cost advantage over Option (b) in a state like WA where restructuring can trigger duty.

**What "fixed distributions to pre-nominated beneficiaries" means practically, based on the exposure draft commentary available so far:**

- **"Pre-nominated"** — the beneficiaries who will receive distributions are named/locked in at the time of the election, not left to the trustee's year-to-year discretion. Nominated beneficiaries can generally only be added or changed later in limited circumstances — commentary specifically mentions a nominated beneficiary's death or a family breakdown as the triggering events, not routine variation `[VERIFIED — Sterlinx Global, 2026-09-03]`.
- **"Fixed"** — the trustee commits to a specified distribution pattern (percentage or dollar amount) to each nominated beneficiary. Distributions cannot then be varied year to year based on cash flow, each beneficiary's marginal tax rate that year, or business performance swings — the trustee is trading discretion for the tax exemption `[VERIFIED — Sterlinx Global, 2026-09-03]`.
- **Consistency is enforced with a real penalty.** If the trustee makes a distribution inconsistent with the elected fixed pattern, the election is **automatically revoked**, and the trust is taxed at the top marginal rate plus Medicare levy for that year, with the 30% minimum tax then applying in subsequent years `[VERIFIED — Accountants Daily / Sterlinx Global, 2026-09-03]`. This is not a soft guideline — getting a distribution wrong in a single year has a real, immediate tax cost.
- **No stated numerical limit** on how many beneficiaries can be nominated, and eligible beneficiaries can reportedly include individuals, eligible companies, or eligible trusts `[VERIFIED — Sterlinx Global, 2026-09-03]`.

**The core tradeoff, stated plainly:** a discretionary trust's entire structural value is the trustee's year-to-year discretion — the ability to vary who receives what, and how much, based on each beneficiary's circumstances that year (this is literally Mechanism 1 in `docs/revenue-extraction-options.md`, and it is the reason YETI Holding Trust was set up as a discretionary trust rather than a fixed trust or company in the first place). Electing fixed distributions to avoid the 30% minimum tax means giving up exactly that flexibility, on an ongoing and only narrowly reversible basis (reversible via revocation, but at real tax cost in the revocation year). Whether that trade is worth it for YETI Holding Trust specifically is a numbers question a general research document cannot answer — it depends on Anthony and Imara's actual respective marginal tax rates, how GTT Center Perth's income is likely to be distributed between them in practice, and whether other purposes the trust serves (asset protection, flexibility for future beneficiaries) matter enough to outweigh locking in a fixed pattern now, years before the tax actually applies.

**What remains genuinely unresearched at the time of writing:** the actual text of the exposure draft bill itself (as opposed to news/advisory summaries of it) was not located and read directly during this pass — the citations above are all secondary commentary from the ATO's own explanatory page and reputable tax-advisory/news sources (ATO, Accountants Daily, Financial Standard, William Buck, Sterlinx Global), not the primary legislative text or Treasury's own consultation paper. `[PLACEHOLDER — primary source not yet obtained]`. ## 3. What This Means for the Accountant Conversation

`docs/financial-setup.md` Step 1 already lists engaging a Perth-based accountant with discretionary-trust experience as a Week 1, blocking action, and already flags "2028 trust distribution tax risk — should we add an operating PTY LTD under the trust now?" as one of the six things to brief them on. That flag was written before the 2026-09-03 draft existed. It should now be treated as a three-way question, not a two-way one.

**Specific questions to bring to the accountant, given all three options are now live:**

1. Does YETI Holding Trust, as currently constituted, fall within the scope of the 30% minimum tax as drafted, or is there any basis (structure, income type, size) on which it might not be captured? `[PLACEHOLDER — genuinely accountant's question, not answerable from general research]`
2. Is the fixed-distribution election (Option c) actually available to YETI Holding Trust given its trust deed as written, or does the deed itself need to be reviewed/varied first to support a fixed distribution pattern?
3. If Option (c) is taken: what would a realistic fixed-distribution pattern look like for GTT Center Perth's income specifically, given Anthony's and Imara's actual respective tax positions (Anthony's TPI/Commonwealth Super status, Imara's employment income as a social worker) — and what happens if GTT Center Perth's income varies significantly year to year (a real possibility for a business this early-stage) against a fixed distribution commitment?
4. If Option (b) is taken: what would it actually cost, in dollars and process, to move the trading activity — the lease (once signed), employment contracts, the in-progress WDP/PathWest/Clinipath pathology partnership agreement, and the Fresha/EFTPOS/Xero financial infrastructure already being set up per `docs/financial-setup.md` — into a new company? Would any of it need to be re-negotiated or re-executed, and does the timing (before vs. after lease signing, before vs. after first booking) change that cost?
5. Is there a "do nothing yet, decide later" path — i.e., does taking no action now foreclose either Option (b) or (c) later, or can the decision genuinely wait until closer to the 1 July 2028 effective date without losing optionality? (`docs/dva-tpi-research.md`-adjacent research elsewhere in this venture found a restructuring rollover window and separate stamp-duty-relief timing considerations exist for company restructures more broadly — whether an equivalent applies to *this* specific choice should be asked directly rather than assumed.)
6. Does the accountant have a view on the public consultation itself (closing approximately 18 September 2026) — is there any reason for YETI Holding Trust / Anthony to make a submission, or is this squarely a "wait for the final legislation" situation given the venture's early stage?
7. Independent of the 2028 tax question: does the accountant, from a pure liability/asset-protection standpoint (separate from tax), think a physical venue with clinical activity like GTT Center Perth warrants an operating company regardless of what happens with the trust tax legislation? (This is the liability question Option (b) was originally raised for, before the 2026-09-03 legislation existed at all — it doesn't disappear just because Option (c) now offers a tax-only alternative.)
8. Whichever option is discussed, confirm explicitly: does it change anything about Anthony's TPI Disability Compensation Payment status, given his "cannot work" condition and the existing confirmed non-operational trustee/administrative-only role described in `docs/revenue-extraction-options.md`? This should be a formality given the existing non-means-tested finding, but should be confirmed for each option specifically, not assumed to carry over automatically.

**This document does not recommend an answer to any of the above.** The decision sits with Anthony and the accountant, using real figures for YETI Holding Trust that this general-research document does not have access to.
