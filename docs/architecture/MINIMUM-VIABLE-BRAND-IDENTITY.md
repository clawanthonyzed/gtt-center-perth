## 1. Business Naming Framework

**Not a decision. `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own governance remains unchanged: naming is Anthony's own decision, on his own timeline — this section deepens the decision-support material already started in `docs/architecture/BRAND-IDENTITY-FRAMEWORK.md` §1, it does not narrow toward a choice.**

### 1.1 Naming Criteria

Derived directly from this venture's own already-established positioning and trust requirements, not invented fresh:

- **Credibility signal** — must read as a genuine healthcare/wellness venue, not a generic startup name, given this deliverable's own stated objective (credible to pregnant customers, referral partners, landlords, and future investors).
- **Warmth without cutesiness** — `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own "maternal knowing... not condescending" pillar rules out infantilising or overly playful naming.
- **Does not overclaim clinical authority** — the venture is explicitly not a diagnostic or treating clinical practice in its own right (pathology is WDP/PathWest's role); a name should not imply otherwise.
- **Works across the full service range** — not so narrowly tied to "GTT" that it undersells the PM wellness revenue stream, nor so generic that it loses the specific differentiator that makes this venture unique.
- **Availability, unverified in this repo** — domain, Instagram handle, ASIC business name, and any trademark consideration must be checked before any direction is locked in; not attempted here.
- **Future-expansion compatibility** — per `docs/architecture/BRAND-IDENTITY-FRAMEWORK.md` §7, a Perth-specific name complicates (does not prevent) a future second venue; a genuine, low-cost consideration for whichever direction Anthony eventually pursues, not a disqualifier for any of them.

### 1.2 Emotional Associations Wanted

Calm, trust, competence, genuine care (not rushed or transactional) — directly from `docs/brand-guide.md`'s brand pillars (clinical confidence, premium warmth, maternal knowing, calm authority).

### 1.3 Maternity/Pregnancy Trust Considerations

Pregnancy is a high-vulnerability, high-anxiety period for many clients — medical uncertainty layered on genuine excitement, often a first-time experience. A name should respect the client as a competent adult navigating a mandatory medical process, not infantilise the experience or lean on maternity cliché (`docs/brand-guide.md` §2's own explicit "no bright pink, no maternity cliché" rule, extended here to naming). Should reduce anxiety by being clear and calm, not obscure or gimmicky.

### 1.4 Healthcare Professionalism Considerations

The clinical component of this venture (a client's GTT result genuinely matters for a real GDM diagnosis) means the name must sustain trust in that outcome, not just the wellness experience around it — `docs/brand-guide.md` §11's own explicit rule ("We are NOT a pregnancy day spa... don't lead with spa") is a naming-relevant constraint, not just a tone rule. An overly playful or purely lifestyle-branded name risks undermining confidence in the clinical rigour underneath.

### 1.5 Future Expansion Considerations

Per `docs/architecture/BRAND-IDENTITY-FRAMEWORK.md` §7, a second venue's timeline is not set (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 37) — this is a light consideration, not a planning constraint. A Perth-anchored name is not wrong; it simply carries a real, disclosed re-branding cost if a second city venue is ever pursued, worth knowing now, not a reason to avoid a Perth-anchored direction if Anthony otherwise prefers it.

### 1.6 Shortlisted Name Directions — Options, Not a Pick

**Three directions, each grounded in options already surfaced elsewhere in this repo (`docs/architecture/BRAND-IDENTITY-FRAMEWORK.md` §1, `docs/poppy-marketing.md` §3) — deepened here against the criteria above, not replaced with newly-invented candidates.** A fourth, more abstract wellness-brand-style direction was considered but deliberately not included here, since no example of it exists anywhere in this repo — inventing one now would cross into generating brand direction from nothing, which this deliverable is explicitly instructed not to do.

**Direction A — Descriptive/Clinical Clarity** (e.g. "GTT Center Perth" / "GTT Centre Perth," the current placeholder)
- *Rationale:* immediately communicates what the venue is and where — reduces client uncertainty at first contact, reinforces clinical credibility (directly serving §1.4), and captures a real, already-confirmed SEO advantage (`docs/poppy-marketing.md` §4: zero wellness-experience competitor pages currently rank for "GTT test Perth").
- *Risks/limitations:* currently reads as a placeholder because it is one — needs the full supporting visual/tone system to carry the "premium warmth" pillar, since the name alone doesn't. Ties the whole brand tightly to "GTT," a risk against §1.1's "works across the full service range" criterion if PM wellness ever becomes the larger revenue driver. Perth-specific, the most direct future-expansion friction of the three directions.

**Direction B — Metaphor/Warmth-Led** (e.g. "Wait and Glow," already flagged in `docs/poppy-marketing.md` §3, never adopted)
- *Rationale:* genuinely memorable, captures this venture's own core positioning claim (the wait, reimagined) intrinsically rather than needing supporting copy to explain it. Carries emotional warmth (§1.2) without extra explanation. City-agnostic — the strongest of the three directions against the future-expansion criterion (§1.5).
- *Risks/limitations:* loses immediate clarity about what the business actually does — weakens the confirmed "GTT test Perth" SEO advantage specifically. Carries a real risk (flagged, not dismissed) of reading as too casual for the healthcare-professionalism trust requirement (§1.4) if the surrounding visual/tone execution doesn't actively counterbalance it — the exact "day spa" drift `docs/brand-guide.md` §11 already warns against.

**Direction C — Place + Reassurance** (e.g. "The GTT Room," "GTT Lounge")
- *Rationale:* a middle ground — retains "GTT" for searchability while softening the clinical framing via "Room"/"Lounge." Has some existing brand-voice precedent, since "lounge" language is already used descriptively in this venture's current copy (Instagram bio: "Perth's first GTT wellness lounge").
- *Risks/limitations:* that same existing descriptive usage is also the direction's biggest risk — adopting "Lounge" as the literal name could dilute or conflict with its current generic descriptive use elsewhere in this venture's copy, a real, disclosed tension, not a clean option. Still substantially Perth/GTT-anchored, carrying similar (if slightly softer) future-expansion friction to Direction A.

**No direction is recommended over another here.** All three are grounded in material already explored in this repository; the choice, and the timing of making it, remains entirely Anthony's own.

## 4. Clinic Experience Positioning

### 4.1 What a Customer Should Feel Entering GTT

Per `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own brand pillars, translated into an entry-moment description: a genuine sense of being expected and cared for, not processed — calm rather than clinical, private rather than exposed, comfortable rather than merely tolerated for the required 2+ hour duration.

### 4.2 How the Environment Communicates Trust and Care

Directly tied to the already-established design principles, not a new claim: the full-length, opaque, lockable-track privacy curtain system (`docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §3, confirmed compliant with real fire/acoustic standards per `docs/architecture/CURTAIN-COMPLIANCE-CLOSURE.md`) is the physical expression of the "no client sees another client, pregnancy modesty is non-negotiable" principle — trust isn't only communicated through tone of voice, it is built into the room itself. The open-plan Lounge/Hairdressing/Nails zone (§2 of that same document) communicates warmth and connection where privacy isn't required, while the one fully-enclosed room (Blood Collection Room) signals genuine clinical seriousness exactly where it matters, and nowhere else.

### 4.3 How GTT Center Perth Differs — Specifically

| Compared to | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Standard pathology collection centres** (PathWest, WDP direct) | No wait-time management or wellness offering at all — a purely transactional clinical visit, per `docs/business-plan.md` §7's own competitive analysis. This venture's entire value proposition is the 2+ hour wait itself, reimagined. |
| **Beauty clinics/day spas** | No genuine clinical credibility underneath — `docs/brand-guide.md` §11's own explicit rule: "we are NOT a pregnancy day spa" pretending to also handle a real medical test. This venture's phlebotomy is real, credentialed, and NATA-adjacent-accredited via the pathology partner, not an add-on gimmick. |
| **Traditional healthcare providers generally** | Efficient throughput is usually the whole point; here, the premium comfort/hospitality layer is not an afterthought bolted onto a clinical visit, but co-equal with the clinical function by design (the open-plan/curtain fit-out philosophy itself, `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §2, reflects this — most of the venue is designed for comfort, only the one room requiring real clinical rigour is built clinically). |
| **East-coast comparables (MIWM Melbourne, The GTT Lounge Sydney)** | No direct WA competitor exists (`docs/business-plan.md` §7) — this venture is not differentiating against a local rival, it is establishing the category in WA for the first time. |
