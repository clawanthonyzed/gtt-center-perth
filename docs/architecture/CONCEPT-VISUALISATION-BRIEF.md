# Concept Visualisation Brief — For Professional Concept Illustrations

**Purpose: landlord/investor/customer communication material — NOT construction documentation.** The construction-focused brief remains `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` (two phases ago) — this document is for a professional illustrator/renderer producing concept visuals (mood-board-quality renders, not to-scale architectural drawings) to show a landlord what the venue will feel like, show an investor the concept, and eventually support marketing/waitlist content once real photography isn't yet possible.

**Phase:** Commercial Launch Preparation, focus area 2 of 4. No canonical data or financial model touched.

**Date:** 2026-08-10

---

## 1. What This Brief Is Not

- Not a floor plan, dimensioned drawing, or construction document — see `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` for that.
- Not real photography — `docs/brand-guide.md` §5 already establishes real photography requirements (shoot list, aspect ratios, subject/lighting rules) for once the venue exists; this brief is for concept illustration only, ahead of a confirmed venue.
- Not a substitute for compliance sign-off — every illustrated element must reflect what has actually been confirmed compliant (see §7), not an idealised version that would need correcting later.

---

## 2. Aesthetic Direction — Premium But Calming

Directly sourced from `docs/brand-guide.md` — not reinvented:

- **Palette:** Lounge White (`#FAF8F4`), Sage (`#8FA98A`), Terracotta (`#C47A5A` accent only, one element per composition maximum), Deep Slate (`#2E3330`). No pure black or white. No gradients — solid fills only. No clinical blue, no maternity-cliché pink, no gold/yellow.
- **Lighting:** natural, diffused, warm — golden-hour quality, never harsh or fluorescent-clinical.
- **Mood:** calm, warm, intimate, unhurried — the same three words `docs/brand-guide.md` §5 already uses for real photography, equally applicable to concept illustration.
- **What must never appear:** clinical equipment, needles, vacutainers, sharps containers, or any collection-room paraphernalia — `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own photography rule ("no clinical content... ever appear in brand photography") applies identically here.

---

## 3. Reception Experience

Illustrate: a warm, welcoming check-in moment — low counter section visible (accessibility, per `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §6), retail display shelving in soft focus behind, a staff member greeting a visibly pregnant client with genuine warmth (not a posed corporate smile — `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own stock-photography rule against "overly posed, bright-lit, fake-smile images" applies to illustration style too). Natural light from an entry-facing angle if plausible for the concept.

---

## 4. Customer Journey — A Sequence, Not a Single Image

Per `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §7's one-way flow (Entry → Reception → Lounge → Collection Room → Treatment Room → Exit), request a **sequence** of 4-5 linked concept images following one client through her visit, not isolated unrelated renders:

1. Arrival/reception (§3 above)
2. The Lounge — wide shot, reclining chairs, warm natural light, a sense of the open-plan Lounge/Hairdressing/Nails zone as one continuous, welcoming space (not a corridor of separate rooms — this is the venue's own stated design philosophy, per `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §2, and should read that way visually)
3. A treatment moment — massage or nails, hands visible, calm expression, no clinical framing
4. The privacy curtain moment (see §6 below) — shown accurately, not aspirationally
5. Departure — a client leaving with a sense of having been genuinely cared for, not rushed

---

## 5. Open-Plan Treatment Environment

Illustrate the Lounge, Hairdressing Area, and Nail Station Area as one continuous, connected space — this is a genuine design decision, not a cost-driven compromise to visually downplay. The brief for the illustrator should explicitly ask for a composition that makes the open-plan feel spacious and intentional, not cramped or utilitarian. Reference `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §2 for the exact zone boundaries (only the Blood Collection Room is fully walled).

---

## 6. Privacy Curtain System — Illustrate Accurately, Not Aspirationally

**Critical cross-reference to `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 51 (`docs/architecture/CURTAIN-COMPLIANCE-CLOSURE.md`) — the illustrator must not depict something that would later need correcting once the real fabric is chosen:**

- The curtain must be shown as **full-length, opaque, and closed** in any moment depicting active treatment — never sheer, translucent, or partially open in a way that would misrepresent the actual visual-privacy standard this venture has committed to (`docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §3, "no client sees another client").
- The curtain fabric, if shown in enough detail to read a texture, should look like a genuine acoustic-rated commercial curtain (a substantial, structured drape, not a light decorative sheer) — consistent with the acoustic-fabric decision already funded in `docs/architecture/MVP-OPENING-DECISION-REVIEW.md` §1, and with the confirmed real Australian supplier products identified in the compliance closure (Halcyon-style multi-header acoustic curtains are a reasonable visual reference point for the illustrator, not a literal brand mandate).
- **Do not illustrate the curtain as a whimsical or sheer "spa" aesthetic** — this would misrepresent both the compliance reality (fire/acoustic-rated fabric looks and behaves differently to a decorative sheer) and the brand's own "premium warmth," not "spa cliché" positioning (`docs/brand-guide.md` §11, "We are NOT a pregnancy day spa").

---

## 7. Phlebotomy Room — Compliance-Accurate, Not Softened Into Invisibility

The Blood Collection Room should still appear in the customer-journey sequence (§4) if a moment near it is included, but per `docs/brand-guide.md`'s own explicit rule, **no clinical equipment, needles, vacutainers, or collection paraphernalia should ever be directly visible in these illustrations** — the same standard already governing real brand photography. If the room needs to appear at all (e.g. a door in the background of a Lounge-facing shot), show it as a calm, clean, solid-walled door with signage (per `docs/brand-guide.md` §9's own wayfinding spec — an etched aluminium disc with the leaf mark, "Collection Room" in DM Sans) — never the room's interior with clinical detail visible.

---

## 8. Pregnancy-Focused Design Principles — Visual Translation

Directly from `docs/architecture/CONCEPT-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §8, translated into illustration direction:

- Reclining/comfort seating with visibly adjustable backs, wedge-pillow-compatible — show a client in a genuinely relaxed, supported reclining posture, not upright/formal seating.
- Natural light as a first-class element in every Lounge/reception composition, not an afterthought.
- Warm, dimmable lighting throughout — avoid any lighting treatment that reads as clinical/fluorescent outside the (deliberately not-detailed, per §7) Collection Room.
- Accessibility cues present but not foregrounded — a pram or mobility aid visible in a background/incidental position in at least one composition, normalising accessibility without making it the subject of the image.

---

## 9. Deliverable Set Requested

Consistent with `docs/brand-guide.md` §5's real-photography aspect-ratio conventions, applied to concept illustration for cross-channel reuse:

- 16:9 (landlord/investor presentation, website hero placeholder)
- 1:1 (Instagram grid, waitlist-page teaser)

- At minimum: the 5-image customer-journey sequence (§4), plus one standalone open-plan wide shot (§5) suitable for a landlord walk-through conversation specifically.
