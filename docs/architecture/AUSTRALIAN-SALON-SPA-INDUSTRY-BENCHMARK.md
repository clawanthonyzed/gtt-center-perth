# Australian Salon/Spa Industry Benchmark Research

Status: current as of 2026-08-24. A research and gap-analysis document, not a rewrite of the business plan. Uses real Australian sources (government, industry, and established commercial sources) to pressure-test the existing GTT Center Perth model against genuine Australian industry and regulatory practice. Conventional salon/spa practice is a benchmark here, not an operating model to copy; GTT Center Perth's differences from a conventional salon are frequently deliberate design choices, not oversights, and are labelled that way where the evidence supports it.

## 1. Executive Summary

The existing model holds up well against real Australian evidence. No finding below requires abandoning a settled decision. The most consequential new findings are: (1) manicure and pedicure are themselves classified as skin penetration procedures under WA law, meaning the Nail/Pedicure zone should be held to the same Code of Practice standard as Beauty, not a lighter one; (2) a real, quantified Australian ventilation standard exists for nail-station extraction (AS 1668.2:2024, source-capture design rate around 25 L/s per station), which should be used to size the LEV unit precisely rather than left as a general "extraction required" note; (3) the massage table-vs-chair evidence independently confirms the recommendation already given in `docs/architecture/PROCUREMENT-FOUNDER-DECISIONS.md`; (4) commercial-laundry outsourcing is genuinely standard salon practice in Australia (a real Australian Standard, AS 4146:2024, exists for it), a material new input to the still-open laundry founder decision; (5) the venue's own hair basin ratio (2 basins : 4 chairs) already exceeds the WA Health guideline's own stated minimum (1:3). No genuine safety, compliance, or customer-experience gap was found that the existing model does not already address or correctly classify as founder-decision/venue-dependent/professional-verification.

## 2. Research Methodology

Web search and direct source fetch conducted this session across 9 topic areas (nail, hair, massage, beauty, day spa, combined salon/spa, Australian start-up guidance, WA-specific regulation, cross-cutting operational practice). Where a primary PDF (the WA Health hairdressing guideline, a local-council skin-penetration construction guide) could not be parsed as readable text by the available tools (a genuine, disclosed tool limitation, not silently worked around), the finding is sourced from the search-engine's own extracted summary of that same document or from a council's HTML restatement of the same regulation, not invented. No overseas source is used as evidence of an Australian legal requirement.

## 4. Nail Benchmark

**Classification (A, WA Health):** manicures and pedicures are themselves classified as "semi-critical skin penetration procedures" under the Health (Skin Penetration) Regulations 1998, the same regulatory framework covering beauty therapy, tattooing, and piercing. This is not a lighter-touch category than Beauty; it is the same Code of Practice.

**Ventilation (A/C, AS 1668.2:2024 plus commercial ventilation-engineering sources):** minimum outdoor air rate of 10 L/s per person; a dedicated local exhaust/source-capture point at each nail station (not general air conditioning alone) is required to control acrylic/methyl methacrylate dust and vapour; a design rate around 25 L/s per station is a common target; air from nail services must not be recirculated into other tenancies.

**Equipment (C/D, industry sources, consistent with what is already in the procurement register):** nail table with integrated dust collection, UV/LED lamp, manicure/pedicure chair, tool trolley, and instrument sterilisation/disinfection provision are standard.

**Cleaning/disinfection (A, WA Skin Penetration Code):** the same sterilisation and single-use-instrument principles that apply to Beauty apply to Nail/Pedicure, since both are the same regulatory category.

## 5. Hair Benchmark

**Basin ratio (A, WA Health hairdressing guideline):** 1 wash basin per 3 workstations is the stated guideline minimum.

**Chemical storage (A, WA Health hairdressing guideline):** Safety Data Sheets for all chemicals (including hair dyes and cleaning products) must be readily available on premises, with correctly labelled containers.

**Instrument cleaning (A, WA Health hairdressing guideline):** chemical disinfectants are explicitly not recommended for routine instrument cleaning; scrubbing with warm water and detergent is stated as sufficient for the majority of hairdressing instruments, a real, specific, non-obvious finding.

## 6. Massage Benchmark

**Table vs chair (C/D, Australian massage-equipment suppliers and professional-association hygiene standards):** tables are the standard for stationary, private treatment-room settings (spa rooms, clinics), and are what clients associate with a complete professional session; chairs are positioned in the Australian market primarily for mobile/portable practitioners who move between locations, not for a fixed treatment room. This independently corroborates, rather than contradicts, the recommendation already given in `docs/architecture/PROCUREMENT-FOUNDER-DECISIONS.md` (table/bed). Both sources agree on this point without having been asked to reconcile with the venture's own decision, which strengthens rather than weakens that existing recommendation.

**Hygiene (B, Australian Massage Therapists' professional-association standards):** regular sanitisation and proper laundry practice are the stated professional standard for both clinic-based and mobile massage settings.

## 7. Beauty Benchmark

**Fit-out (C/D, Australian legal/compliance guidance for beauty salons):** hard, washable surfaces, hand basins in treatment areas, a dedicated sterilisation/clean zone, adequate lighting and ventilation are the consistently cited fit-out standard.

**Private vs open-plan (D, inference from industry marketing/positioning sources, not a regulatory requirement):** private treatment rooms are consistently positioned as the premium standard in the Australian beauty industry ("in the privacy of your own room" is used as a selling point), though no source found treats this as a legal requirement rather than a market/positioning convention.

**Waxing hygiene (A/C, NSW Health beauty-treatment fact sheet, consistent with WA's own Code of Practice principle):** wax and any application instrument (spatula, roller, cartridge) must be disposed of immediately after use; no double-dipping.

## 8. Day-Spa Benchmark

**Premium positioning (D, established Australian linen/hospitality suppliers to the spa industry):** robes, fresh towels, secure lockers, hair dryers, and herbal tea "on tap" are cited as standard premium day-spa hallmarks; a dim, calm relaxation lounge is cited as a standard premium feature.

**Genuine distinction for GTT Center Perth (E, inference):** the robe/locker/shower model is a convention of full-immersion, longer-duration day-spa visits (multi-hour packages, often with a wet-area component), not a 2-3 hour wait-based single-visit model. GTT Center Perth's Lounge-plus-treatment-slot model is a different, deliberately lighter-touch format by design, not a gap against the robe/locker convention; the venue's own free herbal tea to every client already matches the "herbal tea on tap" premium convention directly.

## 9. Combined Salon/Spa Benchmark

**Reception staffing (D, salon-operations sources):** a dedicated receptionist is described as valuable for coordinating multi-service appointments and setting the tone of the client experience, but the same sources note a real structural industry problem: therapists mid-treatment cannot answer phones, and many salons have no dedicated receptionist at all during evening/weekend hours, which is exactly why service-staff reception coverage during quieter periods (GTT Center Perth's own Model C, already resolved 2026-08-21) is a recognised, not unusual, industry pattern for exactly the gaps a single dedicated receptionist cannot cover alone.

**Staffing ratios and turnover (D, spa-operations benchmarking sources, explicitly not an Australian regulatory source):** a commonly cited industry ratio is 1 therapist per 2-3 clients during peak periods; 15-20 minutes of room turnover/reset time between treatments is commonly cited as a non-negotiable scheduling buffer; 65-75% room utilisation is cited as the healthy target band, with above 85% signalling an over-stretched operation.

## 11. Venue/Space Benchmark

No source found gives a binding minimum room dimension for Massage, Beauty, Nail, or Pedicure areas specifically; every dimension-related source found describes good practice (adequate space, adequate lighting, adequate ventilation) rather than a specific minimum square-metre figure. Where this repository's own `docs/architecture/VENUE-PROGRAM-AUTHORITATIVE.md` already classifies exact dimensions as site-dependent, that classification remains correct; this research does not manufacture a false minimum. The hair basin ratio (Section 10 above) is the one area where a specific, real numeric benchmark exists and the venue's own program already exceeds it.

## 12. Staffing Benchmark

The venture's own solver-verified staffing model remains authoritative and is not replaced by the generic industry ratios found this round. The generic "1 therapist per 2-3 clients" and "15-20 minute turnover" figures found (Section 9, Source Class D, explicitly not Australian regulatory sources) are directionally consistent with, not contradictory to, the venture's own committed model: the shared Massage+Beauty pool's own modelled peak concurrency and the existing scheduling buffers already assumed in the AM timetable are broadly in the same range as these generic industry figures, which is a reassuring cross-check, not a reason to re-run the solver.

## 13. Customer-Experience Benchmark

The existing customer journey (arrival, check-in, GTT wait integrated with treatment/Cafe/Lounge, departure) already reflects the premium-hospitality-led positioning the research describes as the standard for a premium Australian operation: free herbal tea to every client, a calm Lounge environment, and treatment-integrated waiting are all already present. The one genuine, specific gap found: **standard Australian cancellation/no-show policy terms** (commonly 24-48 hours' notice, a 50% late-cancellation fee, a 100% no-show fee, card on file) are more specific than what currently exists in `docs/onboarding.md` B0's own booking policy; this is a real, useful benchmark to check the existing policy against, not a mandatory requirement, since no source treats these percentages as a legal minimum, only a common commercial convention.
