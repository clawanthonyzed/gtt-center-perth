# Concept Design Brief — For a Future Architect/Interior Designer

**Status: a written brief, not a floor plan.** Consistent with `docs/floor-plan-concept.md`'s own standing determination — "this team lacks real architectural/spatial design capability to produce a workable circulation layout. A professional architect/interior designer is needed for the actual floor plan once a venue is confirmed" — **this document does not attempt a layout, drawing, or illustration.** It consolidates this venture's already-established design principles, room requirements, and the acoustic-privacy decision from the recent Founder Risk Acceptance Review into one brief a future designer can work from directly, once a venue is confirmed.

**Phase:** Commercial Assumption Alignment & External Readiness, focus area 4 of 4 — investigation and documentation only. No financial model or canonical data touched.

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Primary source:** `docs/floor-plan-concept.md` (design principles, room schedule, critical design requirements — reused, not re-derived), `docs/architecture/MVP-OPENING-DECISION-REVIEW.md` §1 (acoustic-treatment decision).

## 1. Purpose of the Venue

A wellness venue built specifically around the mandatory Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT) that every pregnant woman in Western Australia undergoes — transforming a clinically-necessary 2–3 hour wait into a restorative experience (massage, nails, hair, beauty), with an on-site pathology collection room for the blood draws themselves. The design must serve two audiences simultaneously: a genuine clinical function (specimen collection, under pathology-partner accreditation) and a genuine wellness/hospitality function (the wait itself) — without either compromising the other.

---

## 2. Open-Plan Layout Philosophy

**The governing principle, confirmed 2026-07-31, non-negotiable for this brief:** only the Blood Collection Room is fully enclosed with solid walls and a locking door. Every other zone is either fully open-plan or curtain-partitioned:

- **Fully open-plan (no internal walls):** GTT Lounge, Hairdressing Area, Nail Station Area — these three zones share one continuous space.
- **Curtain-partitioned (private bays, not walled rooms):** Massage Rooms (1–2, plus a growth-reservation Room 5) and Facial/Beauty Rooms (3–4, plus a growth-reservation Room 6).
- **Fully enclosed, solid walls, locking door:** Blood Collection Room only — the sole space where venepuncture occurs.

**Why this matters to the design, not just the cost:** this is a genuine architectural philosophy, not merely a cost-cutting device (though it is also that — `docs/floor-plan-concept.md`'s own itemised build confirms a real construction saving from this approach). The open-plan zones are meant to feel like a continuous, welcoming wellness space — clients should experience the Lounge, Hairdressing, and Nails areas as one connected environment, not a corridor of separate rooms. The designer should lean into this, not treat it as a constraint to work around.

---

## 3. Curtain Privacy System — Specification

**Governing standard, from `docs/floor-plan-concept.md`'s Critical Design Requirements:** "Use a full-length, opaque, lockable-track privacy curtain (curtain cannot be pulled open from outside once closed) as the equivalent privacy mechanism" for every Massage and Facial/Beauty bay.

**Non-negotiable specification elements:**
- Full-length (floor-to-ceiling or floor-to-near-ceiling, no visible gap a passerby could see through)
- Opaque fabric — no light-through, no silhouette visibility
- Lockable-track hardware — the curtain must not be openable from outside once a client has closed it from inside
- **Fire-rated fabric, verified against an AS 1530-equivalent test report** — a compliance requirement identified in `docs/architecture/CHINA-ASIA-SUPPLIER-FEASIBILITY.md`, not previously specified anywhere in this repo before this phase. This is a firm brief requirement for whichever supplier (Australian or otherwise) ultimately provides the fabric.
- **Acoustic-rated fabric, minimum specification** — per the Founder Risk Acceptance Review (`docs/architecture/MVP-OPENING-DECISION-REVIEW.md` §1), the opening budget funds acoustic-rated curtain fabric (a heavier, sound-dampening material) as a condition of the approved opening strategy, not a basic curtain. The designer should specify genuine acoustic-rated curtain fabric as the baseline, not an upgrade option.
- **Deferred, not designed out:** additional acoustic mitigation (ambient sound-masking systems, extra bay spacing, supplementary wall/floor padding) was deferred to the first 3–6 months of operation, pending real client feedback — the designer should be aware this may be requested as a Phase 2 addition and, where low-cost to do so, avoid design choices that would make retrofitting sound-masking hardware unnecessarily difficult later (e.g. leave power/data provision for a future ceiling-mounted sound-masking speaker in the two curtain-bay zones, even if not installed at launch).

## 4. Phlebotomy Room (Blood Collection Room)

**The one room requiring full architectural rigour — this venture's clinical credibility depends on it.**

Confirmed requirements, cross-checked against WDP's own detailed room spec (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 30):
- Private room, solid walls, minimum 10sqm (WDP's own floor), target ~18sqm
- 2 phlebotomy chairs (Chair A, Chair B) with privacy screens or a fixed curtain track between them
- A **separate reclining chair or examination couch for vasovagal recovery** — distinct from the 2 active phlebotomy chairs, so a client who feels faint doesn't occupy a chair the next pair needs (a gap only found during a 2026-08-06 cross-check, now a firm requirement)
- Clinical sink — elbow-operated tap, infection-control standard, not a bathroom vanity
- Medical-grade specimen refrigeration with temperature logging
- Centrifuge bench — vibration-resistant, minimum 600mm deep, lockable under-bench storage, plus a genuinely open question the designer should resolve directly with the future Venue Manager/WDP: whether a **separate, broader supply storage cupboard** is needed beyond the centrifuge's own under-bench space (per NPAAC's own "secure storage area for supplies" wording)
- 2× sharps container wall brackets (bench level + stand level)
- Clinical LED downlights over each phlebotomy chair, colour rendering Ra>90 — **specify a verifiable-spec fitting, not a budget import claiming this rating without a checkable test report** (per the supplier feasibility review)
- Sound insulation to adjacent rooms — this is the one room where sound insulation is a firm requirement, not the curtain-bay trade-off described in §3
- **Explicit gaps still needing architect-level resolution, not yet specified anywhere in this repo:** the room's own wheelchair turning circle/doorway width (building-level accessibility exists elsewhere, but not confirmed at this room specifically), general room ventilation/air handling, an explicit impervious bench/floor surface spec, and data/power point counts for the centrifuge, fridge, and any booking-system terminal in this room.

**Adjacency:** must sit adjacent to the GTT Lounge (a phlebotomist should be a few steps from the next pair of clients, not across the building) and must NOT be adjacent to the staff room.

## 5. Treatment Areas

- **Massage Rooms (1–2, curtain-partitioned):** massage table, 1 clinical-hygiene sink (not the 2-sink Code-triggering spec — massage does not trigger the WA Skin Penetration Code), dimmer lighting, minimum 3 hooks + a gown rail at entry, smooth/practical flooring (not the Code-mandated impervious spec, since the Code doesn't apply here, but a genuinely washable surface remains good practice).
- **Facial/Beauty Rooms (3–4, curtain-partitioned):** facial bed, beauty/mirror station, **2 sinks retained** (hand-wash sink with hands-free tap + a separate instrument-decontamination sink in a designated prep area) — brow waxing/threading are real services here and plausibly trigger the Code's "tearing of the skin" language, so this remains the conservative, retained requirement.
- **Nail Station Area (open-plan):** 4 stations at full build, 2 staffed day-one per the approved MVP opening strategy (see `docs/architecture/AM-OPERATIONS-SENSITIVITY-MODEL.md` §3) — the designer should build for the full 4-station footprint from the outset even though only 2 open at launch, since retrofitting LEV extraction later is costly. 2 sinks per station retained (same Code-trigger logic as Facial/Beauty). Minimum 2×10A GPOs per station. Chemical-resistant vinyl or tile flooring, no carpet. **Genuine open design tension, not resolved here:** the open-plan Lounge/Nail zone sits in tension with the Nail Station's own fume-containment requirement — the most likely resolution is per-station downdraft/capture-hood LEV rather than room-level air separation, but this needs sign-off from a WorkSafe WA-familiar LEV contractor before fit-out, not assumed by this brief.
- **Hairdressing Area (open-plan):** 4 chairs at full build, 2 staffed day-one (same phased approach as Nails). 2–3 backwash basins (plumbed hot/cold, neck rest). Full-length mirror wall, front-lit with warm LED. Waterproof, hair-cleaning-friendly flooring, no carpet. No Code-mandated 2-sink requirement here (hair cutting is not skin-cutting).

## 6. Reception

- Line of sight to the entry door — staff must see who enters without turning around
- Counter at standard reception height (900mm) with a low, wheelchair/pram-accessible section (750mm)
- Retail display shelving visible to clients from the Lounge
- Card payment/POS counter space, receipt printer, dual-screen workstation
- The counter itself is a site-built joinery item (not an import candidate, per the supplier feasibility review) — the designer should treat this as a local trades scope item from the outset

---

## 7. Customer Flow

**One-way flow, no backtracking:** Entry → Reception → Lounge → Collection Room → Treatment Room(s) → Exit. The phlebotomist's own walk to the client is deliberately short (Collection Room adjacent to the Lounge). Staff areas (staff room, staff WC) are separated from client areas entirely — not accessible through any client-facing zone.

## 8. Pregnancy-Focused Design Principles

These are the governing principles this venture's own documents already state, reproduced here directly for the designer, not reinterpreted:

1. **"No client sees another client in mid-treatment. Pregnancy modesty is non-negotiable."** This is a **visual** privacy standard, met by the curtain system specified in §3 (already confirmed adequate for this specific purpose by the Founder Risk Acceptance Review) — not to be confused with the separate, narrower acoustic-privacy question also addressed in §3.
2. **Lounge seating must comfortably accommodate the venue's peak simultaneous client count** — under the current committed model, up to 18 clients/day at peak AM cadence; the historical "seats 8" figure is retained as a design-principle floor, not the actual current peak-capacity requirement.
3. **Natural light in the Lounge/reception zone is a genuine priority**, not a nice-to-have — pregnant clients spend 2+ hours here, and non-clinical atmosphere matters to the experience this venture is selling.
4. **Reclining/comfort seating with adjustable backs**, wedge-pillow-compatible, supporting left-lateral tilt — a specific, real physiological consideration for a pregnant cohort, not generic lounge furniture.
5. **Warm, dimmable lighting throughout client-facing zones** — no harsh clinical downlights outside the Blood Collection Room and the areas of Facial/Beauty rooms where clinical-standard lighting is genuinely needed; migraine/nausea sensitivity in pregnancy is a real, disclosed reason for this, not an aesthetic preference only.
6. **Accessibility is a first-class requirement, not an afterthought** — AS 1428.1-compliant accessible WC, pram-width circulation (minimum 1.5m hallways), low reception counter section, and — per §4's still-open gap — the Blood Collection Room's own internal wheelchair turning circle, which the designer must confirm explicitly, not assume covered by building-level provisions alone.
