# Partner Accreditation Standards Comparison — WDP, PathWest, Clinipath, Australian Clinical Labs

**Prepared by:** Grace (Operations Manager)
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Purpose:** Internal research only. Compares (1) the universal baseline every Approved Collection Centre must meet regardless of which pathology partner GTT Center Perth ends up choosing, against (2) each of the 4 candidate partners' own additional collection-centre policies, where any exist and are actually published. Nothing in this document has been drafted for, or sent to, any partner. No new outreach was performed — the provider-specific web research below is genuinely new; the universal-baseline section reuses this repository's own already-completed primary-source research rather than re-deriving it.

**Do not conflate the two layers below.** A requirement is "universal" only if it comes from the Commonwealth NPAAC/ACSQHC standard or the Commonwealth Approved Collection Centre (ACC) approval process itself — those apply to any Approved Collection Centre, under any Approved Pathology Authority (APA), anywhere in Australia. A requirement is "provider-specific" if it is a policy a particular pathology company has chosen to layer on top of that baseline, and it only applies if GTT Center Perth ends up partnering with that specific provider.

## 1. The Universal Baseline (already established in this repository — summarised, not re-derived)

**Source:** NPAAC's *Guidelines for Approved Pathology Collection Centres (Requirements for Medical Pathology Specimen Collection), Third Edition 2013*, NPAAC Tier 3B, now administered by the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC) since 1 July 2021. Full document text was obtained and reviewed directly in a prior session — see `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` items 29 and 29b, and `docs/architecture/WDP-PARTNERSHIP-ACTIVATION-PLAN.md` §2b-i for the companion Commonwealth Approved Collection Centre (ACC) eligibility-criteria research (Services Australia/ABLIS). This applies to any Approved Collection Centre "connected to an Approved Pathology Authority" — a broad premises category, not exclusive to any one provider or to GP practices.

This baseline would need to be met **regardless of which of the 4 candidates GTT ultimately partners with.**

### 1a. Premises requirements (confirmed, already researched)
- Non-porous, easily cleanable flooring and surfaces suitable for a clinical collection procedure.
- Secure storage area for supplies (NPAAC C1.1(iv), broader than just centrifuge-adjacent storage).
- Adequate space for furniture to allow patients to be seated or recumbent according to medical requirements, plus a work surface for collection staff (NPAAC C1.1(ix)).
- Specimens must not be stored with food, drink, or pharmaceuticals.
- The centre's current approval certificate must be displayed.
- (This repository's own `docs/pathology-collection-room.md` and the floor-plan cross-check at `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 30 build a fuller practical fit-out spec — non-slip coved flooring, hand hygiene station, lighting, ventilation, sharps disposal, etc. — on top of this NPAAC baseline; those are this venture's own design decisions for meeting the baseline, not additional NPAAC clauses in themselves, so they are not repeated in full here.)

### 1b. Staffing requirement (confirmed, already researched)
- The centre must be **"staffed by people employed or engaged by the APA"** (Approved Pathology Authority — i.e. the pathology provider itself, whichever of the 4 candidates is chosen). This is a Commonwealth ACC eligibility criterion (Services Australia/ABLIS), not something any one provider invented.
- The word "or" matters: a person can satisfy this either by being the partner's own employee, or by being formally engaged/credentialed by the partner under its own accreditation, quality management system (QMS), and standard operating procedures — even if that person's actual employer (for payroll/HR purposes) is GTT. This is the standard structure the collection-centre industry already uses nationally, and this repository's own existing documents (`docs/pathology-partnership-brief.md` §5, `docs/team-startup.md`) already describe GTT's own phlebotomists operating exactly this way under a partner's QMS.
- What is **not** sufficient under this Commonwealth test: a collector with no formal employment or engagement relationship to any APA at all.

### 1c. What this means practically
Any of the 4 candidates, once chosen, will require GTT's physical premises and staffing arrangement to satisfy 1a and 1b above — this is not a point of negotiation with any individual provider, it is the Commonwealth floor everyone operates on. GTT can prepare its physical fit-out, staffing/engagement structure, and documentation against this baseline **now**, before a partner is confirmed, with reasonable confidence it will still be relevant no matter which partner is eventually chosen.

## 2. Provider-Specific Overlays — What Each of the 4 Candidates Adds on Top (new research this session)

### 2a. WDP (Western Diagnostic Pathology)

**Confirmed, with a real source:**
- WDP has a named, actively marketed **Licensed Collection Centre (LCC) program** — its own "Partner with us" clinician page (`https://www.wdp.com.au/clinicians/getting-started-with-us/`) states plainly: **"Contact us to establish a Licensed Collection Centre in your practice."** This is the strongest documented partnership pathway of any of the 4 candidates.
- WDP's own site-assessment process, already obtained directly from Carole Rivers (Customer & Commercial Manager, Country) in this venture's own correspondence, includes: an annual rental arrangement whose terms depend on expected pathology volume, number of referring doctors on-site, location/accessibility, and overall business opportunity (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 1c/29d). Carole directly confirmed the "referring doctors on-site" factor is **referral-base/commercial pricing context only, not a precondition for site approval** — "the absence of doctors on site would not present an operational concern."
- WDP's own confirmed additional policy, beyond NPAAC: patients must **"remain at the collection centre"** for the full duration of the GTT (roughly 2 hours), not literally "the waiting room" as earlier paraphrased — Carole's own words, confirmed directly, 2026-08-08 email, and confirmed by her in the same email to be **WDP's own policy layered on NPAAC, not an NPAAC mandate itself** (`docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` items 29/29b/29c/29d). This is the one clearly-evidenced example of a genuine provider-specific overlay found anywhere in this research, for any of the 4 candidates.
- WDP also gave a detailed room-requirement list during correspondence (private room, minimum room size, 2 bleeding chairs, handwashing basin, sharps/waste facilities, proximity to a waiting area, privacy/acoustics) — already cross-checked against GTT's own floor plan at `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 30, which found 5 concept-level gaps (reclining chair/couch for vasovagal episodes, room-specific wheelchair accessibility, ventilation, impervious bench/floor surface, data/power points). This list reads as WDP's own practical application of the NPAAC baseline to a specific site, not a separately-published formal document.

**Genuinely unconfirmed / not published:**
- No separate, publicly downloadable WDP "Licensed Collection Centre quality manual," formal site-assessment checklist document, or written accreditation-scope document was found on WDP's website this session (checked directly — the "getting started" page itself contains only the one-line invitation quoted above, no attached checklist or standard). Everything specific known about WDP's own requirements comes from Carole Rivers' direct correspondence with this venture, not a published document — this is the most detail of any of the 4 candidates, but it is negotiated/correspondence-based, not a public standard.

### 2b. PathWest

**Confirmed, with a real source:**
- PathWest is government-operated (WA Health), NATA-accredited to ISO 15189 (Medical Testing) and ISO/IEC 17025 (Biological Testing) — confirmed via PathWest's own Quality Management page (`https://www.pathwest.health.wa.gov.au/About-Us/Customer-Service/Quality-Management`).
- That page states PathWest "employs a quality management system that strives for continuing quality improvement in all areas that impact our testing," with a schedule of internal/external audits and RCPA proficiency-testing participation — but this describes PathWest's **organisational/laboratory** quality approach, not a collection-centre-specific standard, and the page explicitly directs readers to NATA for laboratory accreditation scope rather than publishing its own collection-centre document.
- In this venture's own live correspondence, PathWest (Meera Bennett) stated directly it currently lacks capacity to supply collection staff to non-PathWest sites, and separately floated "a clinic with doctors" as its own alternative collection-room pathway — already researched in depth at `docs/architecture/WDP-PARTNERSHIP-ACTIVATION-PLAN.md` §2b, which concluded (via 3 independent Commonwealth primary-source checks) that a doctor-on-site is **not** a Commonwealth regulatory requirement, and that PathWest's comment most plausibly reflects PathWest's own siting/business preference rather than a WA-Health-specific regulatory framework — still not confirmed directly by PathWest, a question queued but deliberately not yet asked (`docs/pathwest-clinipath-outreach-2026-07-27.md` §4d-v).

**Genuinely unconfirmed / not published:**
- No published WA-Health-specific collection-centre accreditation or quality-standard framework beyond NATA/NPAAC was found anywhere on PathWest's public site this session. Government operation does not appear to translate into a separate published overlay standard — PathWest's site is silent on collection-centre-specific requirements distinct from its general laboratory quality-management language. This is consistent with `docs/reed-partnerships.md` §1's own existing finding that PathWest has "no dedicated non-standard-site program found."

### 2c. Clinipath (Sonic Healthcare)

**Confirmed, with a real source:**
- Clinipath is part of Sonic Healthcare Limited (ASX: SHL), already established in this repository (`docs/reed-partnerships.md` §1c).
- Sonic Healthcare's various divisions/subsidiaries (checked this session: Sonic HealthPlus, Sonic Reference Laboratory) each carry their own accreditations — e.g. Sonic Reference Laboratory is CLIA-certified and CAP-accredited (US-context laboratory accreditations, not directly applicable to an Australian WA collection centre), Sonic HealthPlus is AS/NZS ISO 9001 accredited. Many Sonic Healthcare pathology laboratories worldwide are accredited to ISO 15189:2012.
- Clinipath's own public "Partner with us" page (`https://www.clinipathpathology.com.au/about-us/partner-with-us/`) could not be directly fetched this session (HTTP 403, the same WAF-blocking access pattern already logged elsewhere in this repository for Clinipath and other pathology-sector sources, e.g. `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 29b) — flagged as a genuine access gap, not treated as evidence either way.

**Genuinely unconfirmed / not published:**
- No evidence was found anywhere (Sonic Healthcare's own quality-assurance pages, Clinipath's public site, or general search) of a **Sonic-Healthcare-groupwide collection-centre-specific quality standard** that Clinipath's WA collection sites are required to follow, distinct from each division's own laboratory-level accreditation. The accreditations found (CLIA/CAP, ISO 9001, ISO 15189) all describe laboratory or corporate quality systems, not a published collection-centre siting/operations standard analogous to WDP's LCC program. This is consistent with `docs/reed-partnerships.md` §1's own existing finding that Clinipath has "no documented Licensed Collection Centre-equivalent program." Nothing here should be read as ruling out that Sonic Healthcare has an internal, non-public collection-centre standard used for site assessments — only that no such document is published or discoverable.

### 2d. Australian Clinical Labs (ACL)

**Confirmed, with a real source:**
- ACL's own Quality Assurance page (`https://www.clinicallabs.com.au/about-us/quality-assurance/` / `https://www.clinicallabs.com.au/quality-assurance`) confirms NATA accreditation to AS ISO 15189 (Medical Laboratories), AS ISO 17025 (General Requirements for Testing/Calibration Laboratories), and AS 4308 (drug-testing-specific), and states ACL "maintain[s] consistency across our network of laboratories and collection centres to ensure that the same service level is experienced at all facilities" — a general uniformity commitment, not a published collection-centre standard document.
- The same page cites the Commonwealth *Guidelines for Approved Pathology Collection Centres (Third Edition 2013)* directly by name as "the Standard for Approved Pathology Collection Centres" governing minimum requirements for specimen collection nationally — i.e. ACL's own public messaging treats the universal NPAAC baseline (Section 1 above) as the governing collection-centre standard, without describing any ACL-specific addition to it.

**Genuinely unconfirmed / not published:**
- No dedicated ACL "partner with us" or "host a collection centre" program page, checklist, or quality manual was found anywhere in this session's search — consistent with `docs/reed-partnerships.md` §1c's own existing finding that ACL has "no dedicated public 'partner with us'/host-a-collection-centre program page." ACL's own quality-assurance page, if anything, points back to the universal NPAAC standard rather than describing any ACL-specific overlay.

## 3. Honest Summary — What Is Genuinely New Here vs What Was Already Known

The task asked to check whether each of the 4 providers has published its own detailed collection-centre standards document beyond NPAAC. **The honest finding, checked directly this session for all 4: none of the 4 candidates has a publicly published, detailed, collection-centre-specific quality standard or site-requirements document beyond the universal NPAAC baseline and generic laboratory-level accreditations (NATA/ISO 15189/ISO 17025).** This is not a research gap — collection-centre partnerships in this industry appear to be negotiated individually, not published, which is exactly what this repository's own prior research already anticipated for PathWest, Clinipath, and ACL (`docs/reed-partnerships.md` §1/§1c). The one genuine, confirmed exception is **WDP's "remain at the collection centre" policy**, and that was learned through direct correspondence with WDP (Carole Rivers), not from a published document — no equivalent direct correspondence yet exists with PathWest, Clinipath, or ACL to know whether they have similar unpublished operational policies of their own.

## 4. Practical Comparison Table — What Can Be Prepared Now vs What Needs Direct Confirmation

| Requirement | Universal (NPAAC/Commonwealth) — applies regardless of partner | Provider-specific — varies, needs direct confirmation from whichever partner is chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Non-porous, cleanable flooring/surfaces | Yes — can design and fit out now | — |
| Secure supply storage, specimens not stored with food/drink/pharmaceuticals | Yes — can plan now | — |
| Furniture allowing seated/recumbent collection + collector work surface | Yes — can plan now | — |
| Current approval certificate displayed | Yes — administrative step once approved | — |
| Collection staff employed OR formally engaged/credentialed by the chosen APA | Yes, the "employed or engaged" test itself is universal | The *specifics* of how a partner will formally engage a GTT-employed phlebotomist under its own QMS (credentialing process, training sign-off, ongoing supervision) will differ by partner and is not yet confirmed by any of the 4 |
| Patient must remain at/in the collection centre for the full test duration | No — not an NPAAC/ACSQHC requirement (confirmed, items 29/29b) | **Confirmed WDP-specific policy** (Carole Rivers, in writing). Whether PathWest, Clinipath, or ACL apply an equivalent policy, and how strictly (WDP/PathWest: "remain seated"; Clinipath's own patient-facing GTT page says "stay at the collection centre," a slightly looser framing per `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 29c) is genuinely provider-dependent and would need direct confirmation once a partner is chosen |
| Detailed room-fit-out sign-off (chair count, room size, ventilation, wheelchair access, etc.) | The NPAAC baseline sets minimums only (furniture/seating, secure storage) | The detailed practical checklist GTT has so far (2 bleeding chairs, minimum room size, specific plumbing/ventilation items) comes from WDP's own correspondence, not a published WDP document, and has not been separately obtained from PathWest, Clinipath, or ACL — each partner would need to confirm its own room sign-off criteria before fit-out is finalised |
| Referring-doctor/GP-on-site siting requirement | No — confirmed not a Commonwealth requirement via 3 independent primary-source checks (`docs/architecture/WDP-PARTNERSHIP-ACTIVATION-PLAN.md` §2b-i) | WDP: confirmed NOT a requirement, referral-context/pricing-only. PathWest: raised a "clinic with doctors" alternative pathway, evidenced-but-unconfirmed to be PathWest's own preference rather than universal. Clinipath and ACL: not yet asked, unknown either way |
| Commercial/rental structure, minimum term, staffing-cost model | Not addressed by NPAAC at all — a purely commercial matter | Entirely provider-specific and only partially known (WDP: actively progressing, no figure yet; PathWest: not yet discussed; Clinipath/ACL: not yet discussed) |
| Tube/consumables/glucose-drink supply | Not addressed by NPAAC | Provider-specific: PathWest has confirmed it supplies tubes and biohazard bags at no cost but does not supply the glucose drink (`docs/pathwest-clinipath-outreach-2026-07-27.md` §4e-vi); WDP's and Clinipath's/ACL's equivalent positions are not yet confirmed |

**Bottom line for GTT Center Perth:** the physical premises design (flooring, surfaces, furniture provisioning, secure storage, basic hygiene/safety fit-out already detailed in `docs/pathology-collection-room.md`) and the staffing-engagement model (GTT employs, partner formally engages/credentials under its own QMS) can both be prepared against the universal NPAAC baseline now, with reasonable confidence they will hold regardless of which of the 4 candidates is ultimately chosen. What genuinely cannot be locked down until a specific partner is confirmed: the exact "remain at the centre" policy and its strictness, the detailed room sign-off checklist, the commercial/rental structure, and consumables-supply arrangements — none of these are published anywhere for any of the 4 candidates, and each would need to be confirmed directly once a partner is chosen, exactly as this repository's own existing WDP correspondence has already begun to do for WDP specifically.
