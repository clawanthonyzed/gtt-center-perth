**Prepared by:** Grace (Operations Manager)

**Purpose:** Internal research only. Compares (1) the universal baseline every Approved Collection Centre must meet regardless of which pathology partner GTT Center Perth ends up choosing, against (2) each of the 4 candidate partners' own additional collection-centre policies, where any exist and are actually published. **Do not conflate the two layers below.** A requirement is "universal" only if it comes from the Commonwealth NPAAC/ACSQHC standard or the Commonwealth Approved Collection Centre (ACC) approval process itself — those apply to any Approved Collection Centre, under any Approved Pathology Authority (APA), anywhere in Australia. A requirement is "provider-specific" if it is a policy a particular pathology company has chosen to layer on top of that baseline, and it only applies if GTT Center Perth ends up partnering with that specific provider.

## 1. The Universal Baseline (already established in this repository — summarised, not re-derived)

**Source:** NPAAC's *Guidelines for Approved Pathology Collection Centres (Requirements for Medical Pathology Specimen Collection), Third Edition 2013*, NPAAC Tier 3B, now administered by the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care (ACSQHC) since 1 July 2021. Full document text was obtained and reviewed directly in a prior session — see `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` items 29 and 29b, and `docs/architecture/WDP-PARTNERSHIP-ACTIVATION-PLAN.md` §2b-i for the companion Commonwealth Approved Collection Centre (ACC) eligibility-criteria research (Services Australia/ABLIS). This applies to any Approved Collection Centre "connected to an Approved Pathology Authority" — a broad premises category, not exclusive to any one provider or to GP practices.

This baseline would need to be met **regardless of which of the 4 candidates GTT ultimately partners with.**

### 1a. Premises requirements (confirmed, already researched)
- Non-porous, easily cleanable flooring and surfaces suitable for a clinical collection procedure.
- Secure storage area for supplies (NPAAC C1.1(iv), broader than just centrifuge-adjacent storage).
- Adequate space for furniture to allow patients to be seated or recumbent according to medical requirements, plus a work surface for collection staff (NPAAC C1.1(ix)).
- Specimens must not be stored with food, drink, or pharmaceuticals.
- The centre's current approval certificate must be displayed.
- (This repository's own `docs/pathology-collection-room.md` and the floor-plan cross-check at `docs/VERIFICATION-TRACKER.md` item 30 build a fuller practical fit-out spec — non-slip coved flooring, hand hygiene station, lighting, ventilation, sharps disposal, etc. — on top of this NPAAC baseline; those are this venture's own design decisions for meeting the baseline, not additional NPAAC clauses in themselves, so they are not repeated in full here.)

### 1b. Staffing requirement (confirmed, already researched)
- The centre must be **"staffed by people employed or engaged by the APA"** (Approved Pathology Authority — i.e. the pathology provider itself, whichever of the 4 candidates is chosen). This is a Commonwealth ACC eligibility criterion (Services Australia/ABLIS), not something any one provider invented.
- The word "or" matters: a person can satisfy this either by being the partner's own employee, or by being formally engaged/credentialed by the partner under its own accreditation, quality management system (QMS), and standard operating procedures — even if that person's actual employer (for payroll/HR purposes) is GTT. This is the standard structure the collection-centre industry already uses nationally, and this repository's own existing documents (`docs/pathology-partnership-brief.md` §5, `docs/team-startup.md`) already describe GTT's own phlebotomists operating exactly this way under a partner's QMS.
- What is **not** sufficient under this Commonwealth test: a collector with no formal employment or engagement relationship to any APA at all.

### 1c. What this means practically
Any of the 4 candidates, once chosen, will require GTT's physical premises and staffing arrangement to satisfy 1a and 1b above — this is not a point of negotiation with any individual provider, it is the Commonwealth floor everyone operates on. GTT can prepare its physical fit-out, staffing/engagement structure, and documentation against this baseline **now**, before a partner is confirmed, with reasonable confidence it will still be relevant no matter which partner is eventually chosen.

### 5a. What the NPAAC framework itself requires — checked directly, full text obtained

**Access note:** the exact URL Anthony gave (`.../2022-08/tier_3b_requirements_for_medical_pathology_specimen_collection_third_edition_2013.pdf`) was confirmed blocked in this session too — it returns a 2.2KB Akamai bot-challenge page, not the PDF, identical to what was reported. **However, the ACSQHC's own resource page for the same document (`https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/resources/guidelines-approved-pathology-collection-centres-requirements-medical-pathology-specimen-collection-third-edition-2013`) loads normally and links to a current file path** (`.../2026-04/tier_3b_requirements_for_medical_pathology_specimen_collection_third_edition_2013.pdf`) which downloaded cleanly as a genuine 20-page PDF. The 2022-08 path Anthony used appears to be a stale/superseded file path that now trips the bot protection; the document itself is not actually inaccessible. Full text of both the Tier 3B document (collection centres) and the companion Tier 2 document (*Requirements for Medical Pathology Services*, which Tier 3B explicitly says must be read alongside it for personnel requirements) were obtained and read in full this session.

**Finding: NPAAC's own text is deliberately generic on personnel qualifications — it does not name a specific qualification, course, or credentialing process anywhere.**

- Tier 3B (collection centres), Section 3 "Personnel," in full: staff must wear ID; wear attire "in accordance with the APA's policies" (i.e., left to the pathology company); there must be staff-accessible policies on customer service, privacy, confidentiality and informed consent; staff "must be trained to ensure knowledge of basic first aid"; staff must be able to direct patients to their results. That is the entire personnel section. No qualification, certificate, or named training course is mentioned.
- Tier 2 (overarching document), Standard 6.1: "There must be sufficient medical, scientific, technical and support staff who have the qualifications, training and competence to provide Medical Pathology Services consistent with the scope of accreditation," plus generic commentary on continuing professional development and peer review. This is the closest NPAAC comes to a personnel-competency standard, and it is entirely generic — it does not define what "qualifications, training and competence" means for a collector specifically.
- Searched the full text of both documents directly for the terms "phlebotomist," "collector," "Certificate III," "HLT3" (the VET training-package prefix), and "credential" — **none of these terms appear anywhere in either NPAAC document.** NPAAC sets the *outcome* (competent, qualified, trained staff) and deliberately leaves the *specific standard* to each Approved Pathology Authority's own quality management system, referencing the general laboratory standard AS ISO 15189 rather than a collector-specific competency framework.

**What this means:** "credentialed by an APA" is not a reference to a single, government-defined NPAAC credentialing scheme. NPAAC does not publish one. It is shorthand for "meets whatever competency standard the specific pathology company applies under its own quality system" — which is exactly why none of the 4 candidate providers has a public credentialing checklist (Section 2 above already found this for collection-centre premises standards generally; it holds equally for staff).

### 5b. Base training qualification — genuine national standard, not invented, but recently superseded

- The nationally recognised entry qualification is **Certificate III in Pathology Collection**, VET course code **HLT37215**. Confirmed via training.gov.au (the Commonwealth's own national training register) and multiple RTO providers (Holmesglen, TAFE NSW, North Metropolitan TAFE, IHNA, APC).
- **HLT37215 was marked "Superseded" on training.gov.au as of 21 December 2025.** It has been replaced by **HLT37525 – Certificate III in Pathology**, which merged HLT37215 (Pathology Collection) and HLT37415 (Pathology Assistance) into one qualification. GTT's own documents (`docs/pathology-partnership-brief.md`, `docs/team-startup.md`, `docs/phlebotomist-job-posting.md`) all still cite HLT37215 as current — accurate as the well-established, widely-recognised qualification, but the code itself is now superseded and should be updated to HLT37525 (or noted as "HLT37215, superseded Dec 2025 by HLT37525") the next time those documents are revised. Not fixed in this pass — flagged for whoever owns those files.
- **Genuine, separate, pre-existing error found in this research, not previously flagged:** two GTT documents cite incorrect codes for this same qualification. `docs/team-startup.md` HIRE 7 cites "Certificate III in Pathology Collection (HLT33015)" — **HLT33015 is actually "Certificate III in Allied Health Assistance," an unrelated general allied-health qualification, confirmed via training.gov.au.** The same entry also cites "Certificate IV in Pathology Collection (HLT37215)" for a "Person In Charge" role — **HLT37215 is a Certificate III, not a Certificate IV, and no "Certificate IV in Pathology Collection" qualification exists in the Australian VET system.** `docs/pathology-partnership-brief.md` §5 makes the same "Certificate III or IV Pathology (HLT37215 / HLT47015)" error — **HLT47015 is "Certificate IV in Sterilisation Services," also unrelated to pathology collection, confirmed via training.gov.au.** Pathology collection training in Australia tops out at Certificate III (now HLT37525) — there is no higher-level "Certificate IV in Pathology Collection." Recommend correcting all three references; not corrected in this pass since it touches documents this task was not scoped to edit.
- **Is it a genuine prerequisite, or do some providers train in-house from a lower base?** Genuinely both, confirmed with a concrete WA example (5c below) rather than assumed. The qualification has no formal prerequisite course or prior experience requirement — providers explicitly market it to "unqualified workers," "school leavers," and people "returning to the workforce." Entry generally requires: minimum age (17, with 18 required for clinical placement), basic English/communication skills, and — per one training provider's published entry requirements — a National Police Check, Working with Children Check, and immunisation records **as part of enrolling in the course itself**, not as a separate post-qualification employer step.
- Course structure (confirmed via the current national register unit list and a real PathWest delivery brochure, 5c below): core units cover infection control, clinical-risk recognition and first-aid response, venous blood collection, non-blood specimen collection, workplace health and safety, customer service, and medical terminology, with capillary blood collection as a common elective. Assessment combines e-learning, workbook activities, simulated practical assessment, and **supervised practical observation in a real clinical/collection environment** — typically 35-88 hours of supervised placement plus a minimum count of successfully observed collections (one WA provider requires 200 supervised bleeds; a national skills-assessment guideline cited in search results elsewhere requires a minimum of 20 peer-observed collections). Total minimum volume of learning is roughly 250 hours.

### 5c. Concrete WA-specific finding: PathWest is itself a Registered Training Organisation that trains from scratch

This is the most concrete, provider-specific evidence found in this research, and it is directly relevant to GTT's model.

**Confirmed via a real PathWest-published document** (`HLT37215-Cert-III-Pathology-Collection-Brochure-v1-2022-(002).pdf`, published on health.wa.gov.au, "Presented by PathWest Laboratory Medicine WA in partnership with Learning and Development, Workforce, North Metropolitan Health Service, RTO 50379"):

- **PathWest itself holds RTO status (RTO 50379)** and runs its own 6-week, ~248-hour in-house Certificate III in Pathology Collection program, delivered on-site at Royal Perth Hospital, Fiona Stanley Hospital, and Princess Margaret Hospital/Perth Children's Hospital.
- The program's own stated target participants explicitly include "unqualified workers wanting to pursue a career in the health field," "school leavers," and "unemployed people seeking an entry-level qualification" — i.e., **PathWest does not require a candidate to already hold any pathology qualification before starting; PathWest trains people from zero.**
- The practical assessment schedule (Week 3 of the 6-week program) is literally titled **"Assessment Practical Observation (Glucose Tolerance Test)"** — GTT collection technique specifically is a standard, named part of PathWest's own training curriculum, not a specialised add-on.
- Final competency sign-off requires a self-assessed record of 200 successful supervised bleeds within the course, plus third-party workplace reports, in addition to theory and practical exams.

**What this genuinely confirms and what it does not:** this document shows PathWest *can and does* train collectors from an unqualified base through its own accreditation pipeline — a real, documented in-house training pathway, not an assumption. **What it does not confirm** is whether PathWest would run this exact program for a GTT-employed staff member working at a private, non-PathWest venue, versus requiring GTT to hire someone who already holds the qualification and putting that person through a separate, shorter competency-verification/sign-off process instead. The brochure describes training for PathWest/NMHS/SMHS staff and the general public seeking entry to the PathWest system — it is not evidence of PathWest's process for credentialing an externally-employed collector at a partner venue. That distinction is genuinely unconfirmed and would need to be asked directly in partnership negotiation.

### 5e. What a credentialing process reasonably looks like in practice — labelled clearly as informed inference, not provider-confirmed

Based on (i) NPAAC's own generic "qualifications, training and competence" language, (ii) the way the Certificate III itself is assessed (supervised practical observation, workplace third-party reports, a minimum successful-collection count), and (iii) the one confirmed example of a provider-specific overlay found anywhere in this research (WDP's "remain at the collection centre" policy, layered on top of the NPAAC baseline, per Section 2a) — a pathology company's own sign-off process for a GTT-employed collector would plausibly involve some combination of:

- Verification of the base Certificate III (or equivalent) qualification and its currency.
- A practical competency assessment or observed-collection sign-off against the company's own procedures, separate from the original course assessment.
- A defined supervised-practice period before working unsupervised.
- Provision of a current National Police Check, Working with Children Check, and immunisation records (already a Certificate III course-entry requirement per 5b, so a candidate who has completed the course would already hold these — carrying them over to a specific employer's onboarding is a reasonable but unconfirmed assumption).
- Formal agreement to follow the company's own collection SOPs — the same category of company-specific rule as WDP's confirmed "remain at the collection centre" policy.

**This list must not be presented to any partner, or treated internally, as a confirmed WDP/PathWest/Clinipath/ACL requirement — it is a reasonable, evidence-informed expectation of what this kind of process typically involves, not something any of the 4 candidates has actually published or confirmed.** The only way to get the real, provider-specific version is to ask directly once a partner relationship is far enough along — the same conclusion Section 3 already reached for premises standards.

### 5f. Practical Output — What Can Be Prepared Now vs What Genuinely Cannot Be Known Yet

**Can prepare now, regardless of which partner is chosen:**
