## 1. Who GTT Center Perth Is — For the Supplier

GTT Center Perth is Western Australia's first premium waiting experience built around the Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT), a mandatory 2-3 hour pregnancy blood-screening test. Instead of a bare waiting room, the client spends her 2-3 hours in a genuine premium wellness destination — massage, nails, hair, beauty — while three timed blood draws happen around her, delivered by a pathology partner's phlebotomists inside one dedicated clinical room. In the afternoon, the identical physical venue operates as a standalone premium women's wellness centre (PM), independent of the morning clinical service, using the same rooms and staff.

**Operating window (current, A — CONFIRMED):** AM 07:00-13:00 (GTT + wellness/beauty, every AM service ≤45 minutes, an absolute scheduling rule, not a room-size constraint), PM 13:00-18:00 (premium wellness/beauty, no duration ceiling, 30-120+ minutes). **Older references to a 12:00-12:30 "soft transition" window are superseded — do not use.**

**Design daily target (A):** 18 GTT clients/day (12/day is a downside/sensitivity reference only, not the design target). Two synchronised phlebotomy chairs, 25-minute pair cadence, zero collision-verified.

**What must never be true of the finished space:** it must not read as a medical centre, a generic hair salon, a nail bar, a day spa, or a shopping-centre beauty shop. It must read as one unified premium destination in which only one room (the Blood Collection Room) is permitted to look and feel clinical.

**Business model note for the supplier's own understanding (not a design requirement):** the pathology partner (WDP or equivalent) supplies the accreditation umbrella; beauty/wellness practitioners are GTT Center Perth's own employed staff, not subtenants. No NATA accreditation is required of the venue itself.

## 2. Brand and Design Direction

### 2.1 Positioning

Premium, not exclusionary-luxury. Warm, calm, natural, earthy, sophisticated, feminine without being childish, elevated rather than clinical, boutique rather than hospital, hospitality-quality rather than transactional. Contemporary European/luxury residential influence, not a spa-cliché or a maternity-brand softness. The governing design idea, independently confirmed across this venture's own brand work: **restraint is the luxury signal, not decoration.** One or two genuinely good, consistently-executed material choices outperform many mediocre ones.

**Explicitly not:** a medical centre, a generic hair salon, a nail bar, a day spa, a shopping-centre beauty shop, a boho/plant-filled wellness aesthetic, clinical-white minimalism, cliché-luxury (excess gold/marble), or anything trend-locked that will date within a few years.

### 2.2 Name — Not Yet Decided, Do Not Design Around Either

**SOLENA and ELOWEN both remain open (A, `docs/DECISION-LOG.md` 2026-08-14).** SOLENA is the current conditional weighted leader (71.6% vs 68.8%), not locked — trademark clearance is deliberately deferred. **Do not ask the supplier to design a logo, wordmark, or signage around either name at this stage.** Any signage/wayfinding concept work should use a neutral placeholder (e.g. "[BRAND]") until the name is locked.

### 2.3 Locked Colour Palette (A — CONFIRMED, LOCKED 2026-08-15)

Source: `outputs/brand/warm-stone-tokens.css`. **Do not substitute, lighten, darken, or "improve" these values** — they are locked exactly as given.

|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant background | Warm Ivory | `#FAF6EE` | ~55-60% of any surface — walls, large panels |
| Dominant secondary surface | Warm Stone | `#E8DAC5` | Cards, secondary panels, joinery field colour |
| Primary dark / text | Deep Brown | `#33261E` | Never pure black — ink, fine detail, primary dark surfaces |

**Approximately 85-90% of any surface should be Warm Ivory/Warm Stone/Deep Brown; the four accent colours together should occupy a genuine minority share.**

### 2.4 Typography (A — CONFIRMED)

Primary serif: **Fraunces** (wordmark, headings, signage — chosen for its dedicated small-size-legible "text" optical cut, not just a display face). Secondary sans: **DM Sans** (body copy, UI, functional signage). No third typeface.

### 2.5 Material Language

| Category | Preferred | Acceptable alternative | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Limestone, honed travertine | Engineered stone with a genuine honed-limestone visual match | Heavily veined polished marble (cliché-luxury); raw/split-face stone (rustic) |
| Timber | A single considered species, warm mid-tone (American oak, warm walnut), used consistently | Convincing timber-look laminate only where budget genuinely requires it, never mixed with real timber in the same sightline | Reclaimed/distressed timber; pale bleached "Scandi" timber |
| Wall finish | Textured plaster/render in a warm neutral | High-quality matte paint with genuine depth | Flat builder-grade paint; exposed raw concrete |
| Textiles | Natural fibres — linen, wool, cotton bouclé | High-quality natural-look synthetic blend where genuine natural fibre isn't durable enough | Saturated jewel-tone velvet; anything glossy/synthetic-obvious |
| Metal/hardware | Brushed brass or bronze, warm-toned | Warm-toned brushed bronze-look powder coat where solid metal isn't budget-feasible | Chrome, polished nickel, brushed steel (all read cold/clinical) |
| Glass | Reeded/fluted glass for privacy screening | Clear glass, warm-toned frame | Frosted acrylic (reads budget/clinical) |
| Greenery | One well-placed specimen plant per zone | A small number of genuinely well-chosen plants | Distributed generic planters everywhere |

**Finish level:** refined-matte throughout — nothing high-gloss, nothing raw/unfinished. **Joinery:** clean, confident, minimal-detail lines — restrained architecture, not ornate cabinetry.

### 2.6 Lighting Philosophy (see §14 for the zone-by-zone schedule)

Calm luxury is **not** dim spa lighting. The venue should read bright enough to feel confident and clean, warm enough to feel calm — not dark enough to feel hushed. Warm colour temperature (~2700-3000K) throughout, except the Blood Collection Room, which should sit slightly cooler/neutral (~3000-3500K) for genuine clinical task accuracy without tipping into cold blue-white.

### 2.7 Only One Room Is Permitted To Look Clinical

Per `docs/strategy/BRAND-ARCHITECTURE.md`'s "named experience, not department" principle: the Blood Collection Room is the only space where genuinely clinical fittings (elbow taps, sharps disposal, medical fridge, clinical-grade lighting) belong. Everywhere else, medical credibility is communicated through competence and process (on-time draws, credentialed staff, a clear itinerary), never through visible medical signalling.

## 4. Blood Collection Room — Clinical Requirements

**This is a clinical specification, not a furniture specification.** No Chinese furniture supplier is the authority on Australian clinical compliance — every dimension/standard below is either sourced from this repository's existing WDP-informed research (`docs/pathology-collection-room.md`, cross-checked against Carole Rivers' (WDP) 2026-08-06 room-spec reply per `docs/floor-plan-concept.md`'s changelog) or explicitly marked for Australian professional verification.

### 4.1 Room Purpose and Privacy

- Dedicated enclosed room, solid door (not a curtain), not an open bay (A).
- Located adjacent to the Lounge/reception, reached without passing through treatment rooms (A).
- Soundproofed — Collection Room must not allow sound to pass to adjacent spaces (A, medical privacy).
- Signage: plain-language ("Pathology Collection — By Appointment"), avoiding alarming clinical language (A).
- Companion/support-person access to the room itself: **NOT decided — genuine open founder item.** Current policy excludes companions/children from the room, with one disclosed, unresolved exception under consideration (a support person for a specific clinical reason, e.g. needle phobia) — see §29.

### 4.2 Dimensions

- Minimum floor area: 9sqm (3m x 3m) per room (A, `pathology-collection-room.md`).
- Recommended: 12-14sqm to allow wheelchair manoeuvrability and multiple bays (A).
- Currently-costed single-room target (2-chair model): 18sqm, plus an additional ~2-3sqm allowance for the vasovagal recliner/couch not yet folded into that figure (A, `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` changelog 2026-08-07).
- Door: minimum 850mm clear width (A, DDA/wheelchair access reference figure from the 2026-06-09 creation guide) — **exact compliant width per AS 1428.1: AUSTRALIAN ACCESSIBILITY / NCC VERIFICATION REQUIRED.**
- Internal turning circle / wheelchair positioning within the room itself: **AUSTRALIAN ACCESSIBILITY VERIFICATION REQUIRED** — flagged as a real, previously-identified gap (2026-08-06), not yet resolved anywhere in this repository.

### 4.3 Construction / Finishes

- Flooring: commercial-grade vinyl or tile, non-slip, seamless (no grout lines to trap blood), chemically resistant, coved skirting (A).
- No carpet (A — cannot be adequately decontaminated after a blood spill).
- Walls: light-coloured, washable, moisture-resistant, semi-gloss minimum, no soft furnishings/fabric panels/artwork that can't be wiped down (A) — **but see §2.7: the wall tone should still sit within the venue's warm-neutral palette where clinical requirements allow, not a jarring cold-white break from the rest of the building (C, `docs/strategy/VISUAL-BRAND-DIRECTION.md` §7).**
- Ceiling: smooth, wipeable, no exposed air-conditioning vents directly above the collection area (A, aerosol risk).
- Impervious bench/floor surface: **flagged as a real gap in the existing repository documents** (specified elsewhere for Facial/Beauty/Nail areas, not yet explicitly restated for the Blood Collection Room specifically) — treat as A by clinical logic (venepuncture, specimen handling), formal confirmation: **AUSTRALIAN CLINICAL/BUILDING VERIFICATION REQUIRED.**

### 4.4 Lighting

- Minimum 400 lux at the work surface; recommended 600-800 lux, LED, no shadows (A, `pathology-collection-room.md`).
- Colour rendering: the more recent gap-audit (`floor-plan-concept.md`, 2026-08-06) specifies **Ra >90** at the phlebotomy chairs specifically — this supersedes the earlier document's CRI >85 figure; both point in the same direction (true colour rendering for patient assessment), Ra >90 is the current, more conservative figure to design to.
- Colour temperature: ~3000-3500K, slightly cooler/neutral than the rest of the venue, without tipping into cold blue-white (C, `docs/strategy/VISUAL-BRAND-DIRECTION.md` §8).
- Adjustable task lighting over each phlebotomy chair preferred (A).

### 4.5 Ventilation

- Minimum 6 air changes per hour (A, `pathology-collection-room.md`).
- Must not recirculate air through shared HVAC without HEPA filtration; separate exhaust fan, ducted to outside, strongly recommended (A).
- Dedicated air-conditioning unit acceptable, not a shared return-air system (A).
- General room ventilation was flagged as a genuine gap in `floor-plan-concept.md`'s own 2026-08-06 audit (only the Nail Station's LEV extraction was previously specified elsewhere) — treat the figures above as the current best answer; final spec: **AUSTRALIAN MECHANICAL/HVAC VERIFICATION REQUIRED.**

### 4.6 Hand Hygiene and Clinical Surfaces

- Hand hygiene station at room entry, non-negotiable per NSQHS Standards (A).
- Basin with elbow-operated or sensor taps — hands-free operation required (A).
- Liquid soap dispenser (wall-mounted), paper towel dispenser (not cloth), ABHR wall-mounted dispenser separate from soap (A).
- Splashback: stainless steel or fully sealed tile (A).
- Centrifuge bench: vibration-resistant, minimum 600mm deep, lockable under-bench storage (A).

### 4.7 Power, Data, Storage

- Minimum 4 power outlets per room, all at bench height (not floor level, wet clinical environment) — phlebotomy-chair lighting, centrifuge, label printer, device charging (A).
- Data point (RJ45 or WiFi access point) for electronic patient identification (A).
- General supply-storage cupboard beyond the centrifuge under-bench provision — NPAAC's own premises standard calls for a broader "secure storage area for supplies," not yet confirmed as separately provided: **PARTIAL, flagged not resolved.**

### 4.8 Sharps, Waste, Spill Management

- Sharps containers: AS/NZS 23907:2023 compliant, wall-mounted at elbow height (A).
- Biohazard waste bin, labelled and lined, separate from sharps (A).
- Medical waste disposal contract required before opening — **whether WDP's own collection-centre arrangement already covers this is a genuine open dependency, not yet answered (D, see §27/WDP follow-up items).**
- Spill kit (absorbent granules, TGA-listed disinfectant, heavy-duty waste bag, gloves) stored in the room (A).

### 4.9 Emergency, Comfort, Accessibility

- Emergency call button, wall-mounted, connected to reception (A).
- First aid kit accessible within 30 seconds (A).
- **Vasovagal reclining chair or examination couch, separate from the 2 phlebotomy chairs** — a genuine 2026-08-06 gap finding, now added as a real costed line (A$500-900, `docs/CURRENT-STATE.md` changelog 2026-08-07), spec not yet locked (see §5.4).
- Room-level wheelchair accessibility (doorway width, turning circle, chair positioning): **AUSTRALIAN ACCESSIBILITY VERIFICATION REQUIRED**, distinct from building-level provisions (accessible WC, low reception counter) already assumed elsewhere.

### 4.10 Sightlines / Companion Presence During a Draw

The venue's current companion policy excludes companions from the room during a draw, with one disclosed and genuinely unresolved exception (a support person for a specific clinical need, e.g. needle phobia) — not defaulted either way, see §29. This affects whether the room needs any allowance for a second person's presence/seating; not designed for here, pending that decision.

**UPDATE 2026-08-21 (Round 2) — this classification has since been stress-tested, not just repeated, against real sourcing-agent and inspection-industry research.** `docs/architecture/CHINA-AUSTRALIA-SOURCING-STRATEGY.md` §6 re-examined every category below against that research; no category moved between tiers, but Lounge seating's Category C placement was specifically re-confirmed (not weakened) rather than assumed, and a genuine procurement-model recommendation (a Hybrid model — an Australian-based sourcing agent for Category A/B items, independent third-party pre-shipment inspection regardless of route, Australian sourcing unchanged for Category C) is now available at that document's §5. ### Category A — Good Candidates for China OEM/ODM

Custom joinery components, nail tables/tool trolleys, pedicure chair frames (with a fabric/upholstery caveat, §21 note below), reception counter structural components (with local custom-top fabrication), café counter structural components, retail cabinetry, mirrors, styling-chair frames, backwash-basin hardware (non-electrical components), decorative fixtures, signage substrate/hardware (not the final wordmark artwork, gated on the name decision), non-electrical ancillary/side-table furniture, flat-pack commercial storage/cabinetry, curtain track hardware (**not the fabric itself — see Category C**), décor and small display items.

### Category B — Possible China Sourcing, Requires Australian Verification

### Category C — Prefer Australian/Locally Certified Procurement

**Purpose restated:** this is procurement risk management, not a categorical legal claim about what China-sourced goods can or cannot legally be sold in Australia. **The distinguishing principle throughout, per this venture's own prior research: the risk with China/Asia sourcing is verification of a genuine, checkable certification/test report — not the country of origin itself.**
