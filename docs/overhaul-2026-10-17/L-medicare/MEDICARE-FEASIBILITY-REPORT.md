**Headline finding on the "employed or engaged" challenge.** Anthony was right that the two texts differ, but the discrepancy does not help as much as hoped. Collection-centre approval (Principles 2020 s9(3)(b)) accepts staff "employed by the APA" or "engaged directly or indirectly by the APA under a contract that gives the APA power to generally direct or control the work". The Medicare payment rule (Health Insurance Act s16A(5AB)(c), current compilation in force from 19 September 2026) says "an employee of an approved pathology authority". So an engaged contractor can staff an approved centre, but the Act's wording for when Medicare pays does not mention contractors. The earlier finding from a 2014 compilation still stands in the current text. Whether a tightly controlled contractor counts as "employee" is a legal question for the Department of Health and a health lawyer. Do not spend fit-out money on the assumption that it does.

## 2. What the law requires, in order (A3a, A3b)

Who applies: the APA, not the premises and not GTT (Act s23DNBA(1); Principles s7(1); Services Australia: "be owned by a current Approved Pathology Authority (APA) which owns a Category S, GX or GY Accredited Pathology Laboratory"). Criteria (exact sources in PRIMARY-SOURCES.md):

1. The applicant is a current APA that operates and is the proprietor of a category G (GX or GY) laboratory, or a category S laboratory collecting only for its approved test kinds.
2. The collection centre is on premises the APA owns, leases, sub-leases or is otherwise entitled to occupy. A non-financial arrangement needs "a signed agreement with the owner of the premises" with "the standard clauses and conditions expected in a standard lease".
3. Equipment for collection and preparation of specimens is on the premises (Principles s9(2)).
4. Staff include persons trained in collection and are employed by the APA, or engaged under a contract that lets the APA generally direct or control their work (Principles s9(3)).
5. The APA gives a written undertaking to comply with the NPAAC collection guidelines and to notify the Chief Executive Medicare within 24 hours of any failure (Principles s8(2)(d)).
6. A$2,000 tax for a 2-year approval is paid before the grant (Tax Act s5), unless the centre sits with the lab's own GX or GY laboratory (not GTT's case).
7. Supporting documents: lease or agreement naming the APA with address and area, and a floor plan with the collection room marked in colour or hash marks, and shared and paid areas equal to the lease area. Submit no more than 3 months before the proposed start date.
8. Lodgement: through Pathology Registration in Health Professional Online Services (HPOS), or the paper "Application for approval, amendment, renewal or cancellation of an approved collection centre" form. One application per location.
9. Inspection: a person authorised by the Minister may enter and inspect premises to be used as a centre once an application is made (s23DNJ). The approval then lasts 2 years.
10. Keeping it: the approval notice must be displayed; the centre must stay "reasonably accessible to the public" and under "reasonable hours" (s23DNG(1)(ba),(bb)), and must meet a minimum six-month benefit threshold set by regulation (amount TO CONFIRM).

A3b answer: the lab does not have to "inspect and approve" in a named step in the statute, but it signs the undertaking and carries the consequences, so every lab will inspect and approve the room against the NPAAC guidelines before applying. NPAAC S1.1 to S6.1 cover premises law compliance, displayed approval certificate, no public access through laboratory areas, cleanable non-porous surfaces, space for patients to sit or recline plus a staff work surface, secure supply storage, dedicated and secure specimen storage with fridge at 2 to 8 degrees Celsius and no food, drink or pharmaceuticals alongside, staff identification, privacy, consent and first-aid-trained staff, and hand hygiene. These support the F workstream decision for specimen fridges inside each collection room, provided the fridge is secure and kept for specimens only. No minimum room area is stated in the guidelines (so the 17.6 versus 19.4 square metre question must be decided on the lab's advice, TO CONFIRM).

## 5. Could GTT form a subsidiary that becomes an APA? (A2a, A2b)

**Verdict: not feasible for GTT's opening plan; feasible only as a long-term, capital-heavy venture. Use a partner lab.**

What the law requires (all in PRIMARY-SOURCES.md):
- An APA is a person (company) whose undertaking the Minister has accepted (s23DF). Fit and proper checks cover directors, shareholders, officers and associates. The undertaking is accepted for periods up to 12 months. A company can be an APA by itself.
- But an APA only matters for collection if it can hold an ACC, and for that it must be the proprietor of an eligible laboratory (category GX, GY or S). A "collection-only" company cannot hold an ACC. It also cannot make Medicare payable on its own, because the service must be rendered in an accredited pathology laboratory (s16A(2)(b)) by or on behalf of an approved pathology practitioner (an APP, a medical practitioner approved by the Minister).
- A category GX laboratory needs "full-time onsite direction and control of a designated person, who is a pathologist" and full-time supervision of a pathologist (APL Approval Principles 2017 s17). Category S has a narrower scope: a limited range of testing under a medical practitioner with specialised scope, for a target patient population or specialised nature. Gestational glucose testing is routine general chemistry, and whether a category S licence could be granted for it is TO CONFIRM; I found no basis for it. Category M is for a medical practice's own patients only.
- One entity holds one APA approval and the Department asks corporate groups to consolidate (undertaking s16). A GTT subsidiary APA would need its own separate approval, not GTT's.

**A2b: employing or contracting a supervising pathologist.** The law requires a pathologist as designated person for a GX laboratory and as the approved pathology practitioner who renders the service. A pathologist can be an employee or contractor of the APA (undertaking s9 and s13 require a written agreement with the APA). A pathologist alone does not create an APA or a laboratory; the laboratory premises, accreditation and Medicare approval must exist first. Engaging a pathologist is only useful as part of an existing lab.

**The lawful lighter alternative:** contract with an existing APA (PathWest, WDP or Clinipath). GTT supplies the room, the lounge and the booking engine; the lab holds the ACC and the Medicare relationship.

Decision tree:
1. Will a partner lab hold the ACC at our rooms and supply its employed collectors? Yes: workaround 1, model Case 1, lab fee zero. No: go to 2.
2. Will the lab accept GTT-supplied collectors under a direction-and-control contract AND confirm in writing (backed by the Department of Health) that Medicare pays? Yes: workaround 3. No: go to 3.

4. Can GTT absorb A$12,251.35 a month and still clear break-even at 10 clients? Yes: Case 3 (price A$275.85 restores the baseline). No: reconsider the package price.
Building a subsidiary laboratory sits outside this tree.

## 6. WA Department of Health and local approvals (A4)

What I read: the Private Hospitals and Health Services Act 1927 (WA), Reprint 8 (2016): licences are issued to private hospitals and private psychiatric hostels; "hospital" takes its meaning from the Health Services Act 2016 s8, which (by the search summary, not the primary text) includes day hospital facilities, nursing homes and nursing posts. A nursing post is "a place at which a nurse is stationed and at which facilities exist for medical attention but which is not normally used for the accommodation of in-patients". Risk to flag: if GTT staffs the venue with a registered nurse and offers "facilities for medical attention", a regulator could argue it is a nursing post. Keep the clinical role to lab collectors and first aid, and ask LARU in writing. The day hospital facility determination lists anaesthetic, sedation, invasive and dialysis procedures, none of which GTT performs.

What I could not read (marked TO CONFIRM): the WA Department of Health licensing page (HTTP 503 on 2026-10-17; the 2026-10-16 R note records it as listing no pathology collection category); the current compilation of the 1927 Act; the Health Services Act 2016 s8 primary text; the Public Health Act 2016 and its regulations; the Health (Public Buildings) Regulations 1992; the Health (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1911 (largely replaced; status TO CONFIRM); local government planning, health and building approvals; the National Construction Code class (a clinic or consulting-room use is commonly a different class from a beauty salon; TO CONFIRM with a building surveyor).

Answer to "can GTT obtain a site licence or room accreditation": no body issues one for a private blood collection room that I could find. What exists is: (1) Commonwealth approved collection centre (held by the lab); (2) NATA and NPAAC accreditation (lab, includes its collection centres); (3) local government planning approval or change of use, building permit or certificate of occupancy for the class of use, and any local health approval; (4) infection prevention and sharps arrangements (lab manual, NPAAC S5.2, WA clinical waste rules TO CONFIRM). Registered health professionals are separately regulated by AHPRA, not by site.
