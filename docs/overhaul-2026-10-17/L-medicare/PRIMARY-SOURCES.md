# Primary sources for Medicare, pathology and WA Health (workstream L)

This is general knowledge, not your personal documents. Verify with a qualified adviser before acting.

All fetched 2026-10-17 (AWST) unless stated. Legislation texts were pulled from the Federal Register of Legislation API (api.prod.legislation.gov.au), which serves the official current compilation. Quotes are exact. Nothing was sent to or lodged with any body.

Correction to the brief: in the current Act the approved collection centre rules are in Part IIA, Division 4A (s23DNBA onward), and the approved pathology authority rules in Part IIA, Division 2 (s23DF). Part IIBA is the "prohibited practices" Part (benefits to requesters), which matters for rent and staff arrangements.

## 1. Health Insurance Act 1973 (Cth), compilation 136, in force from 2026-09-19 (register ID C2026C00411)
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00101/latest/text

### s16A(5AA) and (5AB): who may collect (the "employee" wording)
> (5AA) A medicare benefit is not payable in respect of a pathology service that has been rendered in relation to a person by or on behalf of an approved pathology practitioner ... unless the pathology specimen required for the rendering of the service:
> (c) was collected from the person: (i) by the person himself or herself; or (ii) by the treating practitioner; or (iii) on behalf of the treating practitioner, by an employee of, or by a person engaged under a contract for services by or on behalf of, the treating practitioner; or (iv) if the treating practitioner is employed, or engaged under a contract for services, by a medical entrepreneur ... by another employee of that medical entrepreneur, or by a person engaged under a contract for services by or on behalf of that medical entrepreneur; or
> (d) was collected from the person by a person to whom this paragraph applies at: (i) the place where the person was residing; or (ii) an approved collection centre (within the meaning of Part IIA); or (iii) premises of a recognised hospital ...; or (iv) a private hospital in which the person is a patient; or (v) a residential care home ... or other institution, in which the person is receiving care; or
> (e) was collected from the person by: (i) a member of the staff of a hospital in which the person is a patient; or (ii) a member of the staff of a residential care home ... or other institution, in which the person is receiving care.
>
> (5AB) Paragraph (5AA)(d) applies to: (a) the rendering pathologist; and (b) the referring pathologist (if any); and (c) an employee of an approved pathology authority that is the proprietor of the laboratory in which the service is to be rendered; and (d) an employee of an approved pathology authority from which the pathology specimen in question was referred to: (i) an approved pathology authority to which paragraph (c) applies; or (ii) an approved pathology practitioner who is to render the service in a laboratory of which such an approved pathology authority is the proprietor.

Reading: at an approved collection centre the collector must be the pathologist or "an employee" of the approved pathology authority (APA). The statute uses "employee" for the APA route. The words "engaged under a contract for services" appear only in the treating-practitioner route (c)(iii) and (iv), which shows Parliament knew how to write "engaged" and did not do so in (5AB). "Employee" is not defined in the Act (searched the full text; no definition). Subsection (5AB) exists. Whether a contractor controlled by the APA counts is a legal question: TO CONFIRM with the Department of Health and a health lawyer.

### s10(2): benefit percentage
> (a) ... as part of an episode of hospital treatment ... an amount equal to 75% of the Schedule fee; ... (b) in any other case-an amount equal to 85% of the Schedule fee.
Pathology is not among the 100 per cent items in Health Insurance Regulations 2018 reg 28 (checked: only general medical and diagnostic imaging items), so the out-of-hospital pathology benefit is 85 per cent (MBS Online agrees, section 4).

### s23DA definitions (Part IIA)
> eligible collection centre means a specimen collection centre on premises that are owned, leased or sub-leased by an approved pathology authority that is also the sole proprietor of at least one eligible pathology laboratory.
> specimen collection centre means a place for collecting pathology specimens from persons in relation to whom pathology services are to be rendered.
> approved pathology authority means a person in respect of whom there is in force an undertaking given by the person, and accepted by the Minister, under section 23DF. (section 3)
> proprietor ... in relation to a pathology laboratory-means the person or authority having effective control of: (i) the laboratory premises ...; and (ii) the use of equipment used in the laboratory; and (iii) the employment of staff in the laboratory. (section 3)

### s23DNBA: the approval belongs to the APA
> (1) The Minister may grant an approval to an approved pathology authority for an eligible collection centre conducted (or to be conducted): (a) on premises of which the authority is the owner, lessee or sub-lessee; or (b) on premises the authority is otherwise entitled to occupy.

### s23DNJ, s23DNK, s23DNL
> 23DNJ (1) A person authorised in writing by the Minister ... may at any reasonable time: (a) enter and inspect: (i) the premises occupied or to be occupied by a specimen collection centre in respect of which an application for an approval has been made ...
> 23DNK (1) The approved pathology authority operating an approved collection centre must ensure that at all times there is on display in a prominent place at the centre a notice that lets the public know that the centre is approved ...
> 23DNL A person who operates a specimen collection centre that is not an approved collection centre must cause to be taken such steps as are reasonable in all the circumstances to ensure that, before a pathology specimen is collected at the centre ..., that person ... and the approved pathology practitioner ... are informed that, if the approved pathology practitioner were to use the specimen in rendering a pathology service, a medicare benefit would not be payable in respect of the pathology service. Penalty: 20 penalty units.
Consequence for GTT: if GTT ever runs collection in a room that is not an approved collection centre, GTT itself must warn each client that no Medicare benefit is payable.

### s23DN, s23DF: laboratory approval and APA undertaking
> 23DN(2A) An approval ... of premises as an accredited pathology laboratory must specify: (a) the kind of pathology services ...; (b) the category of accreditation ...; and (c) the period (not exceeding 3 years) for which the approval is to have effect.
> 23DF(1) ... an undertaking ... is signed by or on behalf of a person (including a State, the Northern Territory or a public authority); and ... gives the undertaking to the Minister together with an application ...: (c) accept the undertaking ... and determine the period (being a period ending not later than 12 months after the day on which the undertaking comes into force) ...
> 23DF(6) The Minister shall not accept an undertaking ... unless the Minister is satisfied that the person is a fit and proper person to be an approved pathology authority.

### Part IIBA (prohibited practices): s23DZZIE, s23DZZIF
> 23DZZIE(1) ... a person is a requester of a kind of pathology service if the person is ...: (a) a practitioner, a participating midwife or a participating nurse practitioner; (b) a person who employs, or engages under a contract for services, a person specified in paragraph (a); (c) a person who exercises control or direction over a person specified in paragraph (a) ...
> 23DZZIF(7) However, the benefit is not a permitted benefit if: (a) the benefit is related to the number, kind or value of requests ... made by the requester; or (b) the benefit consists of the provision of staff or equipment at premises of the beneficiary for the purpose of providing pathology services ..., whether the staff or equipment are stationed at the premises full-time or part-time ...
> 23DZZIF(5) ... (b) the amount of the benefit is not substantially different from the market value ...; (c) if the benefit is a payment for the use or occupation, by a provider of a kind of pathology service, of premises ...: (i) an approved collection centre or an accredited pathology laboratory is established in the premises or space at the time, or within 60 days after, the arrangement ... is entered into; ... and the premises or space are not used or occupied under the arrangement for any other purpose.
Consequence: these limits bite only on a beneficiary who is, or is connected to, a "requester". GTT is not a requester unless it employs, engages or controls a doctor, midwife or nurse practitioner (a requester includes a person who "employs, or engages under a contract for services" one). Keep any doctor, midwife or nurse practitioner OFF GTT's payroll and contracts, or take advice. Also the space paid for must be used only for the lab's ACC, which affects a shared curtained room used for wellness.

## 2. Health Insurance (Approvals for Eligible Collection Centres) Principles 2020 (F2020L01179, in force since 2020-09-18)
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2020L01179/latest/text
> 7(1) The application must be made by an APA who is covered by one of subsections (2), (3) and (4).
> (2) This subsection covers an APA who operates, and is the proprietor of, an accredited pathology laboratory that is a category G pathology laboratory.
> (4) ... an APA who operates, and is the proprietor of, an accredited pathology laboratory that is a category S pathology laboratory that is proposing to collect only specimens for the kind of pathology services in respect of which the premises of the laboratory are approved ...
> 8(2) The application must include: (a) a response to each question in the approved form; (b) information ... to substantiate ...; (d) a written undertaking that the applicant: (i) will comply with the Collection Centre Guidelines ... (ii) will, within 24 hours after a failure to comply ..., give the Chief Executive Medicare written notice ...
> 9(2) On the premises there must be the equipment necessary for the collection and preparation of specimens for pathology procedures. (3) The staff at the premises must: (a) include persons trained in procedures for the collection and preparation of pathology specimens; and (b) be persons who are either: (i) employed by the APA who has made the application for the approval; or (ii) engaged directly or indirectly by the APA under a contract that gives the APA power to generally direct or control the work of the persons on the premises.
> 11(1) An approval for an eligible collection centre has effect for 2 years ...

Key point for Anthony's "employed or engaged" question: the APPROVAL test (s9(3)(b)) accepts staff "engaged directly or indirectly by the APA under a contract that gives the APA power to generally direct or control the work". The BENEFIT test (Act s16A(5AB)(c)) says "employee". The two do not match on their face. The approval can be granted with engaged staff; whether Medicare then pays for specimens they collect is not settled by the text. TO CONFIRM in writing with the Department of Health (policy owner) and Services Australia.

## 3. Other instruments
- Health Insurance (Approved Pathology Specimen Collection Centres) Tax Act 2000 s5 (compilation 2018-12-01, C2018C00480): "(2) For an approval that is granted for a period of 2 years, the tax is $2,000. ... (4) The tax is payable by the approved pathology authority that applied for the approval." Not payable if co-located with a category GX or GY lab (s3 "approval"). https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00727/latest/text
- Health Insurance (Accredited Pathology Laboratories-Approval) Principles 2017, compilation from 2026-07-13 (F2026C00750), s17 table: Category GX requires premises "under the full-time onsite direction and control of a designated person, who is a pathologist" with services "under the full-time supervision of a pathologist"; Category M is for a medical practice's own patients; Category S needs "a designated person, who is a medical practitioner with specialised scope of practice" and a "limited range" of testing. s18: premises "must be under the direction, control and supervision of a designated person". Schedule 1 lists the NPAAC collection guidelines (2013) as accreditation material. https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01291/latest/text
- Health Insurance (Eligible Pathology Laboratories) Determination 2026 (F2026L00272): eligible labs are categories GX, GY and S.
- Health Insurance (Approved Pathology Undertakings) Approval 2017 (F2017L01293): APA Part 1 s16 "Each entity to hold one approval as a pathology authority"; s17 requires the APA to give a copy of Part IIBA to any person from whom it leases premises for an ACC; Part 3 lists items an APA may provide free (includes "Glucose drink for GTT" and "Fridge, where refrigeration is vital ... labelled with Pathology Company name, and used exclusively for pathology purposes"), but Part 3 is for items supplied to requesting practitioners. s12(3)(a): the Department may ask for "copies of agreements, arrangements or contracts under which the Authority employs or engages staff at its premises". https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01293/latest/text
- Health Insurance (Application for Acceptance of Approved Pathology Authority Undertaking) Determination 2018 (F2018L01381), Schedule 1: an APA application lists proprietor ABN/ACN, directors, principal shareholders, principal officers and "fit and proper person" disclosures.

 The Act licenses "private hospitals" and "private psychiatric hostels". The Health Services Act 2016 s8 definition (not fetched as primary text; search summary only) covers an institution for treatment, including day hospital facility, nursing home or nursing post.
