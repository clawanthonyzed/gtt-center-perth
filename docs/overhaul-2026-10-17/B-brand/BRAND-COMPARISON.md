## 3. Name quality

### Meaning and origin (sources opened)
- ELOWEN: a Cornish feminine name meaning "elm tree"; described as a recent addition to the Cornish naming tradition (Behind the Name, https://www.behindthename.com/name/elowen ). Its rise followed the Cornish language revival of the 1970s and 80s, and the name appeared in the 1970 book "Names for the Cornish" (name pages found by search; not opened in full). A second, less supported reading links it to Welsh "el" (bright) and "oen" (fawn). Elm is a symbol of strength and new beginnings in name sites; this is popular naming lore, not a fact about the word.
- ELOWYN: a variant spelling of Elowen. Its origin is described as debated, a "relatively modern creation" mixing Cornish and Welsh roots, with the -wyn ending borrowed from Welsh ("fair, blessed"). Name Census shows 114 UK births in 2024 (rank 377 in England and Wales), a rising name (https://namecensus.co.uk/first-names/elowyn-meaning-and-history ). Verdict: ELOWEN has the cleaner, verifiable Cornish meaning; ELOWYN's is a decorative respelling.
- SOLENA: the repo describes it as built on the sun root "sol"; no established dictionary meaning was verified.
- BELMIRA: a Portuguese and Spanish given name; read here as "bela" plus "mira" (beautiful view), as in the 2026-10-16 proposals paper. That reading was not verified against a name dictionary today (TO CONFIRM).

### Sound, phone test, spelling (judgement, not a measured test)
No spoken or phone test was run; recommended below. These are reasoned judgements, marked as such.
- ELOWEN: EL-oh-wen (three syllables, no awkward clusters). Likely mishearings over the phone: "Ellen", "Eloween", "Elowin". Likely misspellings: Elowin, Elowyn, Ellowen, Elouen. The repo scored spelling ease 7/10 for ELOWEN.
- ELOWYN: sounds the same as ELOWEN, so every phone mishearing applies, and the written form has an extra trap: callers will not know whether to write -en, -yn or -in. The repo scored it 4/10 for spelling and 3/10 for referral clarity, the lowest in the set, and referral by word of mouth is the main growth channel in the brand strategy. Two separate design reviews in the repo flagged this independently.
- SOLENA: soh-LEE-nah in the repo, but soh-LAY-nah is also plausible to an Australian ear. The repo scored spelling 9/10.
- BELMIRA: bel-MEE-ra; likely mishearings "Belmore", "Bellmira", "Belmeera". Likely spellings: Belmeera, Belmyra. Unfamiliar to many Australians; a phone test matters most here.

### Fit with a luxury pregnancy wellness lounge
Brand direction per docs/strategy/VISUAL-BRAND-DIRECTION.md and BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md: a "considered private house" feel, warm stone, restraint, a serif wordmark (Fraunces), the afternoon beauty business must stand on its own with nothing "pregnancy-coded" visible to a non-pregnant guest. The visual direction was built to work for either SOLENA or ELOWEN.
- ELOWEN: natural, quiet, rooted; the "elm, new beginnings" story suits pregnancy without saying so; a recognised baby name, so it may read as "named after a baby" to some, a mild risk for the adult afternoon business. Strongest of the set on clinical credibility in the repo (9/10).
- ELOWYN: the same story with a more fantasy-leaning spelling; the repo's design review calls its look the most striking but "a slightly odd greeting" for a fasting, nervous client.
- SOLENA: warmest and most detached from pregnancy, best for the afternoon business; weakest on distinctiveness (the repo says so).
- BELMIRA: warm, premium, feminine; no pregnancy link and no Cornish or heritage story. Not yet tested against the colour or wordmark work. Looks workable in Fraunces (8 letters, open vowels), but that is untested.

## 6. Verdict and what to do next

### Ranked recommendation
1. BELMIRA: cleanest by far on domains, ABN Lookup and IP Australia. Run a spoken phone test first and have the attorney confirm. It has no design testing yet.
2. ELOWEN: strongest meaning and fit and the founder's earlier preference, but not clean: one WA exact-name user and several ELOWEN registrations in other classes. Keep as the live alternative, subject to the attorney.
3. SOLENA: keep only if the attorney clears SOLENNE (Class 44) and the Class 3 mark; its domains are gone.
4. ELOWYN: do not pursue. It does not solve the ELOWEN problems (same sound), it loses the domains, and it adds a spelling trap that hurts word-of-mouth.

This ranking is my reading of the evidence, not a decision. Anthony decides; nothing in the repo was changed.

### What to do next (all are Anthony's decisions; nothing has been done)
1. Spoken test, free: say each of ELOWEN, BELMIRA and SOLENA to 10 people by phone and ask them to write the name; count correct spellings. Target at least 8 of 10.
2. Ask a registered trade marks attorney for a formal search and a written opinion on ELOWEN and BELMIRA (and SOLENA if it stays alive) across Classes 44, 41 and 3, covering sound-alike marks, ELOWEN LLC, Elowen Counselling and SOLENNE. Attorney fees were not researched; get a quote.
3. If the attorney is positive, file. IP Australia fees read today: A$250 per class using the picklist, A$400 per class with custom wording, or TM Headstart from A$330 per class (A$200 plus A$130).
