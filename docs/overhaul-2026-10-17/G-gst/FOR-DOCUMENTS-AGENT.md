- Statute text: now on file (GST Act Compilation No. 96, 1 January 2026). Section 38-10 is a closed list of 21 services; phlebotomy is not on it.

- What a split would be worth if a genuine GST-free component existed: A$5,385.68 (50/50), A$6,462.81 (60/40), A$7,539.95 (70/30) a month. Not available today.
- What the evidence supports: the lab's whole fee for the visit is A$25.85 (about 10% of A$250) and GTT's own collection cost is A$30.73 a client (about 12%). A 50% to 70% clinical share is not supportable.

## 5. Answers to Anthony's questions (2 to 3 lines each)

1. "Find the text for s 38-10." Done. Full text of Subdivision 38-B (s 38-7 to 38-60), the definitions and the related Health Insurance Act s 16A are in the second brain and in the project folder G-gst. Phlebotomy is not on the 21-item table.
2. "What would the net look like if we itemise?" Fully taxable (the only available case): A$23,442.58 a month. With a hypothetical GST-free share: A$28,828.26 (50/50), A$29,905.39 (60/40), A$30,982.53 (70/30), A$34,213.94 (100%). The gap is the money at stake, not money available.
3. "Is a correct split not avoidance?" Yes if each line is a real supply by a supplier the statute covers, at a stand-alone market price. Today GTT supplies nothing GST-free, so the correct split is 0 / 100. A 50 to 70% clinical line would fail the market-value test (the lab's whole fee is A$25.85).
4. "Can merchant routing do it?" No. GST follows the supply, not the bank account. A separate entity only helps if it genuinely makes the GST-free supply.
5. "What is the workaround?" Real GST-free services (nursing or allied health) priced separately, or lab-run collection in GTT's rooms, confirmed by the accountant and an ATO private ruling.
