## 1. The answer in plain English

1. The real text of s 38-10 has now been read. It is a closed list of 21 services. Blood collection (phlebotomy) is not on the list, and nothing in the Act defines "nursing", so a phlebotomist cannot make a GTT charge GST-free under s 38-10.
2. The GST-free pathology service is the lab's, under s 38-7 ("a service for which medicare benefit is payable"). When Medicare bulk-bills the lab, the client pays GTT nothing for pathology, and GTT's invoice is for lounge, wellness and refreshments, which are taxable.
3. So, on GTT's current model, no part of a GTT package can be GST-free in GTT's own hands. The fully taxable case (GST-stress, +A$23,442.58 a month) is the legal base.
4. Anthony's instinct that a correct split is not avoidance is right in principle (GSTR 2001/8 requires apportionment where one supply has GST-free and taxable parts). But a split only has something to work on if GTT genuinely supplies a GST-free service. Today it supplies none, so there is nothing to put in Component A.
5. There are lawful routes to a real GST-free component (section 6). They need real services, real qualified people and, ideally, an ATO private ruling. None is an invoice-layout trick.

## 3. Section 38-7 (medical services), applied

Text: "(1) A supply of a *medical service is GST-free." Definition (s 195-1): "medical service means: (a) a service for which medicare benefit is payable under Part II of the Health Insurance Act 1973; or ... (b) any other service supplied by or on behalf of a *medical practitioner or *approved pathology practitioner that is generally accepted in the medical profession as being necessary for the appropriate treatment of the *recipient of the supply."

| Test | GTT application | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Limb (a): Medicare benefit payable | The GTT pathology service is a "pathology service" (a medical service to which an item of the pathology services table relates, Health Insurance Act s 3(1)). Medicare pays the lab (item 66548). The supplier is the lab | GST-free, in the lab's hands |
| Limb (b): supplied by or on behalf of an approved pathology practitioner | ATO: a service is on behalf of a medical professional if "it is billed in the name of the medical professional, and they accept full responsibility for the service", it is part of their service, and they are involved in at least part. ATO example: Dr Jackson's nurse takes blood; the pathology service is GST-free. The supplier there is Dr Jackson | GST-free only as the practitioner's or lab's supply |
| Could GTT be the supplier of that GST-free service? | Only if GTT is, or acts for and in the name of, the approved pathology practitioner or authority, with that body billing and accepting responsibility | Not on the current model |
| GTT charges the lab for staff or room | ATO: "Medical and other health services supplied to any other third party are not GST-free." The exceptions are insurers, compulsory third party scheme operators and Australian Government agencies (s 38-60). A pathology company is none of these on the facts known (whether PathWest is an "Australian government agency" under s 195-1 was not read: TO CONFIRM) | Taxable at 10%. The lab, whose own supplies are GST-free, recovers that GST as an input tax credit, so GST costs the lab nothing |
| s 38-7(3) goods at the premises | Covers goods supplied with the medical service at the premises where it is supplied. It follows the lab's service, not GTT's lounge | No help to the lounge |
| s 38-7(2) exceptions | Prescribed Medicare provisions and cosmetic services without Medicare benefit | Not relevant |

## 4. Section 38-10 (other health services), applied

Operative text, quoted from the Compilation: "(1) A supply is GST-free if: (a) it is a service of a kind specified in the table in this subsection, or of a kind specified in the regulations; and (b) the supplier is a *recognised professional in relation to the supply of services of that kind; and (c) the supply would generally be accepted, in the profession associated with supplying services of that kind, as being necessary for the appropriate treatment of the *recipient of the supply."

Table (all 21 items): 1 Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander health; 2 Acupuncture; 3 Audiology, audiometry; 4 Chiropody; 5 Chiropractic; 6 Dental; 7 Dietary; 8 Herbal medicine (including traditional Chinese herbal medicine); 9 Naturopathy; 10 Nursing; 11 Occupational therapy; 12 Optometry; 13 Osteopathy; 14 Paramedical; 15 Pharmacy; 16 Psychology; 17 Physiotherapy; 18 Podiatry; 19 Speech pathology; 20 Speech therapy; 21 Social work. The GST Regulations 2019 contain only reg 38-7.01 in Subdivision 38-B for medical services (and aged-care provisions), so no extra "kind of service" has been added by regulation for s 38-10(1)(a).

"Recognised professional" (s 195-1): the person is registered, permitted or approved under a State or Territory law prohibiting the supply of that kind of service without it; or, where no such law exists, is a member of a professional association with uniform national registration requirements; item 3 (audiology) has its own rule.

### Can the phlebotomist's collection be a "nursing" service?

- A Certificate III phlebotomist is not a nurse, so item 10 does not apply to the phlebotomist in their own right.
- ATO assistant rule (read): a part of a listed service done by an assistant is included in the professional's service only if it is "billed in the name of the recognised health professional, and they accept full responsibility", the professional is involved in at least part, and directly supervises by "attending to the patient at the start of each treatment", "being readily available for the whole time the assistant works with the patient", "being available to take appropriate action if there is an emergency", "planning all the treatment the assistant provides" and "being able to provide evidence that they monitor the assistant's services". So a registered nurse could in principle bill a collection and use phlebotomist assistants. Whether "each treatment" means each draw or each client visit is not stated: TO CONFIRM.
- Even if that works, the collection is already paid for as part of the lab's Medicare service. A second GTT charge for the same collection risks double charging the client and may conflict with the lab's bulk-billing (a Medicare question for the L workstream). The sensible use of item 10 is a separate, genuine nursing service, not a relabel of the draw.
- Condition (c): ATO "appropriate treatment" page (12 June 2024): the professional assesses health, works out a course of action and supplies a treatment generally accepted by their profession. GTT's own protocol says GTT makes no clinical judgements. A draw taken on the doctor's request, without GTT assessing anything, is the weakest fit.

## 7. Flags and conflicts

- The earlier finding that phlebotomy is not on the s 38-10 list is confirmed by the real text. The earlier note that the operative paragraphs were "TO CONFIRM against the Act text" is settled: the ATO summary matched the Act.
- The earlier finding that GST-free pathology depends on the lab's Medicare billing is confirmed by limb (a) of the definition.
- Item numbers: the pregnancy test is item 66548 (L-medicare). The MBS page for item 66542 (A$18.95, test for diabetes generally) was also read and is not the GTT item.
- Part IVA (Income Tax Assessment Act 1936) is an income tax rule. The GST anti-avoidance rule is Division 165 of the GST Act. Anthony's brief names Part IVA; both are covered in the invoice guide.
