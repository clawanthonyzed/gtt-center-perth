### 2.1 Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 (VEA), https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03268/latest/text

**s24(1)(b) the "TPI" limb:**
> "the veteran is totally and permanently incapacitated, that is to say, the veteran's incapacity from war-caused injury or war-caused disease, or both, is of such a nature as, of itself alone, to render the veteran incapable of undertaking remunerative work for periods aggregating more than 8 hours per week"

**s24(1)(c) the work loss limb:**
> "the veteran is, by reason of incapacity from that war-caused injury or war-caused disease, or both, alone, prevented from continuing to undertake remunerative work that the veteran was undertaking and is, by reason thereof, suffering a loss of salary or wages, or of earnings on his or her own account, that the veteran would not be suffering if the veteran were free of that incapacity"

**s24A(1)(c) the continuation rule (this is the ongoing test):**
> "(1) Subject to subsections (1A) and (2), if the Commonwealth is or becomes liable to pay a pension to a veteran at the rate applicable under section 23 or 24, that rate continues, while a pension continues to be payable to the veteran, to apply to the veteran unless: (a) the decision to apply that rate of pension to the veteran would not have been made but for a false statement or misrepresentation made by a person; ... (c) in the case of a veteran to whom section 24 applies - the veteran is undertaking or is capable of undertaking remunerative work for periods aggregating more than 8 hours per week."

**s5Q (dictionary) definition:**
> "remunerative work includes any remunerative activity."

**s28 how capacity is judged:**
> "In determining, for the purposes of paragraph 23(1)(b) or 24(1)(b), whether a veteran who is incapacitated from war-caused injury or war-caused disease, or both, is incapable of undertaking remunerative work, and in determining for the purposes of section 24A whether a veteran who is so incapacitated is capable of undertaking remunerative work, the Commission shall have regard to the following matters only: (a) the vocational, trade and professional skills, qualifications and experience of the veteran; (b) the kinds of remunerative work which a person with the skills, qualifications and experience referred to in paragraph (a) might reasonably undertake; and (c) the degree to which the physical or mental impairment of the veteran as a result of the injury or disease, or both, has reduced his or her capacity to undertake the kinds of remunerative work referred to in paragraph (b)."

**What this covers, definitively.** The test has two halves. (1) Actual: "undertaking" remunerative work over 8 hours a week. (2) Capacity: "capable of undertaking", judged under s28 on skills, kinds of work and the effect of the impairment. It is hours based, not earnings based, and not title based. "Any remunerative activity" is wide enough to cover employment, self-employment, running or managing a business, and a paid directorship. The statute does not mention shareholdings, trust beneficiary interests, dividends, rent or investment returns. A return on capital that needs no personal work from the veteran is not "work" in the ordinary meaning, and nothing in ss24, 24A or 28 makes it so. That is a reading of the text, not a DVA ruling; see Question 1.

**s85(3) Gold Card trigger (health treatment):**
> "Where a veteran: (a) is in receipt of a pension under Part II at the general rate or at a higher rate; or (b) is in receipt of a pension under Part II in respect of incapacity from a war-caused injury or a war-caused disease of a kind described in column 1 of the table in subsection 27(1); the veteran is eligible to be provided, from and including the date as from which a pension is so payable to the veteran, with treatment under this Part for any injury suffered, or disease contracted, by the veteran, whether before or after the commencement of this Act."

The Act's definition of a gold card is "a card known as the Repatriation Health Card - For All Conditions, that evidences a person's eligibility ... to be provided with treatment for all injuries or diseases." There is no income or asset condition in s85(3). The condition is the status of receiving a Part II pension at the general rate or higher (the Special Rate is a higher rate than the general rate under s22). If the Special Rate were ever lost under s24A, the veteran would still be on a Part II pension, so s85(3) could still apply at the general or intermediate rate. TO CONFIRM with DVA (Question 3).

**s5H(8)(b) the TPI is not income for the VEA income test:**
> "The following amounts are not income in relation to a person for the purposes of this Act: ... (b) a payment of an instalment of pension under Part II or IV"

**s54 the notification power is limited to income-tested payment recipients:**
> "(1) The Secretary may give a person: (a) to whom a service pension, income support supplement or a veteran payment is being paid; or (b) whose claim or application for a service pension or income support supplement, or whose eligibility for a veteran payment, is under consideration by the Commission or the Administrative Review Tribunal; or (c) who is receiving benefits under Division 12; a notice that requires the person to inform the Department ... if: (d) a specified event or change of circumstances occurs ..."

I did not find an equivalent standing power over a Part II pension recipient in the sections read (ss13 to 34). TO CONFIRM (Question 2). Note this is not permission to stay silent about work: s24A(1)(a) removes the Special Rate if it was granted on a false statement or misrepresentation, and DVA can reconsider under s31 (reconsideration, applying MRCA s347), so a veteran should give accurate answers whenever DVA asks.

**Private trust and company attribution, relevant only if an income-tested VEA payment is claimed (Part IIIB, Division on private companies and trusts). s52ZZH(2)(a) and (da):**
> "(2) For the purposes of this section, the individual passes the control test in relation to a trust if: (a) the individual, or an associate of the individual (other than an associate covered by paragraph 52ZQ(1)(j)), is the trustee, or any of the trustees, of the trust; or ... (da) either or both of the following apply: (i) the individual is eligible to receive an application of the corpus or income of the trust; (ii) one or more of the individual's associates are eligible to receive an application of the corpus or income of the trust; and the aggregate number of entities covered by subparagraphs (i) and (ii) is 50% or more of the total number of entities eligible to receive an application of the corpus or income of the trust"

**s52ZZK(1) attribution, in outline:** where the company or trust "derives an amount that is ordinary income" and the individual is an attributable stakeholder, "the individual is taken to receive, during that attribution period, ordinary income at an annual rate equal to the individual's income attribution percentage" of that amount (s52ZZJ sets the percentage at 100% unless the Commission determines lower). This attributes the entity's income to the individual whether or not it is distributed, and it applies only when the individual is being assessed for an income-tested payment (service pension, income support supplement and similar). Both discretionary trusts (YETI Holding Trust and the Operational Trust) are "designated private trusts" under s52ZZB (a trust is designated unless it is a fixed trust with 50 or more unit holders and so on), and a company is designated if it is a private company under s52ZZA. Whether Anthony or his partner is an "associate" or is "eligible" for a given trust is a question of the unseen deeds (TO CONFIRM).

### 2.2 Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 2004 (MRCA), https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A01285/latest/text

The venture file records "TPI + SRDP + CSC Class A confirmed". Whether Anthony's Special Rate is paid under the VEA (the s24 rate) or as an MRCA Special Rate Disability Pension (SRDP) is not in the file. TO CONFIRM (Question 1). Both are covered here.

**s197 (outline):**
> "This Part provides a choice for severely impaired people (at least 50 impairment points) who are unable to work more than 10 hours per week to receive a pension under this Part instead of compensation worked out under Division 2 of Part 4. The rate of the pension is the same as the rate applicable under section 24 of the VEA ... A person who works more than 10 hours per week stops receiving the Special Rate Disability Pension."

**s199(1)(d):** "the person is unable to undertake remunerative work for more than 10 hours per week, and rehabilitation is unlikely to increase the person's capacity to undertake remunerative work."

**s209(b):** "The Commonwealth is no longer liable to pay a Special Rate Disability Pension to a person if the Commission is satisfied that: ... (b) the person is able to undertake remunerative work for more than 10 hours per week."

**s282 Gold Card for SRDP eligibility (note: the directive cites ss284 and 285; those are not the Gold Card sections):**
> "A person is entitled to be provided with treatment under this Part for any injury or disease of the person if: (a) the person satisfies the eligibility criteria in section 199 (persons who are eligible for Special Rate Disability Pension), or has satisfied those criteria during some period of his or her life; and (b) the treatment is provided to the person after paragraph (a) begins to apply to the person."

s284 is treatment for certain wholly dependent partners and eligible young persons; s285 is treatment at hospitals and other institutions (administration). The words "or has satisfied those criteria during some period of his or her life" mean the MRCA Gold Card entitlement under s282 does not fall away if the person later works more than 10 hours. TO CONFIRM how DVA applies it (Question 3). Also relevant: s281 gives treatment for any injury or disease at 60 or more impairment points.

**Hours threshold differs:** VEA Special Rate is 8 hours a week; MRCA SRDP is 10 hours a week. The MRCA also has a Return to Work Scheme (s210) that pays weekly compensation if a former SRDP recipient becomes able to work more than 10 hours.

**Passive ownership versus working in the business.** DVA's published VEA policy does not set an earnings or ownership limit. The only quantitative test is the 8 hours. DVA's incapacity manual (for a different Act) draws the line at "own labour": returns from family trusts and rental properties are not counted, work the person does or is able to do is. Applying that logic by analogy, owning shares, being a trust beneficiary and receiving distributions are passive; managing, rostering, hiring, supervising, clinical or customer-facing work in the business is labour and counts towards the 8 hours, whether or not it is paid and regardless of the job title. Unpaid work for a profit-making business is not "voluntary work" in DVA policy, so "unpaid" does not help. I found no DVA text that specifically addresses a veteran who is a nominal director of a corporate trustee or a company; that is exactly what Question 1 asks.

## 4. Answers to Anthony's questions

**C2b: Does the Special Rate (TPI) or the Gold Card have an income test?**
No. The pension is excluded from income by VEA s5H(8)(b) and SSA s8(8), DVA policy 4.1.6 says in terms that it "is not income or asset tested, nor is it taxable income", and the Gold Card trigger in s85(3) is the receipt of a Part II pension at the general rate or higher, with no means condition. The condition that can end the Special Rate is a work-hours test (more than 8 hours a week under the VEA; more than 10 under the MRCA SRDP), and the Gold Card does not depend on the rate being Special if a general-rate Part II pension continues (TO CONFIRM).

**C2c: How do trust distributions, company dividends and director fees interact with DVA reporting?**

| Receipt | TPI payment and Gold Card (statute) | Reporting duty (statute) | Other effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust distribution to Anthony as beneficiary | Not counted: no income test applies | None found for a Part II pensioner (s54 covers service pension, income support supplement, veteran payment). TO CONFIRM | Assessable income for personal tax (accountant). Counts in any income-tested payment test via ss52ZZH and 52ZZK |
| Company dividend to Anthony | Not counted | As above | Taxable (franking credits apply, accountant). Same attribution rules for any income-tested payment |
| Director fee to Anthony | Money is not counted against the pension. **But a fee implies work.** Hours in the role count towards the 8-hour test (s24A(1)(c)) | None found as a statutory income report; **the work itself must be disclosed truthfully if DVA asks** (s24A(1)(a)) | Taxable. Triggers Superannuation Guarantee and PAYG considerations (accountant) |
| Unpaid director or manager work for a company Anthony benefits from | Money not counted. Hours still count; "unpaid" is not "voluntary work" for a profit business (CLIK 4.1.6) | As above | Higher DVA risk than passive ownership |
| Anthony signing as director of a trustee company (paperwork only) | Hours count only if they are "remunerative activity" and exceed 8 hours a week across all roles; a few signing hours a month is far below the threshold. Keep a log. | As above | Directors carry personal legal duties regardless (see entity file) |

**Resolution of the earlier conflict.** The venture file says "Trust income = ZERO effect on TPI". That is correct for the TPI payment and the Gold Card, because neither is income tested. It omits (i) the 8-hour work test and (ii) the private trust and company attribution rules that apply if an income-tested payment is ever claimed. Anthony's brief says distributions count as income. That is correct for tax and for income-tested payments (including any claim his partner may make for Centrelink payments, where the equivalent Social Security Act Part 3.18 rules apply to a person who controls or benefits from a private trust; TO CONFIRM, not read in this pass), and incorrect for the TPI payment. Net: structure the business so (1) Anthony does not work in it more than a token amount, and (2) decide distributions on tax grounds, not DVA grounds. The standing instruction that business revenue stays outside Anthony's personal income remains Anthony's choice and is a tax decision, not a DVA requirement.
