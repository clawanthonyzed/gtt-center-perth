## 2. What the DVA findings mean for entity design

From DVA-TPI-DEFINITIVE-ANALYSIS.md: the TPI and Gold Card have no income or asset test. The constraint is the work-hours test (more than 8 hours a week of remunerative work ends the Special Rate under VEA s24A(1)(c); 10 hours for an MRCA SRDP). So the structure must put **work** (management, clinical, rostering, hiring) in other people, and only **ownership** and high-level decisions with Anthony's trust. Money reaching Anthony or his family is a tax question, not a DVA question, unless an income-tested payment is claimed later. Consequences for design:

1. Anthony should not be a director of the trading company, and not its manager. Use an employed Venue Manager and an independent director.
2. Anthony being a shareholder or beneficiary (directly or through a trust) is permitted on the statute and is not "work". Recommended anyway: shares held by a trust, not by Anthony personally (also consistent with the standing revenue rule).
3. His current administrative role as a director of the trustee company YETI Tipi Holdings stays small and logged, or is handed to a professional trustee. Ask DVA (Question 1).

## 3. How asset protection really works (and where it fails)

| Mechanism | How it works | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Company limited liability | A claim against a company is met from the company's assets and insurance, not its shareholders' | Shareholders lose their investment. Does not stop personal guarantees. Directors can be personally liable (below) |
| Trust trading directly | Contracts are made by the trustee, who is personally liable to creditors, with a right to be indemnified from the assets of that trust | The creditor can pursue the trustee company's own assets. The indemnity covers only that trust's assets and can be lost if the trustee breaches trust. If one corporate trustee runs two trusts, a failure in one trust can bring down the trustee company and disturb the other trust's assets (changing the trustee, freezing, disputes). TO CONFIRM by solicitor against the deeds |
| Separate trusts and separate trustees | The Holding Trust's assets are not the Operational Trust's assets; a creditor of one cannot reach the other's property | Only works if the trustees are different companies and nobody cross-guarantees. Same trustee for both is a recognised weak point |
| Personal and cross guarantees | Landlords, banks and equipment lenders often ask for these | A guarantee from the Holding Trust, its trustee or Anthony hands the guarantor's assets (including Withers) to that creditor. This is the single biggest practical leak |
| Insurance | Public liability, professional indemnity for the clinical and wellness work, product liability, workers compensation. Held by the entity that carries the risk | Insurers will ask who the insured entity is and who employs the staff. Wrong insured entity can void cover. Exclusions and limits apply. TO CONFIRM with a broker |
| Insolvent trading and directors' duties | A director must not allow a company to trade while insolvent (Corporations Act s588G) and carries liability for unpaid PAYG withholding and superannuation through director penalty notices (Taxation Administration Act 1953, Schedule 1) | The independent director and the Venue Manager would carry these exposures; an unpaid company can pull personal liability onto any director. Division 7A (ITAA 1936) catches loans or use of assets from a company to shareholders or their associates |
| Trust deed clauses | Many deeds limit trustee liability or define the indemnity | Unseen. [DEED] |

Source note: these are general principles of Australian law stated from general knowledge; I did not fetch primary sources for them in this pass. Sections named are those I am confident of (Corporations Act 2001 s588G; Taxation Administration Act 1953 Schedule 1 Division 269; ITAA 1936 Division 7A); all are TO CONFIRM with the solicitor and accountant.

## 4. The options

Fixed facts for every option: GTT employs its practitioners (casual or permanent), a pathology partner does the blood draws, and the clinical wellness venture has public contact with pregnant women. It needs a lease, a lab or collection-service agreement, payroll, GST registration, workers compensation and public liability insurance in one clean legal person.

### Option A: trade under the YETI Operational Trust directly

- Trustee (unknown company) signs lease and contracts, employs staff, holds insurance.
- Trustee is personally liable; indemnity from Operational Trust assets only.
- If the Operational trustee is YETI Tipi (also trustee of the Holding Trust), the same legal person is both the trading debtor and the title-holder for Withers. That is the worst case for C1b.
- Profit: discretionary distributions. 2028 draft minimum tax on discretionary trusts would apply unless a fixed-distribution election is made (status TO CONFIRM; see section 6).

### Option B: new Pty Ltd ("GTT Pty Ltd") owned by the Operational Trust

- The company trades. The Operational Trust's trustee holds the shares.
- Profit stays in the company (company tax, probably the 25 per cent base rate entity rate if the conditions are met; TO CONFIRM), then flows to the trust as dividends, then to beneficiaries.
- The Operational trustee must be a different company from the Holding Trust's trustee for the separation to hold (C1b).
- Anthony is not a director. Independent director plus employed Venue Manager.

### Option C: a new structure separate from both trusts

- **C1:** a new company owned by a new discretionary (or unit) trust with a **new corporate trustee**, set up clean for this venture, with a beneficiary class and appointor drafted with DVA and tax in mind. Needs a deed drafted by a solicitor.
- **C2:** a new company owned directly by individuals or investors who are not Anthony (for example the Venue Manager with a small equity stake, or outside investors). Cleanest legal separation, but profit does not flow to Anthony's family unless the owners are his family trust.
- **C3:** a partnership or joint venture. Partners have unlimited liability for partnership debts, so this is a poor fit for clinical risk. Considered and rejected.

### Option D: hybrids

- **D1: Operating company plus separate equipment or property entity.** An owning entity holds chairs, ultrasound-related equipment (if any), fit-out where legally severable, and leases to the operating company. Protects equipment from trading claims. Adds a second set of books and related-party lease paperwork. Fit-out in a leased tenancy mostly cannot be taken back, so the benefit is limited.
- **D2: Company owned by the Operational Trust with Anthony as non-director and an independent director or manager.** This is Option B with the DVA-driven governance in section 2. Recommended governance for B.
- **D3: Licence structure between the clinical operator and the lounge.** One company runs the clinical side (blood-collection arrangements with the pathology partner, phlebotomy employment) and licenses the lounge and customer relationship from, or to, a second company that runs wellness and beauty services. Separates a clinical claim from the wellness business. Adds cost, intra-group pricing and GST complexity (see the GST-split workstream). Relevant if the A2a workaround of a separate pathology-authority entity goes ahead; that is another workstream's finding and is not decided here.
- **D4: Unit trust trading with a corporate trustee, units held by trusts.** A fixed unit trust is outside the 2028 draft minimum tax on discretionary trusts, and units suit investors. But it has the same personal-liability profile as Option A (the trustee company is the debtor).
- **D5: Company owned by a new unit trust whose units are held by the Operational Trust and any investors.** Clean investor entry (units), company liability shield, flexibility on distributions upstream. More entities and cost.

## 8. C1b: what protects Withers, option by option

**A (trade under the Operational Trust).** Protection depends entirely on the Operational Trust and the Holding Trust being separate trust estates with **different corporate trustees**. A GTT claimant sues the Operational trustee; recovery is limited to the trustee's indemnity over the Operational Trust's assets plus the trustee's own assets. Withers sits in a different trust and should be out of reach. The weaknesses: if YETI Tipi Holdings is trustee of both, the same legal person is the defendant and also the title-holder of Withers, and an insolvent trustee can force replacement of the trustee, litigation over which trust owns what, and loss of the indemnity if there was a breach of trust. Any guarantee given by the Holding Trust or its trustee removes the protection. [DEED] [TO CONFIRM trustee identities]

**B / D2 (company owned by the Operational Trust).** The mechanism is the company's limited liability: GTT claims stop at the company's assets and insurance, and the Operational Trust risks only the value of its shares (unless it guarantees or lends). Withers is protected by two layers (company, and separate trusts). Weaknesses: same-trustee problem if the Operational trustee is YETI Tipi; any guarantee from a trust or from Anthony personally; loans from the trust to the company that become exposed in a liquidation; Division 7A traps; directors' liability (insolvent trading, unpaid PAYG and super) for the independent director; deed clauses limiting the trustee's right to hold shares or trade [DEED].

**C1 (new company under a new trust with a new trustee).** Protection by construction: no legal link to either existing trust or to Cudan Studio. A new trust deed drafted for this venture can address DVA and tax design (beneficiary class, appointor, how control is held). Withers is protected by three layers. Weaknesses: more setup cost and time; guarantees still defeat it; capitalising the new trust must be done by gift or loan from outside the Holding Trust, otherwise value from Withers' trust moves into the new structure (gifts from a discretionary trust need deed and tax advice) [DEED] [accountant].

**C2 (company held directly by non-Anthony shareholders).** Strongest separation (Withers has no link), because Anthony and the trusts own nothing in it. The cost is that Anthony's family receives nothing unless the shareholders are linked back to them, and giving up control. Weakness: control and succession depend on the other shareholders; a shareholders' agreement is required.

**C3 (partnership or joint venture).** Partners are liable without limit for partnership debts. Withers would be reachable if the Holding Trust or its trustee is a partner; if the partner is a trustee of an unrelated trust, only that trust is reachable. Not recommended for clinical risk.

**D1 (operating company plus equipment company).** Operating-company liability shield plus a second shelter for equipment. Weaknesses: related-party leases must be genuine and commercial, otherwise a liquidator or creditor may look through them; guarantees of the equipment lender; added cost for a small asset base.

**D3 (clinical company and lounge company with a licence).** Separates the highest-severity risk (a collection or specimen incident) from the wellness business, each with its own insurer. Weaknesses: highest cost; GST and pricing between the two companies must be commercial (see the GST-split workstream); one customer-facing brand across two legal entities creates confusion over who contracted with the customer; the structure assumes a pathology collection arrangement that is still being settled in another workstream.

**D4 (unit trust trading, corporate trustee).** Like A: the trustee company is the debtor with an indemnity over the unit trust's assets. Withers is protected only by separation of trusts and trustees. Fixed units are useful for investors and are outside the discretionary trust draft minimum tax, but the liability protection is weak.

**D5 (company owned by a new unit trust, units held by the Operational Trust and investors).** Same company-level shield as B, with a clean way to admit investors. Weaknesses: an extra trust and trustee to run, and the units held by the Operational Trust are only as safe as its deed.

## 9. Recommendation

**Trade GTT through a new, dedicated Pty Ltd, owned by a trust whose corporate trustee is not the trustee of the Holding Trust, with Anthony neither director, manager nor guarantor.**

- **Preferred if the deeds allow:** Option B/D2, using the existing Operational Trust as shareholder, provided the accountant and solicitor confirm after reading the Operational Trust deed that (1) its trustee is a different company from YETI Tipi Holdings (or a new trustee is appointed), (2) it may hold shares and carry on this activity, (3) its beneficiaries and appointor are acceptable, (4) it has no existing liabilities that would sit alongside the shares. This reuses an existing structure and keeps cost lowest.
- **Fallback, and the cleaner design:** Option C1 (a new trust with a new corporate trustee) if any of those checks fail. It scored highest (87 per cent) because it does not depend on unseen deeds.
- **Not recommended:** A (trading directly), C3 (partnership), D4 (weak liability shield).
- **Hold in reserve:** C2 or D5 if outside investors or a Venue Manager equity stake are wanted later; D3 only if the pathology arrangement requires the clinical activity to sit in a separate company.
- **Governance (all company options):** an independent director who is not Anthony and not Imara; an employed Venue Manager; Anthony's involvement limited to ownership-level decisions through the trust, logged; no guarantees from the Holding Trust, YETI Tipi Holdings or Anthony; capital provided by loan or share subscription documented at arm's length; GTT insurance in the company's name before the first booking.
- **Cudan Studio:** keep it completely out. Nothing about GTT depends on it. If Cudan is closed, its company should not be the employer, lessee or insured for GTT.
- **Tax design is the accountant's call.** Anthony's standing instruction that business revenue stays outside his personal income can be met with company tax followed by dividends to the trust, with distributions decided each year. The DVA analysis shows distributions to Anthony would not reduce the TPI or Gold Card; the reason to avoid them is personal tax.

**Status of the 30 per cent minimum tax on discretionary trusts (draft from 1 July 2028):** as at the 2026-09-04 investigation it was an exposure draft released 2026-09-03 with consultation to 18 September 2026 (sources listed in docs/architecture/ENTITY-STRUCTURE-INVESTIGATION.md). I have not re-checked whether it has since been introduced or passed. TO CONFIRM with the accountant. A company and a fixed unit trust sit outside it; a discretionary trust sits inside it unless a fixed-distribution election is made.
