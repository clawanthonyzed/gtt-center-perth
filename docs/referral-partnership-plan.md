# GTT Center Perth — Referral Partnership Plan (Midwife / OB / GP)

**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-07-19 (Phase 7 new deliverable)
**Purpose:** GP/obstetrician/midwife referral relationship strategy — distinct from `reed-partnerships.md`, which covers pathology-lab (WDP/PathWest/Clinipath) partnership outreach. This document is specifically about the referral network that sends patients to GTT Center Perth, not the pathology partner that processes their blood tests.

---

## Why This Matters

GTT Center Perth's primary customer discovery channel is the referring practice, not paid advertising — per `business-plan.md`'s target customer profile ("referral pathway: midwife, OB/GYN referral, online search, word of mouth") and `afternoon-marketing-plan.md`'s framing ("the main marketing plan is built around midwife/OB referrals → GTT patients → morning shift"). A GTT referral already exists as a mandatory medical event for every pregnant woman in the 24-28 week window — the opportunity is making the referring practice aware that a wellness-integrated option exists for where their patient goes to have it done, not creating demand from nothing.

## Referral Partnership Approach (General Strategy)

Since a full formal outreach program has not yet been executed (this is a planning document, not a report of completed outreach), the approach below is derived from the tactics already documented in `poppy-marketing.md` and `afternoon-marketing-plan.md`, consolidated here:

### Outreach Sequence (per `afternoon-marketing-plan.md` §Partnership Approach)
1. Personal visit or call — not just an email, given the relationship-dependent nature of referral generation
2. In-person introduction (offered as a short, low-commitment "come see what we do" visit)
3. Leave behind: a premium-printed referral card, service menu card, business cards
4. Offer: complimentary wellness visits so the practice's own staff can experience the service firsthand and become genuine advocates, not just distribute cards on faith
5. Follow-up: a "how can we make this work for your clients?" check-in call after a short interval

### Formal Partner Offer (for top referring practices, per `afternoon-marketing-plan.md`)
- Referral credit tracked via the booking system's "How did you hear about us?" field
- Alternative: partner-specific pricing for their clients
- Quarterly in-person check-ins with the top referring practices (Venue Manager, once hired — see `staff-plan.md`)

### Distribution Mechanics (per `poppy-marketing.md` §8 Referral Card Design Brief)
- Referral cards explicitly framed as "your midwife/OB gave you this because they care about your experience" — positions the referring practice as the trusted source, not GTT Center Perth self-promoting
- Distribution: in-person drop to each midwife/OB practice (or by post for regional/suburban), approximately 50 cards per practice, restocked quarterly

## Practical Execution Framework (Added 2026-08-23, Per Direct Founder Instruction)

Moves this from "there is a referral opportunity" to "who we approach, what we ask, and how we measure it," without repeating the market/referral-mechanics research already completed in `docs/architecture/MARKET-RESEARCH-BIRTHS-GDM-REFERRAL-2026.md` (that document already establishes: a standard pathology request form from any GP/OB/midwife works regardless of which provider's letterhead it carries, so a client referred to GTT Center Perth does not need a WDP-specific form; the one remaining genuine gap is WDP's own site-specific intake requirement, to be confirmed with Carole Rivers as part of operational handover, not re-researched here).

**Target partner profile:** a practice or clinic that (1) sees pregnant clients in the 24-28 week screening window regularly, (2) is independent enough to recommend a specific collection venue to its clients (private midwives and independent OB/GYN clinics fit this far better than a large hospital-based public clinic, which typically has its own default pathway), (3) is geographically plausible for the eventual venue location once confirmed. This is why the existing named-practice list above (private midwifery practices, independent OB/GYN clinics) is the right starting set, and why the Public Sector / Maternal Child Health Nurse channel is lower-priority (less independent referral discretion per client).

**Number of initial targets:** 12 (the full private midwifery list above) + 10 (the full OB/GYN clinic list above) = **22 named practices for the first outreach wave**, all already identified, none contacted. This is a deliberately bounded, executable list, not "outreach to the whole Perth referral network at once."

**Information required per target before outreach:** confirmation the practice/clinic is still operating and the contact details above are current (flagged in the existing gap note below as not yet independently re-verified), and, once available, the confirmed venue address/suburb, so outreach can honestly state where the collection service will be.

**Outreach objective, stated as a measurable outcome, not just "raise awareness":** for each of the 22 first-wave targets, the objective is one of 3 concrete outcomes per visit/call: (1) the practice agrees to display referral cards, (2) a staff member from the practice accepts a complimentary visit to experience the service, or (3) the practice declines, recorded as a genuine "not now" rather than left ambiguous. A visit with no recorded outcome is not counted as completed outreach.

**Referral process, concrete not conceptual:** the client's own existing pathology request form (from their GP, OB, or midwife) is what gets them collected at GTT Center Perth, per the already-researched industry norm; GTT Center Perth does not need its own referral form for the clinical request itself. What GTT Center Perth does need, and does not yet have, is a **simple one-page practice information sheet** (venue description, hours, the wellness-services concept, how to find it, a QR code or short link to the booking page once the website exists) to leave with each of the 22 targets, distinct from the client-facing referral card already specified in `poppy-marketing.md` §8. This is a genuine, small, buildable gap: a one-page practice information sheet does not exist yet and should be built once the venue address and website are confirmed (both currently missing, see `docs/architecture/OPENING-READINESS-EXECUTION-PLAN.md` Section 8).

**Tracking method:** the booking system's own "How did you hear about us?" field (Fresha), already specified in this document's existing Formal Partner Offer section, is the correct mechanism; it requires no new system, only a discipline of actually recording and reviewing it, which should be a named Venue Manager responsibility from Week 1 of opening, not left implicit.

**Response/conversion metrics, genuinely new, not previously defined anywhere in this repository:** (1) practices visited / 22 (outreach completion rate), (2) practices displaying referral cards / practices visited (adoption rate), (3) bookings tagged to each practice via the Fresha "how did you hear" field / total AM bookings (referral-channel share), (4) bookings per referring practice per month (identifies the top referring practices for the existing quarterly check-in mechanism). None of these can be populated with a real number yet, since outreach has not started; they are the measurement framework to apply once it does, not a forecast.

**Test batch (added 2026-08-24, per direct founder instruction): the first 3 practices to approach, before expanding to the remaining 19.** Kathleen Johnson (Earthside Midwifery, 0411 856 265); Peta Bissett (Hand in Hand Midwives, 0409 422 287); Sunshine Walker (Journey of Birth, 0400 140 298). Chosen because they are the 3 named practices with a direct personal contact already identified (not a generic clinic number), giving the outreach test the highest chance of an actual conversation rather than a message left unreturned.

**Contact objective:** a short in-person or phone conversation, not a cold email, per the existing outreach-sequence approach (personal visit/call first). **Message, kept plain, not marketing copy:** GTT Center Perth is a new Perth venue combining the mandatory GTT wait with wellness services (massage, beauty, nail, hair); introduce the concept, offer to have them experience it once open, and ask whether they would be open to mentioning it to clients due for a GTT. **What we are asking for:** permission to leave referral cards/a practice information sheet, and a genuine yes/no/maybe on whether they would refer clients. **What we are offering:** a complimentary wellness visit for the practitioner or a staff member to experience the service firsthand (already specified in the existing Outreach Sequence above), positioned as an invitation to see the venue, not a payment for referrals.

**Evidence to record per contact (this is the actual test, not the outreach itself):** date of contact, method (phone/in-person), whether the conversation happened at all, the practice's own stated response (yes/no/maybe, in their own words if possible), whether they accepted the complimentary-visit offer, and any objection or hesitation raised. **Success criteria:** at least 1 of the 3 gives a genuine "yes, we'd refer clients" response with no major objection. **Failure criteria:** all 3 decline, or none can be reached after 2 genuine attempts each. **What a failure would mean:** not that referrals don't work, but that the message/offer or the specific target profile needs revisiting before expanding to the remaining 19, per the standing instruction to test the demand assumption rather than assume it. **When to expand:** only after all 3 have been contacted and a genuine outcome recorded for each, not before, and only if at least the success criterion above is met; if it is not met, the message/offer should be revised and re-tested on a small batch again before expanding to the full 19, not scaled up on an unproven approach.

**GP channel, addressed directly rather than left as "needs research":** no specific GP practice is named anywhere in this repository, and inventing specific GP clinic names here would not be genuine research. The defensible, bounded next step if GP outreach is wanted alongside the midwife/OB channel: target bulk-billing GP clinics with high antenatal patient volume in the same suburbs as the eventual confirmed venue, identified once that venue address exists, since "high antenatal volume" is a locality-specific fact, not a Perth-wide one. This is stated as the correct method, not a completed target list, since the venue address is the missing input, not additional research effort.
