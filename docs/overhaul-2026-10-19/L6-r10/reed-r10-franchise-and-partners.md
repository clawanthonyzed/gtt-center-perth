| Could GTT purchase one? | No product exists to buy. So GTT cannot own an ACC unless GTT itself becomes that authority and owns a Category GX, GY or S lab (R9 Rank 3: 12 to 24 months, specialist doctor on staff, accreditation body assessment). | [LIB] APA-LAB-CATEGORIES-AND-ACC-ACCESS.md Q4 |
| Could one be "set up in GTT"? | Yes. That is already the R9 Rank 1 route: the lab applies for ACC approval of a room inside GTT's premises, using GTT's lease (sub-lease) and floor plan. Co-location in someone else's premises is the normal market pattern (section 3). | [LIB] R9; [SEC] |

**Plain point:** GTT does not need to buy anything. GTT needs a lab to agree to take a sub-lease of the collection room. The commercial question is the terms, not the purchase.

## 2. How the usual deal works (who pays what)

### 2.1 Usual structure
A law-firm guide to collection centre leasing says the pathology provider "leases (or sublets or licences) space from a requester of services" (a requester is a doctor, participating midwife or participating nurse practitioner, or someone connected to one). Collection centres are usually under 50 sq m, often a single room. [SEC] pmlawyers.com.au factsheet "A guide to pathology collection centre leasing" (July 2020).

| Question | Usual answer | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the head tenant? | The host (medical centre, practice, or in GTT's case GTT). | [SEC] |
| Who holds the lab's right to the room? | Sub-lease or licence from the host. HW035 (the ACC application form) needs the lease or sub-lease and a floor plan; since 1 July 2018 new and renewed leases go through Services Australia online (HPOS). | [SEC]; [LIB] HW035 notes in R9 |
| Who pays rent? | The lab pays the host. Rent must not be "substantially different" from market value; more than 20 per cent above market is "substantially different" (Health Insurance Regulations 2018 s 76; market value s 77). | [SEC] pmlawyers; [SEC] hwlebsworth.com.au (13 Dec 2018) |
| Outgoings? | A proportionate share is permitted (example: lab uses 30 per cent of the building, pays 30 per cent of charges). Being charged 100 per cent would be a prohibited benefit. | [SEC] pmlawyers |
| Who employs the collector? | The lab. Medicare pays only where the specimen is collected by the pathologist, or an employee of the approved pathology authority that owns the lab doing the test (s 16A(5AA)(d), (5AB)(c)). | [LIB] HIA-SECTION-16A.md |
| Who bills Medicare? | The lab (approved pathology authority and its approved practitioner). The host cannot. | [LIB] |
| Per-collection fee or rent share to the host? | Not permitted if it depends on the number, kind or value of requests (s 23DZZIF(7)(a) in the current compilation). No source found for any network paying per-collection fees. | [LIB]; NOT FOUND |
| Does the host supply the staff? | A benefit that consists of providing staff or equipment at the beneficiary's premises is excluded from "permitted benefits" (s 23DZZIF(7)(b) in the current compilation). This bites when the host is a requester. | [LIB] |

Note: the 2020 law-firm guide numbers this staff exclusion as "(8)". The current compilation held in the library has it at (7)(b). Cite the library text, not the blog numbering.

### 2.2 Correction to the question's section references
The brief listed "s23DN, s23DNK-L" as payment rules. They are not. In the library: s 23DNK is the notice that a centre is approved (offence if missing); s 23DNL is the offence for operating an unapproved centre without warning clients that Medicare will not pay. The payment limits are in **Part IIBA, ss 23DZZIA to 23DZZIU** (permitted benefits s 23DZZIF; civil penalties s 23DZZIK and 23DZZIL; offences 23DZZIQ and 23DZZIR). [LIB]

### 2.3 What Part IIBA means for GTT
- The prohibitions bite where a benefit goes to, or comes from, a **requester or a person connected to a requester**. Rent paid by a lab to a landlord who is not a requester is outside this regime on its face. R9 already records that GTT is not a requester today. [LIB] R9 Q4.1; HW035 questions 10 and 22 ask if the lessor is a requester or connected to one.
- **Condition to protect:** no GTT owner, director, family member or other connected person may be a medical practitioner, participating midwife or participating nurse practitioner who requests pathology. If any is, the arrangement is scrutinised, rent must meet the market test, and the room must be used for nothing else under the arrangement. [LIB]; Anthony or the accountant should confirm. Do not infer.
- Where the rules do apply, the rent route in s 23DZZIF(5) needs: market value (not substantially different), an ACC or lab established in the space within 60 days of the arrangement, and the space not used for any other purpose under the arrangement. A shared lounge is therefore awkward if a requester is involved (R9 design D1 handles this by declaring the lounge a common area). [LIB]
- The Department has written "please explain" letters to parties to about 450 collection centre leases (about 10 per cent of co-located leases) and says it will start civil proceedings if rents are not reduced to market. [SEC] hwlebsworth.com.au; medicalrepublic.com.au. The Department's compliance page (health.gov.au/topics/pathology/compliance) was not opened this round (connection reset).
- Penalties quoted by law firms: A$126,000 (individual) and A$1.26 million (company) in 2018 and 2020 articles; a later search summary quotes A$187,800 and A$1.878 million, which looks like indexation. Check the current figure. [SEC]/[SNIP]
- Practical rules for GTT (judgement, for a health lawyer to review): (1) fixed annual rent set from an independent valuer's market rent for the room plus a fair share of the common area; (2) no per-test, per-client, per-referral or volume-linked payment, in either direction; (3) no payment from the lab to GTT or its staff for "recruiting" that scales with collections; (4) keep a valuer's letter on file; (5) lodge the lease the way Services Australia requires. [JUDGE]

**Caution on WDP's model:** the "venue-rental" wording suggests WDP pays rent to the host for a room it operates, which matches the usual direction (lab pays host). Carole's site criteria (GPs on site, referral volumes) are medical-centre criteria and may not suit GTT. Ask her what GTT's volume (up to 18 tests a morning, appointment-only) is worth to WDP. [JUDGE]

**Franchise code note:** a franchise normally needs a business system, a brand and a fee paid to the franchisor. None of the lab pages describe this. A deal where the lab pays GTT rent, employs the collector and GTT recruits is a lease plus services deal, not a franchise. [JUDGE]; a lawyer should confirm.

## 4. Three ranked commercial structures GTT can propose

GTT keeps: head lease, brand, recruitment of collectors, luxury experience. Lab keeps: Medicare approval, clinical control, billing, employment of collectors.

### Rank 1: Lab-operated ACC sub-lease with GTT-nominated collectors (R9 Rank 1)
| Element | Terms |
|---|---|
| Premises | GTT head-leases the building. Sub-lease to the lab of the collection room (exclusive area) and the lounge as declared common area, at independently valued market rent plus proportionate outgoings. |
| Approval | Lab lodges HW035 and pays any ACC tax (A$2,000 per R9, unless co-located with an eligible lab). Lab owns the ACC approval. |
| Collectors | GTT advertises, interviews and nominates; lab is sole employer, trains, rosters and carries clinical responsibility. Lab collector is in the lounge for the full 2 hours per client (R9 D3). |
| Brand | Lab brand only in the legally required places (ACC approval notice, results paperwork). GTT brand everywhere else. Written co-branding clause. |
| Hours | Exclusive use 07:00 to 10:30 weekdays; appointment-only booking through GTT, listed on the lab's public site finder (R9 W1). |
| Why best | Lab keeps everything Medicare cares about; GTT gets control of the experience and recruitment; matches the committed 07:00 model. |
| Risks | Lab may refuse GTT recruitment control, appointment-only, or co-branding. ACC revocation grounds "reasonably accessible to the public" and "reasonable hours" (s 23DNG(1)(ba),(bb)) are undefined; R9 W1 to W4 are the answers. |

### Rank 2: Lab-run "venue-rental clinic" with a session roster (WDP's own model, adapted)
| Element | Terms |
|---|---|
| Premises | Licence (not lease) of the collection room for fixed sessions; lab pays an annual fee at market rent. |
| Collectors | Lab supplies and employs the collector. GTT drops recruitment but keeps a veto over a named collector who is a poor fit, plus a bench of 2 named people. |
| Brand | Lab brand inside the room; GTT brand in the lounge; protocol for a joint welcome. |
| Hours | Same 07:00 to 10:30 window; sessions confirmed 2 to 4 weeks ahead from GTT bookings. |
| Money effect | If the lab employs and charges GTT nothing, the repo model says GTT saves A$6,682.28 a month per collector (A$13,364.52 for two) and break-even falls to 6.235 morning clients. [LIB-repo] DECISION-LOG 2026-10-17 |
| Why second | Fastest to agree (it is WDP's standard offer), but GTT loses recruitment and roster control. Anthony closed in-house employment on 2026-09-19, so this is a fallback, to be taken only if no lab will accept Rank 1. |

### Rank 3: Two-lab panel with one primary lab
| Element | Terms |
|---|---|
| Idea | Offer the room to one primary lab for 07:00 to 10:30, and a second lab a defined slot (for example afternoon standard bloods) or a right of first refusal, so labs compete on terms. |
| Why | Creates competitive pressure and fall-back cover if a lab withdraws. Helps R9's public-accessibility concern if the second lab collects walk-in standard tests in posted hours. |
| Risks | Two authorities in one room needs two ACC approvals, floor plan arrangements and a clear who-collects-what rule; labs may refuse any sharing. Most complex. [JUDGE] |

**Not ranked (long-term option):** GTT's own Category S glucose lab and ACC (R9 Rank 3). "Buying a franchise" is not available. Buying a small existing approved authority is a legal and commercial acquisition; no listing found; not recommended. [JUDGE]

## 7. Verified vs judgement summary

**Verified (official page or statute text):** WDP, Laverty, Dorevitch and QML (and Abbott) use the identical "Licensed Collection Centre in your practice" line with no published terms; PathWest has 76 licensed collection points and works with practices; Clinipath publishes a BusinessDevelopment email and a property manager for new approved collection centres; collectors must be employees of the authority for Medicare to pay (s 16A(5AB)(c)); permitted-benefit tests in s 23DZZIF; s 23DA(1) site ownership rule; s 23DNK and s 23DNL are not payment rules.

**Secondary (check):** the 20 per cent market-rent test and regulations 76 and 77; proportionate outgoings example; HPOS lodging since 2018; the Department's 450 letters; penalty amounts; third-party directory entries for Carousel, Cannington and Forrestfield.

**Judgement:** every "likely yes/no" in section 5; the three rankings; that a lease-plus-services deal is not a franchise; that no WA pharmacy-hosted centre exists (only NOT FOUND); that WDP's volume-linked rent wording should be reset to a valuer's figure.
