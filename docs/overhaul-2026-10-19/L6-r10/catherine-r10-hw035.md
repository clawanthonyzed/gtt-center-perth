## Job A: the "non-financial agreement" idea
1. The line "Total leased area: other correspondence signed by both parties" is not on form HW035. It is on the Services Australia ACC web page (updated 26 May 2026), in a table about CHANGES to an existing approval. It only corrects the area figure. It cannot replace the occupancy agreement.
2. Good news on the same page: "If the APA has a non-financial arrangement to occupy the space, we require a signed agreement with the owner of the premises" with "the standard clauses and conditions expected in a standard lease". So rent-free occupancy is a recognised route.
3. Catches: it says "owner" (GTT will be a tenant, so get the landlord to sign or consent); the agreement must look like a real lease; and the law's definition of an eligible collection centre says "owned, leased or sub-leased", so a licence alone is less safe.
4. Safest structure: GTT holds the head lease; lab gets a nil-rent sub-lease of the collection room ONLY, plus a licence over the lounge as a shared area. Control of everything else stays with GTT by contract. A one-room sub-lease does not let the lab run the venue.
5. Nothing to "transfer": the ACC approval always belongs to the lab (APA). GTT cannot hold one.
6. Staff: the approval rule (Principles 2020 s 9(3)) would accept contracted staff, but Medicare only pays if the collector is an EMPLOYEE of the lab (Act s 16A(5AB)(c)). So the lab must be the sole employer. GTT may recruit and nominate.
7. Do not let GTT direct clinical work: it risks making GTT a "provider" under Part IIBA, which would make gifts or perks to GPs and midwives illegal.
8. Rent-free and free collectors are lawful ONLY while GTT is not a requester and not connected to one. If GTT ever employs, contracts or directs a GP, midwife or nurse practitioner, the free staff become a prohibited benefit (s 23DZZIF(7)(b)). Penalty up to 6,000 penalty units.
9. Correction to Round 9: the market-rent rule applies only where the landlord is, or is connected to, a requester. It is not a general rule.
10. Never take payments per test or per referral, and never give clinicians gifts or commissions (s 23DZZIR, up to 5 years jail).
11. A 20-point clause list for a health lawyer is in the full file (Part A6).

## Job B: special circumstances
12. Correct instrument: Health Insurance (Approvals for Eligible Collection Centres) Principles 2020, F2020L01179, in force, no amendments found.
13. s 8(2)(d)(i) is NOT an approval power. It sits inside the lab's undertaking to follow the NPAAC Collection Centre Guidelines "except so far as the Minister accepts that compliance ... is not reasonably practicable in particular circumstances".
14. The Explanatory Statement says this acceptance is "an administrative action" and the Principles "are not the source of the Minister's power". It cannot waive anything in the Act (lab category, occupancy, tax, employee rule, Part IIBA).
15. "Special circumstances" in the ACC rules is about backdating only, and Services Australia says backdating is not available for a new ACC.
16. No special-circumstances ACC approval exists. No remote-area exception exists. Real levers: written guidance; item-by-item Guideline acceptance requested by the lab; discretionary (not automatic) revocation under s 23DNG; reconsideration after refusal.
