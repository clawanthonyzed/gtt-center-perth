# GTT Center Perth: Can we say "no men"? (Catherine, Legal Consultant, 2026-10-19)

General legal information from the statute text, not formal legal advice. Get a WA discrimination lawyer or the Equal Opportunity Commission (EOC) to sign off before launch wording is printed.

## The short answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pregnancy-only lounge (item 66548), men not booked | **Safe (LOW risk)**, as long as the rule is "you must be pregnant", not "you must be a woman". |
| Turning away a pregnant trans man or non-binary person | **Not safe (HIGH risk).** That is gender identity discrimination. Book every pregnant person. |
| Non-pregnant women's glucose test (item 66542) with men barred | **Not safe as it stands (HIGH risk).** No automatic exemption clearly covers it. |
| Should we stick with the pregnancy model only? | **Yes, for the lounge.** It is the one model the law clearly supports. |

## A) Pregnancy-only model

**What the law says (plain English):**
1. Refusing a service because of someone's sex is unlawful: federal Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (SDA) s 22, WA Equal Opportunity Act 1984 (EOA) s 20.
2. But a pregnancy test is refused to a man **because he is not pregnant**, not because he is a man. A non-pregnant woman is turned away too. "Not pregnant" is not a protected ground, so this is not direct sex discrimination (SDA s 5(1), EOA s 8(1)).
3. A "must be pregnant" rule does affect men more than women. That is checked as indirect discrimination, and it is lawful if it is reasonable (SDA s 7B, EOA s 8(2)). A pregnancy test that only works in pregnancy is about as reasonable as a condition gets.
4. Extra shields on top: giving women "rights or privileges in connection with pregnancy, childbirth" is not sex discrimination against a man (SDA s 31, EOA s 28). Services that by nature can only go to one sex are outside the rules (SDA s 32, EOA s 30). Special measures for pregnant women are allowed (SDA s 7D(1)(c), EOA s 31(b)).

**Is it 100% exempt?** No law says "100%". But on the text, a rule that is really "pregnant people only" has three separate defences. Risk: **LOW.**

**The one real trap: gender identity.** The SDA protects gender identity (s 5B, s 22). A trans man or non-binary person can be pregnant and can be referred for item 66548. Turning them away because they are "not a woman" is **direct gender identity discrimination**. The Full Federal Court confirmed this kind of exclusion is unlawful in *Giggle for Girls Pty Ltd v Tickle* [2026] FCAFC 64 (15 May 2026): a "women-only" app that removed a trans woman paid A$20,000 damages plus up to A$100,000 costs. WA also protects people with a recognised gender change (EOA s 35AB, s 35AL). SDA s 31 only protects against a claim by "a man on the ground of his sex", so it would not cover refusing a pregnant trans man.

**Safe wording:** "A glucose tolerance test lounge for pregnant clients." "Designed around pregnancy." Women-centred branding, colours and imagery are fine. Never write "women only" or "no men" as a rule.

## B) Non-pregnant women's test (item 66542), men barred

**Item 66542 is the general diabetes glucose test.** Its Medicare description has no sex limit. Men need it too. So:

| Shield | Does it cover a women-only 66542 service? |
|---|---|
| One-sex services (SDA s 32, EOA s 30) | **No.** The test can be given to anyone, so it is not a service that "can only be provided to members of one sex". |
| Pregnancy, childbirth (SDA s 31, EOA s 28) | **No** for general testing. **Arguably yes** for women having their follow-up glucose test after gestational diabetes, because that is "in connection with pregnancy, childbirth". |
| Special measures (SDA s 7D(1)(a), EOA s 31(b)) | **Maybe.** Needs real evidence that women face a barrier and the women-only setting fixes it. The Human Rights Commission accepted women-only gym and pool sessions as a special measure (Griffith City Council, 20 June 2008), backed by surveys and a women's refuge need. Without evidence: weak. |
| Formal exemption | Possible. Federal: Human Rights Commission, SDA s 44 (up to 5 years, can carry conditions). WA: State Administrative Tribunal, EOA s 135 (up to 5 years, publicly advertised, EOC Commissioner takes part). One does not cover the other, so you would need both. |

**Risk if we just bar men from 66542 with no exemption: HIGH.** A man refused a referred test could complain to the Human Rights Commission or the WA EOC.

**Safest ways to still serve non-pregnant women:**
1. **Best: keep the lounge pregnancy-only.** Offer 66542 tests in separate "open session" times that anyone with a referral can book (men included). The lounge stays pregnancy-only on its own legal footing.
2. **Narrow it:** offer a post-birth follow-up glucose test for people who had gestational diabetes. That links to pregnancy and childbirth (s 31, s 28). Still admit trans and non-binary post-birth clients. Risk: LOW to MEDIUM.
3. **Women's sanctuary for 66542 only after:** collecting evidence (client surveys, cultural and religious needs, trauma-informed care) for a special measure, **and** ideally getting both exemptions. Allow 3 to 6 months. Risk with both exemptions granted: LOW. Without: MEDIUM to HIGH.

## Partners and support people

- Make the rule **the same for every partner, whatever their sex.** For example: "One support person per client, seated in the partners' area." Do not let female partners into the lounge while sending male partners elsewhere. That is sex discrimination in the use of facilities (SDA s 22, EOA s 20), and s 31 does not cover it because the partner is not the pregnant client.
- A separate partners' area for **all** partners is lawful and keeps the calm, client-only feel.

## If a man asks to book

Script: "Our lounge is a pregnancy service, so we can only book pregnant clients for this test. If you need a glucose tolerance test, you can book our open session on [day and time], or use any pathology collection centre." Never say "we don't serve men."

## Medicare point

Medicare sets no sex rule. Item 66548 is limited by **pregnancy** (its description says "in pregnancy"). Item 66542 has no pregnancy or sex limit. So any "women only" rule is ours, not Medicare's, and we must defend it ourselves.

## Recommendation

**Stick with the pregnancy model for the lounge.** Word everything around "pregnant clients". Book every pregnant person who has a referral. Run any non-pregnant testing either as open sessions or as post-gestational-diabetes follow-up. Only go "women's sanctuary" for general testing after both exemptions are granted.

| Option | Risk |
|---|---|

## Checklist before launch

- [ ] All signs, website and booking pages say "pregnant clients", never "women only" or "no men".
- [ ] Booking form asks "Are you pregnant and do you have a referral for a pregnancy glucose test?", not "Are you female?"
- [ ] Staff told in writing: every pregnant person with a referral is booked, whatever their gender.
- [ ] Partner policy is the same for all partners.
- [ ] Polite script for men and non-pregnant callers, pointing to an open session or another collection centre.
- [ ] No women-only 66542 sessions until the evidence file and both exemptions are done.
- [ ] Lawyer or WA EOC review of final wording (the EOC fact sheet invites this).
