## 5. Consult capacity, stated honestly

Inputs (Anthony): 18 clients a day, 20 minute consult, one consult room, one midwife a day.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Consult time needed a day | 18 x 20 min = 360 min = **6.0 hours** |
| Paid hours a day | 7.5 |
| Utilisation of paid hours | **80 percent** (6.0 / 7.5) |
| Utilisation of hours on site | 75 percent (6.0 / 8.0) |
| Time left for notes, phone calls, urgent results, referral letters | 90 paid minutes, about 5 minutes a client |
| Earliest finish if all 18 consults run back to back from 09:00 | 15:00 |

So the room fits in the day with no spare buffer on a full 18-client day. It is not an under-used midwife.

Two problems the arithmetic shows (neither is decided here):

1. **Timing against the 25 minute cadence.** Two clients start every 25 minutes (a pair). Two 20 minute consults need 40 minutes, so one room cannot keep pace with the cadence. If every client has the consult after the final draw (first draw plus 120 minutes), the first pair is ready at 09:00 and the ninth pair at 12:20. The room serves a pair every 40 minutes, so the ninth pair waits 120 minutes and the average pair waits about 60 minutes (15 minutes more per pair). The last consult ends at 15:00.
2. **When does the consult happen?** Anthony says the client comes only for the GTT referral. If the consult must come before the blood is drawn, a midwife starting at 09:00 cannot cover the 07:00 to 08:55 draws, and one room cannot feed the cadence. The repo does not say when the consult happens (not in repo). Options for Anthony: (a) consult after the test; (b) consult on an earlier day or by telehealth; (c) midwife starts earlier. Whether the request must exist before the draw is a question for Catherine; this file does not state the law.
