## 2. The honest clinical-synergy test

Facts: the glucose test is done at 24 to 28 weeks (standard window in many guidance pages; V in search results, though none were WA Health pages, so the WA referral pathway is U). Fertility clinics' published pathways end well before then: Monash IVF's early pregnancy service ends monitoring at 16 weeks.

Verdict (J):
- A fertility clinic is not the person who requests the glucose test and has no clinical reason to be in the loop at 24 to 28 weeks. The request comes from whoever manages the pregnancy: obstetrician, private midwife or GP. Fertility clinics are a good audience for brand awareness and patient education, not the main referral source.
- They are a source of early trust and a waitlist: a patient can be told, in general terms, that a dedicated test lounge exists for when she reaches that stage. The booking must follow her own doctor's request, not the clinic's steering.
- Real clinical overlap is strongest where one doctor is both fertility specialist and obstetrician and delivers at SJOG Subiaco (Love, McCreery), and where the fertility group runs an early pregnancy service (Monash). Those doctors may be the requester for their own patients, which makes the legal guardrails in section 3 more important, not less.
- I found no verified source on whether IVF pregnancies carry a different test schedule or higher gestational diabetes risk, so make no such claim.

## 3. Partnership models and legal guardrails (Round 10 library)

Source texts: HIA-PATHOLOGY-REQUESTS-AND-ARRANGEMENTS.md (verbatim Health Insurance Act 1973, compilation of 19 Sept 2026, Part IIBA ss 23DZZIA to 23DZZIU) and r10/reed-r10-summary.md. Research, not legal advice. Health lawyer review is already on the R10 next-steps list.

What the Act says (V, library):
- A requester is a doctor, participating midwife or participating nurse practitioner who requests tests, and anyone connected to one. A provider is the lab side.
- s 23DZZIL: a provider breaches if it offers or gives a requester (or connected person) a benefit that would be reasonably likely to induce requests, or relates to the pathology business, unless it is a "permitted benefit". Civil penalty 600 penalty units (individual) or 6,000 (company). s 23DZZIK is the mirror for requesters who ask for or accept.
- s 23DZZIF permitted benefits include market-value payments for property or services, proportionate shared-cost payments, and room payments only where the space is used for nothing else under the arrangement.
- s 23DZZIF(7): never permitted if related to the number, kind or value of requests, or if it is staff or equipment provided at the requester's premises to provide pathology (so no collector stationed in a fertility or obstetric clinic).
- s 23DZZIF(8): a benefit counts as related to requests if it depends on the requester sending all or a share of requests to one provider.

What follows (J, for a health lawyer to confirm):
- GTT should treat itself as on the provider side, because it hosts the lab's room. So nothing of value goes to a requester: no payment, free service, gift, sponsored event, free staff or equipment, discount for the doctor or family, revenue share or commission.
- A clinic or doctor must not ask for or take any such benefit. If a doctor owns part of GTT she is a connected person; R10 flagged this for Anthony to confirm and I have not assumed.
- What a clinic can say to a patient: "You may choose any approved collection centre. Your request form is valid at any of them." This follows the R10 direction; I did not re-verify the sentence in the statute this round, so Catherine should confirm the exact legal basis before it goes into any material.
- Realistic: (a) an information sheet describing the lounge, hours, how to book, naming the lab, and saying it is one of several approved centres; (b) a patient-education talk or stand at an antenatal class the clinic organises, at GTT's own cost, under the clinic's rules; (c) a neutral listing among options, no exclusivity; (d) clinical content on what to expect on the morning, reviewed by the lab. Each needs lawyer sign-off.
- Not realistic or unsafe: co-location of the lounge inside a clinic; co-branding that implies endorsement or preferred-provider status; any referral agreement; any arrangement where a clinic's benefit rises with volume.

## 4. Why the lounge could appeal, and an outreach framework (notes, not an email)

Verifiable context only:
- Fertility groups already position on care and privacy: Monash "Our patients come first" (V https://monashivf.com/locations/wa); Oasis built two receptions "to maintain a boutique feel and a degree of privacy" (V https://8i.net.au/case-study/oasis-fertility-centre); Fertility North describes a nurturing, empathetic environment (search summary).
- Monash itself says there is "a significant gap in care" after fertility discharge (V release above). That shows a recognised need for support between clinics; the link to a calm morning test lounge is J.
- Private maternity hospitals already sell comfort add-ons (Ramsay Joondalup post-birth staycation, V https://www.ramsayhealth.com.au/News/General-News/Luxury-post-birth-staycation-for-parents), so a luxury-adjacent maternal service is not out of step with the market.
- Not found: any published survey showing fertility patients prefer a luxury test setting. Do not claim it.

Outreach framework (J, notes only)
Offer:
- A short plain information sheet: 07:00 start, appointment only, pregnant clients only, named approved lab, and the "any approved centre is valid" statement.
- Willingness to speak at a patient education session the clinic organises, at GTT's own cost, no payment either way.
- A named contact for clinical questions, with the lab able to answer the doctor's questions about the test process.
- A patient-experience feedback loop that shares no results with anyone but the requester.
Never offer or accept:
- Payment, rent, gifts, meals, free treatments, discounts, sponsorship, free staff or equipment, revenue share, commission, or any benefit tied to volume or to sending patients to GTT.
- Exclusivity, "preferred provider" wording, or claims that others are inferior.
- Collectors or lounge staff placed in a clinic.
- Any use of patient data for marketing.
- Any approach to a doctor connected to GTT's owners without legal advice first.
Sequence (J): health lawyer review and lab agreement first; then information-only approach, starting with obstetricians at SJOG Subiaco and one group-level contact at Monash; Anthony signs off every message first.
