## 1. Brand Vision

**B.** GTT Center Perth (working name) becomes the premium women's wellness destination every expecting Perth mother wants to attend for her GTT — and, for a meaningful share of them, the place she keeps choosing long after the test is behind her. Longer-term, it becomes the reference model a second Perth site and eventual other-city ventures are built from, not a one-off (`OPERATING-COMMERCIAL-ARCHITECTURE.md` §14, `VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §17).

## 2. Brand Ambition

**B.** Near-term: prove the Perth flagship delivers both its scheduling promise (18/day, zero collisions) and its emotional promise (the "unexpectedly lovely" reaction) at real volume (`18-CLIENT-COMMERCIAL-STRESS-TEST.md` §13). Mid-term: a second Perth site, standardising what this document defines as constant (§12, §17). Long-term: a genuine multi-city premium women's wellness brand for which GTT is the credibility anchor, not the ceiling — PM/standalone wellness is explicitly the growth engine (`OPERATING-COMMERCIAL-ARCHITECTURE.md` §3, `docs/experience/RETURN-LOOP.md`).

## 3. Category Definition

**B.** The question this section answers, directly: *what is this business that does not already exist in the customer's mind?* Not a pathology lab (no wait management, no hospitality, purely transactional — `MINIMUM-VIABLE-BRAND-IDENTITY.md` §4.3). Not a day spa (no genuine clinical credibility underneath — `docs/brand-guide.md` §11's "we are NOT a pregnancy day spa" rule, retained). Not a maternity boutique (not baby-themed, not product-retail-led). Not generic "women's health" (too broad, implies ongoing clinical care this venture doesn't provide). **The actual answer: a premium women's wellness house that happens to deliver a mandatory clinical test as its entry point** — the clinical event is real and credible, but it is one room's job, not the whole building's (`VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §10). No direct WA competitor exists (`docs/business-plan.md` §7); the two named east-coast comparables (MIWM Melbourne, The GTT Lounge Sydney) both lean descriptive/literal in naming and narrower in scope (single-client, smaller menu) — this venture is establishing a new category in WA, not fighting for share of an existing one.

## 4. Target Customer

**A**, extensively established, not re-litigated. Two personas (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`):
- **AM/referred:** a woman referred for a GTT at 24-28 weeks. She didn't choose this appointment, is fasting, and carries some anxiety about the needle, the result, or both.
- **PM/self-directed:** any woman booking massage, nails, hair, or brows for herself — pregnant or not, GTT client or not — with zero patience for anything that reads as medical or maternal.

## 5. Positioning

**B.** Position relative to seven adjacent categories, none copied wholesale:

| Relative to | Position |
|---|---|
| Pathology/collection centres | Same clinical rigour, radically more humane delivery — the wait is the product, not dead time to be endured |
| Maternity care | Not a maternity clinic; doesn't focus on the baby — focuses on the woman, in this specific moment |
| Women's health generally | Narrower and more specific than "women's health" — one real clinical event, not ongoing care |
| Boutique wellness/day spa | Higher clinical credibility, broader service breadth, multi-client concurrency without feeling processed |
| Beauty salons | Embedded clinical trust a beauty salon has no reason to carry |
| Luxury hospitality | Borrows the *service register* (unhurried, name-known, anticipatory) — not the price-tier exclusivity signalling |
| Premium lifestyle brands | Aspirational, but the A$250 entry package must still feel like it belongs to the founder's stated "every expecting mother" ambition, not a top-decile-income-only proposition |

## 6. Brand Promise

**A**, already tested and reused across every downstream document without revision: *"However she arrives — nervous, fasting, referred, unfamiliar with the building — she leaves cared for, and she wants to come back on a day she didn't have to."* (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`)

## 7. Functional / Emotional Benefits

**A.** Functional: a mandatory ~2.5-hour medical appointment converted into productive, enjoyable time — collection happens on schedule, the wait is filled with a real service. Emotional: feeling looked after, without feeling managed.

## 8. Differentiation

**B.** Multi-client concurrent model (not single-client boutique, unlike MIWM); full AM+PM breadth in one venue; real clinical rigour under genuine NATA-adjacent accreditation, not a lifestyle brand improvising healthcare; the explicit design principle that the wait is the product, not friction to minimise; and — the least imitable of the five — the confirmed dual-business architecture (a credible clinical morning *and* a credible standalone afternoon under one roof), which no identified comparable commits to fully on both sides at once.

## 9. Brand Personality

**B**, independently convergent across three separate bodies of work — genuinely reliable, not a single team's opinion. Carried from `docs/brand-guide.md`'s original sketch: **a woman in her mid-30s with excellent taste and no interest in performing it — professional but not corporate, warm but not gushing, knows a great deal and doesn't need to prove it, remembers your name.** Both Claude Design naming explorations independently arrived at compatible variations of this same person (Elowen: "considered, knowing, unhurried"; Solena: "warm, generous, sensory") — this is the shared spine underneath whichever name is chosen, not two different people.

## 10. Naming Territory

**B.** The frozen seven candidates investigated (`docs/naming/CLAUDE-NAMING-INVESTIGATION.md`) — ELOWEN, SOLENNE, SOLENA, ELAVEN, ELOWYN, ELAWEN, ELOVEN — define one clear, consistent territory: **real, established-sounding given names** (not invented or algorithmically-generated strings), with Cornish/French/Spanish-adjacent European roots, feminine and elegant without being overtly floral, maternal, or clinical. This is a deliberate, evidence-tested territory, not an arbitrary starting point — it satisfies the founder's own brief (fashion/beauty character, European luxury influence, "established-feeling rather than obviously invented/startup-like," able to stand alone as a master brand) better than the three alternative directions logged earlier in the project's history (descriptive/clinical — "GTT Center Perth" itself; metaphor/warmth-led — "Wait and Glow"; place+reassurance — "The GTT Room") ever did, per `docs/architecture/BRAND-IDENTITY-FRAMEWORK.md` §1's own comparison. **A required-qualities checklist for the final name, derived from this venture's actual, current commitments** (not the older, since-revised brand-guide.md framing):
1. Works as a standalone master brand independent of "GTT" — the confirmed PM/standalone business (§13 below) has to carry the name on its own.
2. Scales to a multi-venue, multi-city ambition (§2) without needing a rename.
3. Carries genuine premium/luxury register without reading as cold or exclusionary (§9's personality).
4. Passes a spoken referral test cleanly — this is a word-of-mouth-driven business (`docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` §3's own weighting).
5. Clears, or is realistically clearable, on Australian trademark/business-use grounds (§11).

**A discrepancy worth flagging honestly, not silently smoothed over:** this round's brief references "Solara, Aven, Avela, Avella" as part of the previously-covered territory. None of these four names appear anywhere in this repository's actual naming research (`docs/naming/CLAUDE-NAMING-INVESTIGATION.md`'s frozen seven, `NAME-INVESTIGATION-REPORT.md`, `NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md`, or the two Claude Design boards) — the closest real candidates on record are ELAVEN and ELAWEN, which were investigated, found commercially clean but weak on brand fit, and deprioritised (never eliminated on evidence, just never preferred over ELOWYN). Rather than fabricate assessment for four names with no actual research behind them, this section names the discrepancy directly: if Anthony has explored Solara/Aven/Avela/Avella in a channel not yet committed to this repository, that work should be added and read before treating them as part of the established territory — they are not currently assessable here.

## 11. Candidate Name Assessment

**Status: D — genuinely unresolved, correctly so.** This section does not select a name.

The three names that survived to a full, fair comparison (`docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` §3, weighted 17-criterion matrix) are:

|---|---|---|

The prior strategic audit (`docs/naming/SOLENA-ELOWEN-STRATEGIC-HANDOVER-DECISION-AUDIT.md`) found a near-statistical-tie on creative/strategic grounds alone (70.8% vs 71.1%) even *after* weighting trademark risk as heavily as the founder's own top priority. The gap that does exist (SOLENA 71.6% vs ELOWEN 68.8% in the later, more complete matrix) is real but not decisive — it moved mainly because the founder's own confirmed retail strategy (third-party resale only, `docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` §0) correctly reduced how much weight SOLENA's Class 3 trademark conflict should carry *as a strategic-fit question*. **That trademark conflict has not gone anywhere as a legal question, and this document does not treat it as resolved or diminish it:** AU 2202790 (SOLENA, word mark, Class 3 — cosmetics/skin/hair/face/fragrance, registered 2022, owner Owen Matthews) remains a live, unresolved registration directly in one of this venture's two most relevant trademark classes. `docs/naming/AUSTRALIAN-TRADEMARK-CLEARANCE-BRIEF.md` is written and ready to send to a licensed Australian trade marks attorney; per founder instruction, that engagement is deliberately deferred to a later funding/validation milestone, not happening now. **This means the honest answer to "is one candidate strategically superior" is: not on brand/creative grounds — the two leaders are close enough that the deciding factor is the legal question neither creative analysis nor this document can settle.** See §28/§29 and the separate Founder Decision Register for how this is carried forward as a CHOOSE BETWEEN item pending attorney input, not a NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT item requiring a decision today.

**Brand architecture implication (new to this document, not previously stated):** whichever of the three is eventually chosen, or if none is, the master brand name must do double duty from day one — carrying the GTT/clinical-adjacent premium concept *and* standing entirely on its own for the PM wellness business, per §13's confirmed AM/PM architecture. All three surviving candidates satisfy this; a return to the original "GTT Center Perth" placeholder or a similarly descriptive direction would not (§10's required-qualities #1).

```
  · a short descriptor (e.g. "Women's House · Perth" — tested, not adopted, docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md §2)
        │
        ├── CLINICAL — the GTT, expressed as a named experience ("the morning"), not a department
        ├── WELLNESS — massage (future: recovery/relaxation-adjacent, not yet committed)
        ├── BEAUTY — nails, hair, brow/beauty
        ├── WOMEN'S SERVICES (future, not committed — no scope invented beyond what exists)

```

**The governing principle, unchanged:** every branch reports to one brand promise (§6), not to a shared visual department-store logic. The failure mode this architecture exists to avoid: reading as "medical clinic + day spa + gift shop, stapled together."

## 13. AM / PM Relationship

**A** (the operating facts) **+ B** (the brand translation). Per the now-locked rules: AM is GTT-constrained (every service ≤45min, absolute), PM begins 12:30 with no duration ceiling, 12:00-12:30 is a soft transition not a closure.

**What stays constant across both:** the master brand voice and personality (§9, §15); the physical rooms and stations (confirmed shared, `VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §16 — no second premises, no separate PM-only zone); the dual-qualified staff pool; the core hospitality principles (name-based reception, no clipboard, the welcome-card and leaving-gesture mechanics, `docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`); the "only one room reads clinical" rule.

**What changes:** the emotional register — AM leads with *reassurance* (a captive, somewhat anxious audience who didn't choose to be there), PM leads with *desire* (a self-directed guest who chose this appointment for herself); appointment duration/flexibility (AM fixed ≤45min slots, PM 30-120+min); the specific service mix skew (AM favours fast, showcase-value services; PM supports the full catalogue including hero/destination services, `OPERATING-COMMERCIAL-ARCHITECTURE.md` §7.3).

**How the transition should feel:** per the operational stress test's own recommendation (independently arrived at, matching the founder's locked 12:30 decision) — a quiet, already-finished-feeling handover, not a visible "shift change." The founder's own language (avoid it feeling "like a busy clinic changing shifts") is the design brief for this specific 30-minute window (`18-CLIENT-OPERATIONAL-STRESS-TEST.md` §5, §12).

**Does PM need a separate sub-identity? B, no.** Every source document that has touched this question converges on the same answer: same brand, two registers, not two voices (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`'s own explicit framing). A visibly different PM sub-brand would undermine the exact thing the return-loop economics depend on — a woman recognising, at 3pm on a Thursday she chose for herself, the same trustworthy place she was cared for at 8am on a morning she didn't choose.

## 14. Customer Experience Principles

**B**, the five governing principles (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`, unchanged, retained in full): (1) the clinical process is real and should look it, exactly once; (2) nothing that happens should require her to manage it herself; (3) the wait is scheduled, not endured; (4) the afternoon customer must never feel like she's in someone else's clinic; (5) leave her with something specific to remember and repeat.

**The 11-stage journey, mapped per the founder's specified structure** (extends, does not duplicate, `docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s own more granular map):

| Stage | She sees / hears / feels / expects | Deliberately avoid | What makes it premium |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DISCOVERY** | A specific, describable sentence from a friend or referring midwife, not a generic pitch (`docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s "specificity travels, genericness doesn't") | Generic "wellness experience" marketing language; overclaiming clinical authority | The category itself is the surprise — nobody expects this to exist |
| **BOOKING** | A simple, modern flow, first name asked early and used from that point | Anything reading as a clinical intake form | Speed and clarity, not a form |
| **ARRIVAL** | Deliberately under-signed exterior, doesn't look like a medical building from the street | A clinical shopfront (lightbox/vinyl-heavy signage) | Restraint as a premium signal, not compromise |
| **CHECK-IN** | Expected by name, no clipboard, no reference number | "What's your name/DOB/reference number" | The environment itself does credibility work here |
| **GTT** | Competence delivered warmly at the needle moment; one pre-emptive acknowledgement of the needle before it happens | Over- or under-acknowledging the needle; visible clinical equipment outside the one room that needs it | Competence, not performance, at the single highest-anxiety point |
| **WELLNESS SERVICE** | A real, full-quality treatment inside the fixed slot — "a real massage, not some pampering" | Rushed or truncated service to protect the clinical clock | The slot is sized so the service never feels compromised by the schedule underneath it |
| **LOUNGE** | An itinerary card handed over on arrival — she should never have to ask "what happens next" | Unstructured dead time; silence about timing (the single largest source of anxiety in a multi-hour wait) | The single most valuable physical object in the business does more brand work than the website |
| **DEPARTURE** | "That was unexpectedly lovely," not "my appointment is finished" — food and a considered small gesture at the exact point she's fasted and just had blood drawn | A rushed or generic send-off | The lowest-cost, highest-emotional-effect moment in the entire visit |
| **POST-VISIT** | A genuinely useful follow-up (when results reach her doctor) before any PM mention, riding alongside it once, briefly | Leading with an upsell before the outstanding clinical question is addressed | Usefulness first, invitation second |
| **PM RETURN** | Discovery of the PM business at departure, not during the clinical visit; a specific single next-booking suggestion, not a menu | A sales pitch during the AM visit itself | The invitation mechanism itself is not yet designed (the previously-described physical departure gesture is REMOVED, not approved) — not a separate hard-sell ask either way |
| **ADVOCACY** | A specific, one-sentence-describable experience — "they gave me breakfast and did my nails after my last blood draw" (corrected 2026-08-16 — the client remains fasting throughout the GTT and receives no results at the venue; food/treatment happen after the final draw, not "while waiting for results," and results are never given on-site — see `docs/gtt-clinical-protocol.md`) | Asking directly for reviews/referrals before the experience has earned them | Advocacy is a byproduct of specificity, never requested |

## 15. Tone of Voice

**B**, extending the existing, well-tested voice rules (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`) into the founder's specific register progression: **calm → intelligent → warm → reassuring → sophisticated → quietly luxurious.** Complete sentences, plain English, active voice. No clinical jargon ("patient," "reference number," "results") in customer-facing copy — a first name and a plain description instead. Reassurance where it's needed (fasting instructions, the morning of), invitation where it isn't (afternoon bookings, service menus) — same brand, two registers.

**Explicit avoid list:** clinical language outside the one room that needs it; wellness clichés ("journey," "empowered," "self-care," "wellness warrior"); forced femininity or performed warmth; corporate healthcare language ("patient," "provider," "service delivery"); fake luxury language ("bespoke," "curated" used generically, "elevated" as a filler adjective); excessive exclamation marks; "mummy"/"mumma" language, which is not used anywhere in this venture's confirmed customer-facing copy and should not be introduced — she is a woman having a medical test, not being addressed by her future relationship to her child.

## 16. Visual Identity Principles

**B.** Restraint over decoration — fewer, better choices, evidenced independently by both Claude Design naming explorations and `PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`'s own cost-discipline findings as the single most reliable premium-perception lever available. Wordmark-led, not symbol-first — a monogram or device is earned only if it does real, ownable work (e.g. the Elowyn board's "EY" monogram, tested and found genuinely distinctive), never added by default. Consistency across every touchpoint matters more than any single beautiful asset — the same handful of low-cost, high-leverage decisions (§14) repeated without drift is the actual premium signal, not an expensively finished single room.

## 17. Colour Direction

**C — a fresh direction is required, not a continuation of the existing sage/terracotta system.** This needs to be stated plainly rather than silently carried forward: `docs/brand-guide.md`'s original palette (sage, terracotta accent, deep slate) and this session's own earlier `outputs/brand/BRAND-SYSTEM.md` (also sage-led) were both built for a different brief — a botanical, "wellness lounge" register, not this round's explicit "natural, earthy... avoid generic spa green" direction. Sage-as-primary sits close enough to "generic spa green" that it should not be assumed to survive this new brief unmodified. **Recommended territory (not final, no hex codes locked):** warm natural neutrals as the base (limestone, warm stone, unbleached linen, soft travertine) — closer to the register the Elowen naming board explored (limestone/mushroom/warm neutrals) than to brand-guide.md's cooler sage system; one grounding deep tone for text/ink — an umber, walnut, or deep espresso rather than a cool charcoal, keeping warmth even in the darkest value; one restrained accent used sparingly, not as a field colour — a genuinely earthy tone (terracotta, aged clay, or a muted olive/khaki that reads as mineral rather than minty) rather than sage-green or blush. **Explicitly avoid:** pink, lavender, baby blue, and any colour combination that reads as maternity-cliché; an overly beige, flattened "Instagram luxury" palette with no real depth; sage/mint as a dominant field colour (too close to "generic spa green," the founder's own named avoid). This direction should be developed and finalised as real swatches once a name exists (§11), since the Cornish/botanical-adjacent territory (Elowen) and the Spanish/French warmth territory (Solena) could each reasonably pull the exact palette slightly differently — a genuine, disclosed dependency, not a delay tactic.

## 18. Typography Direction

**C.** Full hierarchy required: wordmark/logotype, headings, body, digital UI, signage, print. **Explicit correction carried forward:** Italiana was already tested and found too hard to read for primary brand use in earlier work this session — it is not being reintroduced here, and any future designer should be told this directly rather than rediscovering it. No ornate fashion serif chosen purely because it photographs as luxurious — legibility at small sizes (appointment reminders, signage from a distance, mobile UI) is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. **Recommended territory:** a considered, editorial-register serif for the wordmark and major headings (in the spirit of what both Claude Design boards independently reached for — a didone or a refined old-style serif such as the Libre Caslon Display / Bodoni Moda territory already tested, not a decorative script), paired with a clean, highly legible humanist sans for body copy, UI, and signage support text (DM Sans or a comparable grotesque, already proven to work well in this session's landing-page build). Full weight/size hierarchy and exact typeface selection should be finalised once §11 resolves, since the wordmark itself needs to be drawn/set against a real name, not a placeholder.

## 19. Photography Direction

**B**, an already-well-established, name-independent direction carried forward without change: natural, diffused light; real, diverse, Perth-representative women, not stock-generic; unhurried and intimate rather than posed-corporate; no clinical equipment ever visible outside the one room that needs it; documentary-style over staged. Real photography requires a real venue and is correctly sequenced to post-venue-confirmation (`MINIMUM-VIABLE-BRAND-IDENTITY.md` §5) — stock photography against the same search terms and criteria is the appropriate interim substitute, not a lesser standard, simply an earlier-stage one.

## 20. Interior Design Language

**B**, translating `VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md`'s already-confirmed clinical-vs-wellness zoning (§10 of that document — only the Blood Collection Room reads clinical; Reception/circulation is neutral-transitional; the Lounge and departure moment are warm/hospitality-led; treatment rooms, Nail Station, and Hairdressing Area are wellness-led for both AM and PM) into a materials/mood direction, **C** below that point specifically:

- **Materials:** warm, natural, tactile — timber (one considered species used consistently, not multiple competing woods), natural stone or a convincing plaster finish, soft natural-fibre textiles, warm metal hardware (aged brass or bronze, not chrome or brushed steel, which read cold/clinical-adjacent).
- **Curves vs. straight lines:** a considered mix — architectural restraint (clean, straight joinery lines) in circulation/reception, softened by curved furniture silhouettes and rounded edges in the Lounge and treatment spaces specifically, where the body is meant to relax.
- **Lighting:** warm, dimmable, layered — never a flat clinical downlight outside the Blood Collection Room, already an `VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` **A**-tier commitment.
- **Acoustic treatment:** a real, already-disclosed open tension (`VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §9/§11/§23) — curtain-partitioned treatment rooms trade sound insulation for cost and flexibility; soft furnishing, textile wall treatment, and careful zoning can mitigate but not fully solve this, and it should not be presented as resolved.
- **Greenery:** used sparingly and considered, not as a default "spa" signifier — a single well-placed specimen plant reads more premium than distributed generic planters, consistent with the restraint principle (§16).
- **Scent:** genuinely unaddressed anywhere in this repository (**D**, already flagged in `VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §9) — a real, open design decision for a future phase, not attempted here.
- **Artwork/signage inside the venue:** minimal, considered, consistent with the wordmark-led visual identity (§16) — no decorative wall text/quotes, no generic "wellness" motivational language, consistent with §15's tone rules extended into the physical space.

## 21. Materials / Finishes

**C**, extending `PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`'s own evidenced finding (one or two consistently-executed material choices outperform many mediocre ones) into specifics: one genuinely good timber finish used at every guest-facing joinery point; one plaster or render finish used consistently across walls that need texture; one warm metal finish for all hardware, door pulls, and fixtures — the same finish everywhere, not mixed. **Explicitly avoid:** marble/gold cliché-luxury signalling (reads as trying, the opposite of restraint); raw/exposed industrial concrete or steel (too cold, risks reading clinical-adjacent); an excessive pastel material palette; laminate counters or anything that reads as a budget clinical waiting room (`docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s own "what signals cheap or clinical" list — fluorescent downlights, laminate counters, forms on a clipboard).

## 22. Signage

**C**, extending `PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`'s spend-discipline finding directly: a deliberately under-signed exterior (street-visible, not attention-seeking), engraved or cut lettering over standard vinyl/lightbox signage — a genuine cost premium already named by prior work as the correct trade against a "generic strip-mall clinic" read. Internal wayfinding should be minimal and typography-led, consistent with the wordmark-only visual identity direction (§16) — no separate iconography system invented for this purpose.

## 23. Uniform Direction

**C.** Tailored, natural-fibre fabrics, a muted tonal palette consistent with the interior direction (§17/§20) — not clinical scrubs, not a branded polo-shirt retail-staff look. **One genuine, unresolved nuance worth flagging rather than glossing over:** the phlebotomist role operates inside the one genuinely clinical room and may have real infection-control/practical requirements (closed shoes, easy-clean fabric, a functional pocket for supplies) that a purely aesthetic uniform brief can't override — this is a **D**, requiring input from the pathology partner (WDP) or a genuine occupational-health source before a final phlebotomist uniform spec is set, distinct from the treatment/reception staff uniform direction, which has no such constraint.

## 24. Digital / Web Direction

**B**, building directly on real, already-built work this session (`outputs/landing-page/`, not a hypothetical): mobile-first, clear value proposition above the fold, explicit trust signals, pregnancy-specific relevance stated plainly, simple two-path service explanation (AM/PM), strong single waitlist CTA, no fake testimonials/statistics/partnerships/credentials — a hard line already established and held. **What needs revision, not rebuilding:** the existing landing page's colour system (`outputs/brand/brand-tokens.css`) is sage-led and should be revisited once §17's earthy-neutral direction and §11's naming outcome are both resolved — the page's structure, copy discipline, and UX findings (including the real mobile-viewport bug found and fixed this session) remain valid and reusable regardless of the colour/name refresh.

## 26. Brand Do / Don't Rules

**B**, consolidated from every principle established above — a working checklist, not new content:

**Do:** lead with restraint; let the one clinical room look clinical and nowhere else; treat the wait as the product; use a first name before a form; make every visual/material choice traceable to a strategic reason; keep AM and PM as one brand in two registers; cost the low-impact/high-leverage welcome-card moment without hesitation (the previously-paired "leaving gesture" is REMOVED, not approved).

## 29. Brand Acceptance Criteria

**B — a working evaluation checklist for every future creative deliverable (logo, photography, interior fit-out, website, uniforms, signage) built from this foundation**, mirroring the design-critique discipline already proven effective on this venture's own landing-page work this session:

1. Does it look like a real Australian premium women's wellness business — not a pathology clinic, not a generic day spa, not a maternity brand?
2. Would a fasting, somewhat anxious, first-time GTT client trust it within the first ten seconds?
3. Would a referring midwife or OB feel comfortable recommending it to a patient?
4. Does it read as premium without reading as pretentious or exclusionary?
5. Does it read as warm without reading as childish or overtly maternal?
6. Is the one clinical room the *only* thing that reads clinical?
7. Does it work identically well at 8am (AM, reassurance-led) and 3pm (PM, desire-led) without feeling like two different businesses?
8. Is every visual, material, and language choice traceable to a specific principle in this document — or is it decoration without a strategic reason (§16, §26)?
9. Does it avoid every item on the explicit avoid lists (§15, §17, §26)?
10. Would it still look distinctive and correct in five years, or does it depend on a visual trend that will date?

If the honest answer to any of these is no, the work goes back for revision before it is treated as finished — the same standard this venture's own prior creative-production phases have already held themselves to.
