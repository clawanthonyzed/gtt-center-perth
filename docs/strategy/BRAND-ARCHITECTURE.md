# Brand Architecture

**Status:** Current, name-agnostic. Companion to `docs/strategy/BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`. Addresses how the business's current and reasonably foreseeable service lines sit under one master brand without it becoming a generic "everything for women" business — a real risk both the founder's brief and the Phase 1/Phase 2 design work flagged explicitly.

---

## The Architecture

```
[MASTER BRAND — the venue name]
  · Women's House · Perth  (descriptor, see docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md §2)
        │
        ├── CLINICAL
        │     GTT / pregnancy pathology collection
        │     Delivered by employed phlebotomists under a NATA-accredited partner's umbrella
        │     Expressed as a named experience ("the morning"), not a department name
        │
        ├── WELLNESS
        │     Massage
        │     (Future: recovery/relaxation-adjacent services, not yet committed)
        │
        ├── BEAUTY
        │     Nails, hair, brow/beauty treatments
        │
        ├── WOMEN'S SERVICES (future, not committed)
        │     Potential future offerings within the "for women" remit —
        │     not specified further here; no scope invented beyond what
        │     docs/business-plan.md actually commits to
        │
        └── RETAIL
              Third-party premium brands (confirmed: Gaia, Weleda, Mustela)
              Not a name-branded product line — see docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md §0
              (founder-confirmed 2026-08-14: not a strategic priority)
```

## The Governing Principle

**Every branch reports to one brand promise (`BRAND-STRATEGY-NAME-AGNOSTIC.md`'s "however she arrives, she leaves cared for"), not to a shared visual department-store logic.** The failure mode this architecture is designed to avoid is a business that reads as "medical clinic + day spa + gift shop, stapled together" — three different registers under one roof rather than one register expressed across three activities.

## Why "Named Experience," Not "Department"

Both design explorations independently arrived at the same structural insight and it's adopted here as a standing architecture rule: the GTT should be presented to the guest as **"the morning"** (or equivalent), not as "our pathology department" or "the GTT service." This is not cosmetic — a department name implies a business organised around service categories; a named experience implies a business organised around the guest's day. The same logic should extend to any future branch: "the afternoon," not "our beauty department."

## How Each Branch Is Expressed Without Diluting the Master Brand

**Clinical:** The only branch permitted to look and feel clinical, and only in the one room where it happens (`CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`'s arrival/GTT stages). Everywhere else, the clinical function is present in substance (real accreditation, real credentialed staff) but absent in surface signals (no visible equipment, no clinical language in front-of-house copy).

**Wellness and Beauty:** Presented as a single coherent "treatments" offering to the guest, not two separate categories — the distinction between massage and a manicure matters operationally (different staff, different rooms) but shouldn't matter brand-facing; both serve the same job (fill the wait, or stand alone as a booking) and should share one menu language and one booking experience.

**Women's Services (future):** Deliberately left unspecified here. The architecture has a slot for it because the founder's brief names it as a category worth holding open, but nothing should be invented to fill it — any real future service should be evaluated against the governing principle above (does it serve the one brand promise, or does it start to make the business read as generic) before it's added.

**Retail:** Structured explicitly as **curation, not manufacturing** — the master brand's role is selecting and presenting third-party premium brands well (Gaia, Weleda, Mustela), the same way a considered boutique curates rather than badges everything with its own name. This is the confirmed, founder-validated model (2026-08-14) and should not drift toward a name-branded product ambition without a fresh, explicit decision — see `docs/naming/NAMING-DECISION-STATE.md`.

## Guardrail: What Would Make This Architecture Fail

- Any branch adopting its own sub-identity (a different palette, a different voice) instead of expressing the master brand's one voice in a different context.
- The clinical branch's surface signals leaking into guest-facing spaces outside the one room that needs them.
- The "Women's Services" slot being filled reactively (whatever seems easy to add) rather than against the governing principle.
- Retail drifting from curation into house-branding without the founder-decision that would justify revisiting the Class 3 trademark question (`docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` §0).

## Multi-Venue Note

Per `docs/business-plan.md`, multi-venue expansion is an acknowledged future possibility, not yet committed or scheduled. This architecture is written to extend by address (a second venue running the identical branch structure) rather than by adding new categories at the first venue — consistent with the naming comparison's own scalability findings (`docs/naming/NAMING-FINAL-COMPARISON.md` §3), which favour "house of addresses" logic over category sprawl regardless of which name wins.
