# Visual Brand Direction

**Date:** 2026-08-14 | **Status:** Creative brief. Develops the visual territory locked in principle by `docs/strategy/BRAND-EXPERIENCE-FOUNDATION.md` §16-§20 into something a real designer/interior designer could work from — colour, typography, logo territory, materials, interior language, lighting, photography, digital and physical touchpoints. **This is not final production.** No name is selected, no logo is commissioned or drawn, no website is built, no packaging artwork is created, no property research resumes, and the locked operating model (18/day, 45-min AM ceiling, 12:30 PM start, 4/4/4 rooms) is not reopened.

**Governing brief, carried forward without dilution:** premium, luxurious, warm, natural, earthy, calm, sophisticated, feminine without being stereotypically feminine, timeless, quietly aspirational. *"A place every expecting mother wants to go to — and wants to return to."* Should feel like entering a beautiful private women's wellness house, not a medical appointment.

**Naming status, unchanged:** SOLENA and ELOWEN both remain viable (`docs/naming/NAMING-DECISION-STATE.md`), legal clearance still pending, no decision forced here. Per this round's specific instruction, §5 stress-tests the recommended visual system against both names directly — this is the load-bearing exercise of this document, not a formality.

---

## 1. Visual Identity Concept

The identity should feel like a **considered private house that happens to run a real clinical service in one room** — not a clinic with a beauty layer, not a day spa with a blood-test bolted on. The visual language borrows its warmth from residential/hospitality design (natural materials, soft light, restrained furniture) and its credibility from quiet material confidence rather than any clinical signalling (`docs/strategy/BRAND-ARCHITECTURE.md`'s "named experience, not department" principle, extended into materials for the first time here). The single organising idea: **restraint is the luxury signal, not decoration** — already independently proven across every prior phase of this venture's work (`PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`), now extended from a strategy principle into an actual palette, typeface pairing, and material list.

## 2. Brand Mood

Warm, not hot. Earthy, not rustic. Calm, not sleepy. Premium, not cold. Feminine, not pink. Timeless, not trend-chasing. The reference feeling: a beautifully lit, quiet, stone-and-timber private room mid-morning — natural light, nothing competing for attention, nothing announcing itself. **Explicitly not:** a boho/plant-filled wellness aesthetic, a clinical-white minimalism, a maternity-brand softness, or an "Instagram luxury" flattened beige palette with no real material depth.

## 4. Typography

Full hierarchy, each typeface justified on its own merits, not just named.

### Primary serif — recommend **Fraunces**

- **Luxury:** a genuinely warm, characterful contemporary serif with soft, slightly ink-trap-influenced detailing — reads as considered and editorial rather than generic or corporate.
- **Readability:** built with real optical-size ranges (a dedicated "text" cut alongside its display cut) — this is the specific, direct answer to last round's Italiana rejection: Fraunces is designed to still read cleanly at small sizes, not just look good large.
- **Pregnancy/maternity context:** warm and human without being soft in a maternal-cliché way — its serifs carry genuine weight and confidence, avoiding both cold minimalism and saccharine softness.
- **Digital use:** available as a variable font (weight, optical size, and a "soft" axis), giving one typeface family real range across a website's full hierarchy without needing a second display face.
- **Signage/small-format print:** its text-optical cut holds up at appointment-card and small signage scale in a way a pure display serif (Bodoni Moda, Libre Caslon Display — both explored in earlier naming-board work, both display-only) would not.
- **Longevity:** contemporary without being trend-locked to one specific micro-era of type fashion — it draws on old-style serif proportions rather than a single dated reference.
- **Australian market suitability:** free (Google Fonts), no licensing friction for a pre-launch venture, already proven deployable in this project's own prior web work.

### Secondary sans — recommend **DM Sans** (retained, not replaced)

Already proven in this project's own landing-page build (`outputs/landing-page/`) — clean, humanist, genuinely legible at UI scale, warmer in character than a purely engineered grotesque like Inter, which suits "warm not corporate" better. No reason to introduce a second sans when this one is already tested and working.

### Optional supporting typeface — **not recommended**

Two typefaces (Fraunces + DM Sans) is sufficient. A third supporting face was considered and rejected — consistent with the restraint principle (`docs/strategy/BRAND-EXPERIENCE-FOUNDATION.md` §16); adding one without a specific, real gap to fill would be decoration, not function.

## 5. Wordmark / Logo Territory

**Recommend Direction A (pure wordmark) as primary, with Direction B (a restrained monogram) as an optional secondary asset for small-format use only (social avatar, favicon) — not Direction C.**

**Why not Direction C (wordmark + subtle symbolic mark):** every symbolic-mark direction explored anywhere in this venture's prior work (the original `docs/brand-guide.md` leaf motif, any future floral/botanical device) sits closest to the explicit avoid list — floral cliché, maternal cliché, spa cliché. A "subtle" symbol is also the hardest of the three directions to execute with genuine restraint; it is far easier for a symbol to drift into decoration than for a well-set wordmark to. This is not a new conclusion — both prior Claude Design naming-exploration boards independently reached the same territory (wordmark-led, symbol-optional at most), and this document simply confirms it rather than reopening it.

**Why Direction A over B as primary:** the founder's own brief states the name should do most of the branding work. A confident, well-set wordmark in Fraunces, generously tracked, is enough — it does not need a monogram to feel complete, and a monogram used as a co-primary mark (rather than a small-format-only convenience asset) risks diluting the wordmark's own authority.

### 5.1 The Required Test — Does This System Work for BOTH Solena and Elowen?

This is the point of this section, not an afterthought.

**SOLENA, set in Fraunces:** 6 letters, soft consonants (S, L, N), a naturally rounded, symmetrical shape in caps — sits comfortably in a wide-tracked serif wordmark treatment, reads elegantly at both large (signage) and small (appointment card) scale. Works cleanly in any of the three colour options (§3), with a very slight natural lean toward Option 3's warmer register, given its own Spanish-adjacent sonic warmth.

**ELOWEN, set in Fraunces:** also 6 letters, similarly soft and feminine-sounding, with one genuinely distinctive letterform (the W) that needs slightly more careful tracking/kerning attention in a wide-set wordmark — Fraunces' variable width handles this comfortably, but it is a real, disclosed difference in execution effort, not a difference in outcome quality. Works cleanly in any of the three colour options, with a very slight natural lean toward Option 2's more heritage-register palette, consistent with its Cornish etymology.

**Verdict: yes, the core system is genuinely strategic, not name-flattering.** The recommended primary system — Fraunces wordmark, DM Sans support, Option 1 "Warm Stone" palette, wordmark-only territory — works comfortably and without compromise for either name. Neither requires a fundamentally different typeface, palette, or logo territory to feel correct; the differences are proportionate, expected letterform-level adjustments (tracking on the W; nothing structural), not a sign that the system was built around one name and retrofitted to the other.

**One genuine, honest asymmetry worth flagging, not smoothed over — this sits specifically at the *optional monogram* layer (§B), not the primary wordmark system:** SOLENA's initial "S" is a naturally strong, ownable single-letter monogram shape (a confident serif S reads well alone, at favicon scale, on a small item). ELOWEN's initial "E" is a comparatively generic single-letter shape with less inherent distinctiveness — this is exactly why the earlier ELOWYN naming-exploration board built its monogram from two letters ("EY") rather than one, and why ELOWEN's own board went symbol-free entirely rather than force a weak single-letter mark. **Practical implication:** if SOLENA is eventually chosen, a restrained "S" monogram is a genuinely viable optional secondary asset. If ELOWEN is chosen, the wordmark-only direction (no monogram at all) is the stronger path — not a downgrade, simply the better-fitting choice for that specific name. This is a real, name-specific difference, but it lives entirely at the *optional* layer — the primary, load-bearing wordmark system above is unaffected either way.

## 6. Material Palette

Target: **natural + luxurious, explicitly not rustic + boho.** The distinction matters and is easy to lose — rustic/boho reads as raw, undone, and casual (reclaimed timber, exposed rope, macramé, unfinished edges); this brand needs the same natural material *language* executed with precision, consistency, and a refined finish level instead.

| Category | Preferred | Acceptable alternative | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Limestone, travertine (honed, not tumbled/rustic-edged) | Engineered stone with a genuine honed-limestone visual match | Polished marble with heavy veining (reads cliché-luxury); raw/split-face stone (reads rustic) |
| Timber | A single considered species, warm mid-tone (e.g. American oak, warm walnut), used consistently | A convincing timber-look laminate only where budget genuinely requires it, never mixed with the real thing in the same sightline | Reclaimed/distressed timber (rustic); pale bleached "Scandi" timber (reads generic, not warm) |
| Wall finish | Textured plaster/render in a warm neutral | A high-quality matte paint finish with genuine depth, where plaster isn't feasible | Flat builder-grade paint; exposed raw concrete (cold, clinical-adjacent) |
| Textiles | Natural fibres — linen, wool, cotton bouclé — in the Section 3 palette | A high-quality natural-look synthetic blend where genuine natural fibre isn't durable enough for commercial use | Velvet in saturated jewel tones (reads generic luxury-cliché); anything glossy/synthetic-obvious |
| Metal/hardware | Brushed brass or bronze, warm-toned | Warm-toned brushed bronze-look powder coat where solid metal isn't budget-feasible | Chrome, polished nickel, or brushed steel (all read cold/clinical) |
| Glass | Used sparingly — reeded/fluted glass for privacy screening with texture | Clear glass with a warm-toned frame | Frosted acrylic (reads budget/clinical) |
| Greenery | One well-placed specimen plant per zone, considered, not distributed | A small number of genuinely well-chosen plants | Distributed generic planters everywhere (reads "default spa," the exact failure mode this brand must avoid) |

**Finish level:** refined-matte throughout — nothing high-gloss (reads cheap or clinical depending on the surface), nothing raw/unfinished (reads rustic). **Texture level:** present and tactile, but calm — one textured surface (plaster or stone) per zone is enough; texture should be felt, not visually competing for attention. **Joinery character:** clean, confident, minimal-detail joinery lines — restrained architecture, not ornate cabinetry. **Hardware character:** warm-toned, understated, solid-feeling — never a decorative flourish.

## 7. Interior Visual Language

Per `docs/strategy/VENUE-FUNCTIONAL-BRIEF.md` §10's already-confirmed zoning: only the Blood Collection Room reads genuinely clinical; everywhere else moves progressively warm → hospitality → wellness → luxury. Concise per-zone direction:

- **Exterior:** deliberately under-signed, doesn't read as a medical building from the street (`PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`'s restraint principle) — a single considered wordmark sign, engraved or cut lettering, warm material frame around the entry.
- **Entry:** a genuine threshold moment — a change in material or light underfoot signals "you have arrived somewhere," without announcement.
- **Reception:** warm stone/timber counter, low enough for an easy first exchange, no visible screen/clipboard clutter, name-based greeting supported by clean sightlines to both the Lounge and the Collection Room door.
- **GTT Lounge:** the warmest, most hospitality-led room in the building — natural light prioritised, generously proportioned soft seating (not a transactional waiting-room row), textiles doing real comfort work, the single largest material-quality investment outside the Collection Room.
- **Treatment rooms:** wellness-led, calm, slightly more intimate lighting than the Lounge, curtain-partitioned per the confirmed floor plan — soft textiles and warm timber carry the privacy signal alongside the curtain itself.
- **Hair area:** open-plan per the confirmed layout, treated as a considered styling room, not a salon-strip — warm mirror framing, no harsh vanity-bulb lighting.
- **Nails:** open-plan, same material language as the rest of the Lounge zone despite its functional LEV requirement — the extraction hardware should be as invisible as possible, not a visual anchor.
- **Bathrooms:** the same material and lighting language as the Lounge, scaled down — this is a genuine premium-perception detail (`PREMIUM-POSITIONING.md`'s own finding that consistency, not exclusion, is what signals real quality control).
- **Blood Collection Room:** the one room permitted to look and function clinically — but "clinical" here means genuinely clean, calm, and competent, not sterile-cold. Same warm-neutral wall tone as the rest of the venue where clinical requirements allow it, warm (not blue-white) task lighting at the draw chairs specifically, so the room still visually belongs to the same building even while it does its real clinical job. **This is the specific answer to this round's brief on making the clinical room feel like part of the same brand, not a jarring break** — achieved through colour-temperature and wall-tone continuity, not by softening any genuine clinical fixture requirement.

## 8. Lighting Philosophy

**The precise distinction this brief asks for: calm luxury is not dim spa lighting.** Dim, moody lighting reads as either romantic/nightlife or budget-spa-cliché, neither appropriate here — this brand needs *bright enough to feel confident and clean, warm enough to feel calm,* not dark enough to feel hushed.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Colour temperature | Warm throughout (approx. 2700-3000K) — except the Collection Room, which should sit slightly cooler/neutral (approx. 3000-3500K) for genuine clinical task accuracy without tipping into cold blue-white |
| Ambient | The dominant light source everywhere except the Collection Room — soft, diffused, natural-light-first where the tenancy allows, supplemented by warm indirect fixtures, never a single harsh overhead source |
| Feature lighting | One considered feature moment per key zone (Reception, Lounge) — a single well-chosen fixture, not a scheme of multiple competing statement pieces |
| Decorative lighting | Minimal and functional-adjacent — a considered lamp doing real light work, not lighting as pure ornament |
| Task lighting | Precise and warm-toned in treatment rooms (where a therapist needs real visibility); genuinely clinical-grade (colour-accurate, sufficient lux) only at the Collection Room's draw chairs specifically |
| Per-zone summary | Reception: warm, welcoming, evenly lit, no dim corners at the point of first contact. Lounge: layered warm ambient + soft feature light, the calmest room in the building. Treatment rooms: warm and slightly more intimate, dimmable per session. Collection Room: clean, warm-neutral, genuinely sufficient for clinical accuracy — competent, not cold. |

## 9. Photography Direction

Builds on, does not replace, the already-approved direction (`docs/strategy/BRAND-EXPERIENCE-FOUNDATION.md` §19): natural diffused light, real Perth-representative women, unhurried and intimate rather than posed-corporate, no clinical equipment visible outside the one room that needs it, documentary-style over staged.

- **Subject matter:** real moments within the actual customer journey (arrival, the Lounge, a treatment in progress, departure) — not generic stock "pregnant woman touching belly" imagery.
- **People:** genuine diversity of age (within the target range), body type, and ethnicity reflective of real Perth demographics — never a single narrow beauty-standard cast.
- **Styling:** natural, unstyled-looking hair and makeup — she should look like herself, not like she's been styled for a shoot.
- **Skin tones:** photographed and colour-graded accurately across all represented skin tones — no single "brand grade" that flatters one skin tone at the expense of others.
- **Pregnancy representation:** visible and confident, never the sole visual focus of every image (avoid reducing every subject to "a bump") — she is a whole woman having an appointment, not a symbol of pregnancy.
- **Interiors:** shot with real, warm, natural light — never a flash-lit or clinically bright interior frame.
- **Detail shots:** hands, textiles, the leaving-box object, a considered materials close-up — used to carry texture and warmth without needing a person in every frame.
- **Composition:** generous negative space, off-centre and observational rather than symmetrical/posed — consistent with the "documentary, not staged" instruction.
- **Wardrobe:** her own clothes, in the palette's tonal family where possible but never obviously colour-matched to the brand (a real, common styling mistake that reads as staged).
- **Props:** minimal, real, and functional (the welcome card, a real cup, a real robe) — never decorative props with no function in the actual visit.
- **Editing:** warm, true-to-life colour grading consistent with the brand palette's warmth — not a cold, blue-shifted "clinical" grade, and not an oversaturated "influencer" grade either.
- **What must never appear:** visible medical equipment outside the Collection Room; a stock-photography look or obviously AI-generated imagery; forced smiling/performed joy; any image that reads as maternity-catalogue stock rather than this specific brand's real world.

## 10. Digital Expression

Restrained throughout — a luxury brand's digital presence, not a busy social-media aesthetic. Builds directly on real, already-built work (`outputs/landing-page/`), not a hypothetical.

- **Website:** generous whitespace, Fraunces for headlines only, DM Sans for all body/UI text, Option 1 palette, large uninterrupted photography where used, no more than one accent-coloured element per screen (consistent with §3's 5-8% accent-usage discipline).
- **Booking interface:** calm and functional — the booking flow itself should feel like an extension of the reception experience (name-based, unhurried), not a generic third-party widget aesthetic bolted on.
- **Instagram:** the same restrained palette and photography direction, not a separate "social" visual language — grid consistency over trend-chasing, text overlays used sparingly if at all.
- **Email:** the wordmark in the header, Option 1 palette, DM Sans body copy, generous whitespace — consistent with the already-built `outputs/brand/email-header.html` structure, updated to the new palette once selected.
- **Appointment confirmation:** plain-language, warm, first-name-led — the digital equivalent of the welcome card, not a transactional system-generated notice.
- **Digital gift cards:** a single restrained wordmark treatment on the palette's warm-ivory field — no decorative border, no stock gift-card iconography (ribbons, bows).
- **Mobile screens:** the same restraint discipline as desktop — this project's own landing-page work already established mobile-first practice; nothing in this direction changes that.

| Packaging (departure moment) | **REMOVED 2026-08-19** — the "leaving box" concept this row referenced is NOT approved; see `docs/strategy/BRAND-EXPERIENCE-FOUNDATION.md` §25 for the full removal note. No replacement packaging direction proposed. |

## 12. AM/PM Visual Continuity

No visual sub-branding for AM vs. PM, consistent with `docs/strategy/BRAND-EXPERIENCE-FOUNDATION.md` §13's confirmed "same brand, two registers" architecture. The palette, typography, materials, and lighting language are identical across both — what changes is pacing and specific service context, not the visual system itself. The 12:00-12:30 transition (§8's lighting language, §13 of the Foundation document) can carry a subtle, non-branded atmosphere shift (a slightly warmer ambient level, a quiet music change) without introducing any new colour, typeface, or material — the goal is a felt shift in mood, not a visible rebrand mid-afternoon.

## 13. Do / Don't Rules

**Do:** use Option 1's warm-neutral base for 80%+ of every surface; reserve the accent colour for true single moments (a CTA, a single interior detail); set the wordmark in Fraunces with generous tracking; keep DM Sans for everything functional; let material quality and consistency carry the luxury signal; keep the Collection Room warm-neutral in tone even where its fixtures must be genuinely clinical.

**Don't:** introduce sage, mint, dusty pink, lavender, or baby blue anywhere; use a bright/saturated terracotta as a field colour (the accent must stay muted and minority-share); reach for Italiana or any ornate/fashion-cosplay display serif; add a floral, leaf, or symbolic maternal device; use chrome or brushed steel hardware; use raw/reclaimed timber or exposed concrete (rustic/industrial drift); distribute generic planters throughout the space; run dim, moody "spa" lighting anywhere outside is genuinely intended to feel intimate (treatment rooms only, and even then warm-dimmable, not dark).

## 15. Recommended Direction

**Colour:** Option 1, "Warm Stone" — the most genuinely neutral, name-agnostic, timeless of the three developed options, while still being unmistakably earthy and premium. Options 2 and 3 remain strong, real alternates worth revisiting specifically once a name is chosen, given their slight natural lean toward each candidate's own register (§3).

**Typography:** Fraunces (primary serif, wordmark/headings) + DM Sans (secondary sans, body/UI) — chosen on legibility, warmth, digital range, and longevity grounds, not on appearance alone (§4).

**Logo territory:** Direction A, pure wordmark, as the primary system — with a restrained monogram (Direction B) available only as a small-format secondary asset, and only genuinely strong for one of the two remaining name candidates specifically (§5.1).

**The dual-name test outcome, restated plainly:** this system is confirmed to work for both SOLENA and ELOWEN without compromise at the primary wordmark/typography/colour level — the one real, disclosed asymmetry lives entirely at the optional-monogram layer, not the core identity. This is the evidence that the visual direction is genuinely strategic rather than built around a single preferred name.
