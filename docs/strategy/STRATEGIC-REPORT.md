## The Question This Report Works Backward From

**"If we opened this centre tomorrow, what would make a woman tell her friend six months later: 'You HAVE to go there'?"**

Not one grand gesture. The honest answer, drawn from every document synthesised above: she expected a fluorescent-lit waiting room for a mandatory blood test, and instead nobody made her manage anything herself — she was expected by name, handed an itinerary instead of told to wait, and had her nails or hair done while she waited. The sentence she actually repeats to a friend is concrete, not aspirational: *"they did my nails while I waited for my blood test."* Specificity travels by word of mouth; genericness doesn't (`docs/experience/CUSTOMER-JOURNEY.md`). **REMOVED 2026-08-19:** an earlier version of this section additionally referenced a departure "leaving box" gesture and a food item given at the final draw — neither is approved; removed, not replaced with an alternative concept.

Working backward from that sentence is why the welcome/itinerary card is one of the cheapest, most important interventions in this entire project, why the return-loop's unmodelled conversion rate (§12, §13) is the single highest-priority financial gap rather than a footnote, and why the venue's one clinical room should be the *only* room that looks clinical (§9, §10). None of this requires the naming decision to lock, the venue to be confirmed, or new capital to be raised — it requires discipline in a handful of low-cost, high-leverage moments, applied consistently, starting from day one.
